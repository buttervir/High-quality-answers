# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

## The short version

1. **Money is not your problem. Time, skill and the law are.** Your family is worth about ₹70 crore ($8M at about ₹88 per dollar). But you have 8 to 10 free hours a week, no track record, and at 16 you cannot legally sign a contract in India.
2. **Fund the business the way a venture capital (VC) investor funds a startup.** Start with a small cheque. Release the next cheque only when you hit a clear target. Never hand over a big sum up front.
3. **Start with a service business, not a product.** In the first 8 weeks, test three cheap ideas. Keep the one that gets paying customers fastest. The best default for you is a **tutoring agency for younger IB/IGCSE students**. The backup is a **digital and AI setup service for small businesses in your family's network**.
4. **Targets:** first paying customer by week 4. ₹50,000 a month in revenue by the end of DP1. ₹1 lakh a month in **profit** by the end of DP2. That is "Level 1" job-replacing money: about what a fresher earns at a good Mumbai job.
5. **Your IB grades come first.** Your predicted grades decide your university options, and those matter more than anything this business earns before you turn 18. The plan caps your hours and has a rule that cuts them automatically if grades slip.

## Part 1: Open questions, and how this plan answers them

The request leaves a lot open. Here is each question, with the answer this plan uses.

| # | Open question | Answer used in this plan |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What counts as "job-replacing money"? | Monthly **profit** (not revenue) you could live on instead of a salary. **Level 1:** ₹1 lakh/month (about what a good Mumbai graduate job pays). **Level 2:** ₹2.5 lakh/month (an experienced professional). **Level 3:** ₹8 lakh+/month (a senior manager). This plan aims for Level 1 by the end of DP2 and gives the route to Level 2 after age 18. |
| 2 | How much family money should go in? | Very little at first: at most ₹50,000 in the first two months and at most about ₹14 lakh in total before you turn 18, released in stages. With ₹70 crore behind you, capital is never what stops you. Easy money makes students build things nobody wants to pay for. |
| 3 | Is the $8M available as cash? | Assumed **no**. Most family wealth sits in property, a family business or long-term investments. The plan needs only small amounts. |
| 4 | How much time does a DP1 student really have? | About **8 hours a week** during term, **20 to 25 hours a week** in the long summer break between DP1 and DP2, and **2 hours a week** from January of DP2 until your May exams. |
| 5 | Can a 16-year-old legally run a business in India? | Not in your own name. Under the Indian Contract Act, 1872, a contract signed by a minor is void. So a parent owns the business on paper until you turn 18 (see Part 5). You do the work and make the decisions. |
| 6 | Which business? | Not chosen in advance. You test three ideas cheaply and let paying customers decide (Part 4). There is a default if you want one. |
| 7 | Is the goal learning or money? | Both. But if money alone were the goal, there is a quicker way (see Q8). This plan assumes you want to **earn** it and build a skill. |
| 8 | Isn't the easiest route just investing family money? | Yes. ₹2.5 crore invested at about 7% a year gives about ₹1.45 lakh a month. That is job-replacing money, but it teaches you nothing and is not *your* income. Treat it as the safety net that makes risk-taking affordable, not as the plan. |
| 9 | Does the business count for IB CAS? | Usually **not** the paid part, because CAS experiences are meant to be unpaid. A free part, such as free tutoring for an NGO, may count as Service. Check with your CAS coordinator before you assume anything. |
| 10 | What about university abroad? | The plan pauses the business before exams and can be handed to a manager. A real business with real numbers also makes a strong application essay. |

Figures are in Indian rupees at 2026 prices and are estimates, not guarantees. Tax and legal points are general information: confirm them with a chartered accountant (CA) before you act.

## Part 2: The model this plan copies, and where it does not fit

### The solved problem: how a VC fund backs a startup

Venture capital funds solved a hard problem long ago: how do you put money into an unproven founder without wasting it? Their answer has four parts:

1. **Small first cheque.** Seed money, just enough to test the idea.
2. **Milestones.** The next round comes only after measurable proof: paying customers, revenue, growth.
3. **A board.** Someone outside the founder reviews the numbers every month.
4. **Many small bets, kill fast.** Most ideas are dropped early and cheaply. Money flows to the one that works.

Restaurants have a smaller version of the same idea: **run a pop-up stall before signing a lease.** Test demand cheaply, then commit.

### What this plan takes from it

| VC idea | Your version |
|---|---|
| Seed cheque | ₹50,000 from parents for the 8-week test phase |
| Milestones, then the next round | Four written stages (Part 3). Money for the next stage is released only when the gate is passed |
| Board meeting | A 45-minute monthly review with one parent plus one outside mentor |
| Many bets, kill fast | Three ideas tested at once, two dropped by week 8 |
| Pop-up before the restaurant | Sell the service by hand before building a website, brand or app |

### Where the model does not fit, and the fix for each

| Where it does not fit | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **The founder is a minor.** VC founders sign contracts; you cannot. | Contracts, bank accounts, payment gateways, Upwork, Fiverr, Stripe and Google AdSense all require age 18. | A parent is the legal owner until 18. You become director or owner at 18 (Part 5). |
| **The investor is family.** VCs are strict; parents tend to be soft or to step in. | Without pressure, deadlines slip and failure costs nothing. | Write a one-page **Family Investment Agreement** (Part 3, Step 0) and stick to it. A missed gate means no more money. |
| **The founder already has a full-time job.** IB is about 60 hours a week of school and study. | Grades decide your university choices. | Hard cap on hours, plus an automatic grade rule (Part 6). |
| **VCs chase huge exits.** They accept that 9 out of 10 startups die, because one giant win pays for the rest. | You want **steady monthly profit**, not a 1-in-10 lottery ticket. | Pick **cash-flow businesses** (services first) that make money in month one. No app startups and no fundraising at 16. |
| **VCs bring a network.** | You need advice from someone who has run a business. | Your "board" includes a mentor: a relative or family friend who runs a business, who agrees to a monthly call. |

## Part 3: The plan, stage by stage

### Step 0 (Weeks 1–2): Set up the "fund". Budget: ₹0–₹10,000

Do all of these before you sell anything.

1. **Write the Family Investment Agreement** (one page, signed by you and both parents). It says:
   - The business is yours in practice, even though a parent is the owner on paper until you turn 18.
   - Money is released only at the gates in this plan.
   - The hour cap and the grade rule (Part 6).
   - **Profit split:** 50% reinvested in the business, 30% into your long-term savings (a mutual fund folio in your name, run by your guardian), 20% for you to spend.
   - The maximum total loss your family accepts before you turn 18: ₹3 lakh.
2. **Pick a mentor.** Ask one adult who runs a profitable business (not a parent) for a 30-minute call each month for a year.
3. **Set up money tracking.**
   - The parent who is the legal owner opens a **separate bank account** used only for this business, with a **UPI QR code**.
   - Keep a **Google Sheet** with four tabs: *Sales*, *Costs*, *Customers*, *Hours worked*. Every rupee in or out goes in, the same day.
4. **Book the first "board meeting"**: the first Sunday of every month, 45 minutes, with one parent and the mentor (who can join by video). The agenda never changes: revenue, profit, customers, hours, grades, next month's goal.

### Stage 1, the "Pop-up" (Weeks 3–8): test three ideas. Budget: up to ₹50,000

Run all three tests at the same time, by hand, in about 8 hours a week. Do not build websites or logos yet.

**Test A: Tutoring for younger students (the default favourite)**
- *Who pays:* parents of MYP, IGCSE and ICSE students (ages 11–15) in your school's network and nearby areas (Bandra, Juhu, Powai, Lower Parel, Worli and similar).
- *What you sell:* weekly one-to-one or small-group sessions in a subject you are strong in (Maths, Sciences, English), plus study-skills sessions.
- *Price:* ₹1,000–₹1,500 per hour for one-to-one; ₹600 per student per hour for groups of 3–4. (Experienced IB tutors in Mumbai charge far more, so a strong DP student is a good-value option.)
- *How to get the first customers:* message 20 parents you or your parents know. Script:
  > "Hi [Name], I'm [Your name], in DP1 at [School]. I'm starting weekly Maths sessions for Grade 8–10 students: ₹1,200/hour, at your home or online. The first session is free. If [child] doesn't find it useful, you pay nothing. Would [child] like to try one this Saturday?"
- *Pass mark:* 3 or more families paying for a second session.

**Test B: Digital and AI setup for small businesses**
- *Who pays:* small businesses in your family's network, such as clinics, restaurants, boutiques, CA offices and real-estate brokers.
- *What you sell:* a fixed package. Set up their Google Business Profile, WhatsApp Business catalogue and auto-replies, a simple one-page website, and an AI assistant that answers common customer questions.
- *Price:* ₹15,000 for one-time setup, plus an optional ₹8,000–₹15,000 a month to manage it.
- *How to get the first customers:* ask your parents for 10 introductions. Show each business owner a "before vs after" of their own Google listing.
- *Pass mark:* 2 or more businesses pay for setup.

**Test C: Your own idea**
- Something you understand better than adults do, such as a niche product for students, a service at school events, or a skill you already have (design, video editing, coding).
- *Rule:* it must be sellable by hand in 6 weeks, with under ₹30,000 of stock or tools.
- *Pass mark:* ₹15,000 or more in sales to people who are not relatives.

**Gate 1 (end of week 8): choose ONE**
Keep the test that scores best on these, in this order:
1. Most **repeat** paying customers.
2. Most **profit per hour you worked**.
3. Which one you would happily work on for two years.

Drop the other two completely. If **none** passes, run two new tests for another 6 weeks with a further ₹30,000. That is normal. It is exactly what the pop-up stage is for.

### Stage 2, the "Seed round" (Months 3–8, to the end of DP1): build the one business. Budget: up to ₹3 lakh

Goal: turn a working test into a small, repeatable machine.

**If you picked tutoring, build a tutoring agency:**
1. Month 3: write a one-page syllabus for each subject and a simple monthly progress report for parents (parents pay for visible progress).
2. Month 4: recruit 2–3 tutors: strong DP2 students, recent IB graduates or university students. Pay them ₹700–₹900 per hour. Charge parents ₹1,300–₹1,800. The difference is your margin.
3. Month 4: switch to **monthly prepaid packages**, e.g. 8 sessions for ₹10,000, paid in advance by UPI. That ends chasing payments and gives you predictable cash.
4. Month 5: build a simple website or Instagram page with parent testimonials (with permission). Spend at most ₹15,000.
5. Months 5–8: grow through referrals. Give a free session to any family that brings in a new family.

*The maths to reach the Stage 2 gate:* 60 tutor-hours a month × ₹1,500 = ₹90,000 revenue. Tutor pay of about ₹48,000 leaves ₹42,000 gross profit before small costs.

**If you picked the digital/AI service:**
1. Turn your setup into a written checklist so anyone can repeat it.
2. Hire one freelance designer or developer per project (from Instagram or LinkedIn, contracted through your parent's account) so you are not doing everything yourself.
3. Push every setup client onto a monthly plan. Monthly retainers are what replace a salary.
4. Target: 6 retainers × ₹12,000 = ₹72,000 a month.

**Gate 2 (around the end of DP1):** you must hit **all four** to unlock Stage 3:
- ₹50,000 or more in revenue a month, for **3 months running**
- profit margin of at least 30%
- at least 40% of customers are repeat customers
- the grade rule has not been triggered (Part 6)

### Stage 3, the "Series A" (summer between DP1 and DP2, plus DP2 term 1): systems and a manager. Budget: up to ₹10 lakh

The summer break is your biggest window: 20–25 hours a week. Use it to make the business run **without you**, because DP2 will take your time back.

1. **Hire a part-time operations manager** (a university student or young graduate, ₹15,000–₹25,000 a month, contracted by your parent) to handle scheduling, payments and customer messages.
2. **Write the operating manual**: how to onboard a customer, how to hire a tutor or freelancer, what to do if someone complains, and how to collect payment.
3. **Add a second offer** for existing customers, such as exam-season crash courses (tutoring) or ad management (digital). Selling more to existing customers is the cheapest growth there is.
4. **Spend on marketing only where it has worked**: test ₹20,000 of Instagram or Google ads, and keep spending only if each new customer costs less than one month of their fees.
5. **Formalise the company** if revenue is above about ₹1 lakh a month (Part 5).

**Stage 3 target (by December of DP2): ₹1 lakh a month profit. That is Level 1 job-replacing money.**

### Freeze (January to May of DP2): exams first

- Your time on the business drops to **2 hours a week**: one call with your manager, and the monthly board meeting.
- No new products, no new hires, no new spending.
- Losing some revenue during this period is fine. Your final IB score is not something you get a second chance at.

### Stage 4 (after exams, age 18): you take full control

1. Within the first month after your 18th birthday, you are appointed **director** of the company (or become the proprietor) and open accounts in your own name.
2. Choose your path:
   - **University + business:** your manager runs daily operations and you work 8–10 hours a week remotely.
   - **Gap year:** go full time on the business with a target of Level 2 (₹2.5 lakh a month profit).
3. **Bigger family money only now, and only as a loan or equity with terms:** e.g. ₹25–50 lakh for a second city, a learning centre or a product line, released against the same kind of gates.
4. **Your option for later:** once the service business is stable, a product brand (D2C) becomes a sensible next bet. You will have cash flow, customers and the legal capacity to sign with suppliers and marketplaces.

## Part 4: Why these business types, and what to avoid

**Why services first:** you can make money in week one, they need almost no capital, they teach you sales (the single most valuable business skill), and they draw on your two real advantages: being a strong IB student and having a family network of business owners and well-off parents.

**Avoid these, even though they are tempting:**

| Idea | Why not, for you, now |
|---|---|
| Stock futures and options (F&O) trading or crypto trading | SEBI's own studies found about 9 out of 10 individual F&O traders lost money. It is a way to lose money, not a business. |
| Buying a franchise (café, education brand) | It ties up ₹30 lakh or more of family money in something you cannot legally sign for or run during school hours. |
| Dropshipping cheap imported products | Thin margins, quality complaints, and GST registration is usually needed for selling goods across state lines online. |
| Building an app and raising money | That is a VC-style lottery ticket, and it needs full-time work. |
| Any multi-level marketing (MLM) or "passive income course" | These make money for the person selling the course, not for you. |

## Part 5: Legal, tax and accounts (exact setup)

Confirm each step with a CA. Budget about ₹15,000–₹30,000 a year for one.

**Until you turn 18:**
1. **Owner:** one parent runs it as a **sole proprietorship** (no registration needed to start). A free **Udyam (MSME) registration** in the parent's name is optional but useful.
2. **Bank:** a separate account in that parent's name, used only for business, with a UPI QR code for customer payments.
3. **Platforms:** use your parent's accounts for anything with an 18+ rule (payment gateways, Upwork, Fiverr, Google AdSense, marketplaces). Do not lie about your age on any platform.
4. **GST:** for services, registration is needed once yearly turnover passes **₹20 lakh**. Most of Stage 2 stays below that. For physical goods sold online, the rules differ, so ask the CA before selling any product.
5. **Income tax:** in India, a minor's income is generally **added to a parent's income** (the "clubbing" rule, Section 64(1A) of the old 1961 Act, carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025, from April 2026; ask your CA for the current section number). Income from a minor's **own skill**, such as tutoring you do personally, can be an exception. Because the business is in your parent's name, it is simply taxed as your parent's business income. Keep clean records either way.
6. **Mumbai shop rules:** with fewer than 10 workers and no physical shop, you usually need no Shops and Establishments registration. Recheck if you open a learning centre.

**When revenue passes about ₹1 lakh a month, or you hire regularly:**
- Form a **Private Limited Company** (about ₹10,000–₹20,000 through an online CA service). Your **parents are the two directors**. **You hold shares through your guardian**, and the Family Investment Agreement records that the business belongs to you.
- At 18 you are added as a director.

## Part 6: Protecting your IB grades

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Weekly cap** | 8 hours in term (Saturday 3 h, Sunday 3 h, two 1-hour weekday slots). 20–25 hours in long breaks. 2 hours from January of DP2 until exams. |
| **Grade rule** | If any subject's predicted grade drops by 1 point or more, business hours **halve** next month. If it drops twice, the business pauses until the grade recovers. |
| **No-work weeks** | Internal exam weeks, the week before an IA or Extended Essay deadline, and mock exams. |
| **Use it in school, honestly** | Your Business Management or Economics coursework may draw on what you learn. Ask your teachers what is allowed. Do not count paid work as CAS. |
