# The Plan: A 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai Building Job-Replacing Income

## Assumptions

- **Timeline.** It is October 2026. DP1 started mid-2026 and DP2 final exams are in **May 2028**. The plan runs over those ~19 months and then hands over to whatever comes after school (university in India or abroad).
- **"Job-replacing money"** means a **net profit of ₹1,00,000 per month**, sustained for at least 3 months. That is roughly what a good Mumbai graduate job pays in take-home salary. The stretch target is ₹2,50,000 per month. These are profit figures, not revenue.
- **Family wealth** of about $8M (roughly ₹70 crore) is a safety net and a source of contacts. It is **not** the business's fuel. The student has to earn the result. Otherwise it is the parents' business with the child's name on it.
- **The student is a minor until their 18th birthday.** Every legal step below is designed around that.
- Indian law, tax and payment rules change often, and the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. **Before you register anything, have a Chartered Accountant (CA) confirm the tax and registration points below in a 1-hour paid consultation (about ₹3,000–₹10,000).** This plan tells you what to ask.

## Part 1: How this goes wrong (and the rule that prevents each failure)

The best plan is the one that cannot fail in the usual ways. These are the ways a plan like this actually dies, with the guardrail built into the plan for each.

| # | Failure mode | Why it happens to *this* student | Guardrail in this plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Grades crash** and university options shrink | The IB is about 30–40 hrs/week of class plus homework, IAs, the Extended Essay, TOK and CAS | Hard cap: **8 hrs/week** in DP1 and **4 hrs/week** in DP2. Business freezes during exam windows. The business must run without the founder by month 9 (Part 4) |
| 2 | **Rich-kid vanity startup**: lots of capital, no customers | ₹50 lakh is "affordable", so the student builds a brand, website and office before anyone pays | Capital is **released in tranches only after revenue milestones** (Part 5). The starting budget is **₹25,000** |
| 3 | **Parents end up running it** | Parents sign everything, hire everyone, make the calls | Parents sign only what the law requires a major to sign. The student owns sales, delivery, pricing and hiring decisions (written family agreement, Part 3) |
| 4 | **Legally void contracts** | Under the Indian Contract Act, a contract with a minor is void (*Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*, 1903) | Customers pay for each session in advance, so the business never relies on enforcing a contract against them. Any contract the business has to enforce (tutors, vendors, leases) is signed by a parent or a family-controlled entity (Part 3) |
| 5 | **Can't collect money** | Razorpay, Stripe, current accounts and GST registration all need an adult or an entity | Stage A: UPI into the student's own minor savings account. Stage B: an entity with a parent as signatory (Part 3) |
| 6 | **Tax surprise or clubbing** | A minor's income is normally added to the parent's income and taxed at the parent's (likely 30%+) rate | Income from the minor's **own skill or specialised knowledge is not clubbed**. So the business is skill-based and the student earns it personally (Part 3) |
| 7 | **Wrong business type**: needs time, money or adulthood the student lacks | Restaurants, franchises, D2C brands running heavy ads, real estate | Business chosen with a 6-test filter (Part 2) |
| 8 | **Gambling disguised as business** | Family money plus a phone means F&O, crypto and "trading" look like income | **Banned.** SEBI's own study found about 93% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY22–FY24. Minors can't legally trade F&O in their own name anyway |
| 9 | **Academic misconduct** | Tutoring IB/IGCSE students drifts into "just write my IA" | Written rule: **no writing or editing of assessed work**, ever. A breach can cost the student's own IB diploma and the clients' diplomas too |
| 10 | **Child safety / data law breach** | The business serves minors and collects their data | Safeguarding policy plus parental consent under the DPDP Act 2023 and its 2025 Rules (Part 6) |
| 11 | **School conflict** | Some schools restrict students' commercial activity or soliciting on school channels | Tell the school (DP coordinator) in writing up front. Never market in school WhatsApp groups without permission |
| 12 | **Option paralysis / quitting at month 3** | Too many ideas, no kill rules | One primary path, one fallback, **numeric kill/continue gates** at weeks 8, 16 and month 9 |
| 13 | **Business dies when the student leaves for university** | Founder-dependent services stop the day the founder boards a plane. US F-1 visa rules also restrict working in the US | By DP2 the student is the owner/manager, not the worker. A paid manager runs operations (Part 4, Phase 4) |
| 14 | **Unrealistic income math** | "₹1 lakh/month" sounds easy until you count hours | Every phase has its own revenue math, shown below |

## Part 2: Which business, and why

### The 6-test filter

The business must pass **all six**:

1. **Starts for under ₹25,000.** That rules out vanity spending (failure 2).
2. **Paid in advance or on delivery**, so there are no receivables to chase from adults who know you're 16 (failures 4 and 5).
3. **Runs on the student's own skill**, so income is not clubbed with the parent's and tax is low (failure 6).
4. **Fits in 8 hrs/week** and can be handed to others (failures 1 and 13).
5. **Has leverage**: the student can earn from other people's hours, not only their own. One person's hours can't reach ₹1 lakh/month in 8 hrs/week.
6. **Uses an unfair advantage this student actually has**: being inside the IB system, living in an affluent Mumbai network, and having a family business network.

### Primary path: an IB/IGCSE/MYP academic-support business, run student-to-student

**What it is.** Tutoring and exam-prep small groups for **Grades 6–10 (MYP and IGCSE) students** at Mumbai's international schools. The tutors are high-performing **DP2 students and recent IB graduates** (18+ only, see Part 6). The student founder sells, builds the curriculum, quality-checks and manages. They tutor only at the start, to learn the product.

**Why this passes the filter:**
- Mumbai has one of India's densest clusters of IB and Cambridge schools: Bandra, Juhu, Powai, Lower Parel, Worli, BKC, Malabar Hill, Andheri and Navi Mumbai. Parents there already pay for tutoring.
- A 16-year-old who is *currently* doing the IB is believable to a parent of a Grade 8 student in a way that a generic tutor is not.
- The student's own MYP/IGCSE results are proof of skill. The first clients come from the family network.
- It scales by adding tutors, not hours, and it is cash-in-advance.

**Indicative pricing** (verify in Week 1 by calling 5 tutoring providers and asking 10 parents):
- Founder 1:1 sessions: **₹1,000–₹1,500/hr**.
- Small groups of 4 students: **₹600–₹800 per student per hr**. That is ₹2,400–₹3,200/hr of revenue for one tutor-hour.
- Exam crash courses (4 weeks, 8 sessions, groups of 4–6): **₹8,000–₹15,000 per student**.
- Tutor pay: **₹500–₹900/hr**. Target business margin: **40–55%**.

**The math to ₹1 lakh/month profit:**
- 10 tutors × 6 group-hours/week × ₹2,800 revenue/hr × 4.3 weeks = **₹7.2 lakh/month revenue**.
- Minus tutor pay (10 × 6 × ₹800 × 4.3 = ₹2.06 lakh) = ₹5.2 lakh gross margin.
- Minus manager, tools, marketing and contingency (~₹1.5–2.5 lakh) = **₹2.7–3.7 lakh/month profit at full scale**.
- **₹1 lakh/month profit needs roughly 4–5 active tutors at that load.** That is reachable within 9–12 months.

### Fallback path: AI/automation services for family-network SMEs

Switch to this **only if the primary path fails its Week-8 gate**, or if the student hates teaching.

**What it is.** Setting up WhatsApp Business catalogues, Google Business Profiles, simple automations (lead forms → Google Sheets → WhatsApp follow-ups), AI chat responders and basic websites for small and mid-size businesses. Clients come from the family's supplier, customer and friend network.

- **Pricing:** setup ₹15,000–₹40,000, then a **₹5,000–₹12,000/month** maintenance retainer.
- **Math:** ~12 retainer clients at ₹8,500 average ≈ ₹1 lakh/month, mostly profit.
- **Weakness:** payment terms with businesses are often 30–60 days (failure 4). So it needs a parent-signed entity earlier. Always take **50% upfront**.

### Explicitly rejected (and why)

| Idea | Fails test |
|---|---|
| Café / cloud kitchen / franchise | Capital ₹20–80 lakh, a lease contract, staff, daily presence (tests 1, 2, 4) |
| D2C product with paid ads | Ad burn and inventory. The family can cover losses, so losses won't be confronted (test 1, failure 2) |
| Stock/F&O/crypto trading | Not a business. Most participants lose money (failure 8) |
| YouTube/Instagram creator as the *main* plan | Income takes 12–24 months and isn't controllable. Fine as the *marketing channel* for the main plan |
| "Investing" family money in a friend's startup | That is the parents' money and the parents' decision. It earns the student nothing |

## Part 3: Legal, banking and tax setup (exact steps)

### Stage A: from Week 1 until revenue passes ~₹50,000/month or the first non-family tutor is hired

The student operates as an **individual earning from personal skill**.

1. **PAN for the student.** Apply online via Protean (NSDL) or UTIITSL with Form 49A. A parent signs as representative. Cost about ₹100.
2. **Minor savings account in the student's own name** at a bank that lets minors aged 10+ operate it themselves with UPI and a debit card. Most large private banks have "junior"/"teen" accounts; check UPI limits at the branch. Do not use a parent's account, because that muddies the tax trail (failure 6).
3. **Collect payment by UPI** to that account, **in advance**, for each package of sessions. Use a UPI QR plus a payment-confirmation message. No credit and no invoices sent "for later".
4. **Records:** a Google Sheet with one row per payment (date, payer, student served, sessions, amount, UPI reference). From Month 3, move to Zoho Books (free tier) or similar.
5. **Tax:** income from the minor's own skill and specialised knowledge is taxed **in the minor's own hands, not clubbed** with the parent. (This was section 64(1A) proviso under the 1961 Act. Ask the CA to confirm the corresponding Income-tax Act, 2025 section.) Under the new regime, a resident individual's income up to ₹12 lakh/year has **zero tax due to the rebate**. **File an ITR every year anyway.** It creates a clean income record, useful for visas and university financial forms.
6. **GST:** not needed until service turnover approaches **₹20 lakh/year** (the threshold for services in Maharashtra). Private coaching is taxable at 18% above that.
7. **School:** send the DP coordinator a short email describing the activity, the hours cap and the no-assessed-work rule. Keep the reply.

### Stage B: when the business hires non-family tutors or crosses ₹50,000/month

Contracts with tutors and the need for a payment gateway trigger failures 4 and 5. So create an entity.

- **Recommended: a Private Limited Company.**
  - Directors: **one or two parents** (a minor cannot be a director).
  - Shareholding: **the student holds the majority (e.g., 74–90%)** as a minor shareholder, with a parent as guardian.
  - Parents hold the rest. This also satisfies the two-shareholder minimum.
  - Incorporation via SPICe+ on the MCA portal costs about **₹8,000–₹20,000** all-in through a CA or company secretary.
- **Alternative: an LLP** is cheaper to run, but a minor cannot be a partner. Admit the student when they turn 18, which makes the ownership story weaker until then.
- **Ask the CA specifically** about:
  - **Paying the student a salary or consulting fee for work performed.** This may stay outside clubbing as skill income.
  - Dividends to a minor shareholder, which **are** clubbed with the parent's income. So in Stage B the student is paid mainly for work, not through dividends.
- **After incorporation:**
  - Open a company current account.
  - Set up a **payment gateway** (Razorpay/Cashfree) on the company's KYC.
  - Register under the **Maharashtra Shops and Establishments Act** (online intimation for small establishments).
  - Register for GST when approaching the threshold.
- **Tutor contracts** are signed by a parent-director on behalf of the company. Include:
  - Contractor status.
  - Per-hour rate.
  - The no-assessed-work clause.
  - Safeguarding obligations.
  - A non-solicitation clause: they may not take the business's clients privately for 12 months.
- **When the student turns 18:** appoint them director, get a DIN, and have them sign everything themselves from then on.

### The family agreement (one page, signed by student and parents, Week 1)

1. The business belongs to the student. Parents sign only where the law requires an adult.
2. Parents may advise when asked. They may not hire, price, sell or deliver.
3. Capital is released only by the tranche rules in Part 5. Each tranche is recorded as a **loan, repayable from profit**, or a gift. Choose one and write it down.
4. Academic guardrails: if any subject's predicted grade drops by 1 point or more, the business goes to **2 hrs/week until it recovers**.
5. A quarterly review with parents: numbers, grades, hours.

## Part 4: The 19-month execution plan (dated)

### Phase 0, Weeks 1–2 (late Oct 2026): set up and research. Budget ₹0–₹3,000

- [ ] Sign the family agreement (Part 3).
- [ ] Apply for the student's PAN. Open the minor savings account with UPI.
- [ ] Email the DP coordinator (Part 3, step 7).
- [ ] **Interview 15 parents of Grade 6–10 international-school students**, using family and friends' introductions. Ask:
  - "What are you paying for tutoring now?"
  - "What's the hardest subject or exam for your child?"
  - "Would you pay ₹X for a 4-student group led by a current IB student?"
- [ ] Call 5 existing tutoring providers as a parent and note their prices.
- [ ] Pick **2 subjects** where the student scored highest in MYP/IGCSE, e.g., Maths and Sciences, or English Language & Literature.
- [ ] **Deliverable:** a one-page offer with subject, grade band, format, price and schedule.

### Phase 1, Weeks 3–8 (Nov–mid-Dec 2026): first paying customers. Budget ≤ ₹10,000

- [ ] The founder personally teaches **2 small groups** (Saturdays/Sundays, 90 min each). That is about 3 teaching hrs + 2 prep hrs + 1 admin hr = **6 hrs/week**.
- [ ] Sell through:
  - Warm intros from the 15 interviewed parents.
  - A **free 45-minute "how to study for MYP eAssessment / IGCSE" parent workshop** in a housing-society clubhouse or online. Pitch the group at the end.
  - A WhatsApp Business profile with a catalogue.
- [ ] Payment: an **advance 4-week package** by UPI.
- [ ] Collect a written testimonial and the child's before/after test score from every family.
- [ ] **Week-8 gate:**
  - **Continue** if ≥ **6 paying students** and ≥ **₹25,000 collected** in total.
  - **Kill and switch to the fallback path** if fewer than 3 paying students. Run Phase 1 again for the fallback with the same timeline.
  - In between: run 4 more weeks, then re-check.

### Phase 2, Weeks 9–16 (mid-Dec 2026 – Feb 2027): first tutors. Budget ≤ ₹25,000 (tranche 1)

- [ ] **Recruit 2–3 tutors.** Recent IB graduates aged 18+ at Mumbai colleges, on a gap year, or studying online. Find them through school alumni networks and LinkedIn.
  - Screening: an IB score of 38+ or a 7 in the subject, a demo lesson, and 2 references.
- [ ] **Write the curriculum kit** for each offered course. This is the founder's main job now, and it is what makes tutors interchangeable: a session-by-session plan, practice sets and a parent progress-report template.
- [ ] The founder steps down to teaching 1 group and spends the rest on sales and QA.
- [ ] Tools:
  - Google Workspace (~₹160–₹300/user/month).
  - Google Meet/Zoom for online groups.
  - Cal.com or Calendly for bookings.
  - A Google Sheet CRM.
- [ ] Marketing: an Instagram account posting 2 short "study tip" reels a week (also the YouTube-lite channel), parent referral credit of **₹1,000 per referred paying family**, and a parent workshop each month.
- [ ] **Week-16 gate:**
  - **Continue** if ≥ **₹60,000/month revenue** and ≥ 2 tutors active with parent ratings ≥ 4.5/5.
  - Otherwise, fix the weakest link (sales, or tutor quality) for 4 weeks. If still below, cap the business at the founder's own small groups (₹20–40k/month) and reassess after DP1 exams.

### Phase 3, Months 5–9 (Mar–Jul 2027): incorporate and scale. Tranche 2 ≤ ₹1.5 lakh

- [ ] Incorporate (Stage B, Part 3). Move payments to the company gateway. Sign tutor contracts.
- [ ] Grow to **5–8 tutors** and **crash courses before the May 2027 exam season** (sold for students *in other grades*; the founder's own DP1 exams come first).
- [ ] **Business freeze for the founder: 3 weeks around DP1 end-of-year exams.** The tutors and a lead tutor run the schedule. This is the first "can it run without me?" test (failure 13).
- [ ] Hire a **part-time operations manager** (₹20,000–₹35,000/month). This could be a college student or a homemaker with an education background. They handle scheduling, payments follow-up and parent communication.
- [ ] Summer (Jun–Jul 2027) is the growth window: holiday bootcamps and pre-year "head start" courses. The founder can go up to **15 hrs/week for 4 weeks**, but **not during IA/EE writing time**; schedule those first.
- [ ] **Month-9 gate:**
  - Target ≥ **₹1,00,000/month profit** for 2 consecutive months: **job-replacing income reached.**
  - If at ₹40,000–₹1,00,000, continue with the same model.
  - If below ₹40,000 after incorporation, freeze growth and protect DP2.

### Phase 4, Months 10–19 (Aug 2027 – May 2028): DP2. The founder becomes the owner

- [ ] Founder time drops to **4 hrs/week**: a weekly numbers review, tutor hiring interviews and one parent workshop a month.
- [ ] Promote the ops manager to **full-time manager** (₹40,000–₹60,000/month plus 5–10% of profit above ₹1 lakh/month).
- [ ] **Zero founder time from January 2028 to the end of May 2028** (mocks and final exams). The manager runs it.
- [ ] Write the **operations manual**: hiring, onboarding, curriculum, safeguarding, pricing, refunds and monthly reports. It is what makes the business survive the founder leaving for university.
- [ ] After the May 2028 exams, decide:
  - **(a) Keep and own.** Run it remotely as an owner. Note: on a US F-1 visa, actively working is restricted; passive ownership of a foreign company is generally different. Get immigration advice before term starts.
  - **(b) Expand** to Pune, Bengaluru and Delhi international schools, or online across India.
  - **(c) Sell** to an existing tutoring company. A profitable tutoring business with documented systems is sellable.

## Part 5: How family money is used (tranche rules)

The family can afford anything, which is exactly why spending must be **earned**.

| Tranche | Unlocks when | Max amount | Allowed uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed | Day 1 | ₹25,000 | PAN, workshop materials, Workspace, printing |
| 1 | Week-8 gate passed | ₹25,000 | Tutor trial pay, Instagram boosts (≤ ₹5,000), tools |
| 2 | Week-16 gate passed | ₹1,50,000 | Incorporation, CA, gateway, ops manager's first 2 months |
| 3 | 2 months ≥ ₹1 lakh profit | ₹5,00,000 | Manager salary buffer, expansion to a new city/online, a small rented room for groups |

Rules:
- No office, no paid branding agency, no app development **before tranche 3**. Use a ₹0–₹2,000/year website (e.g., Google Sites or Carrd) until then.
- Every rupee is logged. Tranches are loans or gifts as decided in the family agreement. Gifts from relatives (as defined in tax law) are tax-exempt for the receiver. Confirm documentation with the CA.
- Parents' main value is **contacts, not cash**. Ask for **20 warm introductions** to families with Grade 6–10 children, and later to school PTA heads.

## Part 6: Safety, compliance and integrity rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Academic integrity:**
   - Tutors teach skills and give practice.
   - They **never** write, edit or "fix" any IA, EE, TOK essay, coursework or school-assessed task.
   - They never complete homework for a client.
   - Say this in parent terms and tutor contracts. Fire on a first breach.
2. **Safeguarding (clients are minors):**
   - Tutors must be **18+**.
   - Run ID checks and 2 references, and get police verification for anyone teaching in person.
   - Online sessions are in groups, on the business's own account, with the parent able to join.
   - In-person sessions happen only in common areas or with a guardian at home.
   - No private DMs between tutors and children. All communication goes through a parent group or the business's WhatsApp.
   - A written complaint process goes directly to the founder's parent as an adult escalation contact.
3. **Data protection (DPDP Act 2023 and Rules):**
   - Collect only the child's name, grade, school, subject and the parent's contact details.
   - Get **verifiable parental consent** (a signed form or a consent message from the parent's registered number).
   - No behavioural tracking or targeted ads at children.
   - Delete data 12 months after a client leaves.
4. **Child-labour law** is not an issue: a 16-year-old running their own non-hazardous enterprise is permitted. **Tutors** must be 18+ for safeguarding reasons, not labour law.
5. **Refund policy, written:** unused sessions are refundable in full with 7 days' notice. This avoids disputes with parents you can't contractually enforce against.
6. **CAS:** IB guidance treats paid work as not normally counting for CAS. Run a **separate free arm** instead, for example free weekly maths sessions for an NGO or municipal school (counts as Service), using the same curriculum kit. It also gives the business tutor candidates and a real story for university essays.

## Part 7: The weekly operating rhythm

**DP1 (≤ 8 hrs/week):**
- **Saturday:** 3 hrs teaching (Phases 1–2) or sales calls and tutor QA (Phase 3+).
- **Sunday:** 2 hrs curriculum work and content, 30 min on the numbers sheet.
- **Weekdays:** 15 min/day of WhatsApp replies, batched after homework. No business on school nights after 9 pm.

**DP2 (≤ 4 hrs/week):**
- **Sunday:** a 1-hr manager meeting, 1 hr of numbers and decisions, 2 hrs of hiring/QA or a monthly parent workshop.

**The 6 numbers to track every week:**
1. Paying students
2. Revenue collected
3. Tutor-hours delivered
4. Gross margin %
5. Parent rating
6. Founder hours spent

The 6th number protects the IB.
