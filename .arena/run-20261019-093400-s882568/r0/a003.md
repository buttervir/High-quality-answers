# The plan: ₹1 lakh a month by DP2 exams, with one business and almost no setup

## Assumptions
- **"Job replacing money" = ₹1,00,000 a month (₹12 lakh a year).** That is more than most Mumbai graduate jobs pay at the start. It is also the most a resident individual can earn under India's new tax regime and pay no income tax, thanks to the rebate. That is why I chose this number.
- **Timeline:** today is October 2026, so the student is about 2 months into DP1. DP2 final exams are in May 2028.
- **The IB Diploma comes first.** Final grades decide university options, and those are worth more than any income a teenager can earn. So the business gets **8 hours a week at most**.
- **The student was strong in Grade 9–10 Maths or Science** (a 6–7 in MYP or an A/A* in IGCSE). If not, use whichever subject they scored highest in. The plan works the same way.
- Legal and tax points are current Indian rules as far as I know. Have the family's CA confirm them once, in Week 1.

---

## 1. The finished result (May 2028)

This is what "done" looks like:

- **3 batches of 4 students each** in Grade 9–10 Maths (MYP 4–5 / IGCSE) from Mumbai international schools.
- **Fee: ₹8,000 per student per month**, paid in advance. Each batch meets **2 × 1 hour a week, online**. Total: 12 students × ₹8,000 = **₹96,000 a month, about ₹1 lakh**.
- **6 teaching hours a week, plus 2 for prep and admin.** The same lesson set is reused for all three batches.
- **One trained substitute tutor** takes the batches during the student's own exam months. He or she is paid ₹500 per hour of teaching. Without a substitute the income stops exactly when the job-replacing test matters most, so this is the one extra moving part I kept.
- **A waitlist of at least 4 families**, so a student leaving does not cut income.
- **Paperwork:** one bank account in the student's name, a PAN, a spreadsheet, and one tax return a year. No company, no GST, no app, no website, no investors.
- **Proof it is real:** ₹90,000 or more collected for **3 months in a row**.

**What I left out, and why:**
- **No D2C brand, e-commerce, app or reselling.** Each one needs inventory or GST or payment gateways or adult signatures, or money that could be lost. A minor cannot sign a binding contract in India (Indian Contract Act s.11), so every extra counterparty becomes a parent's job.
- **No use of the family's $8M as capital.** Money is not what is holding this back. Time, trust and a skill are. The family's real advantage is its **network**: parents' friends whose children go to IB/IGCSE schools. The plan uses that network, not the money.

## 3. The steps

### Week 1 (now): set up, 4 hours total
1. **Pick one subject and one grade band.** Example: "IGCSE / MYP Grade 9–10 Maths." Only one.
2. **Bank account:** open a minor's savings account that the student operates alone, with a parent as guardian. RBI allows this from age 10 (Kotak, HDFC and ICICI all offer one). All fees go here and nothing else does, so the records stay clean.
3. **PAN for the student:** apply on Form 49A through the NSDL/Protean site, with a parent signing as representative. It costs about ₹110. The student needs it to file their own tax return.
4. **Equipment, ₹40,000 at most:** a drawing tablet (a Wacom One or an old iPad), a USB mic, and a Google Workspace or Zoom Pro account. Teach on Google Meet or Zoom. Online classes avoid Mumbai traffic and allow students from Bandra to Powai in one batch.
5. **One-page offer.** Write it as a PDF or one WhatsApp message:
   > "I'm [Name], DP1 at [School]. I scored [7 in MYP Maths / A* IGCSE]. I'm starting a small-group Grade 9–10 Maths class: 4 students max, 2 × 1 hr/week online, Tue/Thu 6–7 pm. ₹8,000/month, paid in advance. **Free demo class Saturday [date], 11 am.** Reply if your child should join."
6. **Check the rules:** ask the school's DP coordinator whether students may run paid tutoring. If they object to tutoring students from your own school, recruit only from other schools. Note that paid work does **not** count as CAS Service.

### Weeks 2–4: first batch
7. **The student's parents** send the offer to about 30 families with Grade 8–10 children. Use direct personal messages, not group broadcasts. Ask each family for one more name. **Target: 8 at the demo.**
8. **Run the free demo:** 60 minutes, 20 of them on a real topic the students find hard (for example quadratics or simultaneous equations). End with a 5-question quiz and send each parent their child's score that evening.
9. **Close the sale:** "4 seats, first 4 to pay ₹8,000 for [month] get them." Payment by UPI or NEFT to the student's account.
10. **Decision rule:**
    - **4 pay:** the batch is full. Go to step 11.
    - **2–3 pay:** start anyway. Fill the empty seats from the next demo.
    - **0–1 pay:** keep the price but repeat the demo with a new list. If the second demo also fails, drop to ₹6,000. ₹6,000 × 16 students (4 batches) still gives ₹96,000, but it costs 2 more hours a week.

### Months 2–8 (Dec 2026 to Jun 2027): make it work, then fill batch 2
11. **Fixed routine for each batch.**
    - **Tuesday:** teach the topic the school is covering this week.
    - **Thursday:** past-paper questions on it.
    - **Last class of the month:** a 15-minute test.
12. **Progress note on the 1st of each month:** 3 lines per child (test score, what improved, what's next), sent to the parent with the invoice. This note is what drives referrals and renewals. Never skip it.
13. **Collect fees by the 1st, before the class.** A student who has not paid by the 5th loses the seat to the waitlist. No credit, no exceptions. This also avoids the "minor can't enforce a contract" problem entirely.
14. **Open Batch 2** as soon as Batch 1 has 4 paying students for 2 months. Ask each current parent for 2 names, then run another demo. **Target: 8 students, ₹64,000/month by June 2027.**

### Months 9–12 (Jul–Oct 2027, start of DP2): batch 3 and the substitute
15. **June 2027 is the main intake**, because families plan tutoring for the new school year. Run one demo in June. **Fill Batch 3.** Total: 12 students, ₹96,000/month.
16. **Hire the substitute** by October 2027: a strong DP2 student from another school or a first-year undergraduate. Pay ₹500/hour. They teach Thursday classes for 1 month while the student watches. After that they can cover any batch alone.
17. **Freeze growth here.** Put new families on the waitlist and do not open Batch 4 during DP2. EE, IAs and TOK need that time.

### Months 13–19 (Nov 2027 to May 2028): prove it
18. **Hand over during mocks and finals.** During mock exams (around Jan–Feb) and finals (May), the substitute teaches all batches.
    - Cost: about 24 hours × ₹500 = ₹12,000 for the month.
    - The business still nets about ₹84,000 that month.
19. **Hit the proof target:** ₹90,000 or more collected for 3 months in a row, recorded in the spreadsheet.

## 5. Money, tax and law: the whole list

- **Tax on the student's income.** A minor's income is normally added to a parent's income. The exception is income from the minor's own **skill, talent or specialised knowledge**, which tutoring is. That rule is in the old Act's s.64(1A) proviso and carries into the Income-tax Act 2025. So the fees are taxed as the **student's own income**, under the new regime.
- **How much tax.** Up to ₹12 lakh a year, the rebate means **no income tax is payable**. The student must still **file an ITR every July**, and the family's CA does this. ₹96,000 × 12 = ₹11.5 lakh, just under that limit.
- **GST:** no registration is needed until service receipts pass **₹20 lakh a year**, and this plan stays well under.
- **No company or LLP:** a minor cannot validly be a director, and nothing in this plan needs a company.
- **Child safety:**
  - Groups only, online, with cameras on.
  - Parents get the meeting link and may watch.
  - No 1-to-1 in-person sessions with younger children.
- **Records:** one spreadsheet with columns Date | Student | Parent | Amount | Mode | Month covered. Keep every expense receipt.

**Total cash risked: about ₹40,000.** That is equipment the student keeps whatever happens.

## 6. After May 2028 (only once the proof target is hit)

Once 18, the same business scales by doing more of what already works:
- Hire 2–3 tutors at ₹500–600/hour and charge the same ₹8,000.
- Each batch then earns about ₹32,000 in revenue for about ₹4,800–5,800 in tutor cost.
- Register an LLP or proprietorship and GST when receipts approach ₹20 lakh.
- Add a payment gateway, which needs an adult PAN.

This is also the first point at which family money might make sense, for example ads or a physical centre. Use it only if the numbers from the previous 19 months show that a paid family is worth more than it costs to get one.
