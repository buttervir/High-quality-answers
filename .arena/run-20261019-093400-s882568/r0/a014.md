# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

This is for a 16-year-old in DP1 in Mumbai whose family is worth about US$8M (about ₹70 crore). The goal is to start a business that earns enough to replace a salary. The plan starts from your real situation: you have little free time, you are a minor under Indian law, money is not your problem, and your most valuable asset is something most founders don't have. That asset is direct access to Mumbai's IB families.

---

## Assumptions

1. **Timeline.** You started DP1 around June to August 2026. Your final IB exams are in **May 2028**, and you turn 18 around 2028.
2. **"Job-replacing money"** means **₹1,00,000 a month in net profit that keeps coming in**. That is roughly the take-home pay of a good first job in Mumbai (₹12–15 LPA CTC). It should come from a business, not from family money that has been moved into your name.
3. **The IB Diploma comes first.** A plan that costs you 5–7 points has failed, because that drop changes which universities you can get into, and that is worth more than any revenue in the first two years.
4. **Your parents will help** with paperwork that needs an adult (contracts, the payment gateway, bank accounts) and with a capped amount of starting capital.
5. **You are a strong student**, predicted 38 or more, or very strong in two or three subjects. If you aren't, use Path B in Section 6. The legal setup, the time plan and the money rules stay the same.

## 1. Ten questions to answer before starting

**Q1. What does the 16-year-old actually do on Monday?**
They go to school, have IAs, the Extended Essay, TOK and CAS ahead of them, and realistically have **6–8 hours a week** during term. In holidays they have **25–30 hours a week**. Any plan that needs 20 hours a week during term will fail by December.
→ *Effect on the plan: the business must run in small blocks of time, follow the IB calendar, and be able to keep going while you study for exams.*

**Q2. Is capital the bottleneck?**
No. Your family could fund almost any small business. That is a risk in itself. Founders with too much money hide a bad idea behind spending (a fancy brand, an office, ads) and never learn whether customers will pay.
→ *Effect: family money comes in small amounts released only when you hit milestones (Section 5). Your first rupee of revenue must come before any real spending.*

**Q3. What do you have that a 30-year-old founder doesn't?**
Three things: you are inside the IB system right now, your parents' network is affluent Mumbai families, and you know exactly what an IB student struggles with because you are one.
→ *Effect: build where these give you an edge. That points to education services for IB, IGCSE and MYP students in Mumbai, a market where parents routinely pay ₹1,500–₹5,000 an hour.*

**Q4. What does Indian law let a 16-year-old do?**
- A minor **cannot enter a binding contract** (Indian Contract Act 1872, Section 11). Any contract you sign as a minor is void, not just voidable. So vendor agreements, tutor contracts and payment-gateway accounts must be in an adult's name.
- A minor **cannot be a company director or a partner in an LLP**, because a DIN and the LLP agreements need someone legally able to contract. A minor **can hold shares** in a private limited company through a guardian, and **can be admitted to the benefits** of a partnership firm (Indian Partnership Act, Section 30).
- A minor **can have a PAN** and a bank account. Under RBI's April 2025 rules, banks may let minors aged 10 and over open and run savings accounts on their own.
- **Tax clubbing:** a minor's income is normally added to the income of the parent who earns more (Income-tax Act 1961, Section 64(1A); the same rule continues under the Income-tax Act 2025 from 1 April 2026). **The exception is income from your own manual work or from your skill, talent or specialised knowledge.** Tutoring you deliver yourself is taxed as *your* income, so you get your own basic exemption. Profit from an agency where other people do the teaching is likely to be clubbed with your parent's income.
→ *Effect: you earn personally, and a parent-owned business acts as the legal wrapper while you are under 18. Ownership moves to you at 18 (Section 4).*

**Q5. How much does a business have to earn to pay ₹1 lakh a month in profit?**
Selling one hour of your own time at ₹1,200 an hour, you would need about 85 hours a month. That is impossible during the DP. So the plan must move from **selling your own hours** to **earning a margin on other people's hours**, and later to **products that don't need hours at all**.
→ *Effect: the plan has three stages: solo tutor → tutoring agency → courses and bootcamps.*

**Q6. Could family money simply "replace the job"?**
Yes. ₹1.5 crore in debt funds or fixed deposits at about 7% pays out about ₹87,000 a month. But that is a gift, not a business, and income from money a parent gives a minor is clubbed with the parent's income anyway. It doesn't answer the question that was asked, so this plan doesn't use it. It is still worth saying out loud: **your safety net is already there, so you can afford to aim for learning and growth rather than quick cash.**

**Q7. What would get you into real trouble?**
- **IB academic misconduct.** Writing or "editing" someone else's IA, EE or TOK essay for money breaks IB rules and can get *your* Diploma withdrawn. The business never touches graded coursework (Section 8).
- **Child safety.** Your customers are children, so you need safeguarding rules and checks on every tutor.
- **School policy.** Some schools ban students from tutoring their own classmates for money. Check before you start.

**Q8. What happens when you leave for university in August 2028?**
A **US F-1** student visa generally does not allow off-campus work, and a **UK Student visa** does not allow self-employment. So by mid-2028 the business must **run without your hands on it**, with a paid manager and you acting as an owner who receives profit. Agree this with an immigration adviser before you leave.
→ *Effect: Stage 3 hires an operations manager and builds products. That is not optional.*

**Q9. Will this help or hurt your university applications?**
A real business with real customers, hiring and real numbers makes a strong application essay or activity entry. A business funded by your parents that has no customers looks bad. Revenue gives you credibility.

**Q10. What does success look like at each checkpoint?**
- **31 March 2027:** 8 or more paying students, ₹25,000+ a month.
- **31 Aug 2027:** an agency with 6 tutors, ₹60,000+ a month in margin.
- **30 June 2028:** ₹1,00,000+ a month in net profit, with a manager running daily operations.

## 2. The recommended business: an IB/IGCSE/MYP tutoring agency in Mumbai, called "[YourName] Learning"

**Why this business, and not a D2C brand, an app or a café:**

| Test | Tutoring agency | D2C/e-commerce brand | App/SaaS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue in your first month? | Yes | Rarely | No |
| Starting capital | Under ₹50,000 | ₹5–20 lakh | ₹5–30 lakh |
| Uses your IB knowledge and network | Strongly | Weakly | Weakly |
| Works in 6–8 hrs a week | Yes | No (stock, logistics) | No |
| Path to ₹1 lakh/month in 18 months | Realistic | Unlikely | Very unlikely |
| Can run while you're abroad | Yes, with a manager | Partly | Partly |

**Who buys:** parents of MYP, IGCSE and DP students at Mumbai's international-curriculum schools, for example Dhirubhai Ambani International School (BKC), Ecole Mondiale (Juhu), Oberoi International (Goregaon/JVLR), Aditya Birla World Academy (Tardeo) and Jamnabai Narsee International (Juhu), plus families studying online across India.
**What sells:** IB Maths AA/AI, Physics, Chemistry, Economics and English, and especially **"a tutor who just did this exact syllabus"**.
**Where the money comes from:** parents pay ₹1,800–₹3,000 an hour. Tutors are paid ₹1,100–₹1,800 an hour. You keep ₹600–₹1,200 an hour.

## 3. The plan, phase by phase with exact steps

### Phase 0: Setup (2 weeks, before 15 November 2026). About 8 hours in total

1. **The family meeting (1 hour).** Agree, and write on one page:
   - which parent is your "legal partner" (they sign contracts and own the payment account),
   - the capital rules (Section 5),
   - a rule that your **predicted grades stay at or above** a number you agree (for example 38), or business hours drop to 3 a week until they recover.
2. **Check school policy.** Email your DP coordinator: *"Is it permitted for a DP student to tutor younger students from outside school, or from our school, for a fee?"* Keep the reply.
3. **Pick your subjects.** Choose only the 2 subjects where your marks are strongest (7s, or high 6s). Tutor **MYP 4–5 and IGCSE students**, who are 1–2 years behind you. Don't tutor DP students yet.
4. **Paperwork. All of it is free or cheap:**
   - Get a PAN in your own name if you don't have one (Form 49A through NSDL/UTIITSL, about ₹107).
   - Open a **minor savings account with UPI** at a bank you choose (for example HDFC, ICICI or Kotak youth accounts). Personal tutoring fees go here.
   - Keep a simple income register in Google Sheets (date, client, hours, amount, mode of payment). You need it for tax.
5. **Safeguarding rules. Write them now:** sessions happen in the student's home with a parent at home, at a public place, or online with recording and the parents' consent. Never behind a closed door with a child alone. No personal social-media contact with students. Parents get every message.
6. **Set your price:** **₹1,000–₹1,200 an hour** for MYP/IGCSE (cheaper than adult IB tutors, who charge ₹2,500–₹5,000). Sell a **pack of 8 hours paid upfront** at ₹8,800. Never charge by the single session.

### Phase 1: Solo tutor (15 Nov 2026 – 31 Mar 2027). 6 hours a week
**Goal: 8 paying students, ₹25,000+ a month, 10 written testimonials.**

| Week | Exact action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Write a 1-page PDF profile in Canva: name, school, predicted grades, subjects, the 8-hour pack, the safeguarding rules, and your parent's phone number. |
| 1 | Your parents send it to 30 families they know who have children aged 12–15 on IB/IGCSE, using a personal message, not a group blast. Script: *"My son/daughter is in DP1 and scoring 7s in Maths. He's tutoring a few MYP/IGCSE kids. Would X like a free trial session?"* |
| 2 | Offer a **free 45-minute diagnostic session**. Turn at least 1 in 3 of these into a paid 8-hour pack. |
| 3–4 | Make a free **"MYP 5 eAssessment Maths: 20 most-tested questions"** PDF. Give it out through parent WhatsApp groups, with permission. Collect phone numbers. |
| 5–8 | After every 4th session, send parents a 3-line progress note. This is what drives referrals. |
| 9–16 | Ask every happy parent for **one referral and one written testimonial**. Add a referral reward: 1 free hour for each family referred. |

**Numbers at the end of the phase:** 8 students × about 3 hrs a month × ₹1,100 ≈ **₹26,400 a month**, from about 6 hours a week of teaching.
**Tax:** this is income from your own skill, so it is assessed in your hands. Up to the basic exemption limit you will owe little or nothing. Your family's CA should file an ITR in your name.

### Phase 2: Become an agency (1 Apr 2027 – 31 Aug 2027). 6 hrs a week in term, 25 hrs a week in the May–July holidays
**Goal: 6 tutors, 35+ students, ₹60,000+ a month in margin.**

1. **Set up the legal wrapper (April 2027).** Your parent registers a **sole proprietorship** under the trade name. That needs **Udyam registration** (free, online, Aadhaar-based) and a **current account** in the parent's name. A payment gateway (Razorpay or Cashfree) is opened by the parent, because these require someone over 18. *Don't form a company yet.* A Pvt Ltd costs about ₹15–25k to set up plus ₹50k+ a year in compliance, and isn't worth it below about ₹20 lakh a year in revenue.
   - **GST:** private tutoring is a taxable service (18%). Registration is only compulsory once turnover passes **₹20 lakh a year**. Watch the running total every month.
   - **Maharashtra Shops & Establishments Act:** with fewer than 10 workers and no premises (home or online), you only need to file an intimation at most. Confirm this with the CA.
2. **Recruit tutors (April–May 2027).** Your best supply is **IB students who have just finished DP2 and scored 40+**. Their exams end in May and they have about 3 months before university. Also recruit IB-trained teachers looking for side income.
   - Pay **₹1,200–₹1,800 an hour**, depending on level.
   - Screening: transcripts, a 30-minute demo lesson you watch, two references, a signed safeguarding code, and a police verification certificate (PCC, available through the Maharashtra Police online portal) for anyone over 18.
   - Contract: a simple **independent contractor agreement**, signed by your *parent* on behalf of the proprietorship (have the CA or a lawyer draft it once, for about ₹5–10k). It should include a **non-solicit clause**: the tutor can't take your clients directly for 12 months.
3. **Sell DP-level tutoring now.** Charge **₹2,200–₹3,000 an hour** for DP1/DP2 students, using tutors who scored 42+. MYP/IGCSE stays at ₹1,500–₹1,800.
4. **Holiday product (May–July 2027): the "DP Head Start" bootcamp.** It runs 2 weeks, 3 hours a day, for students about to start DP1, covering Maths AA/AI basics, how IAs work, and TOK in plain English. 15 students × ₹18,000 = ₹2.7 lakh in revenue. Venue: a society clubhouse or a co-working room (₹1,500–₹3,000 a day), or run it online.
5. **Systems (keep them minimal):** Google Sheets for students, tutors, hours and invoices; WhatsApp Business with one number, owned by the business, that your parent can see; Google Calendar for bookings; monthly invoices sent from the proprietorship.

**Phase 2 maths (by August 2027):** about 150 billed hours a month × average margin of ₹650 ≈ **₹97,500 gross margin a month**, less about ₹10–15k in costs ≈ **₹85,000 a month**. This income is earned by the proprietorship, so it is your parent's taxable income, **not yours yet**. Section 4 explains how it becomes yours.

### Phase 3: Make it run without you (Sep 2027 – Jun 2028). 4 hrs a week, dropping to 2 in exam season
**Goal: ₹1,00,000+ a month in net profit, with a manager running daily operations.**

DP2 is your hardest year: the EE and IAs are due, university applications go in from October 2027 to January 2028, then mocks and finals. So:
1. **Hire a part-time operations coordinator (September 2027)** for ₹20,000–₹30,000 a month: a graduate or a young mother with tutoring experience who handles matching, scheduling, invoicing and parent calls. You keep **only** these jobs: pricing, approving tutor hires, and a 1-hour weekly review of the numbers.
2. **Raise prices once**, by 10–15%, when the new academic year starts. Waiting lists are proof you can.
3. **Build one product that sells while you sleep**, recorded in the June–July 2028 window after your exams:
   - "IB Maths AA SL Paper 1 & 2 crash course": 20 videos plus worked past-paper solutions you write yourself, never IB copyrighted material. Sell it at ₹4,999 through Graphy, Teachable or Gumroad, with the account held by your parent until you turn 18.
   - Repeat the **DP Head Start bootcamp** in May–July 2028 with 2–3 batches (about ₹6–8 lakh).
4. **Never do this:** "IA/EE writing help", "assignment help", or anything that produces graded work. Your offer is teaching, past-paper practice and feedback on the student's own understanding, and it says so in writing.

**Target by June 2028:**
- ~250 tutoring hours a month × ₹700 average margin = ₹1,75,000
- Plus ~₹40,000 a month averaged from bootcamps and courses
- Minus the ₹25,000 coordinator and ₹15,000 other costs
- **≈ ₹1,75,000 a month net.** That is above the ₹1 lakh target, with buffer for months when it falls short.

## 4. How ownership moves to you when you turn 18

| When | Structure | Whose income for tax |
|---|---|---|
| Now – Mar 2027 | You as an individual, personal tutoring | **Yours** (skill income, not clubbed) |
| Apr 2027 – your 18th birthday | Parent's sole proprietorship (trade name) | Parent's |
| Within 3 months after you turn 18 | **Incorporate a Private Limited company** with you as director and majority shareholder (for example you 90%, parent 10%), with your parent as second director. The proprietorship transfers the business to it under a **business transfer agreement**, which the CA drafts. | The company's, and you take salary or dividends |

Ask the CA about two other options. First, a partnership firm in which you are admitted to the benefits as a minor. Second, a Pvt Ltd set up earlier with your parent and another adult as directors and you as a shareholder through a guardian, holding equity from day one. Either way, keep a **dated family memo**, signed now, recording that you founded and run the business. It avoids arguments later.

## 5. How to use the family's money (rules, not amounts)

The risk is not losing the money. The risk is spending your way past the lessons. Rules:

| Tranche | Amount | Unlocked when | Allowed uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ₹50,000 | Now | Canva Pro, printing, a Google Workspace domain, CA setup fees, a laptop upgrade if needed |
| 2 | ₹2,00,000 | 8 paying students reached | Lawyer-drafted contracts, bootcamp venue deposit, a simple website (Wix/Framer, ₹15–30k), small Meta ads tests (₹20k max) |
| 3 | ₹5,00,000 | ₹60k/month margin for 2 months in a row | Coordinator salary runway (6 months), course production, a better LMS |

- No office, no agency-built app and no paid "brand launch" until revenue passes ₹1.5 lakh a month for 3 months.
- Record family money as a **documented loan** with a simple letter (interest-free, repaid from profits). Repaying it is part of learning the game. Ask the CA about gift and loan treatment.
- In parallel, separate from the business: ask your parents to open a **₹5,000–₹10,000 a month SIP in an index fund** in your name, so you learn about compounding. It is not part of the business case.

## 6. Path B, if tutoring is wrong for you

Use it if your grades aren't top-band, you don't enjoy teaching, or your school forbids tutoring.

**"Mumbai SMB automation and web studio"**: build WhatsApp Business catalogues, Google Business Profiles, simple websites and AI-chatbot or booking setups for local clinics, salons, CA firms and restaurants in your area.
- **Weeks 1–6:** learn Framer or Webflow, WhatsApp Business API basics (through Interakt, WATI or similar) and Google Business Profile, using free tutorials (about 4 hrs a week). Build 3 free sample projects for family businesses or friends' families.
- **Price:** setup ₹15,000–₹40,000, plus a **monthly maintenance retainer of ₹3,000–₹8,000**. The retainer is the part that replaces a salary: 20 clients × ₹5,000 = ₹1 lakh a month.
- **Selling:** visit 5 businesses every Saturday in Bandra, Andheri or Powai with a parent or friend, carrying a printed "before/after" mock-up of *their* Google listing. Your parents' business network is your first 10 clients.
- **Legal and tax:** exactly the same as Section 4. Work you do yourself is skill income; once you subcontract freelancers, it is agency income under the parent's proprietorship.
- **Timeline:** the same phases and the same IB calendar limits.

## 8. Risk register

| Risk | What you do |
|---|---|
| IB academic misconduct (helping with coursework) | A written "no graded work" policy in every tutor contract, and tutors who break it are removed immediately |
| A child safety incident | Safeguarding code, PCC checks, parents present or sessions recorded, two-adult rule for bootcamps |
| Tutors take clients directly | A non-solicit clause, the business owns the client WhatsApp number, and tutors get a share of rebookings to keep them loyal |
| Seasonality (demand drops in June and peaks Feb–Apr) | Summer bootcamps and courses fill the low months |
| Your grades fall | The time guardrail above |
| Visa rules abroad | The manager runs operations from Aug 2028, and you get immigration advice before leaving |
| You lose interest | Fine. By June 2028 the business is sellable or can be handed to the coordinator for a profit share |
