# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

## The short answer

Start an **IB tutoring and revision agency in Mumbai**. You handle the brand, matching, quality and sales. The tutoring is done by IB alumni now in college, who scored 40+, and you take a margin of about 30–45% on every hour they teach. Run it legally as a **sole proprietorship in one parent's name**. That business pays **you** a fair monthly fee for your work, which goes into your own bank account. Use **no more than ₹1.5 lakh** of family money, written down as a loan. The target is **₹1 lakh a month in net profit (about ₹12 LPA, roughly a good Mumbai graduate's starting salary) within 15–20 months**. The business must keep running without you through your DP2 exams.

Below is how I got there. I split the problem into eight parts, solved each one, compared seven business options in a scored table, and then checked the points where the parts depend on each other. Every major choice also says what it costs you.

---

## Assumptions

- **Timing:** you started DP1 around August 2026. Final IB exams are May 2028, and you turn 18 around 2028.
- **"Job-replacing money"** means **₹1,00,000 a month in net profit**, steady for at least 3 months in a row. Revenue does not count.
- **The goal is for *you* to build something and earn from it.** If the family only wants income in your name, see Option G in the table. It is faster, but it is not a business.
- **Your university plans are still open.** Studying abroad from August 2028 is a real possibility, so the plan has to survive it.
- **Laws:** Indian tax and company rules are as of late 2026. Check every legal and tax point below with a CA (chartered accountant) before you act. I flag the ones that matter most.

### Sub-problem 1: Target
- **Target:** ₹1,00,000 a month in net profit for 3 months in a row.
- **Checkpoints:** ₹25,000 a month by month 6 (April 2027), ₹60,000–75,000 by month 12 (October 2027), and ₹1,00,000 or more by month 15–20 (February–June 2028).
- **Measure it with a simple monthly profit-and-loss sheet:** revenue, minus tutor pay, GST, payment fees, tools, marketing, staff and your own fee.

### Sub-problem 2: Time budget
The IB already takes about 50–60 hours a week: six subjects, IAs (internal assessments), the EE (Extended Essay), TOK (Theory of Knowledge) and CAS (Creativity, Activity, Service). Your grades also decide your university options, and those are worth more than any first business. So the business gets fixed hours:

| Period | Hours a week on the business | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nov 2026 – Jul 2027 (DP1) | 8 max: Saturday 3h, Sunday 2h, 20–30 min on weekdays | Setup and growth |
| Jun – Jul 2027 (summer) | 20–25 | Biggest growth window |
| Aug – Dec 2027 (DP2, EE/IA deadlines) | 4–5 | Your coursework comes first |
| Jan – May 2028 (mocks and finals) | 1–2 (one weekly call) | A manager runs operations |
| May – Aug 2028 (after exams) | 30+ | Grow it and prepare the handover |

**This rules out** any business that needs you during working hours, needs you there every day, or needs you during exams.

### Sub-problem 3: Legal capacity of a 16-year-old in India

| Item | Rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Contracts | Under the Indian Contract Act 1872, a contract with a minor is void (*Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*) | Customers, tutors, landlords and payment gateways must contract with an **adult** |
| Company director, OPC, LLP designated partner | Must be 18 or older | You can't be the legal head of a company yet |
| Holding shares | A minor can hold fully paid shares through a guardian | Possible, but income from them is added to a parent's income for tax |
| Bank account | RBI allows minors aged 10+ to run their own savings account, within limits the bank sets | Open **your own** account to receive your pay |
| Tax ("clubbing") | A minor's income is normally taxed as the parent's income. The exception is income from the minor's own **manual work, skill, talent or specialised knowledge**. This was s.64(1A) of the 1961 Act and carries over into the Income-tax Act 2025 | A **fee paid to you for real work** is taxed as *your* income. Under the new regime with the rebate, income up to about ₹12 lakh a year has no tax (FY 2025-26 onward rates). **Confirm with a CA.** |
| Paying a relative | Payments from a business to a relative must be at a reasonable market rate (s.40A(2) principle) | Pay yourself a market-rate fee for a real role, not "all the profit" |
| Child labour | People aged 14–18 may work in non-hazardous jobs | Office, marketing and coordination work is fine |
| Foreign entity (Delaware company, etc.) | Investing abroad as a resident falls under FEMA ODI rules and the LRS limit of $250k per person per year | Not worth it before 18. Skip it. |

**Decision:** the business is a **sole proprietorship of one parent** (call them the "Proprietor parent"). It trades under your brand name and pays you a monthly fee. A Private Limited company comes later (see Sub-problem 8).

**What this costs you:** on paper you don't own the business until you are 18. Your protection is a short signed family agreement (template idea in the checklist) that says the business and brand move to your company when you turn 18. It also means a parent signs everything.

### Sub-problem 4: Business choice (options matrix)

Each option is scored from 1 to 5 on six weighted criteria taken from Sub-problems 1–3:

- **Time fit (25%):** can run on 4–8 hours a week and survive exam months.
- **Profit path (20%):** realistic route to ₹1L a month in profit within about 18 months.
- **Legal feasibility (15%):** workable for a minor in India.
- **Your edge (15%):** an advantage that you specifically have (IB insider, Mumbai IB network, family's trust in the community).
- **Downside (10%):** money, reputation and academic risk. 5 means the safest.
- **Learning and university value (15%).**

| Option | Time fit | Profit path | Legal | Your edge | Downside | Learning | **Weighted** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. IB tutoring and revision agency** (alumni tutors, you run it) | 4 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 4 | **4.15** |
| G. Family gives capital, e.g. ₹2 crore in debt or dividend funds (about ₹1–1.2L a month before tax) | 5 | 5 | 3 | 1 | 4 | 1 | 3.40 |
| C. IB content creator (YouTube/Instagram) plus digital products | 3 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 3.30 |
| E. Freelance skill (web, video editing) for overseas clients | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 3.20 |
| D. SaaS or study app | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 2.95 |
| B. D2C product brand (Shopify or Amazon) | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 2.50 |
| F. Buy a franchise, café or small business with family money | 1 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1.90 |

**Why A wins:**
- **You know the product better than any adult competitor.** You are inside the IB right now: which topics students struggle with, which teachers and schools are known for what, and when the mock exams fall.
- **Customers come through people you already know.** IB families in Mumbai find tutors by word of mouth, and your parents' and classmates' networks are where those recommendations happen.
- **Capital needed is close to zero.** Tutors are paid only after customers have paid.
- **It can run without you.** Online sessions and a matching playbook mean you are not needed in the room.
- **There is a clear route to the target.** About 240 tutoring hours a month gets there (maths in Sub-problem 6).

**Why not the runners-up:**
- **G is the fastest route to the money, but it fails your actual goal.** It isn't a business, you learn nothing, and until you are 18 the income is taxed as your parent's. If the family only wants income in your name, this is the honest answer: gift the capital, invest it in debt or dividend funds, and you are done. This plan assumes that isn't what you want.
- **C is weak on its own** because creator income takes 12–24 months to start. **It is used inside A** as a free marketing channel.
- **E** gives you no edge over millions of freelancers, and since Stripe India stopped onboarding, getting paid from abroad is harder.
- **B and F** both need adult working hours, stock or staff, and real money at risk.

**What choosing A costs you:**
- **The upside has a ceiling.** A service agency grows by adding hours and people. It won't become a ₹100-crore company the way a product or SaaS business might.
- **It depends on how good the tutors are.** One bad tutor damages the brand in a tight community.
- **It is seasonal.** Demand peaks January to April, which is exactly when you sit your own exams. Sub-problem 6 deals with this.

If you want the higher-upside path, the plan's long-term step (a revision course or question bank, see Phase 4) moves A toward a product over time.

### Sub-problem 5: Capital policy
- **Ask the family for ₹1.5 lakh**, written as an interest-free loan. Repay it from profits by month 12.
- **No more money unless you hit the checkpoints** (see the kill and pivot rules in Sub-problem 6).
- **Why so little when the family is worth about ₹70 crore:** a business that only survives on family money tells you nothing. Here the family wealth is best used as a **safety net**: you can afford to fail, so you can afford to try. It should not be used as fuel.
- **What this costs you:** slower early growth. For example, no paid ads at ₹50,000 a month and no office.

**First 6 months of spending (about ₹1 lakh):**

| Item | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Domain, Google Workspace (1 seat), simple website (Carrd, Framer or Wix) | 8,000 |
| CA: proprietorship setup, GST advice, books setup | 10,000–15,000 |
| Trademark application, class 41 (education), e-filing rate for individuals and MSMEs | 4,500 |
| Notion or Sheets, Calendly or Zoho Bookings, WhatsApp Business (free) | 5,000 |
| Marketing: 4 free mock-exam workshops (venue or snacks), printed one-pagers, Instagram tests | 30,000 |
| Lawyer review of tutor and parent agreements (one-time) | 10,000–15,000 |
| Buffer | 25,000 |

### Sub-problem 6: Execution (the core plan)

#### The product (version 1)

| Product | Price to the family (GST included) | Tutor gets | Your margin before costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1 DP tutoring (Maths AA/AI, Physics, Chemistry, Economics, Biology), online | ₹2,000 / hour | ₹1,100 / hour | ₹595 after 18% GST and 2% gateway fee |
| 1:1 MYP / IGCSE / "DP-readiness" | ₹1,500 / hour | ₹850 / hour | about ₹400 |
| Small-group revision bootcamps (5–6 students, March–April and summer) | ₹800 per student-hour, so ₹4,000–4,800 per hour | ₹1,500 / hour | about ₹1,700–2,400 an hour |

- **Tutors:** IB alumni aged 18–23 with a final score of 40+ and a 6 or 7 in the subject they teach. ₹1,100 an hour is very good money for a college student, so recruiting is easy.
- **The pitch:** "Tutors who sat the same papers 2–4 years ago and scored 7s, matched by someone who is in DP right now."
- **Benchmark:** experienced IB teachers in Mumbai charge well above ₹2,000 an hour, so you are positioned as quality at a lower price.

#### Unit economics and how many hours you need
- **GST basis:** all the maths is after GST from day one (₹2,000 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,695 net). Private tutoring is taxable at 18%. Registration becomes mandatory once turnover crosses ₹20 lakh a year for services in Maharashtra, and you'll cross that around month 9–12. Pricing GST-inclusive from the start means no price shock later.
- **Margin:** ₹1,695 − ₹1,100 tutor − ₹40 gateway = **₹555 per 1:1 hour**.
- **Fixed costs at scale (each month):**
  - Operations manager (part-time college student): ₹20,000
  - Tools: ₹3,000
  - Marketing: ₹10,000
  - Your own fee: covered in Sub-problem 3 (it is your take-home pay, so it is on top of profit, not taken from it)
- **Hours needed:** for ₹1,00,000 net plus ₹33,000 in costs, you need ₹1,33,000 ÷ 555 = **about 240 one-to-one hours a month**. That is about 50–60 students at 4–5 hours a month, served by 15–20 tutors. One bootcamp a month cuts this by about 40 hours.
- **Market size:** Mumbai has dozens of IB schools, with several thousand DP students in each year group. Check the count on the IBO "Find an IB school" page. You need roughly 1–2% of them.

#### Phase 0. Validate (weeks 1–3, November 2026). About 15 hours in total.
1. **Interview 15 people** for 15 minutes each: 8 DP students (DP1 and DP2) from at least 3 different schools, and 7 parents. Ask:
   - Which subject they pay for help in now
   - Who the tutor is and how much they charge
   - What frustrates them
   - Whether they would book a trial this month
   - Write the answers in one sheet.
2. **List 25 alumni tutor candidates** from your school's and friends' schools' last 4 graduating years (LinkedIn, school alumni groups, older siblings). Message 25 and aim for 6 yeses.
3. **Pass/fail:** you need **5 families agreeing to a paid trial** and **4 tutors**. If you get fewer than 3 families, switch to Option E or C.

#### Phase 1. Legal and setup (weeks 2–5, in parallel). Mostly done by the Proprietor parent with you.
1. **Pick a name and check it's free:** search the IP India trademark registry, the .in/.com domain, and the Instagram handle. File the class 41 trademark in the Proprietor parent's name (₹4,500).
2. **Udyam (MSME) registration** on udyamregistration.gov.in. It's free and needs the parent's Aadhaar and PAN.
3. **Maharashtra Shops and Establishments intimation.** For fewer than 10 workers this is a free online intimation, which banks often ask for as business proof.
4. **Bank accounts:** a current account in the trade name (Proprietor parent), and **your own savings account** in your name.
5. **Payments:** a Razorpay account (Payment Links and Pages) on the Proprietor parent's KYC. Use UPI Business as a fallback.
6. **Get the CA on board:**
   - Bookkeeping (Zoho Books free tier, or a Google Sheet)
   - A plan for GST registration (register voluntarily once monthly sales pass ₹1.5 lakh)
   - Your fee arrangement
   - Your own income tax return each year, signed by your guardian
7. **Three documents**, drafted by you from templates and checked by a lawyer:
   - **Tutor agreement:** independent contractor, 18+ only, pay rate, no poaching your clients for 12 months, the academic-integrity rules and safeguarding rules from Sub-problem 7, and confidentiality.
   - **Parent terms:** signed by the parent, never the student. Hours are prepaid in packs (4, 8 or 12 hours), 24-hour cancellation, and refund terms.
   - **Family agreement:** your role, your monthly fee, the ₹1.5L loan, and the commitment to move the business and trademark to your company at 18.

#### Phase 2. Pilot (December 2026 – January 2027). Target: 10–12 paying students, about 50 hours a month.
1. **Matching playbook (a one-page form):** subject, level (HL/SL), current predicted grade, target grade, school, timings, and in-person or online preference. Match within 48 hours.
2. **Rules for every session:**
   - Online on Google Meet is the default.
   - In-person sessions happen only at the student's home with an adult present.
   - The tutor sends a 3-line note after each session (topics covered, homework, worries). You forward a monthly progress summary to the parent.
3. **Quality loop:**
   - Send a 2-question survey after the 2nd session.
   - Any rating of 3 or below out of 5 gets a call from you within 24 hours and an offer to rematch.
   - Track **renewal rate**, meaning families buying a second hour-pack. **Target 70%+.**
4. **Hook for the January mocks:** run **free 90-minute "Paper 1 strategy" workshops** for each subject, online or in a borrowed room. Each workshop is led by your best tutor and gets about 20 sign-ups. Aim to turn 10–15% of attendees into paying customers.

#### Phase 3. Grow (February – July 2027). Target: 25 students, 120 hours a month, ₹25k+ net by April.
1. **Referrals:** a family that refers someone who buys gets 1 free hour, and so does the new family.
2. **Content (Option C, used as marketing):**
   - 2 Instagram reels or carousels a week (45 minutes, Sunday batch): "3 mistakes in Maths AA Paper 2", "How the IA criteria are actually marked".
   - Post them as your brand, using your own explanations. **Never share IB past papers or markschemes**, which are IBO copyright.
3. **Revision bootcamps (March–April 2027)** for the current DP2s sitting May 2027 exams: 3–4 subjects, 6 sessions each, 5–6 students each. This is your biggest cash month.
4. **Summer 2027 (you work 20–25 hours a week):**
   - Launch a **"DP-readiness" course** for students entering DP1: an MYP/IGCSE-to-DP bridge course in Maths and Sciences.
   - Recruit tutors up to 15.
   - Write the **operations playbook**: matching, onboarding, payments, complaints, tutor payouts. Every task should be written so that a stranger could do it.
   - Start paying yourself a fee (see below).

#### Phase 4. Hand over operations (August – December 2027). Target: 180 hours a month, ₹60–75k net.
1. **By September 2027, hire an operations manager**: a college student, part-time, ₹15,000–20,000 a month plus ₹2,000 for each new student converted. They handle inquiries, matching, scheduling and payouts by following the playbook.
2. **Your job shrinks to:** a weekly 45-minute review on Sunday (dashboard: hours, renewals, complaints, cash), tutor hiring decisions, and content approvals.
3. **Optional longer-term step:** have tutors write an **original question bank** for one subject, sold as a ₹2,999 online pack. This is the first step toward a product with a higher ceiling. Do it only if Phase 3 hit its target.

#### Phase 5. Run without you (January – May 2028). Target: 240+ hours a month, **₹1 lakh+ net**.
- This is the demand peak (mocks, then finals) and also your own exam season. The manager runs everything. You do one call a week and are reached only for real emergencies, decided by a written rule such as "refund over ₹10,000, or any safeguarding complaint".
- **This phase is the real test:** if the business reaches ₹1L a month while you are sitting your own finals, it really does replace a job.

#### Paying yourself
- **From month 7 (June 2027):** the business pays you a monthly fee for your role as "Head of Growth and Quality". Start at ₹10,000–15,000 a month and rise to ₹40,000–60,000 at scale. The amount must be a fair market rate, backed by a monthly invoice or timesheet.
- **The rest of the profit:** it stays in the business, or it is set aside in the business account for transfer to your company at 18 (agreed in the family agreement).
- **Ask your CA** to confirm this setup.

#### Kill and pivot rules (agreed with your parents in advance)

| Checkpoint | Continue if | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| End of January 2027 | 8+ paying students, 60%+ renewal | Rework the offer once. If it fails again by March, switch to E or C. |
| End of April 2027 | ₹25k+ net in the month | No new money. Cut down to your best 2 subjects. |
| End of December 2027 | ₹60k+ net, manager running daily operations | Run it as a smaller side business, ₹20–40k a month, through DP2 |
| Any time | Predicted grades drop by 2+ points in total | Halve your hours that month, no exceptions |

### Sub-problem 7: Risk controls (written down and in every tutor agreement)
1. **Academic integrity.**
   - Tutors teach the concepts. They **never write, rewrite or substantially edit** any IA, EE, TOK essay or exhibition, or any coursework that gets graded.
   - Tutors can explain the assessment criteria and give general feedback. They can't hand over any material that ends up in the student's submission.
   - Under IB rules this kind of help is malpractice. It can cost the *client* their diploma, and you would be the person linked to it.
   - **One breach and the tutor is removed.**
2. **Safeguarding.** Your clients are minors.
   - Tutors must be 18+, checked with ID and an alumni reference.
   - Online by default. In-person only with an adult present.
   - No private social-media DMs with students: messages go through a parent-included WhatsApp group.
   - Any complaint goes straight to the Proprietor parent.
3. **Data (DPDP Act 2023).** Collect a parent's consent for anything about a student under 18. Keep only the data you need, in one access-controlled Google Drive.
4. **Your own school.**
   - Don't recruit or poach your own school's teachers.
   - Don't market in school groups without permission.
   - Tell your DP coordinator or counsellor what you are doing early, so it reads as initiative rather than a surprise.
5. **CAS.** Paid work does not count as CAS. Run a **separate free weekly session** with an NGO or low-income school partner as a genuine CAS Service project. It's real service, and it's better for your application than claiming the business.

### Sub-problem 8: Turning 18 and going to university
- **At 18 (2028):**
  - Set up a **Private Limited company** with you as director and majority shareholder and one parent as second director. Expect about ₹15–25k to set up and ₹40–60k a year in compliance.
  - Move the business, client contracts and trademark across with a CA-drafted business-transfer agreement and a trademark assignment.
  - Repay any remaining family loan.
  - Don't incorporate earlier unless revenue passes about ₹50 lakh a year. The compliance cost isn't worth it before then, and as a minor shareholder your dividends would be taxed as your parent's income anyway.
- **University abroad:** the plan was built to be online and run by a manager for this reason, but visas restrict working:
  - **UK student visa:** you can't be self-employed during your studies.
  - **US F-1:** actively working in your own business is a legal risk.
  - In both cases, before you leave, set the business up so that you are a **passive owner**: the manager is promoted to general manager with a profit share, and you keep ownership and board-level decisions only. **Get immigration advice before you leave.** This is the main reason the operations hand-off happens in DP2 and not later.
- **University in India:** you can keep running it actively, and it becomes your main income.

## Part 2. Checking where the parts meet

| Where the parts meet | The conflict | How the plan handles it |
|---|---|---|
| Legal (3) and money (1) | The business is the parent's on paper, so is the money really yours? | A market-rate fee for real work is *your* income (the skill exception), plus a written commitment to transfer at 18 |
| Time (2) and seasonal demand (6) | Demand peaks January–April, during your own exams | The manager is hired by September 2027, the playbook is written in summer 2027, and the 2027 bootcamp season tests it once with you still available |
| Brand (4) and academic integrity (7) | Clients will ask for IA and EE "help" | Written rules, a clause in the tutor agreement, and removal after one breach |
| Family wealth (5) and motivation (1) | Unlimited money hides failure | A ₹1.5L loan, kill rules agreed in advance, and no top-ups |
| GST (6) and pricing (6) | Registering mid-year would squeeze margins | GST-inclusive prices and all maths done after GST from day one |
| University (8) and the business (6) | Visa rules and your physical move | Online delivery and a passive-owner setup in place before August 2028 |
| Grades (2) and everything else | The business could hurt the IB score that university depends on | A hard weekly hours cap and a rule that cuts your hours when grades drop |

## Part 4. What you're giving up by following this plan

1. **The higher-upside, slower path:** a startup, SaaS product or brand that could raise money. You can still try one at 18 with real cash, a team, and proof that you can sell.
2. **The guaranteed path:** family capital invested for income (Option G). It pays more and sooner, but you would build nothing.
3. **Some free time and maybe a few marks.** That's why the hours are capped and grades are protected by a rule.
4. **Full legal ownership until 18.** This is handled with the family agreement and the planned transfer.
