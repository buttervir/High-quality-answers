# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

## Assumptions

- **Today is October 2026.** The student started DP1 around June–August 2026, sits final IB exams in **May 2028**, and turns 18 around then.
- **"Job-replacing money"** means steady **net profit of ₹1,00,000 a month** (about ₹12 lakh a year, roughly what a good graduate job in Mumbai pays). The stretch target is ₹1,50,000 a month. These are profit figures, not revenue.
- **Exchange rate:** about ₹88 to US$1. The $8M family net worth is about ₹70 crore.
- **The parents support the plan.** They will act as legal signatory and guardian, and give a limited amount of money as described below.
- **Most universities expect a full IB score.** The student cannot sacrifice the IB, and the plan is built around that.
- **Tax and legal points below reflect Indian rules as of late 2026.** India's new Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, and section numbers changed. Have a chartered accountant (CA) confirm each point before you act on it. This plan gives you the questions to ask; it does not replace the CA.

## 1. The facts the plan has to work within

Every decision below follows from these seven facts:

1. **Time is the scarcest resource, not money.** The IB Diploma means 6 subjects, Theory of Knowledge, a 4,000-word Extended Essay, internal assessments in each subject, and CAS. A realistic business budget is **8–10 hours a week during term, 2–3 hours a week in exam periods, and 30+ hours a week in holidays**. The business has to run without the founder being present at every hour.
2. **A 16-year-old cannot sign binding contracts in India.** Under the Indian Contract Act, 1872, a minor's agreement is void. A minor also cannot be a company director or an LLP partner, and cannot be the KYC holder for most payment gateways. A parent or a company has to sign contracts and collect money until the student turns 18.
3. **Money is not the bottleneck.** ₹5 lakh is about 0.07% of the family's net worth. Raising capital won't decide success. These will: a skill customers pay for, access to customers, and the founder's time. Spending a lot of family money early mostly buys expensive mistakes.
4. **The family could already "replace a job" with capital.** About $1M invested at 6–7% yields roughly ₹55–60 lakh a year. So the real purpose of the business is not survival income. It is skill, independence, a track record, and a strong university profile. That means the downside has to stay small, and learning should be fast.
5. **Profit = customers × price × margin × how often they buy.** With little time, the business needs one of two things: a high price per hour of the founder's time, or a model where other people deliver the work.
6. **The student has real advantages.** They are inside Mumbai's IB/IGCSE school network, have English fluency, have direct experience of exactly what IB and IGCSE students struggle with, and have a family network of affluent parents and business owners.
7. **Minor's income is normally added to the parent's income for tax** (the "clubbing" rule, formerly Section 64(1A)). The exception is income the minor earns from their **own skill, talent or specialised knowledge**, which is taxed in the minor's own name. This affects how the money should flow (see Section 6).

## 3. Track A in full: an exam-preparation and tutoring agency

### 3.1 What you sell

A tutoring and revision business for **students in Grades 8–12 on IGCSE, IB MYP and IB DP**, at Mumbai's international schools (Dhirubhai Ambani International, Cathedral, Bombay International, Ecole Mondiale, Oberoi International, JBCN, Aditya Birla World Academy, B.D. Somani, Jamnabai Narsee International, and others), and online for families in Pune, Bengaluru, Dubai and Singapore.

There are three products, launched in this order:

1. **One-to-one tutoring packages.** Prepaid blocks of 10 hours. The founder teaches only the younger grades, in subjects where they are strong. Older tutors deliver the rest.
2. **Small-group revision bootcamps.** For example, "IGCSE Maths Paper 4 in 5 days" or "IB Chemistry Paper 1 sprint". Run them in the peak seasons (January–April, and the summer and winter holidays), with 6–10 students per batch.
3. **Digital revision resources (from month 9).** Original notes, worked solutions and short video walkthroughs, sold online. This is the product that can scale without the founder's time.

**Never sell:**
- Writing or editing of IAs, Extended Essays or TOK essays for clients. That is academic misconduct under IB rules and would end the business and harm the founder's own IB standing.
- Copies of IB or Cambridge past papers or markschemes. Those are copyrighted. Teach with them in class, but don't resell them.

### 3.2 The numbers

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Price to parents, one-to-one (IGCSE/MYP) | ₹2,000–2,500/hour |
| Price to parents, one-to-one (IB DP) | ₹3,000–4,000/hour |
| Tutor pay | 60–70% of the price (₹1,300–2,500/hour) |
| Agency margin per hour | about ₹700–1,200 (use ₹850 to plan) |
| Founder's own teaching (Grade 8–10, from month 1) | ₹1,000–1,500/hour, kept in full |
| Bootcamp | 8 students × ₹12,000 = ₹96,000 revenue; tutor ₹30,000 + venue/tools ₹6,000 → **₹60,000 profit per bootcamp** |
| Fixed costs once scaled | Coordinator ₹20,000 + software/ads ₹15,000 = **₹35,000/month** |

**The path to ₹1 lakh a month in profit:**
- 160 tutor-hours a month × ₹850 = ₹1,36,000
- plus the founder's own 4 hours a week × ₹1,200 ≈ ₹20,000
- minus ₹35,000 fixed costs
- = **≈ ₹1,21,000 a month**, plus bootcamps in peak months.

160 tutor-hours a month is about **35–40 regular students at one hour a week**. In a city with thousands of IGCSE/IB students paying for tutoring, that is plausible within 12–15 months. Assume it will not happen in 3.

### 3.3 Calendar, phase by phase

#### Phase 0: Set up (October 20 – November 30, 2026; about 6 hours a week)

**Week 1**
- [ ] Hold a family meeting and sign a one-page **family founder agreement**. It covers: the parent is the legal signatory; the money is a recorded loan or investment (amount, tranches, milestones); profits belong to the business and then to the student; IB grades come first, and if any predicted grade drops by 1 point, business hours are halved until it recovers.
- [ ] Get the student a **PAN card**. A minor can hold one; apply through a parent.
- [ ] Open a **minor's savings account**. RBI's 2025 rules let minors aged 10+ operate their own savings accounts. This account receives the student's personal teaching income.
- [ ] Choose a brand name. Check that the name, the domain, and the Instagram and WhatsApp Business handles are all free. Search the trademark register online for Class 41 (education).

**Week 2**
- [ ] Set up the business under the **parent's sole proprietorship** at first. This avoids company costs before you have revenue. The parent's PAN is used for the Udyam (MSME) registration, which is free online, and for the current account.
- [ ] Open a **Razorpay or Cashfree account** (about 2% fees) and set up **UPI for the business**, both in the parent's name.
- [ ] Book a one-hour session with a **CA** (₹3,000–5,000). Ask:
  1. Should we use an agent (commission) model or a principal (resell) model for GST?
  2. What is the clubbing treatment for the student's skill income versus the agency margin?
  3. What TDS applies to tutor payouts?
  4. At what revenue should we incorporate?

**Week 3**
- [ ] Draft three documents. Use a lawyer for a one-time ₹15,000–25,000, or adapt templates and have a lawyer review them.
  - **Tutor agreement:** independent contractor; a 12-month non-solicitation clause stopping tutors from taking clients directly; academic-integrity clause; safeguarding clause.
  - **Parent terms:** prepaid packages, a 24-hour cancellation rule, refunds of unused hours only.
  - **Consent and safeguarding form:** parental consent to collect and store the child's data, which India's DPDP Act 2023 and its 2025 Rules require for anyone under 18; consent to session recordings; meeting rules.
- [ ] Write a **safeguarding policy**:
  - every tutor gets an ID check and two references;
  - online sessions are recorded and kept for 30 days;
  - home sessions only with an adult present at home;
  - no private social media contact with students;
  - a named person handles concerns (the parent, while the student is a minor).

**Week 4**
- [ ] Build a one-page site (Carrd, Framer or Wix; ₹5,000–15,000 a year). It needs subjects offered, tutor profiles and their results, prices, a WhatsApp click-to-chat button, and a Google Form or Calendly booking link.
- [ ] Set up a **Google Business Profile** for search and map listings, plus a WhatsApp Business catalogue.
- [ ] Set up tools: Google Workspace (one account), Zoom or Google Meet, Google Sheets as the CRM (columns: lead, source, grade, subject, status, hours bought, hours used, renewal date), and Notion for tutor notes.

**Weeks 5–6**
- [ ] **Recruit 5 tutors.** Look for:
  - recent IB graduates who scored 40+, now at university in India or abroad (online sessions);
  - DP2 students who scored 7 in specific subjects (for Grade 8–10 work);
  - 1–2 working teachers for DP-level subjects.

  Screen each with a 20-minute demo lesson, which the student and one parent watch.
- [ ] **Find the first customers** with this message, sent to the family's network, school parent WhatsApp groups (where allowed) and younger siblings of friends:

  > "Hi! I'm [Name], a DP1 student at [School]. I've started [Brand] — focused IGCSE/MYP/IB tutoring and exam bootcamps taught by high-scoring IB graduates and teachers. First diagnostic session is free: a 45-minute session that maps exactly where your child is losing marks. Reply 'diagnostic' to book. — [Name], [phone]"

- [ ] Check your school's policy on students tutoring other students for money. Do not market inside school without permission.

**Phase 0 exit test:** 5 tutors signed, 10 free diagnostic sessions booked, payments working.

#### Phase 1: Prove it works (December 2026 – March 2027; 8–10 hours a week)

This is the IGCSE and board-exam peak, the best possible time to launch.

- **Every diagnostic session ends with a written 1-page "mark-loss report"** and a package offer: 10 hours at the full price, or 20 hours with 10% off.
- **The founder personally teaches 3–4 hours a week** of Grade 8–10 Maths or Science. This builds credibility, and the income counts as skill income in the founder's own name.
- **Winter-break bootcamp (late December):** 1 IGCSE subject, 6–10 students.
- **January–March:** 2 more bootcamps before the April–May IGCSE exams.
- **Referral rule:** any family that refers a paying family gets 1 free hour.
- **Weekly 30-minute review every Sunday:** check new leads, conversion rate (diagnostic → paid, target 40%+), hours delivered, cash collected, and any complaints.

**Phase 1 milestone (31 March 2027):**
- 15+ paying families;
- ₹2.5 lakh+ total revenue;
- 70% of families renewing;
- zero safeguarding incidents.

**If this fails:** fewer than 5 families renewed by 31 March → stop Track A and switch to Track B over the April holidays. Total cost by then should be under ₹1.5 lakh.

#### Phase 2: Systems and delegation (April – August 2027)

The student's time is limited here: DP1 end-of-year exams and IAs fall around April–May, and Extended Essay writing starts over the summer. So the goal of this phase is to **hire people to replace the founder's time**.

- **Hire a part-time operations coordinator** (₹15,000–25,000 a month), for example a college student or an experienced admin. They handle scheduling, payment reminders, rescheduling and tutor timesheets. They are paid from revenue, not family money. Write a 2-page operations manual first.
- **Raise tutors to 10–12.** Add DP-level subject specialists for Maths AA/AI, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Economics and Business Management.
- **Summer bootcamps (May–June):** "IGCSE head start" for students entering Grade 10, and "DP1 to DP2 bridge" sessions.
- **Start a content channel.** Post one 60-second exam-tip reel a week, recorded in batches in a single afternoon each month, plus a monthly email to parents. It brings in leads for free.
- **Decide on incorporating.** Once revenue passes about ₹3 lakh a month, or you hire staff, incorporate a **Private Limited Company** through the government's SPICe+ form. Costs: about ₹10,000–20,000 in professional fees, and around ₹40,000–70,000 a year in compliance (CA, ROC filings, audit).
  - **Directors:** two adults (both parents, or a parent and a trusted relative).
  - **Shareholders:** the student holds the majority of shares through their guardian, with a parent holding the rest.
  - **At 18,** the student gets a DIN and becomes a director.
  - Do not use an OPC or an LLP: a minor cannot be the OPC member or an LLP partner.
- **GST.** Private tutoring is taxable at 18%. Registration becomes mandatory once yearly turnover passes **₹20 lakh** (services, Maharashtra). Under a **commission/agent model**, tutors bill parents through the platform and the agency's turnover is only its margin, so you may stay under the threshold for longer. Under a **principal model**, the full fee counts as turnover. Decide this with the CA before you pass ₹1.5 lakh a month in revenue.

**Phase 2 milestone (31 August 2027):** **₹1 lakh+ net profit in at least one month**, with the coordinator running daily operations.

#### Phase 3: Run on autopilot through DP2 (September 2027 – May 2028; 3–5 hours a week)

University applications (US Early Decision about 1 November, UCAS about 14 January, Oxbridge/medicine about 15 October), mock exams and final exams all happen here. The founder's job shrinks to:

- the weekly review (30 minutes);
- tutor quality checks (watch 2 recorded sessions a week);
- 1 hour a week of strategy.

Also in this phase:
- **Launch digital resources.** These are original notes and worked-solution packs for IGCSE Maths and Sciences, priced at ₹999–2,999 each, written mostly by tutors and paid per pack. Sell through Gumroad, Razorpay payment pages or a Shopify store. This income needs no founder time.
- **Pre-book January–April 2028 bootcamps in November 2027**, run by tutors.
- **Freeze rule:** from the start of IB mocks to the end of finals, there are no new products, no new hires, and only the weekly review.

**Phase 3 target:** ₹1–1.5 lakh a month average profit, with 40+ active students.

#### Phase 4: Full-time summer and hand-over (May – August 2028, after the exams)

- The founder works 30–40 hours a week.
- Expand online to Dubai, Singapore and Bengaluru families.
- Add a SAT/AP/university-essay coaching line. Coach only; never ghostwrite essays.
- **At 18:**
  - get a DIN and join the company board;
  - move signatory authority from the parent to the founder;
  - review the family agreement.
- **Decide what happens during university:**
  1. keep the business with a hired general manager and the founder as owner (the most likely option);
  2. sell it to a larger edtech company or tutoring group;
  3. close it after documenting what was learned.

## 4. Track B in brief: an AI-assisted digital agency for local businesses

**Use this if** the student is strong at design, video or tech, or if Track A fails its March 2027 test.

**Who buys it:** restaurants, clinics, salons, boutiques, real-estate brokers, coaching classes and family-network businesses in Mumbai that have poor online presence.

**What you sell:**

| Package | Price | What's included |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | ₹25,000–40,000, one time | Google Business Profile tidy-up, WhatsApp Business catalogue, a 5-page website, review-request system |
| Monthly retainer | ₹15,000–30,000 | 12 social posts and reels made with AI tools plus human editing, monthly report, review replies |

**The numbers:** 6 retainers × ₹20,000 = ₹1.2 lakh revenue. Paying 2 freelance editors (about ₹30,000) leaves about **₹90,000 a month**, plus setup fees.

**Steps:**

1. Build 3 free or discounted case studies for family contacts in weeks 1–4, with before-and-after numbers (calls, reviews, map views).
2. Go door to door on one high street (for example Linking Road or Hill Road) with a printed one-page audit of each business's Google listing.
3. Aim for 2 paying clients by week 8 and 6 retainers by month 6.
4. Hire 2 freelance editors on Upwork, Internshala or from local colleges, and a part-time account manager by month 6.

The legal and tax setup is the same as Track A: the parent's proprietorship signs, and you incorporate later. Note that GST applies at 18% once turnover passes ₹20 lakh.

**Test by month 4:** 3 paying retainers, or stop.

## 5. Track C in brief: a consumer product brand (only with a family supply chain)

**Use this only if** the family already makes or trades a product, such as textiles, food, packaging or cosmetics, and can provide production and warehousing.

**Steps:**

1. Pick one product with a **gross margin of 60% or more** that sells for ₹800 or more.
2. Make a test batch of 300–500 units, using about ₹3–5 lakh of family business capacity.
3. Sell first through your own Shopify store and Instagram, then Amazon/Flipkart. Selling on marketplaces usually requires a GSTIN. Food needs FSSAI registration (basic registration below ₹12 lakh turnover). Register a trademark in your product's class (₹4,500 per class for individuals or small enterprises filing online).
4. **Kill test:** if 300 units haven't sold within 90 days at a positive margin after delivery and ad costs, stop.

This track takes the most working capital and the most time. It is the most likely of the three to hurt IB grades. Choose it only with a family operations team doing the daily work.

## 6. How the money flows, and tax

| Income | Received in | Taxed in the hands of |
|---|---|---|
| Student's own tutoring or teaching (skill income) | Student's minor savings account | **Student** (the skill-income exception to clubbing). The student files their own return and the basic exemption applies. |
| Agency margin, bootcamp profit, resource sales (sole proprietorship stage) | Parent's proprietorship current account | **Parent** (it is the parent's business). Money later given to the student is a gift from a relative, which is tax-free for the student. However, income the student then earns on that money is clubbed with the parent's until 18. |
| After incorporation | The company | **Company** (corporate tax, about 25% plus surcharge and cess). The student receives dividends as a shareholder; until 18 these are clubbed with a parent's income. |

**Bring these questions to the CA:**
1. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, does the student's tutoring qualify as skill income?
2. Should we use a commission model or a principal model for GST?
3. What TDS applies to tutor payments above the threshold?
4. When should we register for Professional Tax and the Shops & Establishments Act? Under Maharashtra's 2017 Act, fewer than 10 workers needs only an intimation. A home-based, online-only business may not need it at all.
5. How should the family's money be recorded: as a loan, as equity, or as a gift?

## 7. Protecting the IB and university applications

**Weekly time plan in term (Track A, Phases 1–2):**

| Day | Business time |
|---|---|
| Mon–Thu | 30 minutes a day replying to leads and tutors on WhatsApp (batched at one time each day) |
| Sat | 3–4 hours: own teaching, demo lessons, a bootcamp when one is running |
| Sun | 30-minute weekly review, 1 hour on content or hiring |
| **Total** | **about 8–10 hours** |

- **Grade protection rule** (written into the family agreement): if any predicted grade falls by one point, business hours are halved until it recovers.
- **CAS:** paid work generally isn't suitable for CAS. A **free** part of the business can be a strong CAS service project, for example weekly free tutoring for students at a municipal school or an NGO such as Teach For India partner schools or Akanksha Foundation, run by your tutors.
- **University applications:** keep a simple impact log covering students taught, hours, grade improvements (with consent), revenue, team size, and problems solved. That gives the activities list and essays real, checkable numbers. Admissions readers value **verified results and genuine responsibility**. Don't inflate any figure.

## 8. Risks and how to handle them

| Risk | How to handle it |
|---|---|
| Tutors take clients directly | Non-solicitation clause, prepaid packages, the agency owns the parent relationship (all messages go through the business WhatsApp), and good pay to keep good tutors |
| Safeguarding incident with a minor | Background checks, recorded online sessions, an adult present at home sessions, and a written escalation process. Know your POCSO Act reporting duties. |
| Academic misconduct claims | A written "we teach, we never write your coursework" policy in the tutor agreement and the parent terms; remove any tutor who breaks it |
| Grades slip | The grade protection rule, the freeze rule during exams, and a hired coordinator |
| Customers come in seasons (peaks in Jan–Apr, quiet months) | Bootcamps in holidays, digital resources, online international families in other school calendars |
| Paying too much tax or breaking rules | A CA from week 2, quarterly check-ins, and incorporating when revenue passes the threshold |
| Wasting family money | Tranches tied to milestones, a ₹5 lakh hard cap, and stop dates written down in advance |
| Founder burnout | The 10-hours-a-week cap, and delegating operations by month 6–8 |
