# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

## The answer in one paragraph

Start a **tutoring business for IGCSE and MYP students in Mumbai, run in your own name. Begin by tutoring yourself, then turn it into a small agency of IB tutors.** Pick it because it fits the rules a 16-year-old in India has to work under. It needs almost no capital, so the family money cannot be lost in it. Indian tax law treats income from your own skill as yours and doesn't add it to your parents' income. You can run it in 4–6 hours a week alongside DP1. Prepaid packages solve the problem that a minor can't enforce a contract. Mumbai has a large, well-paying IB/IGCSE market that you already know from the inside.

Realistic targets:
- **₹25,000–40,000/month by the end of DP1**, tutoring yourself.
- **₹1 lakh+/month margin by about age 18–19**, once other tutors work through you.

₹1 lakh a month is "job-replacing" money in Mumbai (definition below). Every number below is shown with its working or marked as an assumption, so you can check it.

---

## Assumptions (things the request does not say)

1. **"Job-replacing money" = ₹1,00,000 per month of profit** (₹12 lakh/year). That is above a typical fresher graduate salary in Mumbai, which is roughly ₹4–8 lakh a year (*assumption: market estimate, not a cited figure*). Change the target and the maths below scales in a straight line.
2. **The family will back the plan legally**, meaning a parent signs as guardian, but **won't treat the business as a place to put large sums.** At about ₹88/USD (*assumption: late-2025 rate*), US$8M is roughly ₹70 crore. Money is not the constraint. Inexperience is.
3. **The student is reasonably strong academically**, predicted IB 36+ or a 7 in at least one subject. If not, see "If tutoring doesn't fit you" below.
4. **The aim is income the student earns, not income from the family's wealth.** A 4% return on ₹70 crore already "replaces a job" many times over, so that route answers a different question.
5. Laws are as they stood in late 2025. India's **Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026** and renumbers sections. The 1961 section numbers are quoted below because most guidance still uses them. Ask a CA to confirm the new numbers and any rate changes (step 0.4).

## The facts this plan rests on (check each one)

| # | Fact | Source | What it means for the plan |
|---|------|--------|------------------------|
| F1 | A minor (under 18) is not competent to contract, and a contract with a minor is void. | Indian Contract Act 1872, s.11; *Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose* (1903) | You can't enforce a deal against a client. So **take payment in advance**, and have a parent sign anything that needs a signature. |
| F2 | A minor cannot be a member or nominee of a One Person Company. A minor can't be a full partner either, only "admitted to the benefits" of a partnership. | Companies (Incorporation) Rules 2014, Rule 3; Indian Partnership Act 1932, s.30 | **No company or LLP for now.** Trade as yourself (an informal sole proprietorship) until 18. |
| F3 | A minor's income is normally added to a parent's income for tax. The exception is income from **"manual work or any activity involving application of his skill, talent or specialised knowledge and experience."** | Income-tax Act 1961, s.64(1A) and its proviso | Tutoring income is skill income, so it is **taxed as yours**, not your parents'. Interest on money gifted to you would be added to your parent's income. |
| F4 | Under the new tax regime (FY 2025-26), a resident individual pays **zero tax on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh** because of the s.87A rebate. | Finance Act 2025 | Up to the full ₹1 lakh/month target, **your tax is likely nil**. File a return anyway, through your guardian. |
| F5 | GST registration for a supplier of services is required only above **₹20 lakh** aggregate turnover in Maharashtra. | CGST Act 2017, s.22 | No GST until turnover passes ₹20 lakh/year, which is beyond the Phase 3 target. |
| F6 | Since April 2025, RBI lets **minors over 10** open and **operate** savings and term deposit accounts on their own, with limits the bank sets. | RBI circular, 21 April 2025 | You can collect UPI payments into **your own** account. |
| F7 | Adolescents aged 14–18 may work in non-hazardous occupations. | Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act 1986, as amended 2016 | Tutoring and running a small agency are legal. |
| F8 | Processing a child's personal data needs **verifiable parental consent**. | Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and Rules 2025 | Your students are minors, so get a parent's consent on the sign-up form and collect only the minimum data. |
| F9 | The IB DP core includes **CAS** (Creativity, Activity, Service) over 18 months, plus the Extended Essay (4,000 words) and the TOK essay (1,600 words). | IB DP programme documentation | Your time is the real limit. Free peer tutoring can count as CAS **Service**, but paid work cannot. Keep the two separate. |

## The plan

### Phase 0: Setup (weeks 1–2, about 6 hours in total)

| Step | Exact action | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | Get a **PAN** in your own name (Form 49A on the NSDL/Protean or UTIITSL site, with a parent as representative). | About ₹107 |
| 0.2 | Open **your own savings account with UPI** (F6). Use a separate account just for the business. | ₹0 |
| 0.3 | Have the family sign a one-page **"Founder Agreement"** that covers: <br>• the parent acts as guardian signatory<br>• a seed fund (see the rules on family money below)<br>• the time cap (see Phase 1)<br>• the stop rules (see "When to stop or change course") | ₹0 |
| 0.4 | Book **one meeting with a CA** to confirm three things: <br>• s.64(1A) skill-income treatment under the new 2025 Act<br>• how your return will be filed<br>• whether anything changed after these facts were checked | About ₹3,000–5,000 (*assumption*) |
| 0.5 | Write the **policy page**, a one-page PDF and also your website's FAQ:<br>• fees are prepaid in packages<br>• 24-hour cancellation rule<br>• a parent is present or sessions are recorded for students under 14<br>• data consent (F8)<br>• no refunds after the 2nd session of a package | ₹0 |
| 0.6 | Set up the basics:<br>• a Google Workspace or free Gmail address<br>• a WhatsApp Business account<br>• a one-page site (Carrd or Google Sites)<br>• Google Calendar booking slots<br>• Google Forms for sign-up and consent | ₹0–2,000/year |

**Don't do these yet:** company, LLP, trademark, GST, Udyam registration, Shop & Establishment registration, paid ads, an app, or an office. None of them earns money for you before you have revenue, and F2 blocks some of them anyway.

### Phase 1: Tutor yourself (DP1, months 1–6)

**What you sell:** IGCSE (Grades 9–10) or MYP tutoring in your 2–3 strongest subjects. Maths, sciences and English are the usual choices. You are one or two years ahead of these students, you know their syllabus, and you use the same mark schemes.

**Pricing** (*assumption: check it by asking 5 parents and 3 existing tutors in your area during week 1*):
- A trial session is ₹1,000.
- Then sell a **10-session package for ₹15,000** (₹1,500/hour), paid in advance by UPI.
- Online sessions or sessions in the student's home are fine. Online lets you cover more of Mumbai.

**Time cap: 5 hours a week of tutoring and 1 hour of admin.** This is non-negotiable. It works out to about 20 paid hours a month.

**Revenue by month 6:** 20 hours × ₹1,500 = **₹30,000 a month**.

**Finding customers, in this order** (all free, based on warm networks):
1. **Week 1:** Message 30 families you know who have children in Grades 7–10 at IB or IGCSE schools. Include younger siblings of friends, your own former teachers' contacts, and family friends. Send one message with your subjects, your grades as proof, the trial price and a booking link.
2. **Week 2:** Ask every trial family for **two referrals**. Give them one free session for each referral that turns into a paying student.
3. **Month 2:** Post in the parent WhatsApp groups of 3–4 housing societies near you, but only through a parent you know who is already a member.
4. **Month 3 onward:** Every month, publish one free resource, such as a "Grade 10 Chemistry formula sheet" or a "MYP eAssessment checklist," with your contact details on it. Share it in the same groups.

**Proof of results:** Record each student's test score before and after (with permission). By month 4 you want 3 written parent testimonials. These two things let you raise prices.

**Phase 1 is done when:** you have 8+ paying students, at least 3 testimonials, and your own IB predicted grades have **not dropped**.

### Phase 2: Become a small agency (DP1 end to DP2, months 7–18)

You can't scale your own hours in DP2. You **can** scale other people's hours.

1. **Recruit 3–5 tutors:**
   - IB graduates at Mumbai colleges, aged 18 or over (they can sign contracts, unlike you).
   - Strong DP2 students at other schools.
   - Look for a predicted or final score of 38+ and a 7 in the subject they will teach.
   - Find them through your school's alumni, college societies and LinkedIn.
2. **Pay** tutors **₹800–1,000 an hour**, by UPI from your business account, after each session is done.
3. **Charge** clients **₹1,800–2,000 an hour**, now justified by your testimonials and a brand.
   - **Margin is about ₹900 per tutor-hour.** (*Assumption: check against what you see in Phase 1.*)
4. **Your new job:**
   - matching students to tutors
   - quality checks (sit in on 1 recorded session per tutor per month)
   - billing
   - marketing
   - Budget: **4 hours a week**. Stop most of your own tutoring.
5. **Get one parent to sign the tutor agreements** as guardian of the business. Each one is a one-page document covering rate, payment terms, the child-safety rules, a commitment not to poach your students for 12 months, and data handling (F8).
6. **Use a shared Google Sheet as your CRM**, with these columns: student, school, subject, tutor, package start, sessions left, paid (Y/N), latest score.

**Month 18 target** (at the start of the DP2 exam period):
- 5 tutors × 15 hours a month = 75 tutor-hours.
- 75 × ₹900 = **about ₹67,500 a month in margin.**
- During May exams of DP2, reduce your own role to billing only. Write down your processes beforehand so a tutor can cover matching.

### Phase 3: Reach job-replacing income (age 18, after the IB)

**On your 18th birthday:**
1. Update your bank account from minor to major status.
2. Sign contracts yourself.
3. Register for **Udyam** (free) if you want MSME status.
4. Keep operating as a sole proprietorship, with your name as the trade name.
5. Move to a **Private Limited Company** only if you take on a co-founder or an outside investor, or if profit gets large enough that your CA advises it.

**To reach ₹1 lakh a month:** you need about **110 tutor-hours a month at ₹900 margin**, for example 7–8 tutors at 15 hours each.

**Two ways to grow from there:**
1. **Add DP students as customers.** Your tutors are now graduates, and DP tutoring sells for more, roughly ₹2,500–4,000 an hour (*assumption*).
2. **Run holiday and exam crash courses in small groups.** For example, 6 students × ₹1,000 an hour = ₹6,000 an hour of revenue, against a tutor cost of ₹1,500.

**GST:** If revenue gets close to ₹20 lakh a year (F5), register for GST and charge 18%. Raise prices first so your margin stays the same.

**Studying abroad:** If you go to university outside India, the business can keep running online with Indian tutors. Before you leave, check your residency and tax position with your CA.

## Rules for using the family's money

Family capital helps by **removing pressure**, not by being spent.

1. **Seed fund: up to ₹50,000.** It covers the CA fee, the website, a laptop or tablet if you need one, and printing. Treat it as a **gift**: a gift from a relative is tax-free to you (Income-tax Act 1961, s.56(2)(x)). Keep the seed money and the business income in separate records, because interest earned on gifted money may be added to your parent's income (F3).
2. **No more than ₹2 lakh in total** may go into the business before it is profitable for 3 months in a row.
3. **What the family's wealth is really for:**
   - keeping you from needing the income, so you can charge properly and turn down bad clients
   - covering legal and CA advice
   - introductions to families and schools
4. **Don't use family money to buy growth** through paid ads, an app, or a physical centre with a lease. That is how a 16-year-old with access to money loses ₹20 lakh learning what a ₹0 test would have shown.

**Stop rule:** if your predicted grades fall by 2 or more points overall, cut back to admin only until they recover. A 40-point IB score is worth more over your life than ₹30,000 a month at 16.

## When to stop or change course (decide these now)

- **After 6 weeks:** fewer than 3 paying students means the problem is your subject or price, not demand. Switch to your strongest subject and drop the trial price to ₹500.
- **After 3 months:** fewer than 5 paying students means test a different segment, for example Grade 6–8 maths or SAT Maths.
- **After 6 months:** clients leaving after one package means your teaching quality or proof of results is the problem. Fix that before you hire anyone.

## If tutoring doesn't fit you

The same pattern works for any **skill-based service**: no capital, prepaid, sold first to people you know, and later turned into an agency. That pattern is what keeps it simple, legal (F1, F2) and taxed in your own hands (F3). Two other options that work the same way:
- **Video editing or design** for Mumbai's small businesses and creators, at ₹5,000–15,000 per month on retainer (*assumption*).
- **Coding small websites and automations** for local businesses.

Avoid inventory-based D2C products, apps that need fundraising, and anything that involves trading or crypto. These need capital, contracts or regulatory standing that a minor doesn't have, and the income would mostly count as your parents' for tax (F3).
