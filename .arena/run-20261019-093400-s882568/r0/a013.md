# A 19-Month Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

**Short version.** Start a **skill-based service business that bills in advance**. The default pick is a done-for-you "local digital presence" service for Mumbai small businesses, sold as a monthly retainer. Run it in the student's own name, with a parent signing as guardian. Keep total family money at risk under **₹2 lakh until the business proves itself, and under ₹10 lakh before age 18**. Set "job-replacing money" as a target of **₹50,000–₹1,00,000 a month in profit by mid-2028**. That target is realistic but not likely. My estimate is that roughly 1 in 5 committed students who follow this plan reach ₹50k a month. The plan is built so that the outcomes most students actually get, such as ₹10–30k a month, a real skill, a strong university application and a protected IB score, still count as wins. The ₹8M net worth is a safety net, not fuel. The worst realistic outcomes are losing a lot of family money or losing IB points, and the plan is designed around avoiding those two.

## 1. Why this business, and not the obvious alternatives

Every option was scored on what matters for *this* person: little time (IB), little legal capacity (a minor), lots of family capital (which tempts overspending), and a two-year window. My probability estimates are rough judgments from base rates, not measured data.

| Option | P(first ₹ in 60 days) | P(≥ ₹50k/month profit by mid-2028) | Worst realistic loss | Fits IB hours? | Minor-law friction | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Local digital-presence service on retainer** (Google Business Profile, WhatsApp Business catalogue, simple website, review generation for clinics, salons, cafés, coaching classes) | ~70% | ~15–25% | ₹50k–1L | Yes; work is async and batchable | Low; bill in advance | **Default pick** |
| Tutoring MYP / IGCSE / ICSE students | ~80% | ~5–10% (capped by hours) | ~₹10k | Yes | Very low | **Best fallback.** Safe but limited ceiling. |
| Freelance video editing / design for creators and D2C brands | ~50% | ~10–15% | ~₹30k (tools) | Mostly | Platform age limits (most need 18+) | Good if the student already edits |
| Micro-SaaS / app | ~10% | ~3–5% | Time, plus ₹1–5L if developers are hired | Poor; long build cycles | Low | Only for strong coders; see Section 10 |
| D2C product brand with inventory | ~40% | ~3–7% | **₹5–30L** (stock, ads) | Poor; fulfilment is daily | Medium | **Avoid before 18** |
| YouTube / Instagram creator | ~5% | ~2–5% (power law) | Time | Medium | Platform rules | Do it as a side channel, not the business |
| Stock F&O, crypto, "trading" | n/a | Negative expectation | Unlimited | No | Minors can't trade F&O | **Banned** (Section 2) |
| Buying a franchise or an existing business | n/a | ~5% | **₹10L–1Cr** | No | High (minor can't sign) | **Banned** before 18 |

**Why the default wins.** The default isn't the option with the highest ceiling. It wins because it holds up in almost every scenario:

1. **Demand is real and close by.** Mumbai has hundreds of thousands of small establishments. Many have incomplete Google listings, no reply system for reviews, and no WhatsApp catalogue. The student can check this personally in one afternoon on Google Maps.
2. **Recurring revenue.** A retainer means income builds month on month instead of resetting.
3. **Capital needed is tiny.** If it fails, the family loses less than a mid-range laptop costs.
4. **It's skill-based, which matters for tax** (Section 3). Income from a minor's own skill can stay in the minor's hands instead of being added to the parent's income.
5. **It builds the skills that carry over:** selling, pricing, delivering, managing people. These still matter whatever the student does after 18.

**Main threat to the default (confidence: medium):** AI tools keep pushing down the price of building websites. So the offer is **not** "I build websites." It is "I keep your listing, reviews and WhatsApp running every month and report the numbers." That is execution and accountability, which AI cheapens much less than one-off builds.

## 2. Hard rules (each one exists because a common failure happens without it)

Write these down, have the student and both parents sign them, and pin them up. Breaking one stops the business automatically until a family review.

1. **IB comes first, and this is measured.** If any predicted grade falls by **2 points or more**, or the total predicted score drops **3 or more points** below the October 2026 baseline, the business goes into maintenance mode (existing clients only, no new sales) until the next report.
2. **Time cap.** During term: **at most 8 hours a week**. During school holidays: up to 30. In the **8 weeks before any major exam block**: at most 2 hours a week, existing clients only. Log hours in a shared sheet; if it isn't logged, it didn't happen.
3. **Money cap with stage gates.** At most ₹2 lakh of family money until Gate 1, and at most ₹10 lakh in total before age 18 (gates in Section 5). The family's net worth never decides the budget. **Evidence decides the budget.**
4. **No credit to customers.** Setup fees are paid 100% upfront. Retainers are paid monthly **in advance**. (A minor's contracts are generally unenforceable, so the student can't easily chase a debt. Billing in advance removes the problem.)
5. **Banned activities before 18:** F&O or margin trading, crypto, MLM or "network" schemes, paid "mentor" or "make money online" courses over ₹5,000, franchises, inventory imports, signing leases, lending to friends, and any partnership where the other side asks the family for money.
   - Why: SEBI's own studies found **about 9 in 10 individual F&O traders lost money** (FY22 and FY24).
   - Separately, a wealthy family with a teenage founder is a **target**. Assume every unsolicited "opportunity" is a sales pitch until proven otherwise.
6. **No sensitive data.** Do not collect or store patients' health records, customers' ID documents, or payment-card data for clients. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules are coming into force in phases, and a teenager should not be anyone's data processor for sensitive data. Work only on the client's *public-facing* presence.
7. **Friends-and-family revenue doesn't count.** Money from relatives or parents' contacts is welcome, but it **is not counted** toward any gate. Gates count only arm's-length customers. Otherwise the family is funding a reassuring illusion.

## 3. Legal and tax setup for a minor in India: what is likely true, and what to verify

| Topic | What is likely true (confidence) | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Contracts** | A minor's agreement is void under the Indian Contract Act, 1872 (s.11; *Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose*, 1903). **High confidence.** | Use a one-page "service order" signed by the client and by a parent **as guardian**. Bill in advance (Rule 4). |
| **Company / director** | A minor in practice cannot be a director or get a DIN, and cannot be a full partner (the Partnership Act only lets a minor share benefits). **High confidence.** | **Don't incorporate before 18.** Operate as an individual. Incorporate after 18 if Gate 3 is passed. |
| **PAN** | A minor can get a PAN (applied for via the guardian). **High confidence.** | Apply now on the NSDL/Protean or UTIITSL portal. It costs about ₹100. |
| **Bank account** | Since RBI's April 2025 directions, banks may let minors **aged 10+ open and operate savings accounts on their own**, within bank-set limits. **Medium-high confidence**; limits vary by bank. | Open a minor savings account with UPI in the student's name. All business money goes through **this account only**, never mixed with pocket money. If the limits are too low, use a separate account with a parent as guardian operator. |
| **Payment gateways** (Razorpay, Stripe etc.) and freelance platforms (Upwork, Fiverr, etc.) | Generally need an 18+ account holder or business KYC. **Medium confidence**; terms change. | Don't open these in a parent's name and pretend it's the parent's account, because that breaks the platform's terms. Use UPI or bank transfer from local clients until 18. |
| **Income tax: clubbing** | A minor's income is normally added to the higher-earning parent's income (old Act s.64(1A)). **But income from the minor's own manual work or skill, talent or specialised knowledge is excluded** and is taxed as the minor's own. This rule is carried into the 2025 Act. **Medium-high confidence.** | Keep evidence that **the student personally does the work**: time logs, deliverables, client messages. Have the CA confirm treatment before 31 March 2027. |
| **Tax on the minor's own income** | Under the new regime for FY 2025-26, total income up to **₹12 lakh** is effectively tax-free because of the s.87A rebate. **Medium-high confidence for that year; check the rates for FY 2026-27.** | If the skill exception applies, the student's own profit up to around ₹12 lakh a year likely costs **₹0 in tax**. Compare that with roughly 30%+ if it is added to a wealthy parent's income. File the student's own ITR every year, even if the tax is zero. |
| **Clubbing trap** | Once subcontractors do most of the work, income is arguably from *others' labour or capital*, not the minor's skill, so it may be added to the parent's income again. **Medium confidence.** | Before hiring anyone (Phase 3), ask the CA. Keep the student as the hands-on lead on every account. |
| **GST** | Registration is required once aggregate turnover passes **₹20 lakh a year for services** in Maharashtra. **High confidence.** | Not needed at the start. Set an alarm at ₹15 lakh in rolling 12-month revenue and consult the CA. |
| **Shop / establishment registration** | Home-based, no employees: under Maharashtra's 2017 Act, small establishments generally need at most an intimation. **Medium confidence.** | Ask the CA in the same session. |
| **Labour law** | Adolescents (14–18) can work in non-hazardous work. Running your own service business from home outside school hours is fine. **High confidence.** | Don't hire anyone under 18 to work for you. Peers can be paid per-task freelancers only after CA advice. |
| **IB CAS** | Paid work generally **does not count** as CAS. **Medium-high confidence**; ask the CAS coordinator. | Don't try to double-count the business as CAS. A *free* service for an NGO, using the same skills, may qualify as a CAS experience or project. |

## 4. Phase 0: Setup (19 October – 8 November 2026, about 3 weeks, about 10 hours in total)

**Family steps**

1. Hold a family meeting and sign the Section 2 rules. Agree on the **Phase 1 budget: ₹75,000**, held by a parent and released against receipts.
2. Apply for the student's PAN and open the minor savings account with UPI.
3. Book a 1-hour CA consultation (₹5,000–15,000). Bring this exact question list:
   - Does the skill-income exception cover a digital-services business run personally by a 16-year-old?
   - What records do we need to keep?
   - What changes once we pay subcontractors?
   - Any Maharashtra professional-tax or shop-registration requirement?
   - How do we file the minor's own ITR for FY 2026-27?
   - At what point should we incorporate after the student turns 18, and as what (OPC or Pvt Ltd)?
4. Set up a basic accounts system: one Google Sheet with tabs for Income, Expenses, Hours, Clients and Gates. Keep every receipt, scanned into a Drive folder.

**Student steps**

5. Record the baseline: October 2026 predicted grades and the total predicted score.
6. Learn the core skills in about 12 hours, all free:
   - Google Business Profile help centre
   - WhatsApp Business catalogue and quick replies
   - Website builders (Google Sites, Wix, Framer, WordPress, or Shopify for retail)
   - Canva
   - A review-request flow using QR codes and short links
7. **Build the proof first:** set up the profile, catalogue and one-page site for **2 real businesses free of charge**. Pick a neighbourhood shop, not relatives. Screenshot every before and after, and record 30 days of the free Google Business Profile Insights (calls, direction requests, views).

**Exit check for Phase 0:** PAN applied, account open, CA consulted, 2 free case studies live. If the CA says the structure is unworkable, switch to tutoring (Section 10). It needs almost no structure.

## 5. The phased plan with stage gates

The dates assume a typical Mumbai IB calendar: a Diwali break around 8 November 2026, a winter break in late December, DP1 end-of-year exams around April–June 2027, and DP2 finals in May 2028. **Swap in your school's actual calendar on day 1.**

### Phase 1: First paying customers (9 November 2026 – 10 January 2027; uses the Diwali and winter breaks)

**Pick a niche.** Choose one area within commuting distance (for example Bandra West, Andheri West or Powai) and **one vertical**. Dental and physio clinics, salons and cafés are good candidates: they depend on local search and reviews and can afford ₹3–6k a month. Clinics: public listing work only (Rule 6).

**Build a list of 100 prospects** from Google Maps. Score each listing on six checks:
- no photos
- fewer than 50 reviews
- unanswered reviews
- wrong hours
- no website
- no WhatsApp

**Outreach:**
1. For each of the top 40, make a one-page **audit**: their score, 3 specific fixes, and a screenshot of a competitor doing better.
2. Deliver it in person on a Saturday with a parent nearby for the first few visits, or on WhatsApp.
3. Use a simple script: *"I'm a student who helps [area] clinics get more calls from Google. I checked your listing and found 3 things costing you patients. Here's the free audit. If you want, I'll fix all of it this week for ₹4,999, and I only start once you've seen the plan."*

**Offer (pilot):** a one-time setup fee of **₹4,999**, paid upfront by UPI. It covers the listing overhaul, 20 edited photos, a WhatsApp catalogue, a one-page site, and a review QR stand (about ₹300 to print).

**Spend limits:** at most ₹20,000 on tools and printing, and at most ₹15,000 on a paid-ads test (Google Local or Instagram), **only after** 2 sales came from direct outreach.

**Gate 1 (by 10 January 2027):** **3 or more arm's-length paying clients**, no refunds, Rule 1 not triggered.
- **Pass:** unlock up to ₹1.25 lakh more (total cap ₹2 lakh).
- **Fail:** do one 4-week retry with a different vertical. If that fails too, switch to tutoring. **Estimated P(pass) is about 55–65%** for a student who actually does the 40 visits. Most failures come from not doing the outreach, not from a lack of demand.

### Phase 2: From one-off jobs to monthly retainers (January – April 2027, term time, at most 8 hours a week)

1. Turn every setup client into a **retainer at ₹3,000–6,000 a month**. It covers:
   - 8 Google posts
   - replies to every review within 48 hours
   - 10 new photos
   - catalogue updates
   - a monthly one-page report (calls, direction requests, review count and rating)
2. Price new setups at **₹7,999–14,999** once there are 3 case studies with numbers.
3. **Systemise:** write a step-by-step SOP for each recurring task, a client onboarding form, and a standard monthly report template. Batch all client work into **two fixed blocks a week** (for example Wednesday 6–8 pm and Saturday 9 am–1 pm).
4. Add one new client a fortnight through referrals. Offer one free month for each referred client who stays 2 months.

**Gate 2 (by 30 April 2027):** **₹25,000 or more in monthly recurring revenue (MRR) from 6 or more clients, no single client over 30% of revenue, monthly churn under 15%, and predicted grades held.**
- **Pass:** total family capital cap rises to ₹5 lakh.
- **Fail on revenue only:** keep running what exists and don't add spending.
- **Fail on grades:** maintenance mode (Rule 1).
- **Estimated P(pass) is about 35–45%** given Gate 1 was passed.

### Phase 3: Leverage (May – July 2027; DP1 exams, then summer break)

1. Pause selling during DP1 end-of-year exams. Existing retainers continue, since about 2 hours a week can be done ahead in batches.
2. Over the summer (up to 30 hours a week, but **Extended Essay research comes first**: block 10 hours a week for the EE before business hours):
   - **Add 1–2 per-task freelancers** (college students, 18+) for photo editing and posting at ₹300–600 per task, **only after a CA check** (Section 3 clubbing trap). The student keeps sales, the client relationship and quality checks.
   - Raise the retainer to ₹5,000–8,000 for new clients, adding WhatsApp broadcast campaigns or a simple booking link.
   - Target **12–15 retainer clients**.

**Gate 3 (by 31 July 2027):** **₹60,000 or more MRR, gross margin of 60% or more after freelancers, cash in the bank covering 2 months of costs.** Pass means the total cap rises to ₹10 lakh, which is still mostly unspent. **Estimated P(pass) is about 30–40%** given Gate 2 was passed.

### Phase 4: Hold steady through DP2 (August 2027 – May 2028)

1. **No new service lines and no new verticals.** Cap of 6 hours a week in term, and 2 hours a week from mid-March 2028 to the end of exams.
2. Before mock exams, write a **"hit by a bus" handbook**: SOPs, logins in a password manager shared with a parent, and every client's renewal date. Then a freelancer or parent can keep things running for 8 weeks.
3. Grow only through referrals and price rises (raise existing clients 10% at their 12-month mark).
4. **University applications** (UCAS or Common App, roughly August 2027 – January 2028): present the business with **real numbers** (clients, MRR, retention), what went wrong, and what was learned. Admissions readers quickly discount ventures funded by wealthy parents. "₹40k MRR from 9 clinics, earned door-to-door, ₹0 family money used after month 3" is far more credible than "founded a startup".

### Phase 5: Turning 18 (around mid-2028, after exams)

1. **Incorporate** if MRR is over ₹50k: an OPC or Private Limited company (the CA advises). Move clients onto new contracts in the company's name, now enforceable. Open a current account and a payment gateway. Register for GST when the CA says to, or by ₹20 lakh turnover.
2. Decide between three paths:
   - **Grow:** hire, add a second city or vertical, and only now consider deploying real family capital, at most the ₹10 lakh cap unless a written plan shows payback within 12 months.
   - **Run it alongside university:** online from abroad, with a local manager.
   - **Sell or hand over the client book:** small retainer books typically sell for around 1–2 years of profit, but the market is thin, so treat this as optional.
3. **Only now** consider the higher-ceiling ideas (SaaS for the clinics he or she now knows well, or a productised tool), funded by the business's own profit first.

## 7. A loud early-warning system (monthly 30-minute family review, first Sunday of each month)

Fill in this table every month. **Any red cell triggers the action shown, with no debate.**

| Metric | Green | Red line | Automatic action |

## 8. First 7 days (start now)

1. **Day 1:** print Section 2, both parents and the student sign it, and fill in the baseline predicted grades. Put the school's real calendar into Section 5's dates.
2. **Day 2:** apply for the PAN. Book a bank appointment for the minor's account. Book the CA.

## 9. What the family should and shouldn't do with its wealth

**Do:**
- pay for the CA and lawyer
- give a safe, quiet workspace
- provide transport to client visits
- make introductions **as a learning channel**, not counted toward gates
- fund a good-quality laptop if one is needed
- keep a ₹1–2 lakh "mistake budget" that isn't a big deal to lose

**Don't:**
- buy the student a business, franchise or shopfront
- fund paid ads at scale before the unit economics work
- let the business become the student's identity at the cost of IB
- open accounts on platforms in a parent's name to get around age rules
- treat the student's revenue as pocket money

Separately, consider giving the student a small **investing** education in a guardian-run demat account: delivery-based index funds only. Teach compounding with ₹50k–1 lakh. Keep it fully separate from the business.

## Summary of the plan

- **Business:** a skill-based local digital-presence service on monthly retainer, billed in advance. Fallback: tutoring.
- **Legal:** operate as an individual minor with PAN and a minor savings account, guardian-signed service orders, no company and no platforms until 18. A CA confirms the skill-income tax treatment.
- **Money:** at most ₹2 lakh until proven, at most ₹10 lakh before 18, released only through gates measured on arm's-length revenue.
- **Milestones:** 3 clients by January 2027; ₹25k MRR by April 2027; ₹60k MRR by July 2027; hold through DP2; incorporate around mid-2028 if MRR is above ₹50k.
- **Odds:** about 15–25% chance of job-replacing profit (₹50k+ a month) by mid-2028. The plan is designed so that the much more likely outcomes are still clearly good ones.
