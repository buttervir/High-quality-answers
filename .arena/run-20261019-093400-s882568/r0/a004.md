# A business plan for a 16-year-old IB DP1 student in Mumbai

## The short answer

Over the next 20 months, start a **small service business that needs little capital and runs on the student's skills**. Run it legally through a parent until the student turns 18. Give it a fixed **8 hours a week**, and fund it in **gated tranches of ₹50,000, then ₹2 lakh, then ₹10 lakh**, never a lump sum. The default choice is to **digitise and sell for the family's own business**, if there is one. If not, it is a **Mumbai SME "WhatsApp + website + AI automation" studio** sold through the parents' network. Tutoring younger students is the cash fallback. The realistic target is **₹40,000–80,000 a month in profit by the end of DP2 (May 2028)**. The real "job-replacing" level (₹1.5–2.5 lakh a month, about what a good Mumbai graduate job pays) comes at **ages 18–21**, once the student can sign contracts. One fact changes the whole question. In a family worth about $8M, income on its own is not scarce. A ₹1.5 crore gift at 18, invested in debt instruments at about 7%, produces about ₹10 lakh a year. Under the current new tax regime that can be close to tax-free in an adult child's hands. So the business before 18 exists to build **skill, a track record and judgement**. Pre-18 income is not the point, and the plan is built so that the IB Diploma, university admission and the law all survive it.

---

## Assumptions

- The student started DP1 around June–August 2026 and sits final IB exams in **May 2028**. They turn 18 around 2028.
- The family's ~$8M (about ₹70–75 crore) is partly illiquid, in property or a family business. The parents are willing to back the student, but only with modest, accountable sums.
- "Job-replacing money" means a steady **net profit equal to a good Mumbai salary**. I use ₹50,000 a month (an entry-level graduate job) as the first bar and ₹1.5–2.5 lakh a month (a strong professional job) as the full bar.
- The student may go to university abroad (US or UK) in August 2028. The plan has to survive that.
- Tax and legal points reflect Indian law as of 2025–26. The Income-tax Act 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 and renumbered sections, so check every tax point with a Chartered Accountant (CA) before acting on it.

## Part 1: Edge cases first, because they decide the design

Most "teen entrepreneur" plans fail on the problems below, not on the business idea. Each row shows the design decision it forces.

| # | Edge case / failure mode | What actually happens | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A minor cannot contract in India** | Under the Indian Contract Act, 1872, s.11, and *Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose*, a minor's contract is void. Payment gateways (Razorpay, Cashfree, Stripe), Shopify, Amazon Seller Central and AdSense all require an adult account holder. A minor cannot be a full partner in a firm (Partnership Act s.30 allows only "admission to benefits"). A minor cannot be a member of a One Person Company. In practice the MCA (the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, which registers companies) does not let a minor be a director. | **A parent is the legal face until 18.** That means a sole proprietorship in the parent's name or the family's existing company. The student can hold shares in a private company through a guardian, but cannot sign. |
| 2 | **Clubbing of a minor's income** | A minor's income is generally added to the income of the parent who earns more (old s.64(1A), carried into the 2025 Act). The exception is income from the child's *own skill, talent or specialised knowledge*. There is also a ₹1,500-per-child exemption. | Do not route business profits into the minor's name to "save tax". It does not work, and it adds paperwork. Keep business money in the parent's or the entity's books. Tax optimisation waits until 18. |
| 3 | **The IB Diploma is the real job right now** | DP1 and DP2 carry 6 subjects plus the Extended Essay (4,000 words), the Theory of Knowledge (TOK) essay and exhibition, Internal Assessments in every subject, and CAS. Predicted grades feed into university applications in DP2. | **Hard cap of 8 hours a week**, dropping to 3 hours a week from January to May 2028. Grade-based kill switches (Part 5). |
| 4 | **Abundant family money** | Teen ventures with lots of money buy offices, branding and ads before they have one customer. Money hides the signal that nobody wants the product. Café, franchise and app-agency ideas soak up ₹20–50 lakh and teach little. | **Gated capital.** Each tranche is unlocked only by revenue, not by enthusiasm. No asset-heavy businesses (cafés, franchises, inventory over ₹2 lakh) before 18. |
| 5 | **University abroad from Aug 2028** | A UK Student visa prohibits self-employment and business activity. On a US F-1 visa, off-campus work is generally prohibited. Passively owning a foreign business is usually fine, but actively running it from the US is a grey area. | The business must be **operable without the student by July 2028**, with a paid operator, documented processes and the student as owner. Take immigration-lawyer advice before leaving. If the student studies in India, this constraint disappears. |
| 6 | **The "rich kid" credibility problem** | Clients in the parents' network buy out of politeness once, then churn. Or they treat the student as free labour. | Charge real prices from day one, take 50% in advance and sell on results (case studies with numbers). Warm intros are fine, discounts for "uncle" are not. |
| 7 | **Ghost-writing and IB malpractice** | "Helping" other IB students with IAs or Extended Essays for money is academic malpractice. It can cost the student their own diploma. | Tutoring is allowed only as concept teaching for *younger* students (Grade 8–10). Never write or edit another student's assessed work. |
| 8 | **Children's data (DPDP Act 2023, DPDP Rules 2025)** | Processing data of under-18s requires verifiable parental consent. The obligations are being phased in through 2026–27. | Avoid consumer products aimed at teenagers (apps, communities) before 18. If tutoring, the customer and data subject is the parent. Collect only names and phone numbers, and get written consent. |
| 9 | **Get-rich-quick traps aimed at teens** | SEBI's study of FY22–FY24 found that about 93% of individual F&O (futures and options) traders lost money. Minors cannot trade F&O anyway. Crypto in India carries a flat 30% tax plus 1% TDS (tax deducted at source) and no loss set-off. Paid "dropshipping" and "AI agency" courses make money mainly from selling the course. | **Banned list:** F&O, crypto trading, forex, paid guru courses, MLM, and dropshipping from Chinese suppliers to Indian customers. |
| 10 | **Paid work and CAS** | IB guidance generally says activities the student is paid for do not count as CAS experiences. | Do not count on the business for CAS. A *pro bono* version (for example, building an NGO's website or WhatsApp system) can count. Confirm with the CAS coordinator. |
| 11 | **Co-founder friendships** | Two 16-year-olds splitting "50/50" with no paperwork break up by DP2, and the friendship goes with it. | No equity promises before 18. Pay friends per task. Revisit equity at 18 with 4-year vesting and a one-year cliff. |
| 12 | **Parents take over** | The founder's parent ends up running the business. The student learns nothing and resents it. | A written **Family Charter** (Part 3, Step 1) defines the parent's role as signatory and adviser, not operator. |
| 13 | **Unpaid invoices** | Small Mumbai businesses pay late (60–120 days is common). | 50% advance on setup work, monthly retainers billed in advance and services paused after 15 days overdue. |
| 14 | **GST thresholds** | GST registration becomes mandatory above ₹20 lakh of turnover for services in Maharashtra (₹40 lakh for goods). It is also generally needed for inter-state supply of goods and for selling goods on marketplaces, with limited intra-state exemptions. Exports of services need a Letter of Undertaking (LUT). | Start with services under the threshold. Register GST (free) once turnover nears ₹15 lakh, or as soon as goods are sold online. |
| 15 | **Burnout or loss of interest** | Common, and fine. The cost has to stay small. | Quarterly kill or continue reviews. A failed experiment that cost ₹50,000 and 100 hours is a good outcome if it is documented. |

## Part 2: The two strongest opposing answers, and the synthesis

### Thesis: "Use the unfair advantage hard: capital plus network, now."
The family has about ₹70 crore and a Mumbai business network, which almost no 16-year-old has. Capital-light ideas are what poor founders have to do. This student can **buy or bankroll** something real now: a franchise, a small profitable business, a D2C brand with a ₹30 lakh launch, or a rental property. Job-replacing income can literally be bought. A ₹1.5 crore allocation earning 7% produces about ₹10.5 lakh a year. Waiting wastes the head start.

### Antithesis: "Don't start a business in DP1 at all."
DP1–DP2 is the most valuable 20 months the student has. A 40+ IB score and a strong university open more options than any teenage business. A minor cannot legally own or run anything, so any "business" is really the parent's. The student also has no job to replace. Businesses funded by family money at 16 are, in practice, admissions-essay props, and admissions readers can tell. Study now and build at 18–22.

### What is true in each
- Thesis is right that **income is not scarce** for this family. That is exactly why the business should not be judged on pre-18 income. It is also right that the **network** is a real, ethical advantage for *distribution* (first customers and introductions).
- Thesis is wrong that capital speeds up learning. Before product-market fit, capital mostly speeds up losses (edge case 4). A bought business teaches the parents' skills, not the student's.
- Antithesis is right that **the IB Diploma and the law are binding constraints** (edge cases 1, 3, 5), and that a vanity project is worse than nothing.
- Antithesis is wrong that the choice is all or nothing. Eight disciplined hours a week is less than many students spend on gaming. Selling to real customers, pricing, collecting money and firing a bad client teach things school cannot. A *real* track record with numbers also reads as authentic in university applications.

### Synthesis (the plan below)
1. **Two separate tracks.** (a) A **skill track**: a small, capital-light, real-revenue business the student actually runs, limited to 8 hours a week. (b) An **ownership track**: at 18 the family can choose to fund a corpus in the student's name that produces job-replacing passive income. That is a family wealth decision, not a business achievement, and it is kept separate so it does not distort the business.
2. **Use the network for distribution, not for capital.** Introductions are free. Money is released only against revenue milestones.
3. **Use a parent as the legal wrapper until 18,** with a written charter. The student formally takes over at 18.
4. **Design for the student's absence from day one** (exam months, university abroad).

## Part 3: The plan, step by step with dates

### Step 1 (Week 1–2, late Oct–early Nov 2026): Family Charter and setup
Hold one 2-hour meeting with the student and both parents and write down a 1–2 page Family Charter. It is not legally binding; its job is to stop arguments later.

1. **Purpose:** "Learn to build a profitable business. Income before 18 is secondary."
2. **Time cap:** 8 hours a week during term, 15 hours a week in school holidays, 3 hours a week from 1 January 2028 until the last exam.
3. **Capital gates** (exact numbers in Part 5).
4. **Roles:** the parent is the legal signatory, holds bank access and is an adviser on request. The student makes every operating decision, including mistakes under ₹25,000.
5. **Where money goes:** all revenue lands in the parent's business account (or the family company's). The student gets a monthly profit statement. Profits are reinvested or held, and transferred to the student at 18 as a gift from the parent. Gifts from a parent are tax-exempt for the recipient, but check how this is reported.
6. **Kill switches** (Part 5) and the quarterly review dates (first Sunday of Feb, May, Aug and Nov).
7. **Outside adviser:** one non-family mentor (a founder or business owner) who meets the student for 30 minutes a month.

**Admin, same fortnight:**
- Student: get a PAN (minors can, via guardian, Form 49A). Open a **minor savings account** (minors aged 10+ can operate one themselves under RBI rules) for personal earnings and learning.
- Parent: if the parent has no business of their own, register a **sole proprietorship**. Get an **Udyam (MSME) registration** (free, at udyamregistration.gov.in). Open a **current account** in the business name. If the business will have premises or staff, file a **Maharashtra Shops & Establishments** intimation (online, for fewer than 10 workers). If the family already has a company or LLP, run the new line inside it as a separate cost centre instead.
- Do **not** incorporate a private limited company yet. Incorporation (SPICe+) costs about ₹10,000–20,000 and annual compliance about ₹30,000–60,000 a year. It is worth it only once revenue passes about ₹15 lakh a year, outside investors appear, or liability risk grows. Reassess at 18.
- Set up tools: Google Workspace (about ₹150–300 per user per month), Zoho Books or a simple spreadsheet ledger, and a domain (about ₹1,000 a year).

### Step 2 (Nov 2026–Jan 2027, 12 weeks): Choose the path and get the first paying customer
Pick the path with the **decision rule** in Part 4. Run it as an experiment:
- Budget: **₹50,000 maximum**.
- Success bar: **at least 1 paying customer at full price, or at least ₹25,000 in revenue, by 31 January 2027.**
- If the bar is missed, run one rewrite: change the offer or the customer type, and give it 6 more weeks. If it is still missed, switch to the next path. Doing that is the plan working as intended.

### Step 3 (Feb–Apr 2027): Repeatability
- Goal: **3 paying customers, ₹1 lakh cumulative revenue**, and a written one-page "how we deliver" checklist.
- Unlocks the **₹2 lakh tranche** (to pay a freelancer and buy better tools, not for branding).
- Write 2 case studies with before-and-after numbers. These are the sales engine and the university-application evidence.

### Step 4 (May–Jul 2027, the summer between DP1 and DP2): Sprint, but protect the Extended Essay
- Up to 15 hours a week, but the Extended Essay first draft and IA work come first. Book those hours in the calendar first.
- Goal: **₹50,000 a month in revenue or recurring retainers** by 31 July 2027.
- Hire a part-time freelancer (₹15,000–25,000 a month) to take over repeat delivery tasks. This is the first step toward running without the student.

### Step 5 (Aug–Dec 2027, first half of DP2 plus university applications): Maintenance mode
- University deadlines: US Early Decision/Early Action around 1 November, UCAS Oxbridge 15 October, other UK courses January. These, IAs and predicted grades take priority.
- Business: 5 hours a week. No new service lines. Raise prices about 20% for new clients. Drop the worst-paying client.
- Goal: hold **₹40,000–80,000 a month in profit** with the freelancer doing most of the delivery.

### Step 6 (Jan–May 2028): Exam freeze
- 3 hours a week: invoicing, one client call and checking the freelancer's work. No sales.
- If the business cannot survive 5 months of this, it is not yet a business. Note that as data, not as a failure.

### Step 7 (Jun–Jul 2028, the student turns 18): Transfer and decide
- The student becomes the legal owner. Either (a) start a new sole proprietorship in their own name with their own GST and current account, migrating clients by sending each one a letter, or (b) incorporate a private limited company with the student as director and majority shareholder. Choose (b) if revenue is above ₹15 lakh a year, or if they are going abroad and need a manager plus a clean ownership structure.
- **If going abroad:** appoint an operator, the existing freelancer or a hired manager, on a fixed fee plus 10–20% profit share. Take immigration-law advice on what the student may do remotely. Keep the student's role to owner-level decisions where the lawyer says that is permissible.
- **Ownership track decision** (Part 6): the parents decide whether to fund a corpus in the student's name.

## Part 4: Which business, with exact numbers

### The decision rule (apply it in order, and take the first "yes")
1. **Does the family own an operating business with a product or service that is not yet sold online or exported?** → **Path A**.
2. **Does the student enjoy and have basic skill in tech, design or marketing, or are they willing to learn no-code tools in 4 weeks?** → **Path B**.
3. Otherwise → **Path C** (cash fallback), and revisit A or B in the summer of 2027.

### Path A: The family-business digital channel (strongest if available)
**What:** the student builds a *new revenue line* for the family business that the business does not have today. Examples: a D2C website or Amazon/Flipkart store for a manufacturer or trader, a B2B export channel (Amazon Global Selling, Alibaba.com, IndiaMART for domestic B2B), or a WhatsApp catalogue and repeat-order system for an existing customer base.

**Why it wins:** product, stock, GST, invoicing and legal capacity already exist (edge cases 1, 14). The student owns one clearly measurable channel.

**Exact setup:**
- A written internal agreement. The student's channel is credited with 100% of its gross margin minus direct costs (ads, fees, packaging, shipping). The student's "share" is 20–30% of channel profit, tracked monthly and held until 18.
- Marketplace economics to model before listing (typical ranges): referral fees of 5–20% by category, closing and fixed fees of ₹5–60 per order, shipping of ₹40–120 per order, returns of 5–15% in fashion (higher in some categories), and payment-gateway fees of about 2% on own-website sales. **Only launch SKUs (individual products) with a contribution margin of at least 30% after all of these.**
- Ad budget: start at ₹500 a day for 14 days on 3–5 hero SKUs. Stop any SKU whose ROAS (return on ad spend) is below 2.5x after ₹7,000 of spend.
- **Targets:** first 50 orders by 31 January 2027. Channel profit of ₹1 lakh a month by July 2027, which means the student's share is about ₹20,000–30,000 a month on top of the profit the channel adds to the family business.

### Path B: Mumbai SME "WhatsApp + Website + AI Automation" studio (the default if there is no family business)
**What:** a productised service for local businesses that live on WhatsApp but have no system. Target clinics (dental, dermatology, physiotherapy), coaching classes, CA and legal practices, real-estate brokers, salons and restaurants in Bandra–Andheri–Powai–Lower Parel–South Mumbai.

**The fixed offer (do not customise endlessly):**
| Package | Includes | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Starter setup | Google Business Profile fixed and optimised, a 1-page website (Framer/Wix/WordPress), WhatsApp Business catalogue and quick replies, review-request flow | ₹25,000 one-time |
| Growth setup | Starter plus WhatsApp Business API via a Meta partner (Interakt, WATI, AiSensy), automated appointment reminders, lead capture form to Google Sheets/CRM, simple AI FAQ bot | ₹60,000 one-time |
| Retainer | Monthly updates, review campaigns, broadcast messages, a 1-page monthly report | ₹12,000–20,000 a month |

The client pays third-party fees directly: the WhatsApp provider plan (about ₹2,500–3,500 a month) and Meta's per-conversation charges. Never carry these on your own card (edge case 13).

**Unit economics (per Growth client):** ₹60,000 setup minus about ₹8,000 in tools and freelancer time leaves about **₹52,000 gross profit**. A retainer of ₹15,000 a month at about 3 hours a month leaves about ₹12,000 a month of profit after a freelancer's share.

**Customer acquisition, exactly:**
1. Parents and their mentor write down **40 business owners** they can introduce. Send a 3-line WhatsApp intro (parent sends it, student follows up within 24 hours).
2. Student's opener: *"I'm building simple WhatsApp and Google systems that bring clinics more booked appointments. I'll audit your Google listing and WhatsApp setup free in 20 minutes and show you 3 fixes, whether or not you hire me."*
3. Expected funnel: 40 intros, about 15 meetings, about 4 audits that turn into proposals, about 2 paying clients. That is roughly a 5% conversion from intro to paying client, which is realistic for warm leads. Repeat with referrals from every happy client.
4. Proposal within 48 hours. 50% advance. Delivery in 14 days.

**Targets:** 2 clients by January 2027, 5 by April 2027, and 6–8 retainers by December 2027. That gives about **₹90,000–1.6 lakh a month in revenue, or roughly ₹50,000–1 lakh a month in profit** after the freelancer, which clears the first "job-replacing" bar by around the end of DP2.

**Skills to learn in weeks 1–4 (about 30 hours, all free):** Google Business Profile help centre, Meta's WhatsApp Business Platform documentation, one no-code website builder, Google Sheets basics plus Zapier or Make, and basic proposal writing. Build the first version free for a family friend's business as a case study, and charge full price from client #2.

### Path C: Cash fallback, concept tutoring for younger students
**What:** maths or science tutoring for Grade 8–10 (IGCSE, ICSE, CBSE or MYP) students, at **₹1,000–2,000 an hour** (typical South and Western Mumbai rates for a strong IB student; experienced professional tutors charge more).
- 5 hours a week at ₹1,500 is about **₹30,000 a month**. Costs are close to zero.
- Rules: parents are the customer, with written consent and minimal data (edge case 8). Never touch assessed work (edge case 7). Check school policy. Group sessions of 3–4 at ₹700 per student per hour raise the hourly rate.
- **Ceiling:** income is capped by the student's hours. It is a fallback and a confidence-builder, not the job-replacing route. Its upgrade path (after 18) is recorded cohort courses or a tutor marketplace.

### Explicitly rejected before 18 (and why)
| Idea | Why not now |
|---|---|
| Café, cloud kitchen, franchise | ₹20–60 lakh capital, FSSAI and other licences, staff management, and the student cannot run it during DP. It becomes the parents' business. |
| Consumer app for teens | DPDP children's-data rules, no distribution and long build time |
| F&O, crypto, forex trading | Loss statistics (edge case 9). Not legal or practical for a minor. |
| Buying a running business | Teaches the seller's playbook, not the student's. Revisit at 20+ with a track record. |
| Dropshipping or "AI agency" courses | The course is the product. |

## Part 5: Guardrails (the rules that stop this going wrong)

### Capital gates
| Tranche | Amount | Unlocked when |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | ₹50,000 | Charter signed |
| 1 | ₹2,00,000 | ₹1 lakh cumulative revenue from at least 3 customers |
| 2 | ₹10,00,000 | ₹1 lakh or more monthly revenue for 3 consecutive months, plus a written 1-page plan reviewed by the outside mentor |
| Beyond | Any amount | Only after 18, with a proper business plan. The family treats it as an investment with terms (equity or a loan agreement), not pocket money. |

Lifetime capital before 18 is **capped at ₹12.5 lakh** (about $14,000, under 0.2% of net worth). If the business needs more than that before 18, the model is wrong.

### IB protection kill switches (checked at each school report or predicted-grade update)
- Any subject's predicted grade drops by 1 point or more → business time is cut to 3 hours a week until the next report.
- Total predicted score falls below the student's university target (for example, below 38) → the business is paused, with only invoicing kept up.
- Extended Essay or IA deadline missed → 2-week pause.

### Business kill switches
- No paying customer 90 days after starting a path → switch path.
- Revenue falls for 3 consecutive months → quarterly review, then fix one thing or wind down.
- Any client more than 30 days overdue → stop work for that client. A second time → drop them.

### Weekly rhythm (8 hours)
- **Saturday 10:00–13:00:** delivery and building.
- **Sunday 16:00–18:00:** sales follow-ups, proposals and invoicing.
- **Wednesday 19:00–20:00:** client calls.
- **Friday 19:00–20:00:** numbers: the revenue, profit, pipeline and hours sheet, sent to the parents every Friday.
- **No business work during school hours or the night before an assessment.**

### The one-page monthly dashboard (send to parents and the mentor)
Revenue, direct costs, profit, cash collected vs invoiced, number of clients, hours spent, one lesson, and next month's single priority.

## Part 6: What "job-replacing money" really looks like for this family

| Route | When | Realistic monthly net | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Path C tutoring | DP1 onward | ₹20,000–40,000 | Capped by hours |
| Path B studio | End of DP2 | ₹50,000–1 lakh | With one freelancer |
| Path A channel | End of DP2 | ₹20,000–30,000 student share (more profit added to the family business) | Depends on the product's margin |
| Path B scaled, after 18 | Ages 19–21 | ₹1.5–3 lakh | 15–25 retainers, 2–3 staff, private limited company |
| **Ownership track (family decision)** | At 18 | About ₹80,000–90,000 | A ₹1.5 crore gift from a parent to the adult child, invested in debt funds or bonds or FDs at about 7%, gives about ₹10.5 lakh a year. Under the FY2025-26 new-regime rules, normal income up to ₹12 lakh carries effectively nil tax because of the s.87A rebate. That rebate does not apply to special-rate income such as listed capital gains. The clubbing rule for a minor child stops at 18. Confirm under the 2025 Act with a CA. |

**The honest conclusion:** if "job-replacing money" only means a monthly income, the family can create it at 18 with a single gift and a CA's advice. What money cannot buy is a 20-year-old who has sold to 30 customers, fired a bad one, hired a freelancer and kept a business alive through IB exams. This plan is built to produce that person, with real income as the scoreboard.
