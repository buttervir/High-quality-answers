# A 16-Year-Old's Business Plan in Mumbai: From IB DP1 to Job-Replacing Income

## Assumptions

- **Today is late October 2026.** The student started DP1 in mid-2026 and sits final IB exams in **May 2028**. They turn 18 around mid-2028. If the birthday or exam session is different, move the dates; the steps stay the same.
- **"Job-replacing money" = ₹1,00,000 per month of profit the student takes home** (about ₹12 lakh a year, roughly the pay of a good graduate first job in Mumbai). The stretch target is ₹3,00,000 per month. A business only counts once it hits that number for 6 months in a row.
- **The money has to be earned, not handed over.** A family worth about $8M (around ₹70 crore) could simply give the child an income. That isn't what was asked. In this plan family money is cheap, capped, written-down capital. It is never a salary.
- **The IB Diploma comes first.** A business that costs the student a strong diploma and a good university place is a bad trade at 16. The plan is designed to give up some business growth before it gives up grades.
- Indian law and tax points below were correct to the best of my knowledge in October 2026. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, and some section numbers changed. **Before acting, have a Chartered Accountant (CA) confirm every legal and tax step.** Budget ₹15,000–₹30,000 a year for one.

## 1. Why most "rich kid starts a business" plans fail

Here are the attacks a harsh expert would make on any plan for this student. Each part of the plan below exists to deal with one of them.

| # | Attack | How this plan answers it |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | "A 16-year-old can't legally sign contracts in India. Minors' agreements are void (Indian Contract Act s.11). They can't be a company director or a full partner either." | Phase 1 uses prepaid, small-ticket work where no contract needs enforcing. Anything bigger goes through a parent-held vehicle. The student incorporates and takes control at 18 (Section 4). |
| A2 | "Most income a minor earns is taxed as the parent's income, at 30%+." | Under the minor-clubbing rule, a minor's income is added to the parent's. The exception is income from the minor's **own skill, talent or specialised knowledge**. So Phase 1 is deliberately a skill-based service. Income that comes from family capital (for example inventory businesses) would likely be clubbed (Section 5). |
| A3 | "With ₹70 crore behind them, they'll open a café or buy a franchise, burn ₹50 lakh and learn nothing." | Capital is released in stages: ₹1.5 lakh, then ₹5 lakh, then ₹25 lakh. Each stage needs a revenue gate to be met first. No retail lease, franchise or inventory before 18 (Section 6). |
| A4 | "Two years of IB is the worst time to start anything. The EE, IAs, TOK, CAS and university applications will eat the business, or the business will eat the grades." | There is a fixed hours budget, a calendar with exam freezes built in, and a grades tripwire that forces the business into maintenance mode (Section 7). |
| A5 | "Upwork, PayPal, Stripe, Wise and Payoneer all require users to be 18 or older." | Phase 1 is sold locally and paid by UPI or bank transfer into the student's own account. Global platforms come only at 18. Never use a parent's account to get around an age rule (Section 4). |
| A6 | "Whatever they build in Mumbai collapses when they leave for university in August 2028. On a US F-1 or UK Student visa they generally can't actively work for their own business." | Every track must be able to run remotely and be delegated. By June 2028 there's a hired operator and written SOPs, and the student moves into an owner-only role. Get immigration advice before departure (Section 8). |
| A7 | "'Here are 10 business ideas' isn't a plan." | There are three fully specified tracks with offers, prices, channels and unit economics, and a 14-day scoring test that picks one. After that the student runs **one** business (Section 2). |
| A8 | "Nice ideas, but no numbers or kill criteria." | Five dated gates with numeric pass/fail criteria, plus a pivot rule (Section 3). |

## 2. Choosing the business: three tracks and one 14-day test

### Why the business is a skill-based service, not a product or app

| Criterion | Skill-based service | Product (D2C/e-commerce) | App/SaaS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to first ₹ | 2–4 weeks | 3–6 months | 6–18 months |
| Capital needed | under ₹50,000 | ₹3–15 lakh (inventory, ads) | ₹2–20 lakh, or deep coding skill |
| Works for a minor legally | Yes (prepaid, low value) | Hard (GST and marketplace onboarding, supplier contracts) | Hard (payment gateways, app stores, contracts) |
| Tax in the student's hands | Likely yes (skill income) | Likely clubbed to parent | Mixed |
| Fits about 7 hrs/week | Yes | No (ops-heavy) | Rarely |
| Can become a product later | Yes. Repeated service work shows what to productise. | n/a | n/a |

The path is: **Service (now) → productised service (2027) → product or company (18+)**. This order holds up because each stage pays for and informs the next.

### The three tracks

Pick the track where the student **already has visible skill** (or can build it in 3 weeks) and **knows at least 10 possible buyers within two introductions**. Use family, school parents and the family's business contacts.

**Track 1: Digital presence and automation for Mumbai small businesses**
- **Customers:** clinics, CA and law firms, coaching classes, restaurants, distributors and dealers. These are the people in the family's network who still run on WhatsApp and phone calls.
- **Offer (exact):** "Get found and get replies." Set up a Google Business Profile, WhatsApp Business catalogue and quick replies, a simple one-page website, a review-collection QR code, and an AI auto-reply or lead sheet using no-code tools.
- **Price:** ₹20,000–₹35,000 one-time setup, plus a ₹5,000–₹8,000 monthly care plan (posts, review replies, monthly report).
- **Tools:** Google Business Profile, WhatsApp Business, Carrd/Framer/WordPress, Google Sheets plus a no-code automation tool, Canva. Under ₹3,000/month in total.
- **Route to ₹1 lakh/month:** 10 care clients × ₹7,000 = ₹70,000 recurring, plus 1–2 setups a month at about ₹25,000. Gross margin 80%+ once a junior freelancer handles care work.
- **Remote-proof:** Yes. Everything is done online.

**Track 2: Short-form video for creators, doctors and brands**
- **Customers:** Mumbai doctors, dentists, real-estate agents, fitness coaches, restaurants and small brands that need Reels and Shorts.
- **Offer:** 12 edited short videos a month from the client's raw footage, plus captions, hooks and a posting calendar.
- **Price:** ₹15,000/month (8 videos), ₹25,000 (12), ₹40,000 (20 + strategy call).
- **Tools:** CapCut/DaVinci Resolve (free), a shared Drive, a Notion or Sheet tracker.
- **Route to ₹1 lakh/month:** 5 clients at ₹25,000 = ₹1.25 lakh revenue. Paying 2 freelance editors ₹3,000–₹5,000 per client per month leaves about ₹1 lakh.
- **Remote-proof:** Yes.

**Track 3: Academic services (tutoring → course product)**
- **Customers:** MYP, IGCSE and ICSE students (grades 8–10) in the student's own school network, in subjects where the student scored at the top.
- **Offer:** small-group tutoring (4 students) plus exam-technique workshops. Productise it later into a recorded course or question bank.
- **Price:** ₹1,500/student per 90-minute group session, so a 4-student group earns ₹6,000 a session.
- **Route to ₹1 lakh/month:** 4 groups × 4 sessions × ₹6,000 = ₹96,000. **This needs 24 hours a month of the student's own time, which is the main weakness.** It only scales by hiring other top students as tutors.
- **Caution:** never offer help with graded IB work (IAs, EEs) for other students. That is academic malpractice and could cost the student their own diploma.
- **Remote-proof:** Partly (online sessions), but income depends on the student's own hours.

**Not before 18:** D2C brands with inventory, cafés and franchises, crypto or trading "businesses", anything that needs a lease, a loan, or hiring full-time adult staff. These need contracts a minor can't sign, need capital whose returns are clubbed to the parent, and need time the IB doesn't leave.

### The 14-day selection test (do this first)

1. **Day 1–3:** For each of the three tracks, write down 10 named possible buyers (real names and phone numbers from family, school parents and relatives' businesses). A track with fewer than 10 names is out.
2. **Day 4–10:** Have 5 conversations per remaining track (15 minutes each, phone or in person). Use the same script every time:
   - "What do you do today for [problem]?"
   - "What does it cost you?"
   - "If I did X for ₹Y, would you pay this month?"

   Record each answer as Yes, Maybe or No.
3. **Day 11–14:** Score each track:

| Criterion | Weight | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|---|
| Number of "Yes, I'd pay this month" | ×3 | |
| Student's current skill / portfolio | ×2 | |
| Fits within 7 hrs/week during term | ×2 | |
| Can run after the student leaves Mumbai | ×2 | |
| Student actually enjoys it | ×1 | |

Highest total wins. **Commit to that track until Gate 2 (31 March 2027).** Don't switch because of boredom. Only switch on a gate failure.

## 3. Timeline with gates (October 2026 → August 2028)

### Hours budget (this rule doesn't bend)

| Period | Business hours/week |
|---|---|
| School term | 6–8 (e.g. 2 weekday evenings × 1.5h + Saturday 4h) |
| School holidays | 20–25 |
| Internal exam weeks, IA/EE deadline weeks | 0–2 (maintenance only) |
| DP2 mocks plus final exams (≈ Feb–May 2028) | 0–2 (business runs on SOPs and contractors) |

### Phase 0: Set-up and test (20 Oct – 30 Nov 2026)

**Week 1 (20–26 Oct)**
- Parents and student sign the **one-page family agreement** (template in Section 6).
- Apply for a **PAN** for the student: Form 49A through Protean (NSDL) or UTIITSL, with a parent signing as representative. Costs about ₹107.
- Open or confirm the student's **own savings account** with UPI. Under RBI's April 2025 directions, banks may let minors over 10 open and operate savings accounts on their own, subject to the bank's policy. All business money goes here and nowhere else.
- Make a Google Sheet called `Business OS` with the tabs listed in Section 9.

**Week 2–3 (27 Oct – 9 Nov):** Run the 14-day selection test (Section 2).

**Week 4 (10–16 Nov)**
- Build the offer page: one page on Carrd or Google Sites with the offer, price, 3 example pieces of work and a WhatsApp button.
- Get a domain (about ₹1,000/year) and a professional email address.
- Set up free invoicing (Zoho Invoice or a Sheet template).

**Week 5–6 (17–30 Nov)**
- Sign the first **3 paid pilot customers** at a 30–50% "founding client" discount, always **paid in advance**.
- Deliver within 7 days.
- Get a written testimonial and permission to show the work.

**GATE 0 (30 Nov 2026):** At least 3 customers have paid at least ₹5,000 each.
- **Fail:** move to the second-highest track from the selection test and repeat weeks 4–6 in December.

### Phase 1: Find what repeats (Dec 2026 – Mar 2027)

- **Winter break (Dec):** a 20 hr/week sprint. Contact all 30 names from the selection test. Aim for 8 customers in total.
- **Jan–Mar:** put every customer on a monthly plan where the track allows it.
  - Write an SOP (a checklist in Google Docs) for every task done twice.
  - Raise prices 20% for every new customer after the 5th.
- **Referral rule:** each happy customer is asked for 2 introductions. Offer a free month for any referral that converts.

**GATE 1 (31 Mar 2027):** All three must hold:
1. ₹40,000/month in recurring revenue, or ₹1.5 lakh cumulative revenue.
2. At least 2 customers have paid in 2 or more separate months.
3. The student averaged no more than 8 hrs/week.

- **Pass:** release Capital Tranche 2.
- **Fail on revenue:** one more 8-week cycle on the same track with a new offer or price. If it fails again, switch track.
- **Fail on hours:** price is too low or there are no SOPs. Fix that before taking any new customer.

### Phase 2: Delegate (Apr – Aug 2027)

- **Apr–May (DP1 end-of-year exams):** maintenance only. Existing customers are served and no new sales are made.
- **June, early summer break:** hire the **first contractor**. That's a college student or freelancer paid per task, for example ₹3,000–₹5,000 per video client per month, or ₹1,500 per care client per month. Train them using the SOPs. A parent signs the engagement letter as guardian until the student is 18.
- **Summer:** a 25 hr/week sales sprint, plus the **Extended Essay draft** (protect at least 15 hrs/week for it).

**GATE 2 (31 Aug 2027):**
1. ₹1,00,000/month revenue.
2. The contractor delivers at least 40% of the work.
3. The student's own delivery time is no more than 5 hrs/week.
4. Customer churn is under 15% a month.

- **Pass:** release Tranche 3 (only if there's a specific use with an expected payback, such as a second contractor or a productised offering).

### Phase 3: Autopilot through DP2 (Sep 2027 – May 2028)

- **Sep–Jan:** university applications, IAs and the TOK essay. The student works 4–6 hrs/week on sales calls and quality checks only.
- **Nov–Dec:** promote the best contractor to **operations lead** with a profit share (for example 10% of gross profit). Customers now talk to them day to day.
- **Feb–May 2028:** exam freeze. The business runs without the student. A parent or mentor gets a weekly 15-minute dashboard check.
- **Productise during this period:** turn the most-repeated service into a fixed package with a fixed price, for example "Clinic Growth Kit ₹30,000 + ₹7,000/month". Or turn it into a digital product: templates, a course, or a done-for-you package sold online.

**GATE 3 (31 May 2028):** The business made at least ₹1 lakh/month during exam months with the student working at most 2 hrs/week.
- **Pass:** this is a real business, not a job in disguise.
- **Fail:** the student owns a job, not a business. That's still valuable, but it needs restructuring in summer 2028.

### Phase 4: Turn 18 and formalise (June – August 2028)

Details in Section 4. Incorporate, sign contracts in the student's own name, and open global payment channels.

**GATE 4 (Aug 2028, "job-replaced"):** At least ₹1,00,000/month of **profit to the owner** for 6 consecutive months.

## 4. Legal structure: right for now and still right in 2 years

### Until 18 (now → mid-2028): no company. Keep it simple.

- **Who earns:** the student, personally, as an individual providing a skilled service. Use the student's PAN, invoices in the student's name, and payment into the student's savings account.
- **The minor-contract problem:** take **payment in advance** and keep ticket sizes small (under ₹50,000). If nothing is owed by either side, nothing needs enforcing. Use a one-page "scope of work" on WhatsApp or email so expectations are clear, not to create a lawsuit.
- **When a customer insists on a formal contract or a GST invoice:** route that customer through an **adult vehicle**, in this order of preference:
  1. A parent's existing proprietorship, LLP or company invoices the customer.
  2. A small sub-contract passes the work to the student.

  Write it down in the family agreement so the economics clearly belong to the student. Ask the CA how the parent's entity should treat this for tax.
- **GST:** generally not needed while total service turnover stays below **₹20 lakh a year** in Maharashtra. Track turnover monthly in the `Business OS` sheet. At ₹15 lakh, start registration preparation with the CA. GST also becomes needed earlier if you sell goods through marketplaces or across state lines, which is another reason to avoid products before 18.
- **Shops & Establishments (Maharashtra 2017 Act):** an online service run from home with no employees generally doesn't need it. Check with the CA once contractors become staff or you take an office.
- **Global platforms:** Upwork, Fiverr, PayPal, Stripe, Wise, Payoneer, YouTube AdSense and similar generally require users to be 18 (or a parent-held account). **Don't open accounts in a parent's name to trade as the student.** It breaks the terms of service, and accounts get frozen with money inside. Wait until 18.
- **No foreign company.** Don't set up a Delaware or Singapore entity "because startups do". For an Indian resident it triggers FEMA/Overseas Direct Investment compliance and cost, and gives nothing at this revenue level.
- **Data:** if the business handles customers' customer data (Track 1) or children's data (Track 3: tutoring minors needs a parent's consent), follow the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules. Collect the minimum, get written consent, and don't keep data you don't need.

### At 18 (June–Aug 2028): formalise once, properly

| Step | Detail | Approx. cost |
|---|---|---|
| Update PAN | Update the minor PAN to an adult PAN (photo and signature) | ~₹107 |
| Choose entity | **Private Limited Company** if you'll raise money, hire, or hold IP. **Sole proprietorship** if it stays a 1–3 person service firm under about ₹50 lakh revenue. **LLP** is in between. Decide with the CA. | Pvt Ltd: ₹10,000–₹25,000 to incorporate, ₹40,000–₹80,000/yr compliance |
| Directors | Student + one parent (a Pvt Ltd needs at least 2) | — |
| Shareholding | Student holds most of the shares. Any family money put in during Tranches 1–3 converts to a stated small stake, or is repaid as a loan, as agreed in the family agreement. | — |
| Bank | Current account in the company's or proprietorship's name | — |
| Payments | Razorpay/Cashfree for India. Stripe India / PayPal / Wise for export-of-service clients. Export of services can be zero-rated for GST under a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) once registered. | Gateway fee ~2% |
| Contracts | Move all customers and contractors onto signed agreements with the new entity | — |
| Trademark | File the brand name (class 35/41/42 depending on track) | ~₹4,500 per class (individual/small-entity e-filing fee) |
| Udyam (MSME) | Free registration. Helps with credit and payment protection later. | Free |

## 5. Tax: keep it clean from day one

- **Skill-based income is the student's.** The minor-clubbing rule (formerly s.64(1A), carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025) adds a minor's income to the parent's **except** income from the minor's own manual work or skill, talent or specialised knowledge. All three tracks are skill-based. Keep evidence: the student's portfolio, time logs and the fact that the student does the work.
- **What that means in rupees:** under the new tax regime (default), a resident individual pays no tax up to ₹4 lakh. With the rebate, there's effectively no tax on total income up to ₹12 lakh. So the student could earn close to the job-replacing target in Phase 1–2 with little or no tax, **if the CA confirms the income qualifies.**
- **Where clubbing likely applies:** returns from money the family gave or lent (bank interest, investments, profit that is really from capital, such as inventory resale). Keep family capital and its returns clearly separate in the sheet.
- **File an Income Tax Return every year** for the student, even when no tax is due: FY 2026-27 is due by 31 July 2027. A filing history matters later for visas, loans and investors.
- **Paying contractors:** pay by bank transfer only, with an invoice from them for every payment. Ask the CA when TDS (tax deducted at source) applies.

## 6. Family money: the capital ladder and the agreement

The family's wealth is the student's biggest advantage and also the biggest trap. Money can buy things, but it can't stand in for customers. The rule: **revenue unlocks capital; capital never comes first.**

| Tranche | Amount | Released when | Allowed uses | Not allowed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ₹1,50,000 | Agreement signed (Week 1) | Laptop/software, domain, CA fees, small ads tests (max ₹20,000), courses (max ₹15,000) | Office, inventory, paid "mentors", agencies |
| 2 | ₹5,00,000 | Gate 1 passed | Contractors' first 3 months, better equipment, a product prototype | Salaries to the student, lifestyle spending |
| 3 | up to ₹25,00,000 | Gate 2 passed **and** a written one-page case with expected payback under 12 months | Second contractor/ops lead, productised offer, marketing | Anything without a payback number |

**Lifetime cap before 18: ₹31.5 lakh, under 0.5% of family net worth.** Losing all of it doesn't hurt the family. Not having the cap is what causes trouble.

### The one-page family agreement (copy and fill in)

> 1. **Purpose:** [Student] will build a business to earn ₹1 lakh/month profit by Aug 2028 without compromising the IB Diploma.
> 2. **Capital:** Family provides capital only as per the tranche table. Each tranche is a [gift / interest-free loan repayable from profits / converts to __% equity at incorporation] (choose one per tranche).
> 3. **Ownership:** All customers, brand, work and profits belong to [Student]. A parent signs documents only as guardian, not as owner.
> 4. **Academics tripwire:** If predicted grades total drop by 3+ points from the October 2026 baseline, or any IB deadline is missed, the business goes into maintenance mode (no new customers) until the next report.
> 5. **Monthly review:** Last Sunday of each month, 45 minutes: student presents the dashboard to one parent + one outside mentor.
> 6. **Parent's role:** guardian signatory, introductions, advice when asked. Not the operator.
> 7. **Review:** this agreement is rewritten at age 18.
> Signed: ______ (Student) ______ (Parent) Date: ______

**The outside mentor:** pick one adult who has built a services business, not a family member. Ask for 45 minutes a month for 18 months, in return for a thank-you and updates. Good places to look are the family's business circle, school alumni, or a founder from the Mumbai startup community (TiE Mumbai events, startup meetups).

## 7. Protecting the IB Diploma

- **Fixed weekly schedule during term:** the business gets 2 weekday evenings (for example Tue/Thu 7:30–9:00 pm) plus Saturday morning. Sunday is for study and the weekly review only.
- **Known crunch points to block out in advance:**
  - DP1 internal exams (≈ Apr–May 2027)
  - EE draft (summer 2027)
  - IA deadlines (DP2 term 1)
  - University applications (Oct 2027 for US Early Decision / Oxbridge / medicine; Jan 2028 for UCAS and US Regular Decision)
  - Mocks (≈ Jan–Feb 2028)
  - Finals (May 2028)
- **CAS:** paid work generally does **not** count as CAS. If the student wants the business to support CAS, add an **unpaid service project**, such as free digital set-up for an NGO or free workshops for a municipal school, and log it properly with the CAS coordinator.
- **Academic links (legitimate ones):** if the student takes Business Management or Economics, the business gives real material for class discussion. Using it for an IA or EE needs the teacher's approval and full disclosure. Ask early.
- **University applications:** a business with real revenue, contractors and SOPs is an excellent activity to write about. One that hurt grades is not. Gate 3 ("runs without me during exams") is the story admissions officers find convincing.

## 8. Planning for August 2028 (the long-term part)

There are three likely paths after IB. Decide by **January 2028**, and set up the business for whichever one you choose.

| Path | What happens to the business | Action by June 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| **University abroad (US/UK/etc.)** | Student visas generally restrict or forbid working for your own business while studying (US F-1: no off-campus work; UK Student visa: no self-employment). Owning shares and receiving dividends is treated differently from working. | Ops lead runs day-to-day under a written agreement. Student is shareholder only. **Get advice from an immigration lawyer for the destination country before departure.** |
| **University in India** | The student can keep working on it. | Keep the ops lead, and grow during college with 10–15 hrs/week. |
| **Gap year (full-time business)** | A 12-month full-time push toward the ₹3 lakh/month stretch target. | Set the gap-year target and a deadline for the university re-application decision in writing with the family. |

Whatever the path: **SOPs, the `Business OS` sheet, all customer contacts and all logins live in shared company accounts, never on one person's phone.** That is what lets the business survive the student moving away.

## 9. The operating system (so it runs without heroics)

**One Google Sheet, `Business OS`, with these tabs:**

1. `Ledger`: date, customer/vendor, amount in/out, category, invoice #, family-capital flag (Y/N).
2. `Customers`: name, contact, plan, start date, monthly value, last delivered, renewal date, status.
3. `Pipeline`: lead, source, stage (Contacted → Call → Proposal → Paid), next action, date.
4. `Dashboard`: monthly revenue, recurring revenue, profit, cumulative turnover (GST watch), hours worked, # customers, churn, gate status.
5. `Hours`: weekly business hours vs. the budget, plus study hours.
6. `Capital`: tranche, date, amount, use, balance.

**Google Drive folders:**

`/01 Legal & Tax`, `/02 Customers/<name>`, `/03 SOPs`, `/04 Contractors`, `/05 Marketing`, `/06 Monthly Reviews`

**Weekly review (Sunday, 30 minutes):**
1. Update the Dashboard.
2. Check hours against the budget.
3. Choose the top 3 actions for the week.
4. Note one SOP to write or improve.

**Monthly review (45 minutes, with parent + mentor):**
1. Dashboard.
2. Gate progress.
3. Decisions needed.
4. Capital requests (only with a written payback case).

**SOP standard:** every repeated task has a checklist that a new contractor could follow on day one without asking the student anything. If they need to ask, the SOP gets fixed, not the person.

## 11. What success looks like and what failure costs

- **Realistic outcome if the gates are followed:** a ₹50,000–₹1,50,000/month service business by summer 2028, run partly by others, plus a documented track record, a CA-filed tax history, and an incorporated company ready at 18.
- **Stretch outcome:** ₹3 lakh/month and a productised offering that can grow during university.
- **Worst case:** all gates fail. The family loses under ₹6.5 lakh (Tranches 1–2), and the student keeps a strong diploma, real sales experience and a documented attempt. Compared with no attempt, or with a ₹50-lakh café, that's a good outcome.

**The single rule to remember:** *customers before capital, gates before growth, grades before both.*
