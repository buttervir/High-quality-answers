ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Tutors studying abroad trigger reverse-charge GST and compulsory registration from day one, which breaks the whole pre-GST margin path
Where: Phase 0, Weeks 5–6: "recent IB graduates with awarded scores of 38–40+, now at university in India or abroad (online sessions)"; Section 3.2: "Do not register voluntarily at incorporation", the "₹1,000" and "₹900 (pre-GST)" margins, and "the first ₹1 lakh month is month 13 (November 2027), while the company is still below the GST threshold".
Problem: A tutor who lives abroad and is paid by the agency is a supplier outside India, so the agency is importing a service for its business. Import of services by a business is taxed under reverse charge (IGST Notification 10/2017, entry 1). The exemption for unregistered recipients (Notification 9/2017-IGST, entry 10) covers only non-business purposes. Under CGST section 24(iii), anyone liable to pay tax on reverse charge must register whatever their turnover. Once registered, output GST applies to every package. So the first time the agency pays one tutor who is studying in the UK or US, which the plan tells it to recruit, the ₹20 lakh threshold logic, the ₹1,000/₹900 pre-GST margins and the "never register before January 2028" advice all stop applying. On the plan's own GST-registered margin (₹750), November 2027 gives 171 × ₹750 − ₹47,000 = ₹81,250, not ₹1,07,000, and the "first ₹1L month" disappears. Payments to non-resident tutors also need withholding under the non-resident TDS provision, with no threshold, and Form 15CA/CB. The plan's only GST "Trap" is a parent's existing registration, and none of its CA questions raises this.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] By the plan's own numbers, the founder-pay switch (condition 3) fails in the post-exam summer, so the headline job-replacing money does not start
Where: Phase 4, condition 3: "the trailing 12-month average pre-tax profit (before founder pay) is at least ₹1 lakh ... The scoreboard puts it at about ₹1.1 lakh for September 2027 – August 2028"; Section 6: "job-replacing money arrives ... in the post-exam summer whatever happens next".
Problem: Section 9 says scoreboard profit "excludes ... bootcamps, which are shown separately", and Section 3.2 says bootcamps are "extra, not part of the target". Here is the 12-month window that ends in June 2028, when the post-exam summer pay is meant to switch on, using the scoreboard's own monthly profit without bootcamps: Jul 2027 ≈ ₹60k, Aug ₹70k, Sep ≈ ₹76k, Oct ₹93k, Nov ₹107k, Dec ≈ ₹121k, Jan ₹105k, Feb ≈ ₹111k, Mar ≈ ₹114k, Apr ₹117k, May ≈ ₹85k, Jun ₹52k. That sums to about ₹11.1 lakh, an average of about ₹92,500, which is below ₹1 lakh. Even the plan's own Sep 2027–Aug 2028 window comes out at about ₹90k without bootcamps. The "₹1.1 lakh" figure only works if bootcamp profit is counted, which the plan says it is not. The window also has another problem: July–August 2027 was the parent's proprietorship's profit, already taxed in the parent's hands, and the company only exists from 1 September 2027. So in June 2028 the company has 10 months of history, and a "company trailing 12-month average" cannot be calculated. The user cannot tell whether pay starts, and on the plan's stated definitions it does not.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The GST check on the parent's PAN tests only for an existing registration, but aggregate turnover counts all the parent's supplies, including exempt ones
Where: Section 3.2: "Parent's proprietorship (parent's PAN): ... FY 2027–28 ... about ₹13–16 lakh ... Both stay under ₹20 lakh, so the proprietorship never registers", and "Trap: if the parent ... already has a GST registration ... GST applies from the first rupee". Phase 0, Week 1: "Check whether that parent already holds a GST registration".
Problem: Aggregate turnover is all taxable and exempt supplies under the PAN, across India, not just this business. A parent in an $8M family who is not GST-registered can easily have such supplies. Residential rent is an exempt supply that still counts. Commercial rent, consulting fees or directors' sitting fees can also count. Counterexample: the parent receives ₹8 lakh a year of residential rent. FY 2027–28 aggregate turnover is then ₹13–16 lakh + ₹8 lakh = ₹21–24 lakh, so registration is mandatory part-way through Phase 2. The plan's "proprietorship never registers" claim and its ₹900 pre-GST margin for May–August 2027 are then wrong. The Week 1 check should be "total supplies under this PAN", not "existing registration", and neither the check nor the CA questions say so.

ATTACK 4 [MINOR] The exam-year drop arithmetic does not add up
Where: Section 3.2: "of the 56 in April 2028: about 12 Grade 10 and 10 DP2 ... one in three re-signing ... (4) ... So June–July 2028 runs on about 34 student-subjects: 132 billed hours × ₹750 = ₹99,000 − ₹47,000 = ≈ ₹52,000".
Problem: 56 − 12 − 10 + 4 = 38, not 34. At 38 student-subjects the month is 148 hours × ₹750 − ₹47,000 ≈ ₹64,000. The ₹52k figure is carried into the scoreboard (M20), the risk table and the pay-rule reasoning, so the user is working from a number that does not follow from the stated inputs.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] "₹6.5 lakh retained by January 2028" is the March 2028 figure
Where: Section 10, "By January 2028 (month 15): ... About ₹6.5 lakh of after-tax profit is retained in the company". Phase 4, condition 4: "Profit retained between September 2027 and March 2028 (about ₹8.7 lakh pre-tax, about ₹6.5 lakh after 25.2% company tax)".
Problem: The success test for January 2028 uses the seven-month September–March total. The September–January scoreboard profit is about ₹76k + ₹93k + ₹107k + ₹121k + ₹105k ≈ ₹5.0 lakh, plus the December and January bootcamps (≈ ₹0.86 lakh), giving about ₹5.9 lakh pre-tax, or about ₹4.4 lakh after tax. Against the plan's own forecast, a user checking in January 2028 would wrongly conclude they are about ₹2 lakh behind.
