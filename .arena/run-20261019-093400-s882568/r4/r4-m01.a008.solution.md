# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old DP1 Student in Mumbai

## 1. The answer in one paragraph

Start an **IB-specialist tutoring and exam-bootcamp business for Mumbai's international-school students** (IGCSE / MYP / early DP). Begin by tutoring yourself. Then grow it into a small agency of vetted tutors (IB graduates aged **18 and over, with the right to work**), with paid group bootcamps held only in exam seasons. Until you are 18, run it legally through a **sole proprietorship in one parent's name**, because a minor cannot sign enforceable contracts in India. Your own tutoring income goes into **your own PAN and bank account**. Cap family capital at **exactly ₹5 lakh (about US$5,700, under 0.1% of net worth) until you turn 18**, and release it in tranches only when the business hits written targets.

There are three honest numbers, and the plan never mixes them up:

1. **Business profit ≥ ₹1 lakh/month (Business Level 1)**, which is what the business earns before income tax. In the base case, the first full year after GST registration (**Aug 2027–Jul 2028**) averages about **₹1.05 lakh/month**, after 18% GST, a paid coordinator, a **5% allowance for tutors taking families direct**, tutor retention pay, TDS and company compliance, and two months of a General Manager. The first month at or above ₹1 lakh is **Dec 2027**; ordinary term months without a bootcamp run at about ₹85k–1 lakh, and bootcamp and summer months carry the average. The margin is thin: about **9% fewer tutor-hours than the model, or the lower price tier, puts you below it** (Section 8.4). The conservative case earns about **₹27k/month**. Two gates tell you which case you are in: **Gate 2 (31 May 2027)** and **Gate 3 (31 Oct 2027)**.
2. **Your own money before 18:** about **₹18k → ₹28k → ₹43k → ₹19k → ₹5k per month** as school pressure rises, ≈ ₹3.9 lakh in total (Section 8.5). Until the business moves into the company, its profit is **your parent's** money.
3. **Your own money after the business moves into a company you own 75% of** (Sections 8.7–8.8), after 25.17% company tax:
   - **Personal Level 1 (₹1 lakh/month reaching you, after your own income tax) is reached in the base case if you stay in India after school** (a gap year or a Mumbai/Indian college) as the company's paid executive director-GM **and** tutor 4 hours a week for the company: **≈ ₹1.02 lakh/month net in the first company year and ≈ ₹1.08 lakh/month from the second**, at base-case volume, with no growth assumed (Section 8.8). At 6 tutoring hours a week it is ≈ ₹1.09–1.19 lakh.
   - **If you leave to study abroad and stay passive:** about **₹22k/month** in the first company year and **₹35k/month** after that. Passive ownership alone reaches ₹1 lakh/month only at about **2.5× base-case volume** (≈550 tutor-hours/month), a post-2030 goal that the plan does not promise.

Your IB grades act as a hard brake on all of this.

## 2. Assumptions (where the request is ambiguous)

1. **"Job replacing money"** is measured in two ways. Both are averaged over 12 months, because tutoring is seasonal.
   - **Business Level 1: ₹1,00,000/month of business profit** (₹12 lakh/year): after GST, costs and staff, and before income tax. It is the proprietorship's (later the company's) profit before your founder fee, plus your own tutoring. It is always measured **as if GST-registered**, because registration is certain at base-case volume. This tells you whether *the business* could pay a ₹12 lakh/year salary.
   - **Personal Level 1: ₹1,00,000/month reaching you**, after company tax, from your own tutoring, your fees or salary and your 75% of dividends. This is the stricter test. A ₹12 lakh salary is roughly tax-free to a resident under the new regime (the s.87A rebate), so this is the like-for-like comparison with a job.
   - **Level 2: ₹3,00,000/month of business profit** (₹36 lakh/year), closer to a well-paid Mumbai professional.
2. You started DP1 in mid-2026. IB final exams are in **May 2028**, results arrive in early July 2028, and university applications run from mid-2027 to early 2028.
3. You turn 18 at some point **between late 2027 and late 2028**. The plan works for **any** birthday in that range (Phase 5). Where a step depends on being 18 (directorship, unclubbed dividends), it starts on the **later of its planned date and the first day of the month after your birthday**, and Phase 5 step 6 gives the fallback for the months in between.
4. Your family supports the plan, and a parent acts as the legal operator. You are **not** being asked to manage family wealth.
5. Exchange rate: **₹88 ≈ US$1**. All Mumbai prices are **estimates that you verify in Phase 1** against at least 10 real parent quotes.
6. This is not legal or tax advice. Every legal or tax step is marked for confirmation by a Chartered Accountant (CA) or Company Secretary (CS). Budget about ₹30–40k/year once GST is registered.

## 5. The business, exactly

**Working name:** something neutral, such as "[YourName] Academic Studio" or "Bridge to IB". **Do not** use your school's name or the IB logo, because "International Baccalaureate" and "IB" are IBO trademarks. Describe the service in words ("tutoring for IB Diploma students") without implying endorsement.

**Customers:** parents of students at Mumbai IB, IGCSE and international schools.
1. **Grade 9–10 (IGCSE/MYP):** subject tutoring and "DP readiness".
2. **DP1 students a year behind you**, and DP2 students only in subjects where you are clearly ahead. Otherwise use an alumni tutor.
3. **Bootcamp windows (the only ones):** the **mock window, Dec–Feb** (DP2 and IGCSE mocks), and the **final window, Mar–Apr** (before the May IB, IGCSE and MYP exams). Run **at most 4 cohorts in a school year**: 3 in 2027 (Feb, Mar, Apr) and 4 in 2027-28 (Dec, Jan, Mar, Apr). DP Readiness runs in **June–July only**.

**Products and prices.** All proprietorship prices are **GST-inclusive** from launch. Before registration the proprietorship keeps the whole price. After registration, 18/118 of it goes to GST, and customers see no change.

| Product | Delivered by | Price to parent (incl. GST) | Net of 18% GST | Paid to tutor | Margin after registration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1 tutoring, you personally (subjects where your school grade is 6–7) | You, on **your own PAN** (no GST at your turnover) | ₹1,400/hr, prepaid per session | ₹1,400 | n/a | ~100% (your skill income) |
| 1:1 tutoring, alumni tutor (Section 5.1) | Tutor via the proprietorship | ₹2,100/hr | ₹1,779.66 | ₹1,200/hr (₹1,100 monthly + ₹100 retention bonus, Section 5.2) | **₹579.66/hr** (₹537.66 after 2% payment fee) |
| Exam bootcamp (8 students, 8 sessions × 2 hrs = 16 hrs) | Tutor alone | ₹16,000/student | ₹13,559 | ₹2,000/hr × 16 = ₹32,000 | 8 × 13,559 − 32,000 = **₹76,475/cohort** |
| "DP Readiness" summer course (Grade 10 → DP1: subject choice, IA/EE/TOK orientation, study systems), 10 students | Tutor, with you teaching 2 sessions in the holidays | ₹21,000/student | ₹17,797 | ₹40,000/cohort | 10 × 17,797 − 40,000 = **₹1,37,966/cohort** |

**Price rule:** collect at least 10 real parent quotes in Phase 1, and keep alumni-tutor prices **15–30% below established adult IB tutors**. If adult tutors charge ₹2,500–3,000/hr, then ₹2,100 is 16–30% below them. If the quotes show adult tutors at or below ₹2,400, drop to ₹1,800 inclusive and tutor pay to ₹1,100. That gives a net margin of ₹425/hr, and **at that tier the base case misses Business Level 1** unless volume rises (Section 8.4).

**Never sell:** IA, EE or TOK writing or editing; grade promises; IB past papers. Your own notes, and explanations of the criteria in your own words, are fine.

### 5.1 Tutor supply: who is eligible, and is there enough of them

**Eligible (all must be true):**
1. **Aged 18 or over.** Check this against a government ID. No current school students, including DP2 seniors: they are minors, so their contracts are void (C2), and unlike you they would tutor without the founder-session supervision.
2. An IB Diploma graduate (**class of 2025 or earlier**) with 40+ points or a 7 in the subject. **Fallback:** an Indian-resident adult with a degree in the subject, at the same pay.
3. **Has the right to do paid freelance work** from where they are. In practice that means they are in India for the whole engagement: at an Indian university (Ashoka, FLAME, St Xavier's, NMIMS, IITs and others), on a gap year, or working. **Excluded:** anyone holding a US F-1, UK Student or similar visa, including during their vacations. They declare their status in writing, and the tutor agreement includes a warranty and immediate termination if the declaration is false.

**Is the pool big enough?** Mumbai has roughly 30+ IB World Schools offering the DP (verify on ibo.org's "Find an IB World School"). Assume each graduates about 40 students a year, take 2 graduating classes, assume about 35% study in India, and assume about 25% scored 40+ or a 7 in a relevant subject: 30 × 40 × 2 × 0.35 × 0.25 ≈ **210 eligible people**. You need 6 tutors by April 2027 and 9 by October 2027, which means converting about 4% of the pool. If **Gate 1** shows fewer than 2 eligible tutors recruited, switch to the subject-graduate fallback before Phase 2.

**Sourcing:**
- your school's alumni office and alumni WhatsApp/LinkedIn groups (graduates only)
- Indian university IB societies
- LinkedIn searches ("IB Diploma" + Mumbai + an Indian university)
- referrals from your first tutors (₹2,000 per referral who completes 20 hours)

### 5.2 Keeping tutors from taking families direct (the agency's main business risk)

The agency keeps about ₹580/hr between what the parent pays (₹2,100) and what the tutor gets (₹1,200). A tutor who offers a family ₹1,700/hr directly saves the family ₹400 and earns ₹500 more, and the agency loses that student for good. **A non-solicitation clause that applies after the contract ends will not protect you**: under s.27 of the Indian Contract Act, post-termination restraints of trade are void, and Indian courts generally refuse to enforce them. (A restraint **during** the contract is enforceable: *Niranjan Shankar Golikari v Century Spinning*, SC 1967.) So the protection has to be mostly economic, and the model assumes some leakage anyway.

| Defence | Exact rule | Cost in the model |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Deferred retention bonus** | Tutor pay is **₹1,100/hr paid monthly plus ₹100/hr paid every 31 January and 31 July** for the hours in that half-year, **only to tutors still engaged and in good standing on that date**. A tutor who leaves, or is terminated for soliciting a family during the contract, forfeits the unpaid bonus. By July a tutor on 3 students has about ₹15,000 at stake. | ₹0 (it is part of the ₹1,200) |
| 2. **Tenure step** | After **300 logged hours** with the agency, the monthly rate rises to **₹1,150** (₹1,250 with the bonus). The first six tutors reach it around Feb 2028. | ₹50/hr on their hours: ₹40k in the Level 1 year, ₹83k a year after |
| 3. **In-term restraint** (enforceable) | While engaged, a tutor may not tutor, directly or through anyone else, any family introduced by the agency. Breach means immediate termination and forfeiture of the bonus. | ₹0 |
| 4. **Agency-owned value the tutor cannot take** | An entry diagnostic, a termly progress report against the IB criteria (written by the coordinator from session reports), a termly mock paper marked by a **second** tutor, priority bootcamp seats for agency families, and a **replacement guarantee**: a new tutor within 7 days if one leaves. The coordinator, not the tutor, owns each family relationship and calls each parent once a term. | Inside the coordinator's hours |
| 5. **Concentration cap** | No tutor holds more than **40 hrs/month** (about 15% of peak hours), so no single departure removes more than about 1/7 of the business. | ₹0 |
| 6. **Leakage allowance** | The model assumes **5% of tutor-hours are lost every year from Aug 2027** to tutors going direct or families leaving with them. | ₹70k a year |

Track leakage on the board sheet: every family that stops within 4 weeks of its tutor leaving is counted. If annual leakage passes **5%**, raise the tenure step to ₹1,200 monthly and review prices; if it passes **10%**, Business Level 1 fails (Section 8.4).

## 7. The plan, week by week

**Hours rule (takes precedence over everything below):**
- at most **8 hrs/week in term**
- **2 hrs/week in school exam and IA-deadline weeks**
- **15 hrs/week in holidays**
- **4 hrs/week in DP2**
- **1 hr/week from 15 Feb 2028**

Log your hours every Sunday. In a 2-hour week, an alumni tutor covers your own students; the proprietorship bills those sessions and you are not paid for them. Your 2 hours go only to the Monday coordination call and urgent parent messages. If you go over the cap two weeks in a row, cut scope, not sleep.

### Phase 0: Set up (26 Oct – 15 Nov 2026, term cap, ~5 hrs/week)
Diwali (Lakshmi Puja) falls on **Sunday 8 November 2026**, so most Mumbai schools break around early to mid November. These tasks fit the normal term cap. Use any break days for steps 2 and 4.
1. Complete Section 6 steps 1–8. The parent and CA do most of the work; your part is about 3 hours.
2. Build a **Carrd** page (about US$19/year) or a Google Site with: who you are, subjects, prices, a booking and parental-consent Google Form, and your safeguarding rules.
3. Set up **WhatsApp Business**. Every student chat is a group that includes the parent.
4. Write the 1-page **Safeguarding & Integrity Code**:
   - Sessions are online only, held in a common area or recorded with consent. There are no private chats. No one writes assessed work.
   - **Every tutor the business engages is 18+.**
   - **Founder-session rule** (because you are a minor):
     - you tutor only students who are not more than one school year above you
     - the tutee's parent is in the WhatsApp group
     - every session is recorded with the parent's consent, and the operating parent can view any recording
     - you never meet a tutee in person
   - Incidents are reported to the operating parent within 24 hours.
5. Pick **your 2 strongest subjects** (e.g. Maths, Physics or Economics).
6. **Academic baseline:** record your school-reported 1–7 grades from your first DP1 tests. Replace them with the **first DP1 term report** (Nov–Dec 2026) as soon as it arrives. If your school issues official predicted grades, use those instead.
7. **Tranche 0 released: ₹25,000.**

### Phase 1: Validate (16 Nov 2026 – 31 Jan 2027, ~7.5 hrs/week)

| Weekly block | Hours |
|---|---|
| Own tutoring: Tue and Thu evenings, 1 × 1.5 hrs each, plus Saturday 2 hrs | 5.0 (mostly sales and free trials in November) |
| Sales and admin: parent calls and tutor screening (Saturday) | 2.0 |
| Sunday review and hours log | 0.5 |
| **Total** | **7.5** |

1. **Weeks 1–3: 20 parent conversations** from your family's network, using 5 questions:
   1. Does your child have a tutor now, and for which subject?
   2. How much do you pay per hour, and how often?
   3. What frustrates you about the current tutor?
   4. Would you try a session with me at ₹[price], with the first one free if it isn't useful?
   5. Who else should I talk to?

   **Output:** at least 10 price quotes and at least 5 trial bookings. **November earns little:** the model assumes only **4 paid hours (₹5,600)**, because trials are free.
2. **From December: tutor 4 students at 1 hr/week each** within your 5 hours (4 paid hours a week, ₹24,248/month). Send a 3-line WhatsApp report after every session.
3. **Weeks 4–10: recruit 3 tutors** under Section 5.1. Screen each one:
   - age 18+ checked against a government ID
   - IB transcript (40+, or a 7 in the subject)
   - a **written work-rights declaration** (in India for the engagement; not on an F-1, UK Student or similar visa)
   - a 20-minute demo lesson
   - 2 references
   - signing the Code and a **tutor agreement with the proprietorship**
4. **Weeks 6–11:** route overflow demand to tutors through the proprietorship at ₹2,100 (the tutor is paid ₹1,200). Target: **25 tutor-hours in January**, which is **4 tutor-routed students at about 1.5 hrs/week each** (4 × 1.5 × 4.33 = 26). New students start at 1–1.5 hrs/week and move to about 2 hrs/week as exams approach (the Phase 2 conversion).

### Phase 5: Company, handover and age 18 (start **by March 2028**, so it works for every birthday; the parents and a CS do the work, and your time is about 0)
1. **Incorporate a private limited company**, whatever your age:
   - **Subscribers to the Memorandum: both parents** (adults). A minor cannot subscribe (C2). Use, for example, ₹1,00,000 capital as 10,000 shares of ₹10, fully paid, funded from the capital reserve.
   - **Directors: both parents.** At least one must have been resident in India for 182+ days in the year (Companies Act s.149(3)).
   - File SPICe+ on the MCA portal: about ₹10–25k all-in. Annual compliance, including the mandatory audit, is about **₹40–80k (the model uses ₹60k/yr, ₹5k/month from Mar 2028)**.
2. **Put 75% in your name (immediately after the Certificate of Incorporation):**
   - A parent **gifts 7,500 fully paid shares** to you by a share transfer deed (Form SH-4), which your guardian executes on your behalf. Stamp duty is 0.015% of value (a few rupees). The board approves the transfer.
   - The register of members records: "**[Student], minor, through natural guardian [Parent]**".
   - **Tax:**
     - The gift of shares from a relative is exempt for you.
     - Any dividend on them is clubbed with the parent's income until you turn 18 (C4).
     - A CS confirms the recording, and your CA confirms the tax treatment.
     - **Dividend timing:** the board declares no dividend before your 18th birthday. The first dividend (interim or final) is declared after it, so it is your own income and is not clubbed.
3. **Move the going business into the company (April–June 2028):**
   - **What moves:** brand, domain, content, customer relationships and the trademark (assigned), plus **fresh tutor and coordinator contracts signed by the company**.
   - **How:** a **valued transfer** (a slump sale), at a value your CA or a registered valuer sets.
     - Slump-sale fair value under Rule 11UAE starts from the **book value** of what is transferred, and self-generated brand and goodwill carry no book value. So expect a small figure. **The model assumes ₹2 lakh, paid in 12 monthly instalments out of the company's profit**, so no new family capital is needed and the ₹5 lakh cap holds.
     - The parent's capital-gains tax on the sale is computed on that fair value. Your CA confirms both figures.
   - A tax-neutral proprietorship-to-company conversion (s.47(xiv) of the 1961 Act; check its successor in the 2025 Act) needs the proprietor to keep ≥50% of the votes for 5 years, which conflicts with your 75%. So use the valued transfer.
   - **GST:**
     - Register the company for GST from its first day of trading. It has a new PAN, and its turnover will clearly exceed ₹20 lakh.
     - A transfer of a business as a going concern is generally GST-exempt; your CA confirms.
     - Afterwards, cancel the proprietorship's GST registration (Form REG-16).
   - **TDS and payroll (the company has no threshold to wait for):**
     - Apply for a **TAN** (Form 49B) before the first payment to any tutor.
     - Deduct TDS on every tutor and assistant payout. Your CA decides the section: fees for professional services (s.194J of the 1961 Act, 10%, above ₹50,000 a year per payee) or contract work (s.194C, 1% for individuals, above ₹30,000 per payment or ₹1 lakh a year). At about ₹31k/month every regular tutor crosses either threshold. Check the 2025 Act's section numbers.
     - Deduct TDS on the GM's salary (s.192). At ₹50k/month plus bonus the rebate usually makes it nil, but the return is still due.
     - **Deposit TDS by the 7th of the next month**; file **Form 26Q** (tutors) and **Form 24Q** (salary) every quarter; issue Form 16A / Form 16.
     - Register for **Maharashtra Professional Tax**: PTRC (as employer; deduct ₹200/month, ₹300 in February, from the GM's salary) and PTEC (the company's own ₹2,500/yr; the CA checks whether each director also needs one).
     - Missing TDS means interest, late fees, and 30% of the tutor-pay expense being disallowed (s.40(a)(ia)). The model adds **₹1,500/month for TDS and payroll filing plus ₹2,500/yr PTEC** from June 2028, the transfer date it assumes (if the transfer completes earlier, these costs start earlier).
   - **From the transfer date, the business's profit belongs to the company**, and 75% of that company is yours.
4. **Promote the coordinator to full-time General Manager on 1 June 2028**, contracted **by the company**, which signs through your parent-directors.
   - **Pay:** ₹50k/month, plus **10% of operating profit from August 2028** (both in the model).
   - **Support:** from August 2028, add a ₹10k/month part-time scheduling assistant.
   - **If the coordinator is not up to the role,** hire externally at ₹50–60k. If you also keep the coordinator, that costs about ₹60k more in the Level 1 year (Section 8.4).
   - **On the conservative track,** promote at about ₹30k.
5. **On your 18th birthday** (in India or abroad):
   - get a DIN, and the board appoints you **director** (DIR-12)
   - your shares are re-recorded in your own name, without a guardian, and clubbing ends
   - if you are abroad, you are likely a **FEMA non-resident**: your shares continue to be held on a non-repatriation basis, and you act only as a non-executive board member to stay within C9. Your **income-tax** residency is a separate day count (C9)
   - an immigration adviser and your CA confirm both points for your country
6. **If you stay in India** (a gap year, or a Mumbai college that leaves you about 25–30 hrs/week), you become the **paid executive director-GM** in place of the hired GM, **and** you tutor 4 hrs/week for the company at the standard tutor rate. This is the base-case route to **Personal Level 1** (Section 8.8). The exact sequence:
   1. **December 2027 board:** decide Option A (stay in India) or B (study abroad), once university offers are known. On Option A, the coordinator's promotion on 1 June 2028 is a **fixed-term GM contract ending on the Option A start date**, with a written offer to return as operations coordinator at ₹30k/month afterwards (Phase 4 rates).
   2. **Option A start date = the later of 1 August 2028 and the first day of the month after your 18th birthday.** You need a DIN and directorship (DIR-12) to be the executive director, and both need you to be 18.
   3. **If your birthday falls after July 2028** (e.g. October 2028), then for August until your birthday month: the GM stays in post; you tutor 6–8 hrs/week **on your own PAN** at ₹1,400/hr, prepaid per session, as before (skill income, not clubbed; ≈ ₹36–48k/month); the board declares **no dividend** until after your birthday, so none is clubbed. On the first of the month after your birthday you take over as director-GM, and the 12-month company year in Section 8.8 starts then.
   4. **Board resolution on your pay:** salary ₹50,000/month, the same as the GM it replaces (arm's-length under s.40A(2)); tutoring paid at ₹1,200/hr like every other tutor; no GM bonus.
   5. Then work towards **Level 2 (₹3 lakh/month of business profit)** with:

      - an admissions arm staffed by experienced adult counsellors only

### 8.6 The retained profit: whose it is, and how it can become yours

**Base-case retained profit, Aug 2027 until the business moves to the company:** proprietorship profit minus your founder fee, about **₹90k–1 lakh/month on average before your parent's tax**. It is your parent's business income, taxed at their slab rate (likely the top rate plus surcharge for this family, roughly 39%).

| Route | Works? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Parent gifts the cash to the **company** | **No** | A closely held company receiving money without consideration is taxed on it as income. The "relative" exemption covers only individuals and HUFs (s.56(2)(x) of the 1961 Act, carried into the 2025 Act). |
| Parent uses it to **buy company shares** | **No** | The shares would be the parent's, not yours. |
| Parent **gifts it to you before 18** | Legal, but inefficient | The gift is exempt for you (gift from a relative), but **all income on it is clubbed with the parent's** until you turn 18 (C4). |
| **Parent gifts it to you after your 18th birthday** | **Yes, cleanest** | It is exempt for you, clubbing has ended, and the money is unconditionally yours, ready to invest. |
| **The business itself moves to the company** (Phase 5 step 3) | **Yes. This is the main mechanism.** | **All profit after the transfer date** is earned by the company, 75% of which you own. Sections 8.7 and 8.8 show what that is worth to you in cash. |

So the honest position is this. **Before the transfer, profit is your parent's. After it, three quarters of all future profit, after company tax, and of the business's value, are yours.** Whether the parent also gifts you the pre-transfer profit after you turn 18 is a family decision, and the Memorandum records it. (It is capital, not monthly income: about ₹6 lakh after the parent's tax, worth roughly ₹3–4k/month if invested.)

## 10. Kill, pivot and pause criteria (pre-committed)

- **Pause for 4 weeks** (a tutor covers your students, and you do no business work) if any subject's school-reported grade falls by 1+ from the baseline, or your total falls by 2+. Restart only once it has recovered.
- **Pivot** if Gate 1 fails twice, in this order:
  1. a different segment (Grade 9–10 DP Readiness only)
  2. a different channel (IB specialist for an existing tutoring centre, paid per head)
  3. a different product (written guides and recorded courses)
- **Downshift to the conservative track** if Gate 3 fails (Phase 4 step 2).
- **Leakage response** if annual leakage passes 5% (Section 5.2): raise the tenure step and review prices at the next board; above 10%, treat Business Level 1 as missed for that year.
- **Stop permanently** on any substantiated safeguarding incident, any tutor writing assessed IB work, or any tutor found to be a minor or working in breach of their visa. Refund the affected families in writing.
- **Do not chase Level 2 before June 2028.**
