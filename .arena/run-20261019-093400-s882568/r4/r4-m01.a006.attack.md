ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The student never receives "job replacing money" in any modelled scenario
Where: Section 1 points 2-3; Section 8.5; Section 8.7 "What reaches you" table; Phase 5 step 6 ("the fastest route to Personal Level 1")
Problem: The task asks how the student can "make job replacing money". The plan defines that test itself as "Personal Level 1: ₹1,00,000/month reaching you" and calls it "the like-for-like comparison with a job". Then none of its own scenarios meet it. Before 18: ₹5k-43k/month (8.5). Passive owner abroad: ₹29.5k in year 1, ₹42k after (8.7). Option A, staying in India as director-GM: ₹96,700, and only "≈ ₹84,000 in year 1, after instalments", which is 16% short in the first year. The only route that clears ₹1 lakh personally ("about 5,850 tutor-hours/yr ... 1.8× the base case") is labelled "a 2030 goal at the earliest, and it is not promised". The headline ₹1.14 lakh/month is "Business Level 1": profit that the plan itself says is "your parent's" money until the transfer (8.6). So the requested outcome is met only under a substitute metric. For the actual requirement, the plan gives no concrete path, with steps or numbers, that reaches ₹1 lakh/month for the student; it states the gap and stops there.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The base case fails its own Gate 1 student-count test
Where: Phase 1 Gate 1, "at least 8 paying students across both money flows"; Phase 1 step 2, "tutor 3–4 students ... about 4 paid hours a week"; Phase 1 step 4, "Target: 25 tutor-hours in January"; Phase 2, "Each milestone corresponds to students at about 2 hrs/week each (8.66 hrs/month)"
Problem: Using the plan's own conversion, 25 tutor-hours in January is 25 / 8.66 ≈ 3 tutored students. Add the founder's 3-4 students and the base case has 6-7 paying students on 31 Jan 2027, which is below the threshold of 8. January bootcamp pre-sales cannot fill the gap, because the Gate 1 revenue figure ("about ₹73,500 proprietorship revenue" = Dec ₹21,000 + Jan ₹52,500) leaves them out, so they are not counted in January. Under the plan's own rules, the base case therefore fails Gate 1. That withholds Tranche 1 and the founder fee and sends the student into a 6-week fix cycle. Meanwhile the ₹15k/month coordinator, hired in January to start on 1 February, and the 50-hour February milestone go ahead on schedule. Every later base-case number assumes Gate 1 passed on time. The gate and the model contradict each other, so a user following the plan cannot tell which one to trust.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The margin depends on tutors not going direct, and the only protection offered is a clause Indian law voids
Where: Section 5 product table (parent pays ₹2,100/hr, tutor gets ₹1,200/hr, agency keeps ₹579.66); Section 11D, "12-month non-solicitation of families"
Problem: The business is a pure markup of about 43% on 19-year-old freelance tutors whom the parents meet every week over WhatsApp and video. Counterexample: a tutor offers the family ₹1,700/hr directly. The family saves ₹400/hr and the tutor earns ₹500/hr more, and the agency loses ₹580/hr on that student for good. The plan's only defence is a 12-month post-termination non-solicit. Under s.27 of the Indian Contract Act, post-termination restraints of trade are void, and Indian courts generally refuse to enforce them. The plan has no loyalty pay step, no agency-owned assets (diagnostics, reports), no cap on the share of hours one tutor holds, and no leakage allowance in its hours model. Yet the plan's own sensitivity analysis says Business Level 1 fails at 13% fewer hours. A handful of defections is therefore enough to sink the headline result, and the plan neither models this nor guards against it.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] Wrong tax-residency claim for the student studying abroad, which distorts the passive-income figures
Where: C9, "Studying abroad also typically makes you a non-resident under FEMA and for tax"; Section 8.7, "Non-resident (abroad): there is no rebate ... the passive numbers fall by roughly 10–20%"
Problem: Under Indian income-tax law, the extended 182-day test applies only to citizens leaving India for employment (or as crew). A student who leaves for study stays resident in any year with 60+ days in India, provided they spent 365+ days in India over the previous four years. A student leaving in Aug-Sept 2028 has spent about 150 days in India in FY 2028-29 before leaving, so they are almost certainly resident for that year. They also stay resident in later years if they spend 60+ days at home in vacations. For a resident, the s.87A rebate applies and the 20% non-resident withholding does not. That is the opposite of what the plan tells the user. The figures for the passive scenario, the treaty/Form 10F steps and the "fall by 10–20%" warning are therefore wrong for the most likely case, and the plan gives no day-count check that would let the user tell which regime applies.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] TDS obligations on tutor payouts are missing entirely (no TAN, no 194J/194JA deduction, no quarterly returns)
Where: Section 6 Phase 0 setup table; Phase 5 step 3 ("fresh tutor and coordinator contracts signed by the company"); Section 8.7 cost lines
Problem: From Apr-Jun 2028 the company pays about 9 tutors roughly ₹31 lakh a year (2,618 hrs × ₹1,200), plus a GM salary. A company must deduct TDS on payments to contractors and professionals once they pass the annual thresholds; each tutor earns about ₹31k a month, so every one of them passes. It must also deduct TDS on salary, hold a TAN, deposit TDS monthly and file quarterly 24Q/26Q returns. The proprietorship's FY 2027-28 receipts of about ₹59.8 lakh could also trigger a tax audit if the CA treats tutoring as a profession (the threshold for professional receipts is ₹50 lakh), and that would bring TDS duties into the proprietorship as well. The plan's step-by-step setup never mentions TAN or TDS, and the Phase 5 company setup never mentions Maharashtra Professional Tax employer registration for the GM's salary either. A user following the "exact steps" would miss these filings, which carry interest, a s.40(a)(ia) disallowance of 30% of the tutor-pay expense, and late-filing fees.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Option A and the age-18 steps break for a birthday the plan says it covers
Where: Assumption 3, "The plan works for any birthday in that range [late 2027 to late 2028]"; Phase 5 step 6 and Section 8.7 Option A (paid executive director-GM from June 2028); Section 11A clause 8
Problem: Counterexample: the student turns 18 in October 2028. From June to October 2028 they cannot hold a DIN or be a director (C2), so the "paid executive director-GM" role in Option A cannot start in June 2028 as modelled. Their 75% of any dividend is also clubbed with the parent's income until October (C4). The plan's choice between A and B is made at the December 2027 board and assumes Option A from June 2028, but no fallback for a late birthday is given. The Option A figure of ₹96,700/month therefore does not apply to part of the birthday range the plan explicitly says it covers.
