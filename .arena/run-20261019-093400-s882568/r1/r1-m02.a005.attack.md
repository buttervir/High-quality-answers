ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The headline profit ignores the 18% GST the plan itself says will apply
Where: Phase 2 step 1 ("GST: private tutoring is a taxable service (18%). Registration is only compulsory once turnover passes ₹20 lakh a year") and the Phase 3 target ("≈ ₹1,75,000 a month net").
Problem: Using the plan's own numbers, turnover passes ₹20 lakh well before June 2028. Phase 3 bills about 250 hrs a month. At the stated prices (₹1,500–₹3,000 an hour, so roughly ₹2,000+ on average) that is about ₹5 lakh+ a month in tutoring alone, or ₹60 lakh+ a year, before bootcamps (₹6–8 lakh per summer). Even Phase 2's 150 hrs × ~₹2,000 = ₹3 lakh a month crosses ₹20 lakh within about 7 months. Once the business is registered, 18% of billings goes to GST. The tutors are unregistered individuals, so there is almost no input credit to offset it. If prices stay as quoted (GST-inclusive), 18/118 of ~₹5.5 lakh is about ₹84,000 a month, which cuts the "₹1,75,000 net" to about ₹90,000. That is below the plan's own ₹1 lakh "job-replacing" definition (Assumption 2). If GST is added on top instead, every price in the plan is 18% understated. The plan never models either case, so its central claim, hitting the target with buffer, does not follow from its numbers.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The tutor pool leaves exactly when Phase 3 needs it
Where: Phase 2 step 2 ("Your best supply is IB students who have just finished DP2 and scored 40+. Their exams end in May and they have about 3 months before university"), and Phase 3 ("~250 tutoring hours a month").
Problem: The main tutor supply is, by the plan's own description, available only from about June to August 2027. They leave for university in August or September 2027, which is when Phase 3 starts and billed hours are supposed to rise from 150 to 250 a month. The plan has no replacement or retention step for this cohort beyond "also recruit IB-trained teachers", which it neither prices nor sizes. Counterexample: 6 tutors recruited in May 2027, 4 of them fresh DP2 graduates, leaves 2 tutors in September 2027. Those 2 cannot deliver 250 hrs a month, and that is the assumption both the ₹97,500 Phase 2 figure and the ₹1,75,000 Phase 3 figure depend on.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The margin figures contradict each other, and some pairings lose money
Where: Section 2 ("Tutors are paid ₹1,100–₹1,800 an hour. You keep ₹600–₹1,200 an hour"), compared with Phase 2 steps 2–3 ("Pay ₹1,200–₹1,800 an hour" and "MYP/IGCSE stays at ₹1,500–₹1,800").
Problem: With MYP/IGCSE priced at ₹1,500–₹1,800 and tutors paid ₹1,200–₹1,800, the margin is ₹0–₹600, and it is negative at the worst pairing (₹1,800 tutor, ₹1,500 price). That breaks Section 2's floor of ₹600. For DP, the plan wants "tutors who scored 42+", who would sit at the top of the pay band (₹1,800), giving ₹400–₹1,200. The "average margin of ₹650" in Phase 2 and "₹700" in Phase 3 are asserted, not derived. No hour mix is given, so the user cannot check them or set a tutor's pay without guessing.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The plan schedules owner work abroad that its own visa note says is not allowed
Where: Q8 ("A US F-1 student visa generally does not allow off-campus work, and a UK Student visa does not allow self-employment") compared with Section 7 ("From Aug 2028 (university) | 2–3, remote | Owner: monthly finance review, hiring approvals") and Phase 3 step 1 ("You keep only these jobs: pricing, approving tutor hires, and a 1-hour weekly review").
Problem: Approving hires and setting prices are active management, not passive ownership. On the plan's own reading of the visa rules, these duties are exactly what a student in the US or UK may be barred from doing. The plan adds "within your visa rules" without saying what is left once those rules apply. If the answer is "nothing", the business has no decision-maker from August 2028, because the coordinator is described as handling only "matching, scheduling, invoicing and parent calls".

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The June 2028 target counts revenue that does not exist yet and work the calendar forbids
Where: Phase 3 target ("Target by June 2028: … Plus ~₹40,000 a month averaged from bootcamps and courses"), Phase 3 step 3 (the course is "recorded in the June–July 2028 window after your exams", and the bootcamp is repeated "in May–July 2028"), and Section 7 ("Jan – May 2028 (mocks and finals) | 2 | Owner review only, no new projects").
Problem: By June 2028 the course has not been recorded, so it has earned ₹0. The 2028 bootcamp is scheduled to start in May 2028, the month of the student's final IB exams (Assumption 1), which breaks the plan's own "no new projects" rule for that period. Removing the ₹40,000 leaves ₹1,35,000 before GST, and Attack 1 takes that below ₹1 lakh. The checkpoint in Q10 ("30 June 2028: ₹1,00,000+ a month in net profit") is not supported.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The "Pvt Ltd at 18" timing fails for a large share of students who are 16 now
Where: Section 7 ("Jun – Jul 2028 … set up the Pvt Ltd at 18") and Section 4 ("Within 3 months after you turn 18").
Problem: A student who is 16 in October 2026 was born between about October 2009 and October 2010, so they turn 18 at some point between October 2027 and October 2028. Someone born in August–October 2010 is still a minor in June–July 2028 and is abroad at university by the time they turn 18, so they cannot be the director the plan needs during the window it reserves for this. Someone born in late 2009 could have incorporated about 8 months earlier. Section 7 gives a fixed slot without a birthday-based rule, so the step cannot be carried out as written for part of the target group.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] "Udyam registration" is presented as what a sole proprietorship "needs"
Where: Phase 2 step 1 ("Your parent registers a sole proprietorship under the trade name. That needs Udyam registration").
Problem: India has no registration that creates a sole proprietorship. Udyam is an optional MSME registration. Banks usually ask for two business-identity proofs to open a current account, for example Udyam plus a Shops & Establishments intimation or GST registration. The plan treats Udyam as the single requirement, and says elsewhere that the S&E filing is "at most" needed, so the user may arrive at the bank with too few documents. This is fixable, but as written it is wrong.
