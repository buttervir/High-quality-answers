# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

## The short answer

Start an **IB tutoring and revision agency in Mumbai**. You handle the brand, matching, quality and sales. The tutoring is done by IB alumni and IB teachers **who can legally work from India**, and you keep about ₹555 of every ₹2,000 hour after GST, tutor pay and payment fees.

- **Structure until you turn 18:** a **sole proprietorship in one parent's name**. It pays **you** a fair monthly fee for your work, which goes into your own bank account.
- **Structure from 18:** your own **Private Limited company**, which pays you a salary.
- **Family money:** **no more than ₹1.5 lakh**, written down as a loan.
- **The target:** the business generates **₹1.15 lakh a month before any pay to you** (₹1 lakh for your pay, ₹15k reserve). That ₹1 lakh is about ₹12 LPA, roughly a good Mumbai graduate's starting salary, and at that level it is close to tax-free.

**Here is the honest timeline:**
- **By Feb–Apr 2028 (month 16–18):** the business reaches ₹1.15L in its peak months while a manager runs it during your exams.
- **Before you turn 18:** what you take home yourself is a fee of ₹40–60k a month. The rest of the profit is legally your parent's income, so the plan keeps it low by reinvesting it.
- **From 18:** your own company pays you ₹1L a month. Covering that across a full year, including the off-season, is realistic in **2029**.

Below is how I got there. I split the problem into eight parts, solved each one, compared seven business options in a scored table, and then checked the points where the parts depend on each other. Every major choice also says what it costs you.

---

## Assumptions

- **Timing:** you started DP1 around August 2026. Final IB exams are May 2028, and you turn 18 during 2028.
- **"Job-replacing money"** means **₹1,00,000 a month that you personally take home, after tax, every month of the year**. That is what a ₹12 LPA job gives you. Business revenue doesn't count. Profit legally belonging to your parent doesn't count either.
- **The goal is for *you* to build something and earn from it.** If the family only wants income in your name, see Option G in the table. It is faster, but it is not a business.
- **Your university plans are still open.** Studying abroad from August 2028 is a real possibility, so the plan has to survive it.
- **Laws:** Indian tax and company rules are as of late 2026, with the Income-tax Act 2025 in force from April 2026 and slab rates carried over. Check every legal and tax point below with a CA (chartered accountant) before you act. I flag the ones that matter most.

### Sub-problem 1: Target

**One metric, used everywhere in this plan: founder-available profit (FAP).**

> FAP = revenue − GST − tutor pay − payment-gateway fees − operations manager − tools − marketing

This is everything the business earns *before* it pays you anything. Your fee or salary comes out of FAP. The ₹15k reserve stays in the business to cover refunds, compliance and lean months.

| Test | What it proves | Target | Expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Peak test** | The business can support a ₹1L salary while running without you | FAP ≥ **₹1,15,000** for 3 months in a row | Feb–Apr 2028 |
| **Annual test** (the real "job replacement") | It can pay you ₹1L every month, including the off-season | FAP over the last 12 months ≥ **₹13.8 lakh** | About mid-2029 |

**Checkpoints (FAP per month):**
- ₹20,000 by January 2027
- ₹60,000+ in April 2027 (bootcamp peak)
- ₹65,000+ by December 2027
- ₹1,15,000+ in Feb–Apr 2028

**Who actually gets the money**, so there are no surprises:

| Period | What reaches *your* bank account | What happens to the rest of FAP |
|---|---|---|
| Before 18 | A market-rate fee, ₹10–15k a month rising to ₹40–60k. It is taxed as *your* income: ₹6–7L a year, so nil tax under the rebate | It is legally the Proprietor parent's profit, taxed at their slab (about 36–39% if they are in the top bracket). So the plan **reinvests most of it** (marketing, tutor training, the question bank), which keeps taxable profit low |
| From 18 (your Pvt Ltd) | A ₹1,00,000 a month salary. It is deductible for the company, and for you it is about tax-free under the new regime (₹12L rebate plus ₹75k standard deduction) | The reserve stays in the company |

**Measure it with a monthly P&L sheet:** revenue, GST, tutor pay, gateway fees, manager, tools, marketing, then **FAP**, then your fee, then what's left.

### Sub-problem 2: Time budget
The IB already takes about 50–60 hours a week: six subjects, IAs (internal assessments), the EE (Extended Essay), TOK (Theory of Knowledge) and CAS (Creativity, Activity, Service). Your grades also decide your university options, and those are worth more than any first business. So the business gets fixed hours, and the periods don't overlap:

| Period | Hours a week on the business, maximum | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nov 2026 – May 2027 (DP1 term) | 8: Saturday 3h, Sunday 2h, 20–30 min on weekdays | Setup and growth |
| Jun – Jul 2027 (summer break; match it to your school's calendar) | 20–25 | Biggest growth window, and you write the playbook |
| Aug – Dec 2027 (DP2, EE/IA deadlines) | 4–5 | Your coursework comes first |
| Jan – May 2028 (mocks and finals) | 1–2 (one weekly call) | A manager runs operations |
| Jun – Aug 2028 (after exams) | 30+ | Grow it and prepare the handover or the move |

**This rules out** any business that needs you during working hours, needs you there every day, or needs you during exams.

### Sub-problem 3: Legal capacity and tax for a 16-year-old in India

| Item | Rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Contracts | Under the Indian Contract Act 1872, a contract with a minor is void (*Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*) | Customers, tutors, landlords and payment gateways must contract with an **adult** |
| Company director, OPC, LLP designated partner | Must be 18 or older | You can't be the legal head of a company yet |
| Holding shares | A minor can hold fully paid shares through a guardian | Possible, but income from them is added to a parent's income for tax |
| Bank account | RBI allows minors aged 10+ to run their own savings account, within limits the bank sets | Open **your own** account to receive your fee |
| Tax ("clubbing") | A minor's income is normally taxed as the parent's income. The exception is income from the minor's own **manual work, skill, talent or specialised knowledge** (old s.64(1A), carried into the 2025 Act) | A **fee paid to you for real work** is taxed as *your* income. Under the new regime, income up to ₹12 lakh a year has no tax after the rebate. **Confirm with a CA.** |
| Proprietorship profit | All of a proprietorship's profit is the proprietor's income | Profit you don't receive as a fee is the parent's income, at the parent's slab (30% plus surcharge and cess, about 36–39% if they are in the top bracket) |
| Paying a relative | Payments from a business to a relative must be at a reasonable market rate (s.40A(2) principle) | Your fee is capped at what a part-time growth or operations lead would earn, which is why it tops out at ₹40–60k before 18 |
| Child labour | People aged 14–18 may work in non-hazardous jobs | Office, marketing and coordination work is fine |
| Foreign entity (Delaware company, etc.) | Investing abroad as a resident falls under FEMA ODI rules and the LRS limit of $250k per person per year | Not worth it before 18. Skip it. |

**Decision:** until you turn 18, the business is a **sole proprietorship of one parent** (call them the "Proprietor parent"). It trades under your brand name and pays you a monthly fee. At 18 it moves into your own Private Limited company (see Sub-problem 8).

**What this costs you:** two things.
- **Until 18, the business and most of its profit are legally your parent's.** Your protection is a short signed family agreement that says the business and brand move to your company when you turn 18.
- **Until 18, your own take-home is capped at a fair fee.** You get the full ₹1L salary only after the transfer.

### Sub-problem 4: Business choice (options matrix)

Each option is scored from 1 to 5 on six weighted criteria taken from Sub-problems 1–3:

- **Time fit (25%):** can run on 4–8 hours a week and survive exam months.
- **Profit path (20%):** realistic route to ₹1L a month in FAP.
- **Legal feasibility (15%):** workable for a minor in India.
- **Your edge (15%):** an advantage that you specifically have (IB insider, Mumbai IB network, family's trust in the community).
- **Downside (10%):** money, reputation and academic risk. 5 means the safest.
- **Learning and university value (15%).**

| Option | Time fit | Profit path | Legal | Your edge | Downside | Learning | **Weighted** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. IB tutoring and revision agency** (alumni and teacher tutors, you run it) | 4 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 4 | **4.15** |
| G. Family gives capital, e.g. ₹2 crore in debt or dividend funds (about ₹1–1.2L a month before tax) | 5 | 5 | 3 | 1 | 4 | 1 | 3.40 |
| C. IB content creator (YouTube/Instagram) plus digital products | 3 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 3.30 |
| E. Freelance skill (web, video editing) for overseas clients | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 3.20 |
| D. SaaS or study app | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 2.95 |
| B. D2C product brand (Shopify or Amazon) | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 2.50 |
| F. Buy a franchise, café or small business with family money | 1 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1.90 |

**Why A wins:**
- **You know the product better than any adult competitor.** You are inside the IB right now: which topics students struggle with, which teachers and schools are known for what, and when the mock exams fall.
- **Customers come through people you already know.** IB families in Mumbai find tutors by word of mouth, and your parents' and classmates' networks are where those recommendations happen.
- **Capital needed is close to zero.** Tutors are paid only after customers have paid.
- **It can run without you.** Online sessions and a matching playbook mean you are not needed in the room.
- **There is a clear route to the target.** About 275 one-to-one hours a month gets there, or about 210 in a month with one 20-hour bootcamp (maths in Sub-problem 6).

**Why not the runners-up:**
- **G is the fastest route to the money, but it fails your actual goal.** It isn't a business, you learn nothing, and until you are 18 the income is taxed as your parent's. If the family only wants income in your name, this is the honest answer: gift the capital, invest it in debt or dividend funds, and you are done. This plan assumes that isn't what you want.
- **C is weak on its own** because creator income takes 12–24 months to start. **It is used inside A** as a free marketing channel.
- **E** gives you no edge over millions of freelancers, and since Stripe India stopped onboarding, getting paid from abroad is harder.
- **B and F** both need adult working hours, stock or staff, and real money at risk.

**What choosing A costs you:**
- **The upside has a ceiling.** A service agency grows by adding hours and people. It won't become a ₹100-crore company the way a product or SaaS business might.
- **It depends on how good the tutors are.** One bad tutor damages the brand in a tight community.
- **It is seasonal.** Demand peaks January to April, which is exactly when you sit your own exams, and it drops after May. That is why the plan uses a 12-month test and not only a peak test.

If you want the higher-upside path, the plan's long-term step (an original question bank, see Phase 4) moves A toward a product over time.

### Sub-problem 5: Capital policy
- **Ask the family for ₹1.5 lakh**, written as an interest-free loan. Repay it from profits by month 12.
- **No more money unless you hit the checkpoints** (see the kill and pivot rules in Sub-problem 6).
- **Why so little when the family is worth about ₹70 crore:** a business that only survives on family money tells you nothing. Here the family wealth is best used as a **safety net**: you can afford to fail, so you can afford to try. It should not be used as fuel.
- **What this costs you:** slower early growth. For example, no paid ads at ₹50,000 a month and no office.

### Sub-problem 6: Execution (the core plan)

#### The product (version 1)

| Product | Price to the family (GST included) | Tutor gets | Your margin per hour (after 18% GST and 2% gateway fee) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1 DP tutoring (Maths AA/AI, Physics, Chemistry, Economics, Biology), online | ₹2,000 / hour | ₹1,100 / hour | **₹555** |
| 1:1 MYP / IGCSE / "DP-readiness" | ₹1,500 / hour | ₹850 / hour | **about ₹390** |
| Small-group revision bootcamps (5–6 students, March–April and summer) | ₹800 per student-hour, so ₹4,000–4,800 per hour | ₹1,500 / hour | **about ₹1,800–2,450** (plan uses ₹1,800) |

**Who can tutor.** This is where the visa rules matter: they apply to your tutors as well as to you.

- **Core pool, the people you recruit first:**
  - IB alumni now at **Indian** universities, such as Mumbai colleges, Ashoka, FLAME, Krea, IITs/BITS and NMIMS
  - Alumni on a gap year in India
  - Graduates working in India
  - **Part-time IB teachers**, for a premium tier at ₹2,800+
- **Bar:** a final IB score of **38+** with a **7 in the subject** they teach, or 2+ years teaching that DP subject.
- **Alumni studying in the UK, US or elsewhere abroad:** they may tutor **only while physically in India**, such as during winter and summer breaks.
  - They sign a declaration that they are not working while in their study country.
  - The reason: a UK Student visa bars self-employment, and US F-1 status bars unauthorised work. Your agency must never be the one paying for a breach.
  - Have the CA or a lawyer sanity-check this rule once.
- **Pay:** ₹1,100 an hour is very good money for an Indian college student. Supply is a real constraint, though, so Phase 0 tests it.

**The pitch:** "Tutors who sat the same papers recently and scored 7s, matched by someone who is in DP right now."

**Benchmark:** experienced IB teachers in Mumbai charge well above ₹2,000 an hour, so you are positioned as quality at a lower price.

#### Unit economics and how many hours you need
- **GST basis:** all the maths is after GST from day one (₹2,000 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,695 net). Private tutoring is taxable at 18%. Registration becomes mandatory once turnover crosses ₹20 lakh a year for services in Maharashtra. Pricing GST-inclusive from the start means no price shock later.
- **Margin:** ₹1,695 − ₹1,100 tutor − ₹40 gateway = **₹555 per 1:1 hour**. One bootcamp hour (5 students) is worth about ₹1,800, or about 3.2 one-to-one hours.
- **Fixed costs at scale (each month), about ₹38,000:**
  - Operations manager (part-time college student): ₹20,000, plus about ₹4,000 in conversion bonuses
  - Tools: ₹3,000
  - Marketing: ₹10,000
  - Safeguarding checks for new tutors: about ₹1,000
- **Hours needed for the target:** (₹1,15,000 FAP + ₹38,000 fixed) ÷ ₹555 = **about 275 one-to-one hours a month**.
  - That is about 60 students at 4–5 hours a month, served by 18–22 tutors.
  - In a month with a 20-hour bootcamp you need about 210 one-to-one hours instead.
- **Market size:** Mumbai has dozens of IB schools, with several thousand DP students in each year group. Check the count on the IBO "Find an IB school" page. You need roughly 2% of them.

#### Phase 0. Validate (weeks 1–3, November 2026). About 15 hours in total.
1. **Interview 15 people** for 15 minutes each: 8 DP students (DP1 and DP2) from at least 3 different schools, and 7 parents. Ask:
   - Which subject they pay for help in now
   - Who the tutor is and how much they charge
   - What frustrates them
   - Whether they would book a trial this month
   - Write the answers in one sheet.
2. **List 30 tutor candidates** from the core pool: alumni from your school's and friends' schools' last 4 graduating years who are now in India, plus 5 IB teachers. Use LinkedIn, alumni groups and older siblings. Message all 30 and aim for 6 yeses who meet the bar and can legally work.
3. **Decision rule (covers every outcome):**

| Result after 3 weeks | Do this |
|---|---|
| 5+ families agree to a paid trial **and** 4+ eligible tutors | Go to Phase 1 and Phase 2 |
| 5+ families, but fewer than 4 tutors | Launch only in the subjects where you have a tutor. Spend 2 more weeks recruiting, including teachers for the premium tier. If you still have fewer than 2 tutors by week 5, switch to Option C |
| 3–4 families | One extra 2-week round: 10 more interviews plus one free workshop. 5+ families by week 5 means go. Otherwise switch to Option E or C |
| Fewer than 3 families | Switch to Option E or C now |

#### Phase 1. Legal and setup (weeks 2–5, in parallel). Mostly done by the Proprietor parent with you.
1. **Pick a name and check it's free:** search the IP India trademark registry, the .in/.com domain, and the Instagram handle. File the class 41 trademark in the Proprietor parent's name (₹4,500).
2. **Udyam (MSME) registration** on udyamregistration.gov.in. It's free and needs the parent's Aadhaar and PAN.
3. **Maharashtra Shops and Establishments intimation.** For fewer than 10 workers this is a free online intimation, which banks often ask for as business proof.
4. **Bank accounts:** a current account in the trade name (Proprietor parent), and **your own savings account** in your name.
5. **Payments:** a Razorpay account (Payment Links and Pages) on the Proprietor parent's KYC. Use UPI Business as a fallback.
6. **Get the CA on board:**
   - Bookkeeping (Zoho Books free tier, or a Google Sheet), with FAP as a line in the books
   - A plan for GST registration (register voluntarily once monthly sales pass ₹1.5 lakh)
   - Your fee arrangement and invoices
   - How much of the parent's profit to reinvest each year
   - Your own income tax return each year, signed by your guardian
7. **Four documents**, drafted by you from templates and checked by a lawyer:
   - **Tutor agreement:** independent contractor, 18+ only, a work-eligibility declaration, police verification consent, pay rate, no poaching your clients for 12 months, the academic-integrity and safeguarding rules from Sub-problem 7, and confidentiality.
   - **Parent terms:** signed by the parent, never the student. Hours are prepaid in packs (4, 8 or 12 hours), 24-hour cancellation, and refund terms.
   - **Child-protection policy** (one page; see Sub-problem 7).
   - **Family agreement:** your role, your monthly fee, the ₹1.5L loan, the reinvestment rule, and the commitment to move the business and trademark to your company at 18.

#### Phase 2. Pilot (December 2026 – January 2027). Target: 10–12 paying students, about 50 hours a month, about ₹20k+ FAP.
1. **Matching playbook (a one-page form):** subject, level (HL/SL), current predicted grade, target grade, school, timings, and in-person or online preference. Match within 48 hours.
2. **Rules for every session:**
   - Online on Google Meet is the default.
   - In-person sessions happen only at the student's home with an adult present.
   - The tutor sends a 3-line note after each session (topics covered, homework, worries). You forward a monthly progress summary to the parent.
3. **Quality loop:**
   - Send a 2-question survey after the 2nd session.
   - Any rating of 3 or below out of 5 gets a call from you within 24 hours and an offer to rematch.
   - Track **renewal rate**, meaning families buying a second hour-pack. **Target 70%+.**
4. **Hook for the January mocks:** run **free 90-minute "Paper 1 strategy" workshops** for each subject, online or in a borrowed room. Each workshop is led by your best tutor and gets about 20 sign-ups. Aim to turn 10–15% of attendees into paying customers.

#### Phase 3. Grow (February – July 2027). Target: 25 students, 110–120 hours a month, ₹60k+ FAP in April.
1. **Referrals:** a family that refers someone who buys gets 1 free hour, and so does the new family.
2. **Content (Option C, used as marketing):**
   - 2 Instagram reels or carousels a week (45 minutes, Sunday batch): "3 mistakes in Maths AA Paper 2", "How the IA criteria are actually marked".
   - Post them as your brand, using your own explanations. **Never share IB past papers or markschemes**, which are IBO copyright.
3. **Revision bootcamps (March–April 2027)** for the current DP2s sitting May 2027 exams: 3–4 subjects, 6 sessions each, 5–6 students each. This is your biggest cash month.
4. **Summer 2027 (you work 20–25 hours a week):**
   - Launch a **"DP-readiness" course** for students entering DP1: an MYP/IGCSE-to-DP bridge course in Maths and Sciences. It fills the post-May dip.
   - Recruit tutors up to 15. Alumni home from abroad for the summer can take readiness groups.
   - Write the **operations playbook**: matching, onboarding, verification, payments, complaints, tutor payouts. Every task should be written so that a stranger could do it.
   - Start paying yourself a fee (see below).

#### Phase 4. Hand over operations (August – December 2027). Target: 190 hours a month, about ₹65k+ FAP.
1. **By September 2027, hire an operations manager**: a college student in Mumbai, part-time, ₹20,000 a month plus ₹2,000 for each new student converted. They handle inquiries, matching, scheduling, tutor verification and payouts by following the playbook.
2. **Fill the autumn:** DP1 students' first IAs and DP2 EE/IA season mean more **concept tutoring** (never writing; see Sub-problem 7). Also market to MYP/IGCSE families.
3. **Your job shrinks to:** a weekly 45-minute review on Sunday (dashboard: hours, renewals, complaints, cash, FAP), tutor hiring decisions, and content approvals.
4. **Optional longer-term step:** have tutors write an **original question bank** for one subject, sold as a ₹2,999 online pack. It is the first step toward a product with a higher ceiling, and a sensible place to reinvest pre-18 profit. Do it only if Phase 3 hit its target.

#### Phase 5. Run without you (January – May 2028). Target: 280+ hours a month in Feb–Apr, **FAP ≥ ₹1.15L for 3 months in a row (the peak test)**.
- This is the demand peak (mocks, then finals) and also your own exam season. The manager runs everything. You do one call a week and are reached only for real emergencies, decided by a written rule such as "refund over ₹10,000". Any safeguarding concern goes straight to the safeguarding lead (see Sub-problem 7), not to you.
- **This phase proves capacity:** if the business clears ₹1.15L FAP while you are sitting your own finals, it can carry a full salary.

#### Phase 6. Off-season and the annual test (May 2028 onward)
- In May, DP2 clients finish and FAP drops. Plan for about ₹30k.
- In June and July, the readiness course, DP1 year-end revision and MYP/IGCSE customers bring it back to about ₹80k.
- From your 18th birthday, transfer the business to your Pvt Ltd (Sub-problem 8) and switch from a fee to a salary.
- **Annual test:** FAP over the last 12 months ≥ ₹13.8 lakh. On the projection that is realistic from about **mid-2029**, once a second full season runs with a bigger tutor bench. Until then, pay yourself what the 12-month FAP supports, and keep the ₹15k a month reserve to cover the dip months.

#### Paying yourself
- **From month 7 (June 2027) until 18:** the business pays you a monthly fee for your role as "Head of Growth and Quality". Start at ₹10,000–15,000 a month and rise to ₹40,000–60,000 at scale. The amount must be a fair market rate, backed by a monthly invoice or timesheet. It is taxed as your own skill income, which is nil under the rebate at this level.
- **The rest of FAP before 18:** it is your parent's income, so tax it as such. **Reinvest it** in tutor training, marketing and the question bank, which keeps the parent's taxable profit low. Any cash left over is held in the business account for transfer to your company at 18, as agreed in the family agreement.
- **From 18:** your Pvt Ltd pays you a salary of up to ₹1,00,000 a month, as far as the 12-month FAP supports it.
- **Ask your CA** to confirm this setup.

#### Kill and pivot rules (agreed with your parents in advance)

| Checkpoint | Continue if | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| End of January 2027 | 8+ paying students, 60%+ renewal, FAP ₹20k+ | Rework the offer once. If it fails again by March, switch to E or C. |
| End of April 2027 | ₹60k+ FAP in the month | No new money. Cut down to your best 2 subjects. |

| End of April 2028 (peak test) | 3 months in a row at ₹1.15L+ FAP | Treat it as a ₹40–80k side business. You don't need a salary yet, so the cost of missing is low. Try again in the 2029 season |
| July 2028 (before any move abroad) | Manager able to run it alone, 12-month FAP on track for ₹9L+ | If you're leaving India: promote the manager or close new intake and run down existing packs. **Never** run it actively from abroad (see Sub-problem 8) |
| Any time | Predicted grades drop by 2+ points in total | Halve your hours that month, no exceptions |

### Sub-problem 7: Risk controls (written down and in every tutor agreement)
1. **Academic integrity.**
   - Tutors teach the concepts. They **never write, rewrite or substantially edit** any IA, EE, TOK essay or exhibition, or any coursework that gets graded.
   - Tutors can explain the assessment criteria and give general feedback. They can't hand over any material that ends up in the student's submission.
   - Under IB rules this kind of help is malpractice. It can cost the *client* their diploma, and you would be the person linked to it.
   - **One breach and the tutor is removed.**
2. **Safeguarding (POCSO Act 2012).** Your clients are minors, and some sessions happen in their homes.
   - **Before a tutor's first session:**
     - ID check
     - An alumni or school reference
     - **Police verification**, done through the Maharashtra Police citizen portal or the tutor's home-state police, or a third-party background check that includes a police-record check
     - A signed copy of the child-protection policy
   - **Session rules:**
     - Online by default. In-person only with an adult present.
     - No private social-media DMs with students: messages go through a parent-included WhatsApp group.
     - Sessions on the company Meet account only.
   - **Mandatory reporting:**
     - Under POCSO **s.19**, anyone who learns of a sexual offence against a child must report it to the **local police or the Special Juvenile Police Unit**.
     - Under **s.21**, failing to report is itself an offence, with a higher penalty for the person in charge of an organisation.
     - So the **Proprietor parent is the designated safeguarding lead**. Their job is to *make the report and remove the tutor immediately*, not to investigate or settle the matter privately. Childline (1098) is the backup contact.
     - Every concern, however small, goes into a dated complaints log.
3. **Data (DPDP Act 2023).** Collect a parent's consent for anything about a student under 18. Keep only the data you need, in one access-controlled Google Drive. Tutors' verification documents are kept separately and deleted when they leave.
4. **Your own school.**
   - Don't recruit or poach your own school's teachers.
   - Don't market in school groups without permission.
   - Tell your DP coordinator or counsellor what you are doing early, so it reads as initiative rather than a surprise.
5. **CAS.** Paid work does not count as CAS. Run a **separate free weekly session** with an NGO or low-income school partner as a genuine CAS Service project. It's real service, and it's better for your application than claiming the business.

### Sub-problem 8: Turning 18 and going to university
- **At 18 (2028):**
  - Set up a **Private Limited company** with you as director and majority shareholder and one parent as second director. Expect about ₹15–25k to set up and ₹40–60k a year in compliance, which the ₹15k a month reserve covers.
  - Move the business, client contracts and trademark across with a CA-drafted business-transfer agreement and a trademark assignment.
  - Repay any remaining family loan.
  - Your pay switches from a fee to a **salary of up to ₹1L a month**. It is deductible for the company, and you pay about nil tax up to ₹12.75L a year under the new regime.
- **University abroad:** the plan was built to be online and run by a manager for this reason, but visas restrict working:
  - **UK Student visa:** you can't be self-employed during your studies.
  - **US F-1:** actively working in your own business is a legal risk.
  - In both cases, before you leave, set the business up so that you are a **passive owner**: the manager is promoted to general manager with a profit share, and you keep ownership and board-level decisions only.
  - In that setup, take dividends rather than a salary for work. The company pays corporate tax first, so plan on less than ₹1L a month in your hands.
  - **Get immigration advice before you leave.** This is the main reason the operations hand-off happens in DP2 and not later.
- **University in India:** you can keep running it actively and draw the salary, and it becomes your main income.
