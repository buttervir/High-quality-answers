ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The plan's own Stage 3 crosses the GST threshold, and 18% GST wipes out the ₹1L target
Where: Section 3.6, "GST: service providers only need to register above ₹20 lakh a year in turnover. You won't reach that in this plan before 18." and Section 2, Stage 3: "about 100 billed hours/month at ₹2,500 ... you keep ₹1,000"
Problem: GST registration depends on gross turnover, not on margin. 100 billed hours × ₹2,500 = ₹2,50,000 a month = ₹30 lakh a year, which is 1.5 times the ₹20 lakh threshold. At that run rate, turnover passes ₹20 lakh in 8 months. The Section 6 trigger "turnover nears ₹15 lakh" fires at only about 50 billed hours a month, which is halfway to the target. Private tutoring that doesn't lead to a recognised qualification is not GST-exempt and is charged at 18%. If the ₹2,500 price stays fixed, net revenue is ₹2,119/hr. After the ₹1,500 tutor fee, the margin is about ₹619/hr, so the headline result drops to about ₹62k a month, 38% short of the "job-replacing" figure. Raising the price to ₹2,950 runs into Attack 2. So the claim "You won't reach that" is false, and the core number of the plan doesn't hold once that's corrected.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The agency price contradicts the plan's own market-rate data
Where: Section 2: "University-student tutors: ₹1,000–₹2,000 per hour" vs Stage 3: "8–12 tutors [IB alumni at university, DP2 students] ... at ₹2,500"; Section 5: "Charge ₹2,000–2,500/hr"
Problem: The plan says the Mumbai market rate for university-student tutors is ₹1,000–2,000/hr. Then the whole ₹1L/month formula depends on selling exactly those tutors, plus DP2 schoolchildren, at ₹2,500/hr. That is 25% above the top of the plan's own range and 2.5 times the bottom. It's also close to the "Professional IB/IGCSE tutors: ₹2,500–₹5,000" rate. No reason is given for why a parent would pay professional rates for a 19-year-old placed by a 17-year-old. At the market rate the plan cites (say ₹1,800, with the tutor paid ₹1,200–1,500), the margin is ₹300–600/hr. 100 hours then gives ₹30–60k a month, not ₹1L. Reaching ₹1L would take about 170–330 billed hours a month, or 20–40 recurring students, which the plan's lead sources (a 40-name list, referrals, Instagram) never show how to reach.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The Stage 3 legal and banking structure is left to "ask a CA", and the headline promise breaks there
Where: Opening paragraph: "with the money going into your own bank account"; Section 6, first trigger: "Ask a CA ... whether agency income ... should be added to a parent's income, and whether to run it as a parent's sole proprietorship"; Section 3.2: "self-operated minor savings account ... All business money goes into this account"
Problem: The Section 64(1A) skill exception that the plan relies on covers income from the minor's own skill. An agency margin earned on other people's teaching very likely doesn't qualify. So the ₹1L/month is likely to be clubbed with a parent's income. With an $8M family, that parent is almost certainly in the 30% slab plus surcharge, which makes the "₹12 lakh rebate / tax-free" reassurance irrelevant. At the same stage, a minor's self-operated savings account would take in about ₹2.5L a month and pay out about ₹1.5L to 8–12 adults. Banks set their own limits on minors' self-operated accounts, and commercial flows through a savings account get flagged, which the plan itself admits in Section 9.5. The task asks for "EXACT DETAILS". The step that turns tutoring into job-replacing income (who owns the agency, whose account, who contracts with tutors, whose tax) is left as an open question for a CA, and the likely answer contradicts the opening paragraph.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The exam blackout cancels the plan's best sales window and its fallback niche
Where: Section 9.1: "Zero new students from 4 weeks before any mock or final exam ... Pause the business in exam periods (DP1 end-of-year exams, DP2 mocks, and May of DP2)"; Section 4: "Exam sprint: 6 sessions in the 3 weeks before school exams"; Section 9.3(b): "'IGCSE Maths exam sprint' in the 6 weeks before exams sells itself"
Problem: IGCSE exams (May–June) and Mumbai schools' internal and board exams (Feb–March) fall in the same windows as IB DP1 end-of-year exams and the DP2 mocks and finals. The rescue plan for "can't find students" is a 6-week sprint before exams. The blackout bans new students for 4 weeks before the student's own exams. The two rules collide, and the plan doesn't say which one wins or how the sprint gets taught. During DP2 May, Stage 3 is supposed to produce ₹1L/month with the founder "paused", but no one is named to run intake, matching and payroll during that time.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] The tutor no-poaching clause is unenforceable on two counts
Where: Section 5: "Tutor agreement: ... no direct poaching of students for 12 months"; Section 9.5: "the tutor agreement has a 12-month no-poaching clause"
Problem: (a) The plan itself says the student is a minor and that a minor's contracts can't be enforced. A minor's agreement is void (Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose), so the 16-year-old can't be the contracting party. (b) Even if a parent signs, post-termination restraints like a 12-month non-solicit are generally void under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act. The clause gives no legal protection, and the plan never says who the contracting party should be for adult tutors.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Plan B's arithmetic and hours don't work
Where: Section 7: "₹1L/month = about 10 retainer clients at ₹6k plus 1 new setup a month"; "₹3,000–₹8,000/month for maintenance and posting"
Problem: 10 × ₹6,000 = ₹60,000. Adding one setup at the stated ₹15,000–40,000 gives ₹75,000–1,00,000 in revenue, so it reaches ₹1L only if every setup is at the top of the price range, and that is before any costs. Plan B has no hour cap. "Posting" for 10 businesses each month, plus one new build, won't fit in the 6 hrs/week IB limit the plan says still applies ("IB comes first").

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] The stage timeline contradicts itself
Where: Section 2 table: "2. Groups | Months 3–6" vs Section 5: "Months 2–3: groups" and "Months 3–6: first 2 tutors"; Assumptions: "₹60k by Month 9"
Problem: The user can't tell whether Months 3–6 are for group classes or for hiring tutors. The ₹60k-by-Month-9 milestone doesn't line up with any row of the table (Stage 2 tops out at ₹55k by Month 6, and Stage 3 jumps to ₹1L+). So there's no checkpoint for knowing whether the plan is on track.
