# The Plan: A 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai Building Job-Replacing Income

## Assumptions

- **Timeline.** It is October 2026. DP1 started mid-2026 and DP2 final exams are in **May 2028**. The plan covers those ~19 months, then hands over to whatever comes after school (university in India or abroad).
- **"Job-replacing money"** means **₹1,00,000 per month after tax** reaching the student's side of the business, sustained for at least 2 consecutive months. That is about the take-home pay of a good Mumbai graduate job. The stretch target is ₹2,00,000 per month. "The student's side" means the fee paid to the student plus the after-tax profit kept in a company the student majority-owns (Part 3 explains why it is split this way).
- **Family wealth** of about $8M (roughly ₹70 crore) is a safety net and a source of contacts. It is **not** what funds the business. The student has to earn the result, or it is really the parents' business with the child's name on it.
- **The student is a minor until their 18th birthday.** Every legal and tax step below is designed around that.
- Indian tax law changes often, and the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. **Before registering anything, book a 1-hour paid consultation with a Chartered Accountant (CA), about ₹3,000–₹10,000, to confirm the tax and registration points.** Part 8 lists the exact questions to ask.

## Part 1: How this goes wrong (and the rule that prevents each failure)

| # | Failure mode | Why it happens to *this* student | Guardrail in this plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Grades crash** | The IB takes about 30–40 hrs/week plus IAs, the EE, TOK and CAS | Hard cap of **8 hrs/week in DP1 and 4 hrs/week in DP2**, with a **per-task hour budget for every phase** and a fixed **drop order** when time runs short (Part 7). The business freezes during exam windows |
| 2 | **Rich-kid vanity startup** | ₹50 lakh is "affordable", so the brand, website and office get built before anyone pays | Capital is released in **tranches tied to revenue gates** (Part 5). The seed budget is **₹25,000** |
| 3 | **Parents end up running it** | Parents sign everything and make the calls | Parents sign only what the law requires an adult to sign. The student owns sales, pricing, hiring and delivery, set out in a written family agreement |
| 4 | **Contracts that can't be enforced** | A contract with a minor is void (*Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*, 1903) | Customers pay **in advance**. **No adult tutor works until a company exists to contract with them and pay them** (Week 13) |
| 5 | **Can't collect or pay money** | Gateways, current accounts and GST registration need an adult or an entity. Minor accounts have low transaction caps | Stage A: UPI into the student's own minor account, **for the founder's own teaching only**. Stage B: a company account and gateway before any tutor is paid |
| 6 | **Tax trap: clubbing** | A minor's income is normally added to the parent's (likely 30%+) | Only the founder's **own teaching** is earned personally (skill income, not clubbed). **Margin earned on other tutors' hours is earned by the company** (~25% tax, not clubbed). No dividends until the student is 18 |
| 7 | **Forgetting GST** | Above ₹20 lakh/year of turnover, private coaching attracts 18% GST | All prices are **GST-inclusive** and the profit math already deducts GST |
| 8 | **Wrong business type** | Cafés, franchises and D2C need capital, leases and daily presence | A 6-test filter (Part 2) |
| 9 | **Gambling disguised as business** | Family money plus a phone makes F&O and crypto look like income | **Banned.** SEBI found about 93% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY22–FY24 |
| 10 | **Academic misconduct** | Tutoring drifts into "just write my IA" | **No writing or editing of assessed work, ever.** This is in parent terms and tutor contracts |
| 11 | **Child safety / data law** | Clients are minors | Safeguarding policy, 18+ tutors, and DPDP Act 2023 parental consent (Part 6) |
| 12 | **School conflict** | Some schools restrict commercial activity | Written notice to the DP coordinator in Week 1. No marketing in school groups |
| 13 | **Quitting at month 3 or option paralysis** | Too many ideas, no kill rules | One primary path, **one fully specified fallback**, and numeric gates |
| 14 | **No tutors to hire** | Tutors must be 18+, and a thin pool can stall growth | Defined pool, a screening test, and a **pool check with a widening rule** (Phase 2) |
| 15 | **Company paperwork rejected** | A Pvt Ltd needs 2 directors, and a minor can't be one | **Two adult directors** named before filing (Part 3) |
| 16 | **Business dies when the student leaves for university** | Founder-dependent services stop | A manager and an operations manual are in place by DP2 |

## Part 2: Which business, and why

### The 6-test filter

1. **Starts for under ₹25,000.**
2. **Paid in advance**, so there are no receivables to chase.
3. **The founder can start it alone on their own skill** (clean personal tax in Stage A).
4. **Fits in 8 hrs/week**, and the work can be handed to others.
5. **Has leverage.** One person's hours can't reach ₹1 lakh/month in 8 hrs/week.
6. **Uses an unfair advantage**: being inside the IB system, living in an affluent Mumbai network, and having family business contacts.

### Primary path: academic support for MYP/IGCSE students (Grades 6–10)

**What it is.** Small-group tutoring and exam prep for Grades 6–10 (MYP and IGCSE) students at Mumbai's international schools. The founder teaches at first, to learn the product. After that, the founder sells, builds the curriculum, quality-checks and manages, and **18+ tutors deliver**.

**Why it fits:**
- Mumbai has a dense cluster of IB and Cambridge schools: Bandra, Juhu, Powai, Lower Parel, Worli, BKC, Malabar Hill, Andheri and Navi Mumbai. Parents there already pay for tutoring.
- A current IB student is credible to the parent of a Grade 8 student.
- It is paid in advance and scales by adding tutors.

**The tutor pool (who actually teaches):**
- All tutors are aged **18+**.
- **Recent IB, IGCSE or A-level graduates** on a gap year or at Mumbai colleges, such as St. Xavier's, Jai Hind, NMIMS, IIT Bombay, ICT and the Mumbai campuses of private universities.
- **Strong college students in the subject**, who can also teach online from anywhere in India.
- **18-year-old DP2 students** with a predicted 7 in the subject, online only, max 3 hrs/week.
- **Screening:** a 7 (IB) or A* (IGCSE/A-level) in the subject, or a college major in it; a 30-minute demo lesson to the founder plus one parent volunteer; 2 references; ID and police verification for in-person work.
- **Pool check:** if fewer than **10 qualified applicants** arrive within 3 weeks of posting, widen the criteria to a 6 or an A in the subject plus a passed demo, and raise pay to ₹900–₹1,000/hr. MYP/IGCSE content does not need an IB graduate. It needs someone who knows the subject and can teach it.

**Pricing** (verify in Week 1 by calling 5 tutoring providers and asking 15 parents). **All prices are GST-inclusive**, so nothing changes for parents when GST registration happens:
- Founder or tutor 1:1: ₹1,000–₹1,500/hr.
- **Group of 4 at ₹700 per student per hr = ₹2,800 per tutor-hour of revenue.**
- Exam crash courses (8 sessions, groups of 4–6): ₹8,000–₹15,000 per student.
- Tutor pay: ₹800/hr. (Session plans written to the template are paid ₹1,000 each.)

**The real math, with GST and tax:**

Per tutor-hour: ₹2,800 incl. GST → **₹2,373** after 18% GST, minus ₹800 tutor pay and ~₹50 payment costs = **₹1,520 contribution**.
One tutor at 6 group-hours/week ≈ 26 hrs/month → **₹39,500/month contribution**.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 3 (part-time ops manager) | 4 | ₹2.91L | ₹1.58L | ₹60k | ₹98k | ₹73k |
| **Phase 3 target** | **5** | **₹3.64L** | **₹1.98L** | **₹60k** | **₹1.38L** | **₹1.03L ✔** |
| DP2 (full-time manager) | 6 | ₹4.37L | ₹2.37L | ₹1.0L | ₹1.37L | ₹1.03L ✔ |
| Full scale | 10 | ₹7.28L | ₹3.95L | ₹1.5L | ₹2.45L | ₹1.84L |

Overhead breakdown:
- **Phase 3:** ops manager ₹30k + tools ₹5k + marketing ₹10k + CA/compliance ₹8k + contingency ₹7k.
- **DP2:** manager ₹50k + tools ₹8k + marketing ₹20k + CA ₹10k + contingency ₹12k.
- **Full scale:** manager ₹60k + lead-tutor stipend ₹15k + tools ₹10k + marketing ₹30k + CA ₹12k + contingency ₹23k.

The student's founder fee (Part 3) is paid out of pre-tax profit. It moves money from the company to the student without reducing the total much, so the "after tax" column is a fair measure of the student's side.

**So: job-replacing income needs 5 fully loaded tutors (about 130 group-hours/month) in Phase 3, or 6 once a full-time manager is on payroll.** If the Week-1 research shows parents accept ₹800/student/hr, the same targets need about one tutor fewer.

### Fallback path: WhatsApp and Google lead systems for family-network small businesses

The fallback triggers if the primary path **fails its Week-8 gate**, or if the student finds they dislike teaching. It has its own full plan in **Part 4B**.

### Explicitly rejected

| Idea | Why |
|---|---|
| Café / cloud kitchen / franchise | ₹20–80 lakh capital, a lease, staff, daily presence |
| D2C product with paid ads | Ad burn and inventory. Family money hides the losses |
| Stock/F&O/crypto trading | Not a business. Most participants lose money. Minors can't trade F&O |
| Creator as the *main* plan | 12–24 months to income and not controllable. Use it as a marketing channel later |
| "Investing" family money in a friend's startup | That is the parents' decision and the parents' money |

## Part 3: Legal, banking and tax setup (exact steps)

### Stage A, Weeks 1–12: the founder teaches personally

Stage A covers **only the founder's own teaching**. That is the student's own skill, so the income is taxed in the student's own hands and not clubbed with the parent's. (This was the s.64(1A) proviso in the 1961 Act; the CA confirms the 2025 Act section.)

1. **PAN** for the student, via Protean (NSDL) or UTIITSL, Form 49A. A parent signs as representative. About ₹100.
2. **Minor savings account** in the student's own name, with self-operated UPI and a debit card. Ask the branch for the UPI and daily limits.
3. **Payment:** UPI in advance for each 4-week package. No credit.
4. **Records:** a Google Sheet with date, payer, child, sessions, amount and UPI reference.
5. **Tax:** under the new regime, a resident individual's income up to ₹12 lakh/year has zero tax after the rebate. **File an ITR every year anyway.** It creates a clean income record for visas and university financial forms.
6. **GST:** not required for this personal income at this scale (well below ₹20 lakh/year).
7. **School:** email the DP coordinator describing the activity, the hours cap and the no-assessed-work rule. Keep the reply.
8. **No one else is paid from this account.** No tutors, no vendors.

### Stage B, from Week 9 (starts the day the Week-8 gate passes): the company

Incorporation **must finish before any tutor teaches**. Tutors are recruited during the 3–4 weeks the paperwork takes, and they start in Week 13.

**Structure: Private Limited Company.**
- **Two adult directors are required** (Companies Act 2013, s.149(1)). Use both parents, or one parent plus a trusted adult relative. At least one must be resident in India. A minor cannot be a director, and a One Person Company can't have a minor as member, so an OPC is not an option.
- **Shares:** the two adults subscribe to the memorandum at incorporation. Do not make the minor a subscriber. Immediately after incorporation, **allot or transfer 74–90% of the shares, fully paid, to the student, held through a parent as guardian.** The money for the shares is a documented gift from a parent.
- **Steps and timing:**
  - Week 9: DSCs for both directors and name reservation, with the filing done by a CA or company secretary.
  - Weeks 10–11: SPICe+ filing.
  - About Week 12: certificate of incorporation, PAN and TAN.
  - Week 12–13: current account, Razorpay/Cashfree gateway on the company's KYC, and online intimation under the Maharashtra Shops and Establishments Act.
  - All-in cost: about **₹15,000–₹25,000**, including DSCs and the professional fee.
- **GST:** register when trailing-12-month turnover reaches **₹15 lakh**, to leave headroom before the ₹20 lakh threshold. In practice that is around Month 6–7. Prices already include GST, so parents see no change.

**From Week 13, all customer revenue goes to the company**, including the founder's own groups. That way one entity holds every customer relationship.

**How the money reaches the student (the tax design):**
1. **Company profit** (the margin on tutors' hours) is taxed in the company at about **25%** (the concessional domestic-company rate, about 25.17% including surcharge and cess). It is not clubbed with anyone, because the company is a separate taxpayer.
2. **Founder fee:** the company pays the student a **documented monthly fee for work actually done**: teaching, curriculum writing, sales, quality checks and management. Recommended: ₹25,000–₹40,000/month once profit supports it. It is a deductible expense for the company. Ask the CA to confirm that a fee for the minor's own work counts as skill income and is not clubbed. If the CA says no, cut the fee to a token amount and keep profit in the company.
3. **No dividends while the student is a minor.** Dividends to a minor are clubbed with the parent's income. Retained profit stays in the company, which the student majority-owns, and builds a reserve. After 18, the student can take dividends in their own name.

**Contracts:**
- **Tutor agreements** are signed by a director on behalf of the company. They cover independent contractor status, ₹/hr, the no-assessed-work clause, safeguarding duties, data handling, and no private poaching of the company's clients for 12 months.
- **Ops manager** contract, signed the same way.
- **When the student turns 18:** appoint them as a director (get a DIN) and have them sign from then on.

### The family agreement (one page, signed in Week 1)

1. The business belongs to the student. Adults sign only where the law requires it.
2. Parents advise when asked. They don't hire, price, sell or deliver.
3. Capital comes only through the tranches in Part 5. Each tranche is written down as either a **loan repayable from profit** or a **gift**.
4. If any subject's predicted grade drops by 1 point or more, the business goes to **2 hrs/week until it recovers**.
5. Quarterly review: numbers, grades, hours.
6. Both adult directors agree in writing to act as directors on the student's instructions until the student turns 18.

### The drop order (when the week's tasks exceed the cap)

Cut tasks in this order until the week fits:
1. Content and Instagram.
2. Workshops.

4. New courses.
5. Sales calls.

**Never cut:** tutor QA, safeguarding, or the founder's own schoolwork. **If any week goes over the cap 2 weeks in a row, the next gate is pushed back 4 weeks.** The gate moves; the IB does not.

## Part 4B: The fallback plan (if the Week-8 gate fails)

The fallback runs on the same calendar, starting again at "fallback Week 1". **What it sells:** a **"Lead System" package** for small businesses from the family's network. It needs only the founder's own skill at first, so the income is personal skill income paid into the minor account (Stage A rules).

**Fallback Weeks 1–2: niche and demo (≤ ₹5,000)**
- [ ] **Pick one niche** where the family has 10+ contacts: clinics, CA firms, jewellers, wholesale traders, salons, or coaching classes.
- [ ] **Interview 10 owners:**
  - "How do customers reach you?"
  - "How many enquiries are lost or answered late?"
  - "What do you pay for marketing now?"
- [ ] **Build a free demo for one family business** and record before and after numbers (enquiries per week, response time).

**The package** (each setup takes ~10–15 founder hours):
1. **Google Business Profile:** set up or optimise it, with photos, services, Q&A and a review-request link.
2. **WhatsApp Business app** (free): catalogue, quick replies, a greeting/away message, and labels for lead stages.
3. **Lead capture:** a Google Form or a website form flows into a Google Sheet, then triggers an automatic WhatsApp/email follow-up through **Make.com or Zapier** (free tiers to start).
4. **Optional automated WhatsApp messages** through a Meta-approved provider (e.g., AiSensy, Interakt or WATI). This always runs on **the client's own account**, with the client paying the subscription and Meta's message charges.
5. **One-page site** on Google Sites or Carrd, with an AI-drafted FAQ reviewed by the owner.

**Pricing:**
- Setup: ₹15,000–₹40,000.
- Retainer: ₹5,000–₹12,000/month for updates, monthly report, review replies and new automations.
- **Payment rule:** 50% upfront by UPI, and the remaining 50% **before the automation is switched on**. Everything is built inside the client's own accounts, so there is nothing to "repossess", and the go-live switch is the leverage.
- Some clients may deduct TDS on professional fees once their payments cross the threshold. That is fine: it appears in the student's tax statement (Form 26AS/AIS) and is refunded through the ITR.

**Fallback Weeks 3–8: first clients**
- [ ] Offer **3 "founding client" slots** at 40% off setup in exchange for a testimonial and before/after numbers. Sell them through 20 warm introductions from the parents.
- [ ] **Capacity:** with 8 hrs/week, at most **2 setups a month**. Each retainer then takes 1–2 hrs/month.
- [ ] **Fallback Week-8 gate:**
  - **Continue** if ≥ 3 paying clients and ≥ ₹40,000 collected.
  - **Otherwise:** stop building a business in DP1. Keep any retainers, and revisit in the summer of 2027.

**Fallback Weeks 9–24: retainers and leverage**
- [ ] Convert every setup client to a retainer.
- [ ] **Week-16 gate:** ≥ 5 retainers (≥ ₹40,000/month recurring).
- [ ] **When the first 18+ freelancer is hired** (a college student at ₹300–₹500/hr doing builds), **incorporate first** (Part 3, Stage B). Freelancers are contracted and paid only by the company.
- [ ] **Path to ₹1L/month:**
  - 12 retainers × ₹8,500 = ₹1.02L/month recurring, plus 1–2 setups a month.
  - Retainer upkeep for 12 clients is ~18 hrs/month, about 4 hrs/week, so it fits the DP2 cap once freelancers handle the setups.

## Part 6: Safety, compliance and integrity (non-negotiable)

1. **Academic integrity:**
   - Teach skills and set practice.
   - **Never** write, edit or "fix" an IA, EE, TOK essay, coursework or homework.
   - This goes in the parent terms and the tutor contracts. Terminate the tutor on a first breach.
2. **Safeguarding:**
   - Tutors are 18+, with ID checks, 2 references, and police verification for in-person work.
   - Online sessions run on the company's account, with parents able to join.
   - In-person sessions happen in common areas or with a guardian present.
   - No private DMs with children.
   - A complaints route goes to an adult director.
3. **Data (DPDP Act 2023 and Rules):**
   - Collect the minimum: child's name, grade, school, subject and the parent's contact.
   - Get **verifiable parental consent**.
   - No tracking or targeted ads at children.
   - Delete data 12 months after a client leaves.
4. **Child labour:** a 16-year-old running their own non-hazardous enterprise is permitted.
5. **Refunds:** unused sessions are refunded in full on 7 days' notice.
6. **CAS:** paid work doesn't normally count. Run a separate **free** weekly session for an NGO or municipal school using the same curriculum kit. It counts as Service and becomes a tutor pipeline.
