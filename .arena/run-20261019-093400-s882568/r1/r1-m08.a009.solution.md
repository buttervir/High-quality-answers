# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

## The short version

1. **Money is not your problem. Time, skill and the law are.** Your family is worth about ₹70 crore ($8M at about ₹88 per dollar). But you have about 8 free hours a week, no track record, and at 16 you cannot legally sign a contract in India.
2. **Fund the business the way a venture capital (VC) investor funds a startup.** Start with a small cheque. Release the next cheque only when you hit a clear target. Never hand over a big sum up front.
3. **Start with a service business, not a product.** In the first 6 weeks of selling, test two or three cheap ideas on a fixed hour budget. Keep the one that gets paying customers fastest. The best default for you is a **tutoring agency for younger IB/IGCSE students**, kept small enough to stay outside India's coaching-centre rules. The backup is a **digital and AI setup service for small businesses in your family's network**.
4. **Targets, with the maths shown in Part 3:** first paying customer by week 4. ₹50,000 a month in revenue by the end of DP1. **₹1 lakh a month in profit by December of DP2.** That is a stretch goal; the realistic base case is ₹50,000–60,000. ₹1 lakh a month is "Level 1" job-replacing money: about what a fresher earns at a good Mumbai job.
5. **Your IB grades come first.** The plan caps your hours, works around the Extended Essay summer, and has a monthly grade check that cuts your hours automatically if marks slip.

## Part 2: The model this plan copies, and where it does not fit

### The solved problem: how a VC fund backs a startup

Venture capital funds solved a hard problem long ago: how do you put money into an unproven founder without wasting it? Their answer has four parts:

1. **Small first cheque.** Seed money, just enough to test the idea.
2. **Milestones.** The next round comes only after measurable proof: paying customers, revenue, growth.
3. **A board.** Someone outside the founder reviews the numbers every month.
4. **Many small bets, kill fast.** Most ideas are dropped early and cheaply. Money flows to the one that works.

Restaurants have a smaller version: **run a pop-up stall before signing a lease.** Test demand cheaply, then commit.

### What this plan takes from it

| VC idea | Your version |
|---|---|
| Seed cheque | ₹50,000 from parents for the 6-week test phase |
| Milestones, then the next round | Four written stages (Part 3). Money for the next stage is released only when the gate is passed |
| Board meeting | A 45-minute monthly review with one parent plus one outside mentor |
| Many bets, kill fast | Two or three ideas tested on a fixed hour budget, with a halfway cut |
| Pop-up before the restaurant | Sell the service by hand before building a website, brand or app |

### Where the model does not fit, and the fix for each

| Where it does not fit | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **The founder is a minor.** | Contracts, bank accounts, payment gateways, Upwork, Fiverr, Stripe and Google AdSense all require age 18. | A parent is the legal owner until 18, with a planned handover (Part 5). |
| **The investor is family.** Parents tend to be soft. | Without pressure, deadlines slip. | A one-page **Family Investment Agreement** (Step 0). A missed gate means no more money. |
| **The founder already has a full-time job.** IB is about 60 hours a week. | Grades decide your university choices. | Hard hour caps, a monthly grade check, and no-work weeks (Part 6). |
| **VCs chase huge exits** and accept that 9 in 10 startups die. | You want steady monthly profit, not a lottery ticket. | **Cash-flow businesses** (services) that earn in month one. No apps, no fundraising at 16. |
| **The founder's time is the bottleneck, not money.** VCs fix this by hiring. | Every test and every stage must fit your weekly hours. | Every stage has an hour budget, and the business is built to run on hired tutors or freelancers plus a manager, not on you. |
| **VCs bring a network.** | You need advice from someone who has run a business. | Your "board" includes a mentor: a business-owning relative or family friend. |

## Part 3: The plan, stage by stage

### Step 0 (Weeks 1–2): Set up the "fund". Budget: ₹0–₹10,000

1. **Write the Family Investment Agreement** (one page, signed by you and both parents). It says:
   - The business is yours in practice, even though a parent is the owner on paper until you turn 18.
   - Money is released only at the gates in this plan.
   - The hour caps and the grade rule (Part 6).
   - **Profit split while you live at home:** 50% reinvested, 30% into long-term savings (a mutual fund folio in your name, run by your guardian), 20% for you to spend. This is a choice about using the money, not a reduction in what the business earns.
   - The maximum total loss your family accepts before you turn 18: ₹3 lakh.
   - How the business moves to you at 18 (Part 5).
2. **Pick a mentor.** Ask one adult who runs a profitable business (not a parent) for a 30-minute call each month for a year.
3. **Get your school calendar.** Write down every internal exam week, IA deadline, Extended Essay deadline and mock exam for DP1 and DP2. These become no-work weeks (Part 6).
4. **Set up money tracking.**
   - The parent who is the legal owner opens a **separate bank account** used only for this business, with a **UPI QR code**.
   - Keep a **Google Sheet** with five tabs: *Sales*, *Costs*, *Customers*, *Hours worked*, *Grades*. Every rupee in or out goes in the same day.
5. **Book the board meeting**: the first Sunday of every month, 45 minutes, with one parent and the mentor (by video is fine). The agenda never changes: revenue, profit, customers, hours, grades, next month's goal.

### Stage 1, the "Pop-up" (Weeks 3–8): test ideas on a fixed hour budget. Budget: up to ₹50,000

You have **6 weeks × 8 hours = 48 hours**. That is the whole budget, so every test gets a fixed share. Do not build websites or logos yet.

| Test | Hours over 6 weeks | Where the hours go |
|---|---|---|
| A: Tutoring | 24 | Outreach 3 h, about 12 sessions × 1 h (trial plus paid), lesson prep 4 h, follow-up messages 2 h, buffer 3 h |
| B: Digital setup | 18 | 10 short intro calls 3 h, 2 sales meetings 2 h, 2 client setups × 5 h = 10 h, admin 3 h |
| C: Your own idea | 6 (or 0) | Only if it is something you already do and can sell in 6 hours. Otherwise skip it and give the 6 hours to A |
| **Total** | **48** | |

**Travel rule:** sessions are online, at your home, or within walking distance. No cross-suburb trips: one Mumbai trip can eat two hours.

**Halfway cut (end of week 5):** any test with **zero** paying or committed customers is dropped. Its remaining hours go to the test that is ahead. You never go above 8 hours a week to "rescue" a test.

**Test A: Tutoring for younger students (the default favourite)**
- *Who pays:* parents of MYP, IGCSE and ICSE students (ages 11–15) in your school's network and your own neighbourhood.
- *What you sell:* weekly one-to-one sessions in a subject you are strong in (Maths, Sciences, English), online or at your home.
- *Price:* ₹1,000–₹1,500 per hour. (Experienced IB tutors in Mumbai charge far more, so a strong DP student is good value.)
- *First customers:* message 20 parents you or your parents know. Script:
  > "Hi [Name], I'm [Your name], in DP1 at [School]. I'm starting weekly Maths sessions for Grade 8–10 students: ₹1,200/hour, online or at my home in [area]. The first session is free. If [child] doesn't find it useful, you pay nothing. Would [child] like to try one this Saturday?"
- *Pass mark:* 3 or more families paying for a second session.

**Test B: Digital setup for small businesses ("Starter package")**
- *Who pays:* small businesses in your family's network: clinics, restaurants, boutiques, CA offices, real-estate brokers.
- *What you sell in the test:* only the **Starter package**, about 5 hours of work: set up or fix their Google Business Profile (photos, hours, categories, reply templates for reviews) and a WhatsApp Business account with catalogue and auto-replies. A one-page website (₹12,000) and an AI assistant for customer questions (₹10,000) are **add-ons you sell later**, not part of the test.
- *Price:* ₹8,000 one-time, plus an optional ₹8,000–₹15,000 a month to manage it.
- *First customers:* ask your parents for 10 introductions. Show each owner a "before vs after" of their own Google listing on a short call.
- *Pass mark:* 2 or more businesses pay for the Starter package.

**Test C: Your own idea (optional)**
- Something you already do well (design, video editing, coding) or understand better than adults (a product for students).
- *Rule:* sellable by hand in 6 hours of your time, with under ₹30,000 of stock or tools.
- *Pass mark:* ₹15,000 or more in sales to people who are not relatives.

**Gate 1 (end of week 8): choose ONE.** Keep the test that scores best on these, in this order:
1. Most **repeat** paying customers.
2. Most **profit per hour you worked** (from the *Hours worked* tab).
3. Which one you would happily work on for two years.

Drop the others completely. If **none** passes, run two new tests for another 6 weeks with a further ₹30,000, on the same 48-hour budget. That is normal: it is what the pop-up stage is for.

### Stage 2, the "Seed round" (Months 3–8, to the end of DP1): build the one business. Budget: up to ₹3 lakh

Goal: turn a working test into a small, repeatable machine that does not depend on your hours. Your 8 hours a week now go to selling, hiring and checking quality, not to teaching every session yourself.

**If you picked tutoring, build a tutoring agency:**
1. Month 3: write a one-page syllabus per subject and a simple monthly progress report for parents (parents pay for visible progress).
2. Month 4: recruit 2–3 tutors: recent IB graduates or university students. Pay them ₹700–₹900 per hour. Charge parents ₹1,300–₹1,800. The difference is your margin.
3. Month 4: switch to **monthly prepaid packages**, e.g. 8 sessions for ₹10,000, paid in advance by UPI.
4. Month 5: a simple Instagram page or website with parent testimonials (with permission). Spend at most ₹15,000.
5. Months 5–8: grow through referrals. Give a free session to any family that brings in a new family.
6. Month 7–8: **hire the part-time operations manager now** (see Stage 3), so the summer is spent on systems, not on hiring.

*Stage 2 maths:* 60 tutor-hours a month (about 8 students on 8-session packages) × ₹1,500 = ₹90,000 revenue. Tutor pay of about ₹48,000 (60 × ₹800) leaves **₹42,000 gross profit**.

**If you picked the digital service:**
1. Turn the Starter setup into a written checklist so anyone can repeat it.
2. Start selling the add-ons (website, AI assistant) to clients who already paid.
3. Hire one freelance designer or developer per project (contracted through your parent's account).
4. Push every client onto a monthly plan. Monthly retainers are what replace a salary.

*Stage 2 maths:* 6 retainers × ₹12,000 = ₹72,000 revenue. Freelancer delivery of about ₹3,000 per retainer (₹18,000) leaves **₹54,000 gross profit**, plus one-off setup fees.

**Gate 2 (around the end of DP1):** hit **all four** to unlock Stage 3:
- ₹50,000 or more in revenue a month, for **3 months running**
- profit margin of at least 30%
- at least 40% of customers are repeat customers
- the grade rule has not paused the business (Part 6)

### Stage 3, the "Series A" (summer between DP1 and DP2, plus DP2 term 1): systems and growth. Budget: up to ₹10 lakh

**Your hours:** 12–15 a week in the summer, then 8 a week in DP2 term 1. The summer is also when most schools want the Extended Essay drafted, IA data collected and university application essays started. So: **Extended Essay and IA work every morning, business only in the afternoon**, and the no-work weeks from your school calendar still apply.

**Must-do (even if you only get half the summer hours):**
1. **The operations manager is running daily work** (a university student or young graduate, ₹15,000–₹25,000 a month, contracted by your parent): scheduling, payments, customer messages, tutor or freelancer timesheets.
2. **The operating manual is written**: how to onboard a customer, how to hire a tutor or freelancer, what to do about a complaint, how to collect payment.

**Do if the hours are there:**
3. **A second offer** for existing customers: exam-season group crash courses (tutoring) or ad management (digital).
4. **A ₹20,000 ad test** on Instagram or Google. Keep spending only if each new customer costs less than one month of their fees.
5. **Formalise the company** once revenue is above about ₹1 lakh a month (Part 5).

**Fallback:** if the summer gives you fewer hours than planned, items 3–4 move into DP2 term 1 and the profit target below slides to the base case. Your Extended Essay does not move.

**Stage 3 target (by December of DP2): ₹1 lakh a month profit before tax.** Here is what that needs.

*Tutoring route, monthly:*

| Line | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| One-to-one sessions | 120 hours × ₹1,600 | ₹1,92,000 |
| Tutor pay, one-to-one | 120 × ₹850 | −₹1,02,000 |
| Small-group sessions | 25 hours × 4 students × ₹600 | ₹60,000 |
| Tutor pay, groups | 25 × ₹1,000 | −₹25,000 |
| Operations manager | | −₹20,000 |
| Software, materials, ads, CA | | −₹5,000 |
| **Profit before tax** | | **₹1,00,000** |

That means about **15 one-to-one students** (8 sessions a month each) plus **6 groups of 4** (about 24 students) = **about 39 students**, taught by **about 6 tutors** working 25 hours a month each. Getting from about 8 students at Gate 2 to 39 by December means adding 4–5 net new students a month for 7 months, through referrals, crash courses for existing families, and the ad test. Group sessions matter: they earn about ₹1,400 an hour of margin against about ₹750 for one-to-one.

*Digital route, monthly:* 10 retainers × ₹15,000 = ₹1,50,000, less freelancer delivery of ₹3,500 each (₹35,000), less the manager (₹15,000) = **₹1,00,000**. That needs 4 more retainers than Gate 2 plus a ₹3,000 price rise, which the add-ons justify.

**Honest range:** ₹1 lakh is a stretch goal. If growth comes at half the pace, you land at **₹50,000–60,000 a month**, which is still the base case and still a strong result. Reaching ₹1 lakh within six months after your exams is also on plan.

### Freeze (January to May of DP2): exams first

- Business time drops to **2 hours a week**: one call with your manager, and the monthly board meeting.
- No new products, no new hires, no new spending.
- Losing some revenue is fine. Your final IB score does not get a second chance.

### Stage 4 (after exams, age 18): you take full control

1. Within the first month after your 18th birthday, move the business into your name (exact steps in Part 5).
2. Choose your path:
   - **University + business:** your manager runs daily operations and you work 8–10 hours a week remotely.
   - **Gap year:** full time on the business, with a target of Level 2 (₹2.5 lakh a month profit).
3. **Bigger family money only now, and only as a loan or equity with terms:** e.g. ₹25–50 lakh for a second city, a physical learning centre for **16+ students only** (DP, A-level, SAT and college prep, registered under the coaching rules), or a product line, released against the same kind of gates.
4. **Your option for later:** once the service business is stable, a product brand (D2C) becomes a sensible next bet.

## Part 4: Why these business types, and what to avoid

**Why services first:** you can earn in week one, they need almost no capital, they teach you sales (the most valuable business skill), and they use your two real advantages: being a strong IB student and having a family network of business owners and well-off parents.

**Avoid these:**

| Idea | Why not, for you, now |
|---|---|
| Stock futures and options (F&O) trading or crypto trading | SEBI's own studies found about 9 out of 10 individual F&O traders lost money. |
| Buying a franchise (café, education brand) | It ties up ₹30 lakh or more in something you cannot legally sign for or run during school hours. |
| Dropshipping cheap imported products | Thin margins, quality complaints, and GST registration is usually needed to sell goods online across states. |
| Building an app and raising money | A VC-style lottery ticket that needs full-time work. |
| A physical coaching centre for under-16s | Barred by the 2024 coaching-centre guidelines (Part 5). |
| Any multi-level marketing (MLM) or "passive income course" | These make money for the person selling the course, not for you. |

## Part 5: Legal, tax and accounts (exact setup)

Confirm each step with a CA. Budget about ₹15,000–₹30,000 a year for one.

### Until you turn 18

1. **Owner:** one parent runs it as a **sole proprietorship** (no registration needed to start). A free **Udyam (MSME) registration** in the parent's name is optional but useful.
2. **Bank:** a separate account in that parent's name, business only, with a UPI QR code.
3. **Platforms:** use your parent's accounts for anything with an 18+ rule (payment gateways, Upwork, Fiverr, Google AdSense, marketplaces). Never lie about your age on any platform.
4. **GST:** for services, registration is needed once yearly turnover passes **₹20 lakh**. The Stage 3 tutoring plan (about ₹2.5 lakh a month, ₹30 lakh a year) **will cross it**, so register as soon as you are on track to pass ₹20 lakh in the year. Ask the CA whether your tutoring qualifies for any education exemption; assume it does not until told otherwise. For physical goods sold online, the rules differ.
5. **Shops and Establishments (Maharashtra):** under the Maharashtra Shops and Establishments Act, 2017, a business with **fewer than 10 workers** does not need full registration, but it **must file an online intimation (Form F)** on the Maharashtra Labour Department portal after it starts. It is free. File it in the parent-owner's name as soon as you engage tutors or a manager, and have the CA confirm it applies to a home-based business.
6. **Coaching-centre rules (important for the tutoring default):** the Ministry of Education's *Guidelines for Registration and Regulation of Coaching Centres* (January 2024) say a coaching centre should **not enrol students under 16** and must register with the state. They define a coaching centre as one that coaches **more than 50 students**. States are asked to adopt them, so ask your CA what Maharashtra currently enforces. This plan's hard rules:
   - Keep **total enrolled students under 50**. The Stage 3 plan needs about 39.
   - Teach one-to-one or small groups **online or in the student's or tutor's home**. No rented classroom for under-16s.
   - Any physical centre (Stage 4) serves **16+ only** and registers before opening.
   - If the rules tighten, shift growth toward 16+ students (DP and A-level subjects, SAT prep), taught by your graduate tutors.
7. **Income tax before 18:** because the business is in your parent's name, its profit is your parent's business income and is taxed at your parent's rate. In a family worth ₹70 crore, that parent is probably in the top 30% slab, plus a surcharge and 4% cess: roughly **31% to 39%** depending on their total income. So ₹1 lakh a month of profit is about **₹61,000–69,000 after tax** while it is in the parent's name. (Separately, a minor's income is generally added to a parent's income under the "clubbing" rule, Section 64(1A) of the old 1961 Act, carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025, from April 2026; ask your CA for the current section.)
8. **Income tax after 18:** in your own name, under the new tax regime as it stood for 2025–26, the rebate makes income up to **₹12 lakh a year** (₹1 lakh a month) close to tax-free. Ask the CA to confirm the current limit. This is the strongest reason to complete the handover promptly at 18.

### When revenue passes about ₹1 lakh a month, or you hire regularly

- Form a **Private Limited Company** (about ₹10,000–₹20,000 through an online CA service). Your **parents are the two directors**. **You hold shares through your guardian**, and the Family Investment Agreement records that the business belongs to you.
- The parent's proprietorship transfers its customers, tutor contracts, domain and social accounts to the company. Doing this before 18 makes your 18th-birthday handover simple.

### The handover at 18: exact steps

**If the company exists (recommended):**
1. Update your PAN from minor to adult (new photo and signature).
2. Get a Digital Signature Certificate and a Director Identification Number.
3. The board appoints you as a director (one board resolution plus a filing with the Registrar of Companies, done by the CA).
4. Your shares already belong to you; the guardian simply stops acting for you.
5. Open a personal bank account in your own name for your salary and dividends from the company.

**If it is still the parent's proprietorship:** a proprietorship cannot be renamed to a new owner. Instead:
1. Update your PAN and open a bank account in your own name.
2. Start your own proprietorship or, better, incorporate a company with you as director.
3. Your parent transfers the business assets (client list, domain, social accounts, materials) to you with a simple written gift or business-transfer letter drafted by the CA. A gift from a parent to an adult child is generally not taxable for the child.
4. Register for GST and Udyam in your name (if needed), and have the parent cancel theirs.
5. Re-sign tutor and freelancer contracts in your name, and send customers the new UPI QR code.

## Part 6: Protecting your IB grades

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Weekly cap** | 8 hours in term (Saturday 3 h, Sunday 3 h, two 1-hour weekday slots). 12–15 hours in the DP1–DP2 summer, afternoons only. 2 hours from January of DP2 until exams. |
| **Baseline** | Your grade (1–7) in each subject on your first DP1 term report. Until that report arrives, use your MYP or IGCSE final grades. |
| **Monthly grade check** | At each board meeting, enter in the *Grades* tab every test or assessment mark returned that month, converted to the 1–7 scale your teachers use. If **any subject's monthly average is 1 point or more below its baseline**, business hours **halve** next month. If it happens two months running, or in two subjects at once, the business **pauses** (manager keeps it running) until the subject is back at baseline. A missed school deadline counts the same as a 1-point drop. |
| **Predicted-grade check** | When your school issues predicted grades (usually end of DP1 or early DP2), if your predicted total is below the total your target universities ask for, the Stage 3 "do if hours are there" items are cancelled. |
| **No-work weeks** | Every internal exam week, mock exam, and the week of and before any IA or Extended Essay deadline, all taken from the school calendar in Step 0. |
| **Use it in school, honestly** | Your Business Management or Economics coursework may draw on what you learn. Ask your teachers what is allowed. Do not count paid work as CAS. |
