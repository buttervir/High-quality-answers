ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Most of the "₹1 lakh/month" is not the student's money under the plan's own legal set-up
Where: Section 6 Stage 3 row ("Commissions → proprietorship current account ... Agency profit is the parent's business income ... management fee ... e.g. ₹40,000–60,000/month") vs Section 8 column "Net to you/business" and the headline "₹1,00,000/month net profit by September–December 2027".
Problem: The task's goal is for the student to earn "job replacing money". In the Dec 2027 target month, ₹82,500 of the ₹1,14,500 is commission. Section 6 routes that commission to the parent's sole proprietorship as the parent's income. The student's own money is then own teaching (₹32,000) plus a management fee capped at ₹40,000–60,000, which totals ₹72,000–92,000. That is below the plan's own ₹1L definition. The table hides this by merging "you" and "business" into one column. The management fee is also a payment from a parent's business to the parent's own minor child for about 6 h/week, which works out to roughly ₹1,500–2,300/hour for admin work. That is exactly the kind of related-party payment a CA or assessing officer can disallow as excessive. If it is disallowed, the clubbing protection the plan relies on fails, and still more of the income is legally the parent's, not the student's.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The revenue model needs more hours than the plan's own hour caps allow, from Sep 2027 onward
Where: Section 8 rows Sep 2027, Dec 2027 ("5" own hours, ₹32,000) and Jan–Mar 2028 ("2" hours, ₹13,000) vs Section 9 ("Sep–Dec 2027 ... you manage, not teach ... 6"; "Jan–Feb 2028 ... Tutors only; you do payments and Sunday review ... 2"; "Mar–May 2028 ... 1").
Problem: In Jan–Mar 2028, ₹13,000 = 2 h × ₹1,500 × 4.3. So the whole 2-hour cap is spent teaching, which leaves zero hours for running 8 tutors and roughly 30+ students: assigning leads, weekly session confirmations to every parent (the plan's only control against unpaid commission), collecting commissions on the 1st, and the Sunday review. In Sep–Dec 2027 the plan has 5 h of teaching plus management of 5–8 tutors and recruiting from 5 to 8 tutors, all inside a 6-hour cap that the same row labels "you manage, not teach". Mar–May 2028 is capped at 1 h/week, yet Section 8 still books ₹13,000 of own teaching for March. Either the caps are broken, which is the grade-risk the plan says is "non-negotiable", or the own-teaching revenue and the commission controls disappear.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The target is not met by the plan's own definition once its own costs are counted
Where: Assumptions ("₹1,00,000/month net, kept up for 3 or more months"); Section 8 ("Costs are about ₹6,000–8,000/month from Stage 3 ... Net figures are before those costs"); Gate 3 ("₹1,00,000 net/month in at least one month").
Problem: After the stated costs, the modelled months are: Sep 2027 ₹86,000–88,000, Dec 2027 ₹1,06,500–1,08,500, Jan–Mar 2028 ₹98,000–1,00,000. No run of 3 consecutive months reaches ₹1L net. If you also remove the ₹13,000 of own teaching that Attack 2 shows cannot fit in a 2-hour cap, Jan–Mar falls to about ₹85,000–87,000. Gate 3 quietly lowers the bar to "at least one month", so the plan can declare success without ever meeting its own definition of "job replacing money".

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The bootcamp's money flow contradicts the commission model and the tax wall
Where: Section 5 "Summer product" ("₹1.92 lakh, less ~40% tutor pay → ≈ ₹1.15 lakh net") and Section 8 Jun 2027 ("₹1,15,000 (bootcamp net)" under "Your revenue" plus "2 tutors, 20 tutor hours/week, ₹41,000" commission).
Problem: Under the commission model, parents pay tutors and tutors remit 30%. The bootcamp reverses this: someone collects ₹1.92L and pays tutors 60%, and the plan never says who. If the money goes into the student's minor account, as "Your revenue" implies, then the student's account is receiving and paying out other people's labour. That is not income from the student's "own skill", so it is clubbed with the parent's income, and it breaks the rule in Section 2 that the account must hold only the student's own tutoring fees. If it goes to the proprietorship instead, it is the parent's income, not "your revenue". There is also a double count. The same 2 tutors are teaching bootcamp batches and are also booked for 20 h/week of regular commission tutoring, and the student is booked at 20 h/week of business on top of "EE first: 2 hours/day". June's ₹1,56,000 is therefore overstated.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] No bridge from Gate 1 (3 students) to the January model (about 20 students)
Where: Gate 1 ("3+ paying students and ₹15,000+ collected → go to Stage 2") vs Section 8 Jan 2027 ("6 (2 h 1:1 + 4 h group) ... ₹51,000"), which needs 2 group batches × 4 students = 8 group students paying ₹600/h plus 1:1 students.
Problem: Take a user who passes Gate 1 on 6 Dec with exactly 3 students. In January they need at least 8 group enrolments plus 1:1 students, which is almost 3 times their customer base, within about 3 weeks that include the school winter break. Stage 2 lists price rises, results posts and week-4 referrals, but none of these produces 8+ new students by January. Neither does a referral loop that by then has run only about 4 weeks. The step that fills the "Board Exam Sprint" batches (who is told, when, with what offer) is missing. So the plan's Jan–Mar revenue, and Gate 2's "₹35,000+ average", cannot be acted on without guessing.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The plan contradicts itself on recruiting from the student's own school
Where: Stage 1 source 2 ("or the junior section of your current one ... Younger grades are fine") vs Section 11 ("never tutor at your school or its students").
Problem: One section tells the user to market to and tutor students at their current school's junior section, and the kill-rule table forbids tutoring any of that school's students. A user following Stage 1 would breach the plan's own risk rule in week 2, and that is the rule the DP Coordinator email in Stage 0 is meant to protect.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] There is no response if Gate 3 fails
Where: Gate 3 ("31 Dec 2027: ₹1,00,000 net/month in at least one month, 6+ active tutors, no safeguarding or integrity incidents") and Section 11.
Problem: Gates 1 and 2 each have a fail branch. Gate 3, the gate the target depends on, has none. The plan's own bear case (about ₹55,000/month by Dec 2027) is a fail, and it lands right before DP2 mocks and finals, when the plan caps business time at 1–2 h/week. The user is not told whether to keep hiring, freeze, or wind down, or what happens to the ₹70,000 "Scale" release that is tied to passing this gate.
