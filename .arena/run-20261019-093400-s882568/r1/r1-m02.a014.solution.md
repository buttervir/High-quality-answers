# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

This is for a 16-year-old in DP1 in Mumbai whose family is worth about US$8M (about ₹70 crore). The goal is to start a business that earns enough to replace a salary. The plan starts from your real situation: you have little free time, you are a minor under Indian law, money is not your problem, and your most valuable asset is something most founders don't have. That asset is direct access to Mumbai's IB families.

---

## Assumptions

1. **Timeline.** You started DP1 around June to August 2026, and your final IB exams are in **May 2028**. Being 16 in October 2026 means you turn 18 somewhere between **October 2027 and October 2028**. Nothing in this plan depends on exactly when.
2. **"Job-replacing money"** means **₹1,00,000 a month in net profit before income tax, sustained**, measured as a 6-month rolling average so one seasonal month doesn't decide it. That is roughly the take-home pay of a good first job in Mumbai (₹12–15 LPA CTC). It must come from a business, not from family money moved into your name.
3. **The IB Diploma comes first.** A plan that costs you 5–7 points has failed, because that drop changes which universities you can get into, and that is worth more than any revenue in the first two years.
4. **Your parents will help** with paperwork that needs an adult (contracts, the payment gateway, bank accounts) and with a capped amount of starting capital.
5. **You are a strong student**, predicted 38 or more, or very strong in two or three subjects. If you aren't, use Path B in Section 7. The legal setup, the time plan and the money rules stay the same.
6. **All prices quoted to parents are GST-inclusive.** All margins below are calculated *after* 18% GST and payment-gateway fees, so they hold even once the business is GST-registered.

## 1. Ten questions to answer before starting

**Q1. What does the 16-year-old actually do on Monday?**
They go to school, have IAs, the Extended Essay, TOK and CAS ahead of them, and realistically have **6–8 hours a week** during term. In holidays they have **25–30 hours a week**. Any plan that needs 20 hours a week during term will fail by December.
→ *Effect on the plan: the business must run in small blocks of time, follow the IB calendar, and be able to keep going while you study for exams.*

**Q2. Is capital the bottleneck?**
No. Your family could fund almost any small business. That is a risk in itself. Founders with too much money hide a bad idea behind spending (a fancy brand, an office, ads) and never learn whether customers will pay.
→ *Effect: family money comes in small amounts released only when you hit milestones (Section 6). Your first rupee of revenue must come before any real spending.*

**Q3. What do you have that a 30-year-old founder doesn't?**
Three things: you are inside the IB system right now, your parents' network is affluent Mumbai families, and you know exactly what an IB student struggles with because you are one.
→ *Effect: build where these give you an edge. That points to education services for IB, IGCSE and MYP students in Mumbai, a market where parents routinely pay ₹1,500–₹5,000 an hour.*

**Q4. What does Indian law let a 16-year-old do?**
- A minor **cannot enter a binding contract** (Indian Contract Act 1872, Section 11). Any contract you sign as a minor is void, not just voidable. So vendor agreements, tutor contracts and payment-gateway accounts must be in an adult's name.
- A minor **cannot be a company director or a partner in an LLP**. A minor **can hold shares** in a private limited company through a guardian, and **can be admitted to the benefits** of a partnership firm (Indian Partnership Act, Section 30).
- A minor **can have a PAN** and a bank account. Under RBI's April 2025 rules, banks may let minors aged 10 and over open and run savings accounts on their own.
- **Tax clubbing:** a minor's income is normally added to the income of the parent who earns more (Income-tax Act 1961, Section 64(1A); the same rule continues under the Income-tax Act 2025 from 1 April 2026). **The exception is income from your own manual work or from your skill, talent or specialised knowledge.** Tutoring you deliver yourself is taxed as *your* income, so you get your own basic exemption. Profit from an agency where other people do the teaching, and dividends on shares you hold before 18, are likely to be clubbed with your parent's income.
- **GST:** private coaching is a taxable service at **18%** (the education exemption covers recognised institutions, not private tutoring). Registration is compulsory once a person's aggregate turnover in a financial year passes **₹20 lakh**, and turnover is aggregated **by PAN**, across every business that person runs.
→ *Effect: you earn personally first, and a parent-owned business acts as the legal wrapper while you are under 18. Ownership moves to a company on a fixed date (Section 5). All pricing is GST-inclusive from day one.*

**Q5. How much does a business have to earn to pay ₹1 lakh a month in profit?**
Selling one hour of your own time at ₹1,100 an hour, you would need about 90 hours a month. That is impossible during the DP. So the plan must move from **selling your own hours** to **earning a margin on other people's hours**. After GST and fees, that margin is about ₹490 an hour on MYP/IGCSE and about ₹980 an hour on DP (Section 2). With about ₹60,000 a month in fixed costs, ₹1 lakh net needs roughly **225 tutor-hours a month**.
→ *Effect: the plan has three stages: solo tutor → tutoring agency → a business run by a manager, with courses and bootcamps as extra upside.*

**Q6. Could family money simply "replace the job"?**
Yes. ₹1.5 crore in debt funds or fixed deposits at about 7% pays out about ₹87,000 a month. But that is a gift, not a business, and income from money a parent gives a minor is clubbed with the parent's income anyway. It doesn't answer the question that was asked, so this plan doesn't use it. It is still worth saying out loud: **your safety net is already there, so you can afford to aim for learning and growth rather than quick cash.**

**Q7. What would get you into real trouble?**
- **IB academic misconduct.** Writing or "editing" someone else's IA, EE or TOK essay for money breaks IB rules and can get *your* Diploma withdrawn. The business never touches graded coursework (Section 9).
- **Child safety.** Your customers are children, so you need safeguarding rules and checks on every tutor.
- **School policy.** Some schools ban students from tutoring their own classmates for money. Check before you start.

**Q8. What happens when you leave for university in August 2028?**
A **US F-1** visa restricts off-campus employment, and a **UK Student visa** prohibits self-employment and business activity. Generally, **passive ownership** (holding shares and receiving dividends) is allowed. **Running the business** (setting prices, hiring, managing staff) is generally not. So from August 2028:
- You are a **shareholder only**. You read a monthly report, approve the annual budget as a shareholder, and receive dividends.
- **Pricing and hiring authority** within written bands is delegated to a promoted **operations manager** (Phase 3).
- **A parent-director** decides anything outside those bands, signs contracts and controls the bank account.
- Any hands-on work happens only when you are **physically in India during vacations**, and only after an immigration adviser for your destination country has confirmed it in writing.
→ *Effect: the business must have a real decision-maker other than you by June 2028. That is built into Phase 3.*

**Q9. Will this help or hurt your university applications?**
A real business with real customers, hiring and real numbers makes a strong application essay or activity entry. A business funded by your parents that has no customers looks bad. Revenue gives you credibility.

**Q10. What does success look like at each checkpoint?**
- **31 March 2027:** 8 or more paying students, ₹25,000+ a month (solo).
- **31 August 2027:** an agency with 8+ active tutors, about 150 billed hours a month, ₹85,000+ a month in net margin.
- **30 June 2028:** **₹1,00,000+ a month net profit, averaged over January–June 2028, from tutoring alone**, after GST, with a manager running daily operations. Courses and bootcamps don't count toward this test.

## 2. The recommended business: an IB/IGCSE/MYP tutoring agency in Mumbai, called "[YourName] Learning"

**Why this business, and not a D2C brand, an app or a café:**

| Test | Tutoring agency | D2C/e-commerce brand | App/SaaS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue in your first month? | Yes | Rarely | No |
| Starting capital | Under ₹50,000 | ₹5–20 lakh | ₹5–30 lakh |
| Uses your IB knowledge and network | Strongly | Weakly | Weakly |
| Works in 6–8 hrs a week | Yes | No (stock, logistics) | No |
| Path to ₹1 lakh/month in 18 months | Realistic | Unlikely | Very unlikely |
| Can run while you're abroad | Yes, with a manager | Partly | Partly |

**Who buys:** parents of MYP, IGCSE and DP students at Mumbai's international-curriculum schools, for example Dhirubhai Ambani International School (BKC), Ecole Mondiale (Juhu), Oberoi International (Goregaon/JVLR), Aditya Birla World Academy (Tardeo) and Jamnabai Narsee International (Juhu), plus families studying online across India.
**What sells:** IB Maths AA/AI, Physics, Chemistry, Economics and English, and especially **"a tutor who just did this exact syllabus"**.

### The unit economics (one table, used everywhere in this plan)

| Level | Price to parent (GST-inclusive) | Less 18% GST (÷1.18) | Less gateway ~2% | Tutor pay | **Margin per hour** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MYP / IGCSE | ₹1,800 | ₹1,525 | –₹36 | ₹1,000 (band ₹900–₹1,100) | **≈ ₹490** |
| DP1 / DP2 | ₹3,000 | ₹2,542 | –₹60 | ₹1,500 (band ₹1,400–₹1,800) | **≈ ₹980** (₹680 at the top of the pay band) |

Rules that keep the table true:
- **Pay bands never overlap price bands.** The highest pay (₹1,800, DP only, for experienced teachers) still leaves at least ₹680 an hour after GST. MYP/IGCSE tutors are never paid more than ₹1,100.
- **Before GST registration** the margin is higher (about ₹760 MYP and ₹1,440 DP), because there is no GST to pay out. The plan never relies on that extra margin. It is a cushion.
- **Your monthly profit formula:** `Net = 490 × (MYP/IGCSE hours) + 980 × (DP hours) − fixed costs`. Check it monthly in your sheet.

## 3. The plan, phase by phase with exact steps

### Phase 0: Setup (2 weeks, before 15 November 2026). About 8 hours in total

1. **The family meeting (1 hour).** Agree, and write on one page:
   - which parent is your "legal partner" (they sign contracts and own the payment account). **Choose a parent who doesn't already run a sole-proprietorship business under their own PAN.** GST turnover is aggregated by PAN, so an existing proprietorship would push the tutoring business into GST from day one and mix the accounts. If both parents run proprietorships, ask the CA about a separate partnership firm (Section 5).
   - the capital rules (Section 6),
   - a rule that your **predicted grades stay at or above** a number you agree (for example 38), or business hours drop to 3 a week until they recover.
2. **Check school policy.** Email your DP coordinator: *"Is it permitted for a DP student to tutor younger students from outside school, or from our school, for a fee?"* Keep the reply.
3. **Pick your subjects.** Choose only the 2 subjects where your marks are strongest (7s, or high 6s). Tutor **MYP 4–5 and IGCSE students**, who are 1–2 years behind you. Don't tutor DP students yourself.
4. **Paperwork. All of it is free or cheap:**
   - Get a PAN in your own name if you don't have one (Form 49A through NSDL/UTIITSL, about ₹107).
   - Open a **minor savings account with UPI** at a bank you choose (for example HDFC, ICICI or Kotak youth accounts). Personal tutoring fees go here.
   - Keep a simple income register in Google Sheets (date, client, hours, amount, mode of payment). You need it for tax.
5. **Safeguarding rules. Write them now:** sessions happen in the student's home with a parent at home, at a public place, or online with recording and the parents' consent. Never behind a closed door with a child alone. No personal social-media contact with students. Parents get every message.
6. **Set your price:** **₹1,100 an hour** for MYP/IGCSE (cheaper than adult IB tutors, who charge ₹2,500–₹5,000). Sell a **pack of 8 hours paid upfront** at ₹8,800. Never charge by the single session. Your personal income is far below ₹20 lakh, so you don't need GST.

### Phase 1: Solo tutor (15 Nov 2026 – 31 Mar 2027). 6 hours a week
**Goal: 8 paying students, ₹25,000+ a month, 10 written testimonials.**

| Week | Exact action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Write a 1-page PDF profile in Canva: name, school, predicted grades, subjects, the 8-hour pack, the safeguarding rules, and your parent's phone number. |
| 1 | Your parents send it to 30 families they know who have children aged 12–15 on IB/IGCSE, using a personal message, not a group blast. Script: *"My son/daughter is in DP1 and scoring 7s in Maths. He's tutoring a few MYP/IGCSE kids. Would X like a free trial session?"* |
| 2 | Offer a **free 45-minute diagnostic session**. Turn at least 1 in 3 of these into a paid 8-hour pack. |
| 3–4 | Make a free **"MYP 5 eAssessment Maths: 20 most-tested questions"** PDF. Give it out through parent WhatsApp groups, with permission. Collect phone numbers. |
| 5–8 | After every 4th session, send parents a 3-line progress note. This is what drives referrals. |
| 9–16 | Ask every happy parent for **one referral and one written testimonial**. Add a referral reward: 1 free hour for each family referred. Keep a waiting list. That list is Phase 2's first customers. |

**Numbers at the end of the phase:** 8 students × about 3 hrs a month × ₹1,100 ≈ **₹26,400 a month**, from about 6 hours a week of teaching.
**Tax:** this is income from your own skill, so it is assessed in your hands. Up to the basic exemption limit you will owe little or nothing. Your family's CA should file an ITR in your name.

### Phase 2: Become an agency (1 Apr 2027 – 31 Aug 2027). 6 hrs a week in term, 25 hrs a week in the May–July holidays
**Goal: 8+ active tutors, about 150 billed hours a month, ₹85,000+ a month net margin, and a tutor bench that survives September.**

1. **Set up the legal wrapper (April 2027).** Your parent runs the business as a **sole proprietorship** under the trade name. India has no single registration that *creates* a proprietorship, so you assemble proof of the business instead. Get:
   - **Udyam registration** (free, online, Aadhaar-based; optional, but useful as proof),
   - **Maharashtra Shops & Establishments intimation** (under the 2017 Act, a business with fewer than 10 workers files an intimation online and receives an acknowledgement; free or nominal),
   - with these two documents, open a **current account** in the trade name and a **payment gateway** (Razorpay or Cashfree) in the parent's name.
   - *Don't form a company yet.* A Pvt Ltd costs about ₹15–25k to set up plus ₹50k+ a year in compliance. It gets formed on a fixed date in July 2028 (Section 5).
2. **GST: a rule, not a guess.** Prices are already GST-inclusive. On your plan's numbers, billings run at about ₹3.4 lakh a month by August 2027, so the financial year's running total reaches **₹15 lakh around September 2027**. At that point the CA applies for GST registration, which is compulsory within 30 days of passing ₹20 lakh. Expect to be registered by about **October–November 2027**. From then on the CA files the returns (GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, monthly or under the quarterly QRMP scheme), which is included in the ₹5,000 a month accounting cost. Parents see no price change, because GST was always inside the price.
3. **Recruit tutors (April–May 2027), sized for September, not for summer.** Fresh DP2 graduates are excellent but leave in August–September. **Rule: tutors leaving Mumbai within 6 months may hold at most one third of contracted hours.** Recruit in this order:
   1. **3–4 IB-trained teachers** (current or former, wanting evening and weekend income): for DP subjects, paid ₹1,500–₹1,800. They are the stable core.
   2. **3–4 IB/IGCSE alumni at Mumbai colleges** (for example IIT Bombay, ICT, St. Xavier's, Jai Hind, HR, Mithibai, NMIMS): for MYP/IGCSE, paid ₹900–₹1,100. They stay in the city for 3–4 years.
   3. **2–3 fresh DP2 graduates (40+)** for summer demand and the bootcamp. Those going to Indian universities can continue online after August.
   - Screening: transcripts, a 30-minute demo lesson you watch, two references, a signed safeguarding code, and a police verification certificate (PCC, through the Maharashtra Police online portal) for anyone over 18.
   - Contract: a simple **independent contractor agreement**, signed by your *parent* for the proprietorship (have the CA or a lawyer draft it once, for ₹5–10k). It includes a **non-solicit clause** (no taking your clients directly for 12 months) and the **"no graded work" clause** (Section 9).
4. **Second recruiting round (July 2027) for September.** Replace every departing tutor before they leave. Target **12 tutors on the books for about 150–200 hours**, so you have a **25% bench** above booked hours. Ask departing graduates to refer their juniors at Mumbai colleges, and pay ₹2,000 for each referral who stays 3 months.
5. **Sell DP-level tutoring now** at **₹3,000 an hour**, using teachers and 42+ scorers. MYP/IGCSE is ₹1,800 an hour. Both are sold as 8-hour packs paid upfront. Your own solo students move to the agency price at their next pack renewal, with your parent's approval of the message.
6. **Holiday product (mid-June–July 2027): the "DP Head Start" bootcamp.** It runs 2 weeks, 3 hours a day, for students about to start DP1, covering Maths AA/AI basics, how IAs work, and TOK in plain English. 15 students × ₹18,000 = ₹2.7 lakh in revenue, taught by 2 tutors (two-adult rule). Venue: a society clubhouse or a co-working room (₹1,500–₹3,000 a day), or run it online. This is upside. The monthly targets do not depend on it.
7. **Systems (keep them minimal):** Google Sheets for students, tutors, hours, invoices and the GST running total; WhatsApp Business with one number, owned by the business, that your parent can see; Google Calendar for bookings; monthly invoices sent from the proprietorship.

**Phase 2 maths (August 2027), shown at post-GST margins to be safe:** 90 MYP/IGCSE hours × ₹490 + 60 DP hours × ₹980 = ₹44,100 + ₹58,800 = **₹1,02,900**, less about ₹15,000 in costs (accounting, software, small marketing) ≈ **₹88,000 a month**. Before GST registration the real figure is higher, at about ₹1.4 lakh. This income belongs to the proprietorship, so it is your parent's taxable income, **not yours yet**. Section 5 explains how it becomes yours.

### Phase 3: Make it run without you (Sep 2027 – Jun 2028). 4 hrs a week, dropping to 2 in exam season
**Goal: ₹1,00,000+ a month net profit (Jan–Jun 2028 average, tutoring only), and a manager who can run the business with no input from you.**

DP2 is your hardest year: the EE and IAs are due, university applications go in from October 2027 to January 2028, then mocks and finals. So:
1. **Hire a part-time operations coordinator (September 2027)** for ₹25,000 a month: a graduate or an experienced tutor who handles matching, scheduling, invoicing and parent calls. Once they are on payroll, your parent registers for **Maharashtra Professional Tax** as an employer (the CA does it).
2. **Promote them to operations manager (January 2028)** at ₹40,000 a month plus **5% of monthly net profit**, with a written **delegation of authority**:
   - can hire tutors within the pay bands in Section 2, after the screening checklist,
   - can set prices within ±15% of the price table,
   - can spend up to ₹25,000 a month on marketing,
   - anything outside these limits goes to the parent-director.
   From January 2028 your own role is a **1-hour weekly review of the numbers**, dropping to a monthly review during May finals. This is practice for August 2028, when you step back fully.
3. **Raise prices once**, by 10–15%, in June 2028 for the new academic year, but only if there is a waiting list. All the numbers below use the current prices.
4. **Keep the tutor bench at 25% above booked hours** through every intake (July and December).
5. **Never do this:** "IA/EE writing help", "assignment help", or anything that produces graded work. Your offer is teaching, past-paper practice and feedback on the student's own understanding, and it says so in writing.

**Target, January–June 2028 average, tutoring only (fully GST-registered):**
- 150 MYP/IGCSE hours × ₹490 = ₹73,500
- 150 DP hours × ₹980 = ₹1,47,000
- **Contribution: ₹2,20,500**
- Minus fixed costs: manager ₹40,000 + accounting/GST ₹5,000 + software and phone ₹5,000 + marketing ₹10,000 = ₹60,000
- Minus the manager's 5% profit share ≈ ₹8,000
- **≈ ₹1,52,000 a month net profit before income tax.** That is 300 hours a month, from about 12 tutors × 25 hours and about 38 students × 8 hours.
- **Break-even for the ₹1 lakh target: about 225 hours a month** at a 50/50 mix. If January–June averages fewer than 225 hours, the target is missed. The fix is more DP hours (each one is worth 2 MYP hours), not more spending.
- June is a seasonal low (MYP and DP exam classes end). That is why the test is a 6-month average, not June alone.

**Upside, not counted in the target:**
- **DP Head Start bootcamp, 2028:** 2–3 batches from **mid-June 2028**, after your finals, run by the manager and 2 tutors per batch. That brings about ₹6–8 lakh in revenue (about ₹5–6.8 lakh after GST). Your role is to approve the plan in April, in 1 hour.
- **"IB Maths AA SL Paper 1 & 2 crash course":** 20 videos plus worked past-paper solutions you write yourself, never IB copyrighted material. You record it in **June–July 2028**, after your exams, and it goes on sale from **August 2028** at ₹4,999 (GST-inclusive) through Graphy, Teachable or Gumroad. The account belongs to the business, not to you.

## 5. How ownership moves to you (fixed dates, not your birthday)

| When | Structure | Whose income for tax |
|---|---|---|
| Now – Mar 2027 | You as an individual, personal tutoring | **Yours** (skill income, not clubbed) |
| Apr 2027 – Jun 2028 | Parent's sole proprietorship (trade name) | Parent's |
| **July 2028, whatever your age** | **Private Limited company.** Directors: both parents (or a parent plus another trusted adult). Shareholders: **you 90%** (held through your guardian if you are still under 18), parent 10%. The proprietorship transfers the business to the company under a **business transfer agreement** drafted by the CA, and GST registration moves to the company. | The company's. Dividends to you are clubbed with your parent's income until you turn 18, and are yours after that |
| Your 18th birthday (between Oct 2027 and Oct 2028) | The shares move fully into your own name. You become a **director only if** your immigration adviser confirms in writing that it is compatible with your visa. Otherwise you stay a shareholder only until you are back in India. | Dividends are yours |

Why July 2028: your exams are over, you have about 30 free hours a week for the paperwork, and the business is big enough (₹80 lakh+ a year in billings) that company compliance costs are clearly worth it. If one parent can't be the proprietor (see Phase 0 step 1), ask the CA about a **partnership firm** between the parents, with you **admitted to the benefits** as a minor, from April 2027 instead.

Either way, keep a **dated family memo**, signed now, recording that you founded and run the business and that the 90% shareholding is intended for you. It avoids arguments later.

## 6. How to use the family's money (rules, not amounts)

The risk is not losing the money. The risk is spending your way past the lessons. Rules:

| Tranche | Amount | Unlocked when | Allowed uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ₹50,000 | Now | Canva Pro, printing, a Google Workspace domain, CA setup fees, a laptop upgrade if needed |
| 2 | ₹2,00,000 | 8 paying students reached | Lawyer-drafted contracts, bootcamp venue deposit, a simple website (Wix/Framer, ₹15–30k), small Meta ads tests (₹20k max) |
| 3 | ₹5,00,000 | ₹60k/month net margin for 2 months in a row | Coordinator and manager salary runway (6 months), course production, a better LMS, Pvt Ltd incorporation |

- No office, no agency-built app and no paid "brand launch" until net profit passes ₹1.5 lakh a month for 3 months.
- Record family money as a **documented loan** with a simple letter (interest-free, repaid from profits). Repaying it is part of learning the game. Ask the CA about gift and loan treatment.
- In parallel, separate from the business: ask your parents to open a **₹5,000–₹10,000 a month SIP in an index fund** in your name, so you learn about compounding. It is not part of the business case.

## 7. Path B, if tutoring is wrong for you

Use it if your grades aren't top-band, you don't enjoy teaching, or your school forbids tutoring.

**"Mumbai SMB automation and web studio"**: build WhatsApp Business catalogues, Google Business Profiles, simple websites and AI-chatbot or booking setups for local clinics, salons, CA firms and restaurants in your area.
- **Weeks 1–6:** learn Framer or Webflow, WhatsApp Business API basics (through Interakt, WATI or similar) and Google Business Profile, using free tutorials (about 4 hrs a week). Build 3 free sample projects for family businesses or friends' families.
- **Price:** setup ₹15,000–₹40,000, plus a **monthly maintenance retainer of ₹3,000–₹8,000**. These clients are businesses, so once you are GST-registered, quote **plus 18% GST**. They can claim it back, so your margin doesn't shrink. The retainer is the part that replaces a salary: 20 clients × ₹5,000 = ₹1 lakh a month, less any freelancer costs.
- **Selling:** visit 5 businesses every Saturday in Bandra, Andheri or Powai with a parent or friend, carrying a printed "before/after" mock-up of *their* Google listing. Your parents' business network is your first 10 clients.
- **Legal and tax:** the same as Section 5 and Phase 2 step 1. Work you do yourself is skill income; once you subcontract freelancers, it is agency income under the parent's proprietorship.
- **Timeline:** the same phases, the same IB calendar limits, and the same rule that you become a passive owner in August 2028.

## 9. Risk register

| Risk | What you do |
|---|---|
| IB academic misconduct (helping with coursework) | A written "no graded work" policy in every tutor contract, and tutors who break it are removed immediately |
| A child safety incident | Safeguarding code, PCC checks, parents present or sessions recorded, two-adult rule for bootcamps |
| GST cuts into margin | All prices GST-inclusive and all margins calculated after GST; register at ₹15 lakh; check the running total monthly |
| The tutor pool leaves for university | At most a third of hours with tutors leaving within 6 months; recruit in April and July; keep a 25% bench; referral bonus |
| Tutors take clients directly | A non-solicit clause, the business owns the client WhatsApp number, and tutors get a share of rebookings to keep them loyal |
| Seasonality (demand drops in June and peaks Feb–Apr) | Target measured as a 6-month average; summer bootcamp and course fill the low months |
