ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The batch model ignores that about half the students leave every June, and mixed curricula break the weekly routine
Where: Section 1 "3 batches of 4 students each in Grade 9–10 Maths (MYP 4–5 / IGCSE)"; step 11 "Tuesday: teach the topic the school is covering this week"; step 15 "June 2027 is the main intake"; the "waitlist of at least 4 families".
Problem: Grade 10 students sit IGCSE/MYP eAssessment in May–June and then move to DP1/Grade 11, so they leave a Grade 9–10 class. Counterexample: Batch 1 fills in Dec 2026 with 2 Grade 10s and 2 Grade 9s. In June 2027 the 2 Grade 10s leave, at the same time as the plan tries to open Batch 3. In the proof window (Mar–May 2028), every Grade 10 student stops after their May exams, so the ₹90k-for-3-months test fails just as finals start. A 4-family waitlist cannot cover roughly 6 students leaving at once. The routine also fails for a mixed batch: 4 students from different schools, on IGCSE and MYP, in Grade 9 and Grade 10, are not "covering" the same topic in any given week, and MYP has no past-paper bank that matches IGCSE for the Thursday session. The plan never says whether to group batches by grade or by curriculum, or how to replace the students who leave each year.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The fallback pricing breaks the plan's own hard limits
Where: Step 10 "drop to ₹6,000. ₹6,000 × 16 students (4 batches) still gives ₹96,000, but it costs 2 more hours a week"; Section 4 "Weekly time budget (never exceed it)", total 8; step 17 "do not open Batch 4 during DP2".
Problem: 4 batches × 2 hrs = 8 teaching hours, plus prep and admin, comes to at least 10 hours. That breaks the "never exceed" 8-hour cap. Keeping the 4th batch into DP2 also breaks the "no Batch 4 during DP2" freeze. The substitute cost in step 18 (24 hrs) becomes 32 hrs, or ₹16,000. So the only fallback offered when demand is weak contradicts the plan's own rules. A user who reaches that branch is left with no valid path.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] It produces no job-replacing income past May 2028, the month the student most likely leaves for university
Where: Section 1 "The finished result (May 2028)"; Section 6 "Once 18, the same business scales…".
Problem: The task asks for "job replacing money", meaning income that replaces a job. Every rupee here depends on the student teaching weekday 6–7 pm IST slots, with a substitute only for exam months. The plan ends at May 2028, right before the student starts university. That is often abroad for an IB student from a family worth $8M, which brings time-zone, workload and possibly visa limits on work. Section 6 offers only four bullets: no step for handing the batches over permanently, no answer on who runs operations or collects fees once the student is away, and no figure for what the student would personally net after tutor pay. So the plan delivers a 3-month proof point, not an income that keeps replacing a job.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] A self-operated minor's account will probably hit bank limits at ₹96k/month
Where: Step 2 "open a minor's savings account that the student operates alone… All fees go here".
Problem: RBI's 2014 circular lets minors over 10 operate accounts independently, but it leaves balance and transaction limits to each bank. Kids'/junior accounts (e.g. HDFC Kids Advantage, ICICI Young Stars, Kotak My Junior) usually cap daily transactions and UPI and limit independent operation. ₹11.5 lakh a year coming in, including 12 UPI/NEFT payments of ₹8,000 on the same day (the 1st), can trip those caps or trigger KYC/purpose reviews on a minor's account. The plan gives no check ("ask the bank for the inflow/balance cap before opening") and no fallback, such as a guardian-operated account with records kept in the student's name.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] No branch for the most likely school answer
Where: Step 6 "ask the school's DP coordinator whether students may run paid tutoring. If they object to tutoring students from your own school, recruit only from other schools."
Problem: The only branch covers an objection to own-school students. If the school bans paid outside work or tutoring entirely, or wants parental or written approval, the user has no next step. Since the whole plan is one business, a flat "no" ends it. A pass/fail check with an alternative is missing.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The substitute step contradicts the plan's own legal and child-safety rules
Where: Step 16 "Hire the substitute… a strong DP2 student from another school or a first-year undergraduate. Pay ₹500/hour… After that they can cover any batch alone"; Section 5 child safety; Section 1 "every extra counterparty becomes a parent's job".
Problem: Hiring and paying someone is a contract a minor cannot validly enter (the plan itself cites s.11), but step 16 does not say a parent engages the substitute. A first-year undergraduate is an unrelated adult who would teach 13–15-year-olds alone, yet there is no background or reference check and no rule that a parent must be on the call. Section 5's child-safety list only covers the student. The plan also never says how the substitute is paid (from the minor's account? by UPI?) or how that cost is recorded as an expense in the spreadsheet.
