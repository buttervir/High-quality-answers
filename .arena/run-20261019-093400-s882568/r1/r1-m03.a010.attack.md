ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The headline odds contradict the plan's own gate probabilities
Where: Short version "roughly 1 in 5 committed students ... reach ₹50k a month"; Summary "about 15–25% chance of job-replacing profit (₹50k+ a month) by mid-2028"; Section 6 table "₹50,000 ... Late 2027 (P ≈ 20%)" and "₹25,000 ... Mid-2027 (P ≈ 35–45%)".
Problem: Section 5 gives conditional pass rates: Gate 1 55–65%, Gate 2 35–45% given Gate 1, Gate 3 30–40% given Gate 2. Multiplied out: P(Gate 2) = 0.55×0.35 to 0.65×0.45 = about 19–29%, not 35–45% as the Section 6 table says for ₹25k by mid-2027. P(Gate 3) = about 6–12%. Gate 3 is only ₹60k MRR at 60%+ margin, which is about ₹36k profit, still below ₹50k. So by the plan's own numbers, P(₹50k profit) is below 6–12%, not "1 in 5" or "15–25%". Phase 4 also forbids growth in DP2 ("No new service lines and no new verticals", 6 then 2 hours a week), so nothing in the plan closes the gap from about ₹36k to ₹50k by "late 2027". The main number the family uses to make the decision is overstated by roughly 2–3x.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The hour caps make the Phase 2 and Phase 4 targets impossible to deliver
Where: Rule 2 "In the 8 weeks before any major exam block: at most 2 hours a week, existing clients only"; Phase 2 "January – April 2027 ... Gate 2 (by 30 April 2027): ₹25,000 or more in MRR from 6 or more clients"; the Phase 3 assumption "DP1 end-of-year exams around April–June 2027"; the retainer scope "8 Google posts, replies to every review within 48 hours, 10 new photos, catalogue updates, a monthly one-page report".
Problem: If DP1 exams start in April, the 2-hour cap starts in early February 2027. That leaves about 4–5 weeks of 8-hour weeks (January) to move from 3 clients to 6+, and "existing clients only" bans the new sales Gate 2 needs. In DP2 the same rule applies before mocks (usually Nov–Jan) and from mid-March 2028. At that point the plan expects 12–15 retainers, and each one promises 8 posts, 10 photos, a report and 48-hour review replies every month. Even at 1.5 hours per client per month, that is 18–22 hours a month, or 4–5 hours a week, against a 2-hour cap. The only escape is freelancers, which runs into Attack 3. The calendar, the time caps and the deliverables never get reconciled, so a family that follows Rule 2 strictly will miss Gate 2 by design.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The scaling route undoes the tax advantage the plan relies on
Where: Section 1 reason 4 "It's skill-based, which matters for tax"; Section 3 "Clubbing trap ... Once subcontractors do most of the work, income ... may be added to the parent's income again"; Phase 3 "Add 1–2 per-task freelancers ... The student keeps sales, the client relationship and quality checks"; Section 6, where ₹50k and ₹1L profit assume about 30% freelancer cost.
Problem: Every route to job-replacing profit in Section 6 depends on freelancers doing the delivery. Section 3 says this is exactly the situation where the s.64(1A) skill exception may stop applying, and the income would then be taxed at the wealthy parent's slab (30%+) instead of ₹0 under the rebate. The plan's only answer is "ask the CA" and "keep the student as the hands-on lead". Under the Attack 2 hour caps, the student cannot be the hands-on lead for 12–15 clients. The plan does not say what to do if the CA's answer is unfavourable: whether to accept clubbing, cap the business at the size the student can deliver alone, or delay hiring until 18. That is the deciding trade-off at the point where the business becomes job-replacing, and it is left open.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The default niche (dental and physio clinics plus "review generation") ignores rules on medical advertising
Where: Phase 1 "Dental and physio clinics, salons and cafés are good candidates"; the pitch "found 3 things costing you patients"; the retainer "review generation", "WhatsApp broadcast campaigns"; Rule 6 covers only data protection.
Problem: Registered medical practitioners in India work under professional-conduct rules: the NMC Registered Medical Practitioner (Professional Conduct) Regulations, 2023, and the Dentists (Code of Ethics) Regulations, 2014. These limit soliciting patients, advertising and promotion based on testimonials. Physiotherapists face similar council guidance. A marketing service whose product is soliciting reviews and running promotional broadcasts for clinics can put the client's registration at risk. Clinics that know this will refuse, and the target vertical shrinks. The only clinic safeguard in the plan, "public listing work only (Rule 6)", deals with data, not advertising ethics. The plan should either steer the first vertical to salons and cafés or add a check of the relevant council's advertising rules before pitching clinics. As written, the top recommended vertical carries a regulatory problem the plan never mentions.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] "Job-replacing" is defined as profit, but the gates and the incorporation trigger measure revenue (MRR)
Where: A5 "₹50k–1 lakh a month in take-home profit, not revenue"; Gate 2 "₹25,000 or more in monthly recurring revenue"; Gate 3 "₹60,000 or more MRR, gross margin of 60% or more"; Phase 5 / Summary "incorporate around mid-2028 if MRR is above ₹50k".
Problem: The milestones swap between revenue and profit. A family that sees "MRR above ₹50k" (the incorporation trigger) will think the job-replacing target has been met. At the plan's own 60% margin, that is about ₹30k profit, below the bottom of its own A5 range. Gate 3 at 60% margin is about ₹36k profit. So no gate in the plan before 18 actually tests the stated goal, and the incorporation decision is tied to a number that by definition is not job-replacing. The user cannot tell from the milestones whether the task's goal has been met.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Phase 0 asks for evidence the timeline cannot produce, and Rule 1 relies on a baseline that may not exist
Where: Phase 0 "19 October – 8 November 2026 ... record 30 days of the free Google Business Profile Insights"; Phase 2 "Price new setups at ₹7,999–14,999 once there are 3 case studies with numbers"; Rule 1 and Phase 0 step 5 "Record the baseline: October 2026 predicted grades".
Problem: (a) Phase 0 lasts 3 weeks, so 30 days of Insights cannot exist by its exit check. Insights only become visible after the owner grants the student manager access, and Google reports them with a lag. So the "numbers" the Phase 1 audits and pitch lean on are not available until about December. (b) Many Mumbai IB schools give no formal IB predicted grades in October of DP1 (predictions usually come in DP2 for applications). That leaves the measured trigger for the plan's top safety rule undefined for the first months. Also, a 2-point drop on the 1–7 scale (for example 6 to 4) is a severe fall, so the trigger fires very late. A 1-point drop, or a school-report equivalent, would be a usable trigger.
