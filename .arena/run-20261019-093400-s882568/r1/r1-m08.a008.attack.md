ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Stage 1 cannot fit inside the plan's own 8-hour weekly cap
Where: Stage 1, "Run all three tests at the same time, by hand, in about 8 hours a week", together with Part 6, "Weekly cap: 8 hours in term"
Problem: Weeks 3-8 give 6 weeks x 8 h = 48 hours in total. Test A needs 3 or more families paying for a second session. That means at least 6 one-to-one sessions, plus messaging 20 parents and travel "at your home" across Bandra/Juhu/Powai/Lower Parel/Worli, where one Mumbai cross-suburb trip often takes 1-2 hours each way. Test B needs 2 businesses that pay ₹15,000 for setup, and each setup covers a Google Business Profile, a WhatsApp Business catalogue with auto-replies, a one-page website and an AI assistant. Delivery alone is realistically 10-20 hours per client, so 20-40 hours, plus 10 intro meetings. Test C needs ₹15,000 in sales to non-relatives. Added together, this is well over 48 hours. The plan's tightest constraint (time) is broken in its very first stage, and it gives no rule for which test gets cut when hours run out. A student who follows it either breaks the grade-protecting cap or fails all three pass marks for lack of time. Then the "if none passes" branch fires, which uses up another 6 weeks and ₹30,000.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The headline ₹1 lakh/month profit target has no arithmetic behind it
Where: Stage 3, "Stage 3 target (by December of DP2): ₹1 lakh a month profit", and Short version point 4
Problem: Stage 2 shows its maths (60 tutor-hours x ₹1,500 = ₹90,000 revenue, ₹42,000 gross profit), but Stage 3 shows none. Using the plan's own numbers: the margin is about ₹700/hour (₹1,500 minus ₹800 tutor pay). Stage 3 also adds an operations manager at ₹15,000-₹25,000/month and an ad test. So ₹1 lakh of net profit needs about (₹1,00,000 + ₹25,000) / ₹700 ≈ 180 tutor-hours a month. That is 3x the Stage 2 volume, reached in roughly 5 months, while the founder's hours fall back to 8/week in DP2 term 1. For the digital route, Stage 2 tops out at "6 retainers x ₹12,000 = ₹72,000" of revenue, before paying the freelancers it says to hire per project. Reaching ₹1 lakh profit would take about 10-12 or more retainers with near-zero delivery cost. Nothing shows how to get there. The core deliverable ("job replacing money") is asserted as a target, not planned. The deadline is also stated inconsistently: "by December of DP2" in Stage 3 but "by the end of DP2" in the Short version and Part 1 Q1.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The default business ignores India's coaching-centre rules for under-16 students
Where: Test A, "parents of MYP, IGCSE and ICSE students (ages 11-15)"; Stage 2 "tutoring agency"; Stage 4 "a learning centre"
Problem: The Ministry of Education's Guidelines for Registration and Regulation of Coaching Centres (January 2024) say coaching centres should not enrol students under 16. They also require registration, and they cover centres that coach more than 50 students. States are being asked to adopt them. The plan's default business sells only to 11-15-year-olds. Its Stage 4 path ("a learning centre", "second city") is exactly the kind of set-up these rules target, and at the ~180 tutor-hours/month that Attack 2 shows is needed, the student count nears the 50 threshold. Part 5 claims to give the "exact setup" for legal compliance, but it says nothing about this. A family that scales the recommended business as written could find its core customer segment barred in the very stage that needs the ₹25-50 lakh investment.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The "biggest window" (the DP1-DP2 summer) is when IB's Extended Essay and IA work peaks
Where: Stage 3, "The summer break is your biggest window: 20-25 hours a week"; Part 6, "No-work weeks: ... the week before an IA or Extended Essay deadline"
Problem: Most IB schools set the summer between DP1 and DP2 as the main Extended Essay drafting period (a 4,000-word essay with a 40-hour recommended workload). Many also schedule IA data collection then, and university applications (essays, test prep for students aiming abroad, which Part 1 Q10 assumes) also start that summer. The plan puts its most important build phase (hiring a manager, writing the operating manual, a second offer, ad tests, incorporation) on 20-25 h/week in exactly that window. Its only protection is "the week before" a deadline. The plan's own priority ("Your IB grades come first") is broken at the point where the business needs the most hours, and no fallback is given if the summer hours are not actually there.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The grade rule cannot fire as written, because IB predicted grades are not issued monthly
Where: Part 6, "If any subject's predicted grade drops by 1 point or more, business hours halve next month. If it drops twice, the business pauses"
Problem: IB predicted grades are set by teachers usually once or twice in the whole programme, typically at the end of DP1 or in early DP2 for university applications. They are not a monthly figure that the monthly board meeting can track. Concrete case: a student whose Maths AA scores fall from 6-level to 4-level on unit tests in October-March of DP1 gets no predicted-grade change. The rule never fires, and Stages 1-2 run unchecked through the whole period it was meant to protect. The plan names no measure the student can actually check monthly (unit-test marks, term report grades, or teacher feedback), so the user would have to guess what to track.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Tax drag and the handover at 18 are left vague, so "your" profit is overstated
Where: Part 5 item 5, "it is simply taxed as your parent's business income"; Stage 4 item 1, "you are appointed director ... (or become the proprietor)"
Problem: In a family worth about ₹70 crore, the owner-parent is very likely in the 30% slab plus surcharge. So every rupee of "₹1 lakh a month profit" earned before 18 is taxed at roughly 31-39% in the parent's hands. The "job-replacing" figure is pre-tax and pre-split: the plan's own 50/30/20 split leaves the student ₹20,000/month to spend. Neither point is reconciled with Level 1. Also, a sole proprietorship belongs to its proprietor and cannot be renamed. Moving it from parent to child at 18 means transferring the business (a gift of the business assets), with new PAN-linked bank accounts and a new GST and Udyam registration if those exist. The plan reduces this to "(or become the proprietor)" with no steps.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] The Shops and Establishments claim is inaccurate for Mumbai
Where: Part 5 item 6, "with fewer than 10 workers and no physical shop, you usually need no Shops and Establishments registration"
Problem: Under the Maharashtra Shops and Establishments (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 2017, an establishment with fewer than 10 workers is excused from registration, but it must still file an intimation (Form F) online after it starts. The plan presents this as "need nothing", so a user following the "exact setup" would skip a required filing once tutors or an operations manager are engaged.
