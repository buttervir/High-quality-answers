ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The headline money target is defined two incompatible ways, so the "240 hours" plan is wrong under the plan's own definition
Where: Sub-problem 1: "Measure it with ... revenue, minus tutor pay, GST, payment fees, tools, marketing, staff and your own fee." vs. Unit economics: "Your own fee: ... it is on top of profit, not taken from it" and Part 3 header "Net profit / month (... before your fee)".
Problem: A fee the business pays the student is a business expense. It cannot be "on top of profit". Sub-problem 1 deducts the fee. The hours maths and the Part 3 projection do not. With the plan's own fee of ₹40,000-60,000 a month at scale, the Sub-problem 1 definition needs (₹1,00,000 + ₹33,000 + ₹50,000) / ₹555 = about 330 one-to-one hours a month, not 240. That is about 37% more students and tutors than the plan schedules. Under the definition the plan says to measure with, every checkpoint and the final target fall short. The task asks for EXACT DETAILS, and this is the number the whole plan is built on.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The ₹1 lakh is not the student's money and is not after tax, so it does not "replace a job" for the student
Where: "Run it legally as a sole proprietorship in one parent's name ... The target is ₹1 lakh a month in net profit (about ₹12 LPA ...)"; "The rest of the profit: it stays in the business ..."
Problem: The task's goal is for the student to earn "job replacing money". In a proprietorship, all profit is legally the Proprietor parent's income. In a family worth about ₹70 crore, that parent is almost certainly in the top slab: 30% plus surcharge and cess, roughly 35-39% marginal. So ₹1L of pre-tax profit is about ₹61-65k after tax, and it goes to the parent, not the student. What the student actually receives is only the fee: ₹10-15k rising to ₹40-60k. That is about half of the ₹12 LPA salary the plan says it matches. The plan also compares pre-tax profit with a salary that the plan itself says is tax-free under the rebate. Income tax on the profit is never deducted anywhere in the P&L.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The tutor pool is the group the plan's own visa section says cannot do this work
Where: "Tutors: IB alumni aged 18–23 with a final score of 40+ ... recruiting is easy" and Sub-problem 8: "UK student visa: you can't be self-employed during your studies. US F-1: actively working in your own business is a legal risk."
Problem: Mumbai IB alumni who score 40+ are mostly at university in the UK, US, Canada and similar countries. Paid freelance tutoring for an Indian agency is self-employment, which is barred for UK Student visa holders. For F-1 students it is unauthorised work. The plan sees this risk for the founder but recruits these same people as its core supply. It never says to limit tutors to students at Indian universities or to people who can legally work. Scenario: 4 of the 6 "yes" tutors from Phase 0 are at UK universities. Either they are breaking their visa terms, and the agency is the one paying them, or the supply plan collapses. The "easy recruiting" assumption, and the 15-20 tutors needed, are both unsupported.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] By its own projection, the plan does not meet its own success definition before the founder leaves
Where: Assumptions: "₹1,00,000 a month in net profit, steady for at least 3 months in a row." Part 3: "Mar–Apr 2028 ... ₹1–1.4L", "Jun 2028 ... about ₹1L"; Jul 2027 shown at 110 hours, below Apr 2027's 120 hours plus bootcamps.
Problem: The ₹1L months are only the March-April seasonal peak. May is missing from the table. That is the month DP2 clients sit finals and then stop buying, and the plan itself says demand peaks January to April. Its own 2027 numbers show a post-season dip in July. So "3 months in a row at ₹1L+" depends on Mar, Apr and May/Jun 2028 all holding up. The plan's own seasonality argues against that. By August 2028 the founder is supposed to become a passive owner abroad. The plan never says what to do if the 3-month test fails by then. Its kill rules stop at December 2027.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The safeguarding process is legally wrong for India: complaints go to a parent, not to the police, and there is no police verification
Where: Sub-problem 7.2: "Tutors must be 18+, checked with ID and an alumni reference ... In-person only with an adult present ... Any complaint goes straight to the Proprietor parent."
Problem: The clients are minors, and some sessions take place in their homes. Under the POCSO Act 2012 (s.19 and s.21), anyone who knows of a sexual offence against a child must report it to the police or the Special Juvenile Police Unit. Failing to report is itself an offence, and the person in charge of an organisation faces a higher penalty. Sending complaints only to the Proprietor parent can make that parent personally liable. The plan also leaves out the standard Indian check for people who work with children: police verification, or a Police Clearance Certificate through the Maharashtra police portal. An ID and an alumni reference do not catch a prior offence. The plan says this business serves minors, so this is a real gap, not a style point.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The per-hour margin is stated two ways
Where: Product table: "₹595 after 18% GST and 2% gateway fee" vs. Unit economics: "₹1,695 − ₹1,100 tutor − ₹40 gateway = ₹555 per 1:1 hour".
Problem: ₹595 is the figure before the gateway fee, not after it. A reader who budgets from the table overstates the margin by ₹40 an hour, about 7%, which is ₹9,600 a month at 240 hours. That is on top of the larger error in Attack 1.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] The Phase 0 pass/fail rule has an undefined middle band, and the time budget periods overlap
Where: "you need 5 families agreeing to a paid trial and 4 tutors. If you get fewer than 3 families, switch to Option E or C." Time budget: "Nov 2026 – Jul 2027 (DP1) | 8 max" and "Jun – Jul 2027 (summer) | 20–25".
Problem: The rule says nothing about 3-4 families, or 5+ families with fewer than 4 tutors. That is the most likely outcome of a first validation round, and the student has to guess what to do. June and July 2027 also fall in both rows, with an 8-hour cap in one and 20-25 hours in the other, so the hard hours cap meant to protect grades is ambiguous.
