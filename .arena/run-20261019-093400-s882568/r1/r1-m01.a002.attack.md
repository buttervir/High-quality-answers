ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The tax shield collapses exactly when the plan delegates the work
Where: Section 5 "Skill-based income is the student's ... All three tracks are skill-based. Keep evidence: ... the fact that the student does the work." vs. Gate 2 "The contractor delivers at least 40% of the work", Phase 3 "promote the best contractor to operations lead with a profit share" and Gate 3 "student working at most 2 hrs/week".
Problem: The minor-clubbing exception covers only income from the minor's own manual work, skill, talent or specialised knowledge. The plan deliberately turns the business into one where contractors and an ops lead do the work while the student is still a minor (Sep 2027 to mid-2028). Profit earned from other people's labour, organised with family-funded capital (Tranches 2 and 3 pay for contractors), is business income, not the minor's personal skill income, so it is very likely clubbed with the parent's income at 30%+. Concrete case: by Gate 3 (May 2028) the student works 2 hrs/week and the business makes Rs 1 lakh/month; the plan's own evidence test ("the student does the work") is now false, yet Section 5 still promises "little or no tax" up to Rs 12 lakh. The plan's main tax reason for choosing a service (attack A2) does not survive its own delegation timeline, and it gives no fallback (e.g., time the delegation for after 18, or budget the clubbed tax).

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The worst-case loss figure is wrong
Where: Section 11 "Worst case: all gates fail. The family loses under Rs 6.5 lakh (Tranches 1-2)".
Problem: Tranche 3 (up to Rs 25 lakh) is released after Gate 2 passes, but Gates 3 and 4 can still fail afterwards. Scenario: Gate 2 passes in Aug 2027, Rs 25 lakh goes into a second contractor and marketing, then the business shrinks during the DP2 exam freeze and fails Gate 3. Real loss is up to Rs 31.5 lakh, the very "lifetime cap" stated in Section 6, about 5x the number the family is told. "All gates fail" also cannot release Tranche 2 (Gate 1 failed), so the sentence is internally inconsistent either way.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The plan's legal backbone is agreements a minor cannot make
Where: Section 6 family agreement ("Ownership: All customers, brand, work and profits belong to [Student]"; each tranche is a "loan repayable from profits / converts to __% equity"); Section 4 "Write it down in the family agreement so the economics clearly belong to the student"; Phase 2 "A parent signs the engagement letter as guardian until the student is 18."
Problem: The plan itself cites Indian Contract Act s.11 (minors' agreements are void), then relies on a document the 16-year-old signs to allocate ownership, loan repayment and future equity. It has no legal force for the student's side. Where customers are routed through a parent's entity, the income, the customer contract and the client relationship legally sit with the parent; a one-page void agreement does not move them to the student. Likewise a guardian generally cannot bind a minor to a personal services/engagement contract that is not for the minor's benefit, so the contractor agreement either binds the parent personally or no one. The user is told these steps secure ownership when they do not; the fix (e.g., a parent-held trust or written declaration by the parent, re-executed by the student at 18) is missing.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The gates measure revenue but the goal is profit, so a business can pass every gate and still miss the target
Where: Assumptions "Job-replacing money = Rs 1,00,000 per month of profit the student takes home"; Gate 2 "Rs 1,00,000/month revenue"; Gate 3 "at least Rs 1 lakh/month" (unspecified); Gate 4 "profit to the owner ... for 6 consecutive months".
Problem: At Gate 2 exactly Rs 1 lakh revenue, with 40%+ of delivery by contractors (Track 2: Rs 3,000-5,000 per client), tools (~Rs 3,000/month), CA (Rs 15-30k/yr), then from Nov 2027 a 10%-of-gross-profit ops lead and from mid-2028 Pvt Ltd compliance of Rs 40-80k/yr, owner profit lands around Rs 65-80k. Gate 4 then needs Rs 1 lakh profit for six consecutive months, i.e. starting March 2028, in the middle of the exam freeze when the student works 0-2 hrs/week and no sales are being made. No gate between Aug 2027 and Aug 2028 checks profit, and nothing says how revenue must grow to cover the new costs, so the user only finds out at the final gate.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] All money flows through a minor's savings account with no fallback if the bank refuses or caps it
Where: Phase 0 Week 1 "Open or confirm the student's own savings account with UPI ... All business money goes here and nowhere else"; Section 4 "payment into the student's savings account".
Problem: The RBI April 2025 direction is permissive ("subject to the bank's policy"). Banks set their own limits for minor self-operated accounts (balance caps, transaction limits, restricted UPI/net banking), and savings accounts are not meant for regular commercial receipts. A savings account getting Rs 1 lakh+/month from clinics and brands is a standard AML-review trigger. This is the same "account frozen with money inside" risk the plan warns about for PayPal. Scenario: the bank allows only a guardian-operated account or caps it, or freezes it after Gate 2 volumes; the plan has no Plan B (e.g., which bank products allow this, a guardian-operated account with written segregation, or the CA's view on receipts).

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The academic tripwire has no usable baseline
Where: Section 6 clause 4 "If predicted grades total drop by 3+ points from the October 2026 baseline".
Problem: In October of DP1 (about 6-10 weeks into the course) most IB schools have not yet issued predicted grades or a full first report, so the baseline the whole "grades before both" rule depends on does not exist. The user has to guess what number to write in. It needs a stated fallback (e.g., the first DP1 report card, or MYP/IGCSE results converted to an IB estimate).

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Track 3 pricing is unrealistic for a 16-year-old tutor
Where: Track 3 "Price: Rs 1,500/student per 90-minute group session".
Problem: At 4 sessions a month that is Rs 6,000/month per student for a shared group taught by a 16-year-old peer, which is around what parents pay experienced professional IGCSE/ICSE tutors for group classes in Mumbai. The Rs 96,000/month route depends on this price; at a realistic peer-tutor rate (roughly a third to a half of it) the track gets to about Rs 30-50k/month on the same 24 hours, so the 14-day test may pick it based on numbers that will not hold.
