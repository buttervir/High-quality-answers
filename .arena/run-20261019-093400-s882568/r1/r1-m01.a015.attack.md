ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The ₹50k/month mix does not fit inside the plan's own 8-hour weekly cap
Where: Step 5, "A realistic mix that fits inside 8 hours a week": 3 tutoring groups (₹33,600) + 1 new digital setup a month (₹15,000) + 2–3 maintenance retainers; Step 4 already puts tutoring at "about 4.5 hours a week of teaching plus about 1.5 hours of preparation".
Problem: Tutoring alone uses 6 of the 8 hours. That leaves about 2 hours a week (about 8–9 hours a month) for everything else: finding and selling a new digital client every month, a full setup (Google Business Profile, photos, WhatsApp catalogue, one-page site, per Step 4), 2–3 retainers, invoicing, parent communication for 12 tutoring students, and the monthly family review. A single setup as Step 4 describes it, including on-site photos and a site build, takes longer than 8 hours on its own. So the headline "job-replacing" number is only reachable by breaking clause 5 of the Family Venture Agreement. The plan's central figure and its main safety rule contradict each other, and the plan never shows an hour-by-hour budget that reconciles them.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The ₹50k push is scheduled for the months when demand disappears and DP1 exams happen, and the dates contradict each other
Where: Step 5 "Months 6–10, roughly February–June 2027" vs Calendar "May–Jul 2027 | ₹50,000 a month | ... 3 months in a row at ₹50k+".
Problem: (a) The two sections give different windows (Feb–Jun vs May–Jul). If Step 3 starts in Oct/Nov 2026, "Months 6–10" is roughly Apr–Aug, which matches neither. (b) The "3 months in a row" test lands in May–Jul. That is when the IGCSE Year 11 and MYP 5 students the offer targets finish their board exams (May/June) and leave their groups, when Mumbai schools break for summer, and when most Mumbai IB schools hold DP1 end-of-year exams. Example: a group of four IGCSE Year 11 students paid monthly in advance stops paying after their May exams, so ₹33,600 of tutoring revenue falls by about a third to a half exactly in the qualifying window. The gate in Step 8 ("only if Step 5's test passed") then fails for seasonal reasons, not because of the business. The plan has no seasonality planning (for example recruiting Year 9–10 cohorts or running summer crash courses) and no rule for which date wins.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The grade tripwire, the plan's main safety mechanism, depends on a number most students don't get in DP1
Where: Step 1, clause 4: "If any predicted grade falls by 1 or more points..."; the Tripwires table: "Any predicted grade down 1 or more".
Problem: Many IB schools only issue formal IB predicted grades in DP2, for university applications. In DP1 students usually get term or semester grades, sometimes on school scales. The plan never says which number to use, what the baseline is, or how often it is measured. During DP1, which is the whole build phase (Steps 2–5), the trigger is undefined, so either it never fires or the family argues about it. That is the "awkward conversation" the plan says the rule removes. The user can't put this into practice without guessing. A fix would name the measure, for example "the latest school-reported IB 1–7 grade per subject, baseline = DP1 Term 1 report".

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The OPC recommendation conflicts with the plan's own study-abroad path
Where: Step 8.1: "One Person Company (OPC) (simplest, as sole owner)", followed by Step 9 (going abroad, "Becoming a non-resident").
Problem: Under Rule 3 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014, only a natural person who is an Indian citizen and resident in India (at least 120 days in the preceding financial year) can form or be the member or nominee of an OPC. Step 3 of the plan assumes the student probably goes abroad in Aug/Sep 2028. Example: incorporate an OPC in June 2028, fly to the UK or US in September, spend under 120 days in India in FY 2028–29, and the student is no longer eligible. The OPC then has to be converted or its membership changed, which is exactly the "trap for later" the plan claims to avoid. The plan presents OPC as the default "simplest" choice and never mentions residency. Step 9 even recommends staying owner of the Indian company from abroad. The likely abroad case should point straight to a Pvt Ltd with a resident parent as director.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The 5-month exam pause has no plan for most of the business it built
Where: Step 6.1: the helper "runs one tutoring group"; 6.3: "Tell your customers in November: 'The groups continue with [helper]'"; Step 5 table: 3 groups, digital clients and paid retainers.
Problem: Only one of the three groups gets a named operator. Nothing says what happens to the other two groups, to retainer clients who are paying ₹2,000–₹4,000 a month for updates, or to setups that were paid 50% up front but not finished, during Jan–May 2028. "They can also take over the digital-setup work" gives no capacity, pay split or handover rule. The helper is also a 16-year-old with direct access to every client, and a minor can't sign an enforceable non-solicit. Nothing stops the helper keeping the groups in June. So "I'm back in June" may leave nothing to come back to. Prepaid retainer clients who get no service are also a refund and reputation problem, in the same "tight social circles" the plan warns about. "Let it shrink" isn't a plan for the obligations already sold.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] "Withhold the login details until final payment" doesn't work for Google Business Profile, and it damages the business
Where: Step 4: "50% before you start and 50% before you hand over the login details ... Never hand over the work before the final payment."
Problem: A Google Business Profile has to be owned and verified by the business. Verification usually needs the owner's phone, postcard or video of the premises. So the client normally holds ownership from the start, and the student is added as a manager, which leaves no login to withhold. A WhatsApp Business catalogue is likewise tied to the shop's own number. Holding a client's own listing or number as payment leverage breaks platform expectations and invites disputes with exactly the local referral network the plan relies on. A usable rule would be: final 50% due before the work goes live, or before the site's domain or hosting is transferred.
