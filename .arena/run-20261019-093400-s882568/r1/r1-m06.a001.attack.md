ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The profit math leaves out the 18% GST the plan itself says will apply, so the "4–5 tutors = ₹1 lakh profit" claim is wrong
Where: Part 2, "The math to ₹1 lakh/month profit" ("₹1 lakh/month profit needs roughly 4–5 active tutors at that load") vs Part 3 Stage A step 6 ("GST: not needed until service turnover approaches ₹20 lakh/year... Private coaching is taxable at 18% above that")
Problem: Five tutors × 6 hrs × ₹2,800 × 4.3 = ₹3.61 lakh/month, or about ₹43 lakh/year. That is more than twice the ₹20 lakh threshold, so the business has to register for GST and pay 18%. At the quoted prices, revenue net of GST is ₹3.61L / 1.18 = ₹3.06L. Take off tutor pay (₹1.03L) and the plan's own ₹1.5L minimum overhead, and profit is about ₹0.53L/month, roughly half the target. Reaching ₹1L needs about 7 tutors, or prices 18% higher than the ones the plan validated in Week 1. The Month-9 "job-replacing income reached" date is built on numbers that skip a tax the plan names. Even the full-scale ₹2.7–3.7L figure drops by about ₹1.1L/month.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] Tutors are hired in Phase 2, but the entity that is supposed to contract and pay them only exists in Phase 3
Where: Part 3 Stage B trigger ("when the business hires non-family tutors"); failure 4 ("Any contract the business has to enforce (tutors...) is signed by a parent or a family-controlled entity"); Phase 2, Weeks 9–16 ("Recruit 2–3 tutors"); Phase 3, Months 5–9 ("Incorporate (Stage B, Part 3)... Sign tutor contracts")
Problem: The plan's own trigger for incorporating fires in Week 9, but incorporation is scheduled for Month 5 or later. For Weeks 9–20 the plan never says who signs the tutor agreements (including the non-solicit and no-assessed-work clauses), or how adult tutors are paid from a 16-year-old's minor savings account. Minor accounts usually have low UPI and transaction caps, which the plan admits ("check UPI limits"). As written, the user would have tutors working with no enforceable contract, exactly what failure 4 says to avoid. They would have to guess which instruction wins.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The tax premise falls apart once there is leverage: margin on other people's tutoring is not "the minor's own skill" income
Where: Part 2 filter test 3 ("Runs on the student's own skill, so income is not clubbed") vs test 5 ("the student can earn from other people's hours"); Part 3 Stage A step 5
Problem: The skill proviso (s.64(1A), 1961 Act) covers income from the minor's own manual work or skill. From Phase 2 onward, most of the income is the spread on tutors' hours, which is business income from employing others, not the minor's personal skill. That income is clubbed with the higher-earning parent and taxed at around 30% plus surcharge. The family's ₹70 crore net worth suggests a high marginal rate. The "zero tax up to ₹12 lakh" rebate claim therefore only holds for the founder's own teaching hours, which is Phase 1 only. This is the plan's central guardrail against failure 6, and it breaks just as the business starts to work. The plan sends the dividend question to the CA but says nothing about this.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The founder's 8 hrs/week cap cannot cover the Phase 2–3 workload, so the plan is internally impossible
Where: Phase 2 tasks and Phase 3 tasks vs failure 1 ("Hard cap: 8 hrs/week in DP1") and Part 7 (Saturday 3h + Sunday 2.5h + 15 min/day of WhatsApp)
Problem: Phase 2 puts all of this inside 8 hours a week: teaching 1 group (about 3 hrs with prep), recruiting 2–3 tutors (sourcing, demo lessons, 2 reference checks each), writing a full session-by-session curriculum kit with practice sets "for each offered course", 2 Instagram reels a week, a monthly parent workshop, sales calls and QA. Phase 3 adds incorporation, growing to 5–8 tutors, crash courses and hiring an ops manager. Curriculum writing alone for 2 subjects is tens of hours. The plan gives no hour budget per task and no rule for which task gets dropped. The user will either break the IB cap (failure 1) or miss the gates, and the plan doesn't say which to choose.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The fallback path has no exact steps, though the plan treats a fallback switch as a likely outcome
Where: Part 2 "Fallback path" and the Week-8 gate ("Kill and switch to the fallback path... Run Phase 1 again for the fallback with the same timeline")
Problem: The task asks for "EXACT DETAILS, steps and plans." The fallback is one paragraph: a service list, a price range and the note that it "needs a parent-signed entity earlier". It does not cover what to build first, which tools to use (WhatsApp Business API provider, automation stack), how to land the first 3 clients, the gate numbers, how the entity timing changes, or how a 16-year-old collects 50% upfront from a business on the minor account. "Phase 1 again" is a tutoring-specific plan (parent interviews, group teaching, workshops) and does not carry over. If the Week-8 gate fails, the user is left with no plan.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] A Private Limited Company cannot have "one" director
Where: Part 3 Stage B ("Directors: one or two parents")
Problem: Section 149(1) of the Companies Act 2013 requires at least 2 directors for a private company. Only an OPC can have one director, and an OPC cannot have a minor as member or nominee. A single-parent family, or one where only one parent will act, would follow this and have the SPICe+ filing rejected. The plan needs to name a second adult director, such as a relative.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] The tutor pool is self-contradictory
Where: Part 2 "What it is" ("The tutors are high-performing DP2 students and recent IB graduates (18+ only, see Part 6)")
Problem: Most DP2 students are 17, so "DP2 students" and "18+ only" mostly cancel each other out. Phase 2 then screens for "an IB score of 38+", which a current DP2 student cannot have. The usable pool is only recent graduates scoring 38+ who are in Mumbai and willing to work about 6 hrs/week at ₹500–₹900/hr. The plan never checks that this pool is big enough for the 5–8 tutors its revenue math depends on.
