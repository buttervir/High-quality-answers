# A 21-Month Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

**Short version.** Start a **skill-based service business that bills in advance**. The default is a done-for-you "local digital presence" service for Mumbai salons, cafés, gyms and coaching classes, sold as a monthly retainer. Run it in the student's own name, with a parent signing as guardian. Keep total family money at risk under **₹2 lakh until the business proves itself, and under ₹10 lakh before age 18**. Define "job-replacing money" as **₹50,000 a month or more in after-tax profit, sustained for 3 months** (Gate 4).

**Honest odds** (Section 6 shows the arithmetic). For a committed student who follows the plan:
- About **5–8%** reach job-replacing profit before leaving school (by July 2028).
- About **12–18%** reach it by the end of 2028, once exams are over and the student is 18.
- About **20–25%** reach ₹25k a month in profit by mid-2028.
- Most students end with ₹5–25k a month, a real selling skill, a credible university story and an intact IB score.

The plan is built so that these likely outcomes still count as wins. No plan that respects IB hours does much better before 18; the sections below say why. The ₹8M net worth is a safety net, not fuel. The worst realistic outcomes are losing a lot of family money or losing IB points, and the plan is designed around avoiding both.

## 1. Why this business, and not the obvious alternatives

Each option is scored for *this* person: little time (IB), limited legal capacity (a minor), a lot of family capital (which tempts overspending), and a window of about 20 months. The probabilities are rough judgments from base rates, not measured data.

| Option | P(first ₹ in 60 days) | P(≥ ₹50k/month after-tax profit by mid-2028) | Worst realistic loss | Fits IB hours? | Minor-law friction | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Local digital-presence retainer** (Google Business Profile, WhatsApp Business catalogue, simple site, review replies) for salons, cafés, gyms, coaching classes | ~70% | ~5–8% | ₹50k–1L | Yes; async and batchable | Low; bill in advance | **Default** |
| Tutoring MYP / IGCSE / ICSE students | ~80% | ~2–4% (capped by hours) | ~₹10k | Yes | Very low | **Best fallback.** Safe, low ceiling. |
| Freelance video editing / design for creators and D2C brands | ~50% | ~4–7% | ~₹30k (tools) | Mostly | Most platforms need 18+ | Good if the student already edits well |
| Micro-SaaS / app | ~10% | ~1–3% | Time, plus ₹1–5L if developers are hired | Poor | Low | Strong coders only; Section 10 |
| D2C product brand with inventory | ~40% | ~1–3% | **₹5–30L** | Poor; daily fulfilment | Medium | **Avoid before 18** |
| YouTube / Instagram creator | ~5% | ~1–3% | Time | Medium | Platform rules | Side channel only |
| F&O, crypto, "trading" | n/a | Negative expectation | Unlimited | No | Minors can't trade F&O | **Banned** |
| Buying a franchise or existing business | n/a | ~3–5% | **₹10L–1Cr** | No | High (minor can't sign) | **Banned** before 18 |

The ceilings are low across the board. The default does not win on ceiling. It wins because it holds up in almost every scenario:

1. **Demand is real and close by.** Mumbai has hundreds of thousands of small establishments with incomplete Google listings, unanswered reviews and no WhatsApp catalogue. The student can check this in one afternoon on Google Maps (Section 8, Day 3).
2. **Recurring revenue.** Retainers build month on month instead of resetting.
3. **Capital needed is tiny.** If it fails, the family loses less than a mid-range laptop costs.
4. **It is skill-based, which matters for tax** (Section 3): while the student does the work personally, the income can stay in the student's hands at ₹0 tax.
5. **It builds skills that carry over**: selling, pricing, delivering and operations.

**Main threat (confidence: medium):** AI tools keep pushing down the price of one-off website builds. So the offer is not "I build websites." It is "I keep your listing, replies and WhatsApp running every month and report the numbers." That is execution and accountability, which AI cheapens much less.

**Why clinics are not the default (confidence: medium-high).** Doctors work under professional-conduct rules on advertising and soliciting patients: the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002, which still apply in practice while the NMC's 2023 replacement regulations remain unsettled. Dentists are covered by the Dentists (Code of Ethics) Regulations, 2014, and physiotherapists have their own council guidance. A teenager running review drives and promotional broadcasts for a clinic can put the clinic's registration at risk. Clinics are therefore **optional, listing-accuracy work only** (correct hours, address, photos of the premises, phone number). There are no review campaigns, testimonials or broadcasts, and the student first checks the relevant council's current advertising rules.

## 2. Hard rules (each one exists because a common failure happens without it)

Write these down, have the student and both parents sign them, and pin them up. Breaking one stops the business until a family review.

1. **IB comes first, and this is measured.**
   - **Baseline:** whatever grades the school actually issues first (the term-1 report or internal assessments, on the IB 1–7 scale or the school's scale).
   - **Trigger:** a 1-point drop in two subjects, a 2-point drop in one subject, a 3-point drop in total, or any written concern from a teacher or the DP coordinator.
   - **Action:** maintenance mode (existing clients only, no selling) until the next report shows recovery.
2. **Time caps** (log every hour in a shared sheet; unlogged hours count as over the cap):

   | Period | Cap | Selling allowed? |
   |---|---|---|
   | Normal term weeks | 8 h/week (6 h/week in DP2) | Yes |
   | School holidays | 30 h/week, with Extended Essay hours blocked first | Yes |
   | 3 weeks before DP1 internal exams, and during them | 3 h/week | No |
   | 6 weeks before DP2 mocks, and during them | 2 h/week | No |
   | Mid-March 2028 to the end of IB finals | 2 h/week | No |

3. **Money cap with stage gates.** At most ₹2 lakh of family money until Gate 1, and at most ₹10 lakh in total before age 18. **Evidence decides the budget, not net worth.**
4. **No credit to customers.** Setup fees are paid 100% upfront. Retainers are paid monthly **in advance**. A minor's contracts are void, so the student cannot chase a debt; billing in advance removes the problem.
5. **Banned before 18:** F&O or margin trading, crypto, MLM, paid "make money online" courses over ₹5,000, franchises, inventory imports, leases, lending to friends, and any "partnership" that asks the family for money.
   - SEBI's own studies found **about 9 in 10 individual F&O traders lost money** (FY22 and FY24).
   - A wealthy family with a teenage founder is a target. Treat every unsolicited "opportunity" as a sales pitch.
6. **No sensitive data.** Never collect health records, ID documents or card data for clients. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules are coming into force in phases, and a teenager should not be anyone's processor of sensitive data. Work on the client's *public-facing* presence only.
7. **Clean reviews only.** Ask *every* customer for a review in the same way, with no discounts, gifts or other incentives. Never filter which customers get asked ("review gating"), and never write, buy or swap reviews. Google's policies prohibit incentivised and gated reviews and can suspend the listing. In India, fake reviews also fall under consumer-protection enforcement and the BIS standard on online reviews (IS 19000:2022). One suspended client listing can end the business's reputation in a neighbourhood.
8. **Friends-and-family revenue doesn't count.** Money from relatives or parents' contacts is welcome, but it does **not** count toward any gate. Gates count only arm's-length customers.

## 3. Legal and tax setup for a minor in India

| Topic | Likely true (confidence) | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Contracts** | A minor's agreement is void (Indian Contract Act, 1872, s.11; *Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose*, 1903). **High.** | Use a one-page "service order" signed by the client and by a parent **as guardian**. Bill in advance. |
| **Company / director** | A minor in practice cannot be a director or get a DIN, and cannot be a full partner. **High.** | **Don't incorporate before 18.** Operate as an individual. |
| **PAN** | A minor can get a PAN through a guardian. **High.** | Apply on the Protean (NSDL) or UTIITSL portal for about ₹100. |
| **Bank account** | Since RBI's April 2025 directions, banks *may* let minors aged 10+ operate savings accounts independently, within bank-set limits. **Medium-high**; limits vary by bank. | Open a minor savings account with UPI. All business money goes through **this account only**. If the limits are too low, add a guardian-operated account. |
| **Payment gateways and freelance platforms** | Generally need an 18+ holder or business KYC. **Medium.** | Never open one in a parent's name to get around this. Use UPI or bank transfer from local clients until 18. |
| **Clubbing of income** | A minor's income is normally added to the higher-earning parent's income (old s.64(1A)). **Income from the minor's own manual work, skill, talent or specialised knowledge is excluded** and taxed as the minor's own. The 2025 Act carries this over. **Medium-high.** | Keep evidence that the student personally does the work: time logs, deliverables, client messages. Have the CA confirm before 31 March 2027. |
| **Tax on the minor's own income** | Under the new regime, total income up to **₹12 lakh** a year was effectively tax-free for FY 2025-26 through the s.87A rebate. **Medium-high; confirm for FY 2026-27.** | If the skill exception applies, profit up to about ₹1 lakh a month likely costs **₹0 in tax**. File the student's own ITR every year. |
| **Clubbing trap** | If other people do much of the delivery, the income may count as coming from others' labour, and be clubbed with the parent's income at about **31–39%** (30% slab plus surcharge and cess, depending on the parent's income). **Medium.** | Follow the decision rule below. |
| **Turning 18** | Clubbing applies only while the child is a minor. Income earned after the 18th birthday is the student's own whoever does the work. **Medium-high**; the CA confirms the split for the birthday year. | Schedule real scaling (hiring) for after 18. |
| **GST** | Registration is required once aggregate turnover passes **₹20 lakh a year for services** in Maharashtra. **High.** | Not needed before 18 on this plan. Set an alarm at ₹15 lakh rolling revenue. |
| **Shop / establishment** | Home-based with no employees: at most an intimation under Maharashtra's 2017 Act. **Medium.** | Ask the CA. |
| **Labour law** | Adolescents (14–18) may do non-hazardous work. Running your own home-based service outside school hours is fine. **High.** | Never hire anyone under 18. |
| **IB CAS** | Paid work generally does **not** count as CAS. **Medium-high.** | A *free* service for an NGO using the same skills may qualify. Ask the CAS coordinator. |

### The freelancer decision rule (decide this at the Phase 0 CA meeting, not later)

Freelancers are the only way past solo capacity (about 8 clients; Section 6). They can also cost the ₹0 tax treatment. Ask the CA this exact question: *"If the student personally does all selling, strategy, client contact, quality checks and monthly reporting, and an 18+ freelancer does only photo editing and post publishing, paid per task, does the skill exception still cover the student's net profit?"*

| CA answer (put it in writing, even an email) | What the plan does before 18 |
|---|---|
| **Yes** | Freelancers allowed from Phase 3. Cap of 12 clients. The student stays hands-on lead on every account. |
| **Unclear** | Freelancers only for **exam-crunch cover** (routine posting in the Rule 2 windows), with at most 15% of annual delivery hours done by others. Cap of 8 clients. |
| **No** | **Stay solo.** Cap of 8 clients until 18. Scale only with income earned after the 18th birthday. |

Why "No" means "stay solo", with worked numbers:

| Setup | Pre-tax monthly profit | Tax | After-tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo: 8 clients at ₹7k average, about ₹5k of tools and travel | ~₹51k | ₹0 (skill exception, under the ₹12L/year rebate) | **~₹51k** |
| With freelancers but clubbed: 12 clients at ₹6k = ₹72k, minus ~₹15k freelancers and ~₹5k costs | ~₹52k | 31–39% at the parent's rate | **~₹32–36k** |

Under an unfavourable ruling, growing with freelancers *reduces* take-home money. So it is not done.

## 4. Phase 0: Setup (19 October – 8 November 2026, about 3 weeks, about 10 hours)

**Family steps**

1. Hold a family meeting and sign the Section 2 rules. Set the **Phase 1 budget at ₹75,000**, held by a parent and released against receipts.
2. Apply for the student's PAN and open the minor savings account with UPI.
3. Book a 1-hour CA consultation (₹5,000–15,000) with this question list:
   - Does the skill exception cover this business?
   - The freelancer question in Section 3, word for word.
   - What records do we keep?
   - Is there any Maharashtra professional-tax or shop-registration requirement?
   - How do we file the minor's ITR for FY 2026-27?
   - How is income split in the year the student turns 18?
   - When should we incorporate after 18, and as an OPC or a Pvt Ltd?
4. Set up one Google Sheet with tabs for Income, Expenses, Hours, Clients, Grades and Gates. Scan every receipt into a Drive folder.

**Student steps**

5. Record the grade baseline using whatever the school has issued so far. If nothing is issued until the term report, use teacher-reported internal assessment grades and replace them when the report arrives.
6. Learn the core skills in about 12 free hours:
   - Google Business Profile help centre, including **its review policies**
   - WhatsApp Business catalogue and quick replies
   - One website builder (Google Sites, Wix or Framer)
   - Canva
   - A neutral review-request QR flow (same request to every customer)
7. **Build the proof first:** do a full setup for **2 real businesses free of charge**. Use neighbourhood businesses, not relatives' shops.
   - Get *manager* access to each Google profile from the owner.
   - Take before and after screenshots and an audit score (Section 5) before and after.
   - **Start** the 30-day Insights record now. Calls, direction requests and views will be readable around mid-December and feed the Phase 2 price rise. Phase 1 pitches rely on audit scores and before/after screenshots, which exist on day 1.

**Exit check (8 November 2026):** PAN applied, account open, CA consulted with the freelancer answer recorded, 2 free setups done with before/after screenshots. If the CA says the structure is unworkable, switch to tutoring (Section 10).

## 5. The phased plan with stage gates

Every gate states **revenue (MRR) and profit**, because the goal is profit. "Profit" means receipts minus all business costs (tools, printing, travel, freelancers), counting family-funded spending as a cost.

### Phase 1: First paying customers (9 November 2026 – 10 January 2027; uses the Diwali and winter breaks, about 8–20 h/week)

**Pick one area and one vertical.** Choose an area within commuting distance (for example Bandra West, Andheri West or Powai). Start with **salons**, then cafés, gyms or coaching classes. These depend on local search, are visually photogenic, and can afford ₹3–8k a month.

**Build a list of 100 prospects** from Google Maps and score each on 6 checks:
- no or poor photos
- fewer than 50 reviews
- unanswered reviews
- wrong or missing hours
- no website
- no WhatsApp Business

**Outreach**
1. For the top 40, make a one-page **audit**: their score out of 6, 3 specific fixes, and a screenshot of a nearby competitor doing better.
2. Deliver it in person on a Saturday, with a parent nearby for the first visits, or on WhatsApp.
3. Script: *"I'm a student who helps [area] salons get more calls from Google. I checked your listing and found 3 things costing you customers. Here's the free audit. If you like, I'll fix all of it this week for ₹4,999, and I only start once you've approved the plan."*

**Pilot offer:** a one-time setup at **₹4,999**, paid upfront by UPI. It covers the listing overhaul, 20 edited photos, a WhatsApp catalogue, a one-page site and a review QR stand (about ₹300 to print).

**Spend limits:** at most ₹20,000 on tools and printing. At most ₹15,000 on one paid-ads test, and **only after** 2 sales have come from direct outreach.

**Gate 1 (10 January 2027): 3 or more arm's-length paying clients, no refunds, Rule 1 not triggered.**
- **Pass:** unlock up to ₹1.25 lakh more (₹2 lakh total).
- **Fail:** one 4-week retry with a different vertical (to about 7 February). If that fails, switch to tutoring.
- **P(pass, including the retry) ≈ 55–65%** for a student who actually makes the 40 visits. Most failures come from not doing the outreach, not from missing demand.

### Phase 2: From setups to retainers (11 January – about 14 March 2027 selling; then DP1 exam mode)

1. **Convert every setup client to a retainer.** Two tiers, both written into a one-page service order:

   | Tier | Price/month | Scope | Student time/month (after SOPs) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **Standard** | ₹4,000–6,000 | 4 Google posts, replies to all reviews within 72 h (batched twice a week), 6 new photos, catalogue updates, one-page monthly report | ~1.25 h |
   | **Plus** | ₹8,000–12,000 | Standard, plus 2 WhatsApp broadcast campaigns to opted-in customers and a monthly 1-hour photo shoot on site | ~2.5 h |

   Every service order includes an **"exam-month clause"**. In up to 3 named months a year, service runs in "lite" mode (review replies and the report; posts pre-written in advance). The client pays the full fee only if a backup person keeps posting, otherwise 70%. Clients agree to this upfront, so no exam month breaks a promise.
2. **Raise prices** for new setups to **₹7,999–14,999** once 3 clients have before/after Insights numbers (available from mid-December).
3. **Systemise:** write an SOP for each recurring task, a client onboarding form and a report template. Batch all client work into **two fixed blocks a week** (for example Wednesday 6–8 pm and Saturday 9 am–1 pm).
4. **Sell hard while selling is allowed.** January to mid-March gives about 9 weeks at 8 h/week. Aim for one new client a week. Offer one free month for each referred client who stays 2 months.
5. **DP1 exam window** (3 weeks before and during internal exams, around April–May): 3 h/week, existing clients only, lite mode where the order allows.

**Gate 2 (end of DP1 exams, about 31 May 2027): ₹25,000+ MRR from 6+ clients, about ₹21k+ monthly profit, no client over 30% of revenue, monthly churn under 15%, grades held.**
- **Pass:** the family capital cap rises to ₹5 lakh.
- **Fail on revenue:** keep running what exists, with no added spending.
- **Fail on grades:** maintenance mode.
- **P(pass | Gate 1) ≈ 40–50%.**

### Phase 3: Summer leverage (June – July 2027)

1. Block **10 h/week for the Extended Essay first**, then up to 20 h/week on the business.
2. Move the best clients up to Plus, and replace the lowest-paying client rather than just adding more.
3. Apply the **freelancer decision rule** (Section 3) exactly as the CA answered it. If freelancers are allowed, use 1–2 college students (18+) at ₹300–600 per task for editing and publishing. The student keeps sales, client contact, quality checks and reports.
4. Target **8 clients** (solo) or up to **12** (only if the CA said yes).

**Gate 3 (31 July 2027): ₹40,000+ MRR, ₹35,000+ monthly profit, 2 months of costs held in the business account.**
- **Pass:** the cap rises to ₹10 lakh. It is still mostly a reserve, not money to spend.
- **P(pass | Gate 2) ≈ 35–45%.**

### Phase 4: Hold and lift price through DP2 (August 2027 – May 2028)

Growth in DP2 comes from **price and mix, not volume or new lines**:
- No new service lines or verticals.
- Client count stays at or below the cap.
- Raise existing clients 10% at their 12-month mark.
- Move clients from Standard to Plus.
- Replace churned or low-paying clients through referrals only.

The **time budget at 8 clients** shows this is deliverable:

| Period | Cap (h/month) | Delivery (8 clients) | Admin and reports | Selling and referrals | Fits? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DP2 normal term (6 h/week) | ~26 | ~13 (mix of Standard and Plus) | ~5 | ~8 | Yes |
| Mock and finals windows (2 h/week) | ~9 | Lite mode ~4 (replies and report) | ~3 | 0 | Yes, because of the exam-month clause |

Two more steps for Phase 4:
1. Before mocks, write a **"hit by a bus" handbook**: SOPs, logins in a password manager shared with a parent, and every client's renewal date.
2. **University applications** (about August 2027 – January 2028): present real numbers (clients, MRR, profit, churn), what went wrong and what was learned. "₹40k MRR from 8 salons, sold door to door, no family money after month 3" is far more credible than "founded a startup", because admissions readers discount ventures funded by wealthy parents.

**Gate 4: the actual goal (any 3 consecutive months up to 31 July 2028).** **₹50,000 or more a month in after-tax profit**, measured on contracted full-price retainers, with actual cash collected at 85% or more of that. The 85% allows for exam-month lite billing.
- Solo route: about 8 clients averaging about ₹7k (a Standard/Plus mix), at ₹0 tax if the skill exception holds.
- Freelancer route: about 12 clients, only if the CA said yes.
- **P(pass | Gate 3) ≈ 45–55%.**

### Phase 5: Turning 18 and after exams (June – December 2028)

1. **Incorporate** if the business has made **₹30k or more a month in profit for 3 months**. This is the point where enforceable contracts and a current account pay for the compliance; it is *not* the job-replacing milestone.
   - Form an OPC or a Private Limited company, as the CA advises.
   - Move clients onto contracts in the company's name, now enforceable.
   - Open a current account and a payment gateway.
   - Register for GST when the CA says to, or by ₹20 lakh turnover.
2. **Scale now.** Income after 18 is the student's own whoever does the work, so the clubbing trap disappears. Hire freelancers freely, open a second area, and move to 15–25 clients if staying in Mumbai.
3. Decide between three paths:
   - **Grow full-time** (gap year or Indian university with light hours). This is where most of the 12–18% end-2028 odds come from.
   - **Run it alongside university** abroad with a local manager on profit share.
   - **Sell or hand over the client book.** Small retainer books sell for roughly 1–2 years of profit, in a thin market.
4. **Only now** consider higher-ceiling ideas (a tool for the businesses the student now knows well), funded first by the business's own profit, and family capital only against a written plan with payback within 12 months.

## 8. First 7 days (start now)

1. **Day 1:** print Section 2. Both parents and the student sign it. Record the grade baseline. Put the school's real exam calendar into the Rule 2 table.
2. **Day 2:** apply for the PAN, book a bank appointment for the minor's account, and book the CA. Send the CA the freelancer question in advance.
3. **Day 3: the demand test.** Score 30 salons (or cafés) in the chosen area on the 6 checks. **If fewer than 10 of the 30 fail 3 or more checks, change area or vertical.**
4. **Days 4–5:** do Google's free Business Profile training, including the review policies. Set up WhatsApp Business on a **separate number** (a ₹200 prepaid SIM in a parent's name).
5. **Day 6:** walk into 2 nearby businesses and offer the free setup.
6. **Day 7:** first family review. Ask one question: *"Is anything here already in conflict with IB deadlines?"* Adjust.

## 9. What the family should and shouldn't do with its wealth

**Do:**
- pay for the CA and a lawyer
- provide a quiet workspace and transport to client visits
- make introductions as a learning channel (not counted toward gates)
- fund a laptop if one is genuinely needed
- keep a ₹1–2 lakh "mistake budget" that isn't a big deal to lose

**Don't:**
- buy the student a business, franchise or shop
- fund paid ads at scale before the unit economics work
- open accounts on platforms in a parent's name to get around age rules
- do client work yourselves, because that weakens the skill-income tax position
- treat business revenue as pocket money

Separately, consider a small **investing education** in a guardian-run demat account: delivery-based index funds only, ₹50k–1 lakh, to teach compounding. Keep it fully separate from the business.

## Summary of the plan

- **Business:** a skill-based local digital-presence retainer for salons, cafés, gyms and coaching classes, billed in advance. Fallback: tutoring. Clinics: listing accuracy only.
- **Legal:** operate as an individual minor with a PAN and a minor savings account, guardian-signed service orders, no company and no platforms until 18. The CA's written answer on freelancers decides whether the business stays solo (8 clients) or grows to 12 before 18.
- **Money:** at most ₹2 lakh until proven, at most ₹10 lakh before 18, released through gates measured on arm's-length revenue *and* profit.
- **Milestones:** 3 clients by January 2027. ₹25k MRR (about ₹21k profit) by the end of DP1 exams. ₹35k profit by July 2027. **Gate 4, the actual goal of ₹50k after-tax profit for 3 months, by July 2028.** Incorporate at ₹30k+ profit after 18.
- **Odds (committed student):** about 5–8% for job-replacing profit before leaving school, about 12–18% by end-2028, and about 20–25% for ₹25k a month by mid-2028. The plan is designed so that the far more likely outcomes are still clearly good ones.
