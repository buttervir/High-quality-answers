ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Target 1 fails by the plan's own definition, and the money it counts belongs to the parent, not the student
Where: Assumptions: "Target 1: ₹1,00,000 a month net ... held for 6 months in a row. Due by 31 May 2028"; Section 6.1: "That income is legally your parent's"; Section 6.3: "Net ≈ ₹81,650 a month ... Total ≈ ₹97,000 a month, essentially Target 1"; Bottom line: "Target 1 ... is within reach by the end of IB".
Problem: (a) The plan only reaches 8–10 tutors and about 20 clients by 31 Dec 2027. That leaves exactly Dec 2027 to May 2028 for the "6 months in a row", and its own base case gives ₹81,650, or ₹97,000 with legacy teaching. Both are below ₹1,00,000, so the base case misses Target 1 as the plan defines it. "Essentially" does not meet a numeric target. (b) Before 18, the agency layer is the parent's sole proprietorship, so the ₹81,650 is the parent's business income. It is taxed at the parent's slab, which for a ₹70 crore family is very likely 30% plus surcharge. That makes it neither "net" nor the student's. The only income the student legally owns by May 2028 is the legacy teaching, about ₹15,600 a month. The task asks for the student to make job-replacing money, but the headline result counts money the plan itself says is not theirs, before tax.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] GST threshold is wrong once the agency sits under the parent's PAN
Where: Section 6.2: "Registration becomes mandatory once total turnover crosses ₹20 lakh ... You'll stay below that until about 2028–29"; Section 6.1: "your parent holds that layer as their own sole proprietorship".
Problem: GST registration and the ₹20 lakh threshold work on aggregate turnover per PAN, and that includes exempt supplies. A parent in a ₹70 crore family very likely already has proprietorship, consultancy or professional receipts above ₹20 lakh under their own PAN. A doctor's exempt healthcare fees count toward the threshold too. In that case the parent is, or must become, GST-registered. Every agency hour then carries 18% GST from the first tutor in April 2027. Counterexample: client price ₹1,800 GST-inclusive → taxable value ₹1,525 → margin after the ₹1,200 tutor fee drops from ₹600 to about ₹325. That nearly halves the 6.3 base case. Otherwise parents must be charged ₹2,124, which breaks the price ladder. The plan never asks whether the parent's PAN is already registered. Its CA question 3 covers the transfer, not this.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The Phase 5 path to Target 2 breaks in the most likely scenario: the student studies abroad
Where: Phase 5: "June–August 2028 ... work full-time for 10 weeks", "Hire a general manager by mid-2029 at the latest"; FY targets ₹40L → ₹80L → ₹1.5 crore; Section 6.6: "UK Student visa: the conditions prohibit self-employment and business activity ... Restructure your role to shareholder only".
Problem: An IB student from an US$8M Mumbai family is very likely to start at a UK or US university in September 2028. The plan admits that in that case the student can only be a passive shareholder from September 2028 to 2031. Yet the growth from ₹40 lakh to ₹1.5 crore (SEO library, Gulf expansion, cohort courses, hiring 25+ tutors) has no named owner. The GM it relies on costs ₹50–70k a month, plus a 5–10% profit share, against an ₹80k-a-month business in Aug 2028. That leaves about ₹10–30k a month. The plan's own chain says a GM needs "about ₹70 lakh+ annual revenue by 2029", but the student is forced to hire one in 2028 at less than a quarter of that. End state B also says "the student works 15 hours a week or less", which a UK Student visa does not allow at all. The plan gives no version of the numbers for the abroad case.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] Target 2 is checked before tax and before the GM profit share, but it was defined as net
Where: Assumptions: "Target 2: ₹2,50,000 a month net"; Section 6.3: "Operating profit ≈ ₹30.5 lakh = Target 2 (₹30 lakh a year to the owner, before corporate and personal tax)"; Phase 5 step 3: "GM ... plus a 5–10% profit share or ESOP".
Problem: ₹30.5 lakh is pre-tax operating profit. A 5–10% GM profit share takes ₹1.5–3 lakh off that, and it is not in the overhead table. Then comes about 25% corporate tax on the dividend route, or personal slab tax of roughly ₹4–5 lakh on a ₹30 lakh salary under the new regime. After those, the owner gets about ₹2.0–2.1 lakh a month, not the ₹2.5 lakh net it defined. The cohort revenue (₹16 lakh) is also put through the blanket 33% gross margin, although its own cohort example shows 72%. The model is internally inconsistent, so the Target 2 check is unreliable either way.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The DP1 time cap cannot hold the Phase 1 schedule, especially with in-person sessions in Mumbai
Where: Phase 1: "Tuesday and Thursday 6:00–8:00 pm (up to 4 sessions), Saturday 10:00 am–1:00 pm (up to 3 sessions), Sunday 30 minutes of admin. Maximum 7 teaching hours a week"; diagnostics with "a written '3 weak topics + 8-session plan' PDF within 24 hours"; offer "in-person (at your home with a parent present...)"; Section 6.5: "Hard caps: 8 hours a week in DP1 terms".
Problem: 7 teaching hours, 0.5 hours of admin, the 30-minute diagnostics, the written PDF plans, the 3-line WhatsApp summaries per session and the 4-session mini-test reports already exceed 8 hours. Mumbai home visits add about 30–60 minutes of travel each way per session. Tuesday and Thursday sessions with 2 back-to-back clients in different buildings are impossible in a 2-hour block. Counterexample: 4 weekday in-person sessions at 2 locations can mean 3–4 extra commute hours, which pushes DP1 time to 11–13 hours a week. The plan's own key guardrail, "IB comes first", is broken in its very first phase. It never says to go online-only or same-building only to keep the cap.

ATTACK 6 [MAJOR] The fallback Track B has no route to job-replacing money and runs into the same minor-contract problem
Where: Section 5: "At 10 care clients × ₹4,000 = ₹40,000 a month recurring, hire one freelancer"; scoring table gives Track B "Minor-legal 4".
Problem: The task asks for job-replacing money. Track B stops at ₹40,000 a month, which is 40% of Target 1, and then pays a freelancer from it. It gives no numbers for reaching ₹1 lakh or ₹2.5 lakh a month, and no steps beyond "the same coordinator, freeze and turn-18 incorporation steps apply". So if Gate 1 fails, which the plan treats as a real possibility, the user has no plan for the stated goal. B2B clients such as clinics and CA firms also expect invoices and enforceable service terms, and Section 6.1 says a minor can't give them those. The "4" for minor-legal contradicts the plan's own legal section. It never says that Track B also needs the parent as the contracting party.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] The tutor ramp arithmetic doesn't reach the Dec 2027 target
Where: Gate 3: "Pass: 3+ tutors"; Phase 3 step 2: "Grow to 8–10 tutors ... by 31 December 2027. Add 1 tutor per month"; Phase 3 step 1 hires the coordinator by 30 Sep 2027.
Problem: From 3 tutors on 31 Aug 2027, adding 1 a month (Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec) gives 7 tutors, not 8–10. That holds even before the "only when the waitlist reaches 3 or more" condition slows it. The Dec 2027 state that end state A and the 35-hour base case rest on can't be reached by the plan's own hiring rule unless Gate 3 passes at 4–6 tutors. The plan doesn't say this. Section 6.3 also shows the upside-case overhead as ₹32,500 against ₹27,500 with no explanation of the ₹5,000 difference.
