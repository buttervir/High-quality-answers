ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The headline "Level 1 by mid-to-late 2027" is false by the plan's own numbers once the GST it says will be triggered is applied
Where: Section 8 base-case table ("Proprietorship operating profit ≈ 1,66,158 ... Total ≈ ₹1.84 lakh") and "GST trigger (C5): the base case annualises to ... ₹53 lakh turnover, well above ₹20 lakh"; Section 1 "target is ₹1 lakh/month profit ... by about mid-to-late 2027".
Problem: The profit table is computed with no GST, but the same section says the base case is at ₹4.4 lakh/month proprietorship turnover. GST aggregate turnover is counted per financial year (from 1 April), so at that rate the ₹20 lakh line is crossed around August 2027, which is inside the "mid-to-late 2027" window. Under option (a), which the plan itself prices out, 18% inside ₹4,39,600 of revenue is about ₹67,000/month (439,600 × 18/118). Then add the ₹25,000 coordinator that Section 8 tells you to subtract: 1,66,158 − 67,058 − 25,000 + 18,186 (own tutoring) ≈ ₹92,000/month. That is below Level 1. The only fix offered, raising prices to ₹2,000 inclusive, conflicts with the Phase 1 rule to price "15–30% below established adult tutors" and is never tested. Option (b) is left as "do not do it without your CA". The "trailing 12-month ₹15 lakh" early warning also uses the wrong measure, because the threshold runs per financial year. So the reader is given a profit target the plan's own model does not reach.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The conservative case "arrives in 2028", but the plan bans growth in 2028
Where: Section 8 "In the conservative case it arrives in 2028"; Phase 4 "DP2, defend not grow ... No new products"; Section 10 "Growth beyond Gate 2 during DP2 is not a goal."
Problem: The conservative case is about ₹91k/month before the coordinator, and about ₹66k after subtracting the ₹25k the plan says to subtract. For it to become ≥₹1 lakh in 2028, the business has to grow. But from Aug 2027 to May 2028 growth is explicitly forbidden, and from 15 Feb 2028 the founder does 0–1 hr/week. No mechanism is given that gets the conservative case to Level 1 in 2028. That is a logic contradiction in the plan's main promise.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Almost none of the "profit attributable to you" is legally the student's money
Where: Section 8 "Total profit attributable to you ≈ ₹1.84 lakh"; Section 6 "The parent's ITR shows this business income"; Section 11 Memorandum clause 2 "held by [Parent] for the benefit of [Student]".
Problem: The task's goal is the student's own "job replacing money". In the plan, about ₹1.66 lakh of the ₹1.84 lakh is proprietorship profit. It is legally the parent's income, taxed at the parent's slab rate, and held by the parent until a transfer at 18. Only about ₹18k/month (own tutoring) plus an unquantified "founder management fee" is actually the student's. The fee is left as "a modest rate that your CA agrees is defensible", with no number, so the reader cannot work out their own income. Labelling ₹1.84 lakh "attributable to you" overstates what the student earns by about 10x until age 18.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The weekly schedules break the plan's own 8 hrs/week cap
Where: Section 7 rule "at most 8 hrs/week during term"; Phase 1 "2 sessions × 1.5 hrs of tutoring per weekday evening at most"; Section 5 bootcamp "Tutor (you assist)", 16 hrs per cohort; Phase 2 "3–4 bootcamps" plus sales calls plus a monthly sit-in per tutor.
Problem: 2 × 1.5 hrs × 5 weekdays = 15 hrs/week of tutoring allowed in Phase 1, nearly double the cap, before the Saturday sales hour, the Sunday review, 20 parent conversations and tutor demo lessons. In Phase 2: 3 hrs tutoring + 6 tutor sit-ins/month (~1.5 hrs/week) + assisting 3–4 bootcamps × 16 hrs over 4 months (~3–4 hrs/week) + sales calls + finance. That already exceeds 8 hrs, and it falls during the Jan–May window that includes DP1 school exams, where the cap is 2 hrs/week. A student who follows the stated rhythm breaks the stated rule, and the reader cannot tell which instruction wins.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] Gates and the pause rule depend on "predicted grades" that DP1 students usually do not have
Where: Gate 1 "Your own IB predicted grades unchanged or higher compared with your September 2026 baseline"; Section 10 "Pause everything ... if any subject's predicted grade drops by 1"; Checklist "Record your September 2026 predicted-grade baseline".
Problem: Most IB schools issue official predicted grades in DP2 for university applications, not in September of DP1, which is about one month into the course. A student following the checklist will have no baseline to record, so the "non-negotiable" gate condition and the main academic safeguard cannot be checked. The plan needs a measurable substitute, such as school term grades or internal assessment marks, and it does not give one.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The capital table contradicts itself
Where: Section 9 tranches (₹25,000 + ₹1,00,000 + ₹1,50,000 + ₹1,25,000) and "Total cap ... ₹4,00,000–5,00,000"; Section 1 "Cap family capital at ₹5 lakh"; Tranche 2 "Coordinator salary buffer (6 months), course-content production".
Problem: The tranches add up to exactly ₹4,00,000, so the stated "4–5 lakh" cap and the ₹5 lakh headline cap do not match the table. Tranche 2 also cannot cover both of its uses: 6 months × ₹20–25k coordinator = ₹1.2–1.5 lakh, which leaves ₹0–30k for course content. The reader cannot tell what the actual limit is.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Phase 5 breaks for students whose 18th birthday falls after departure abroad
Where: Assumption 3 "turns 18 ... between late 2027 and late 2028"; Phase 5 "On turning 18: Incorporate ..." and "May–August 2028 (if going abroad): hire ... General Manager"; Memorandum "Within 90 days of [Student] turning 18".
Problem: A student born in Sept–Oct 2010 (allowed by the plan's own assumption) leaves for university in Aug–Sept 2028 while still 17. They cannot sign the GM contract or incorporate before leaving. Incorporation would then happen from abroad while they hold student-visa status (C9) and may be a non-resident under FEMA. The plan gives no steps for this case, which its own date range includes.
