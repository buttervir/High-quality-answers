ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The headline "₹1 lakh/month profit" math ignores the 18% GST and 2% gateway fees the plan itself says apply
Where: Section 3.2 "The path to ₹1 lakh a month in profit: 160 tutor-hours a month × ₹850 = ₹1,36,000 ... = ≈ ₹1,21,000 a month"; Section 3.3 Phase 2 "Private tutoring is taxable at 18%. Registration becomes mandatory once yearly turnover passes ₹20 lakh"; Week 2 "Razorpay or Cashfree account (about 2% fees)"; Scoreboard "Monthly revenue ... Month 12 ₹3L".
Problem: The plan's own scoreboard has revenue at ₹1.5L/month by month 6 and ₹3L by month 12, which is ₹36L a year, well above the ₹20L GST threshold. Yet the profit calculation never subtracts GST or gateway fees, and it never says whether the quoted prices (₹2,000–4,000/hour) include GST. Take the default principal model at the target volume: 160 hours × an average ₹2,800 = ₹4.48L gross. If prices include GST, 18/118 of that (≈ ₹68,000) goes to GST, and 2% (≈ ₹9,000) goes to the gateway. Profit falls from ₹1,21,000 to about ₹44,000, under half the "job-replacing" definition set in the Assumptions. Under the agent model, tutors bill parents directly. That weakens the "prepaid packages, agency owns the relationship" control the plan relies on in Section 8, and 18% GST still applies to the commission. The central claim that this replaces a job does not survive the plan's own tax facts.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The main defence against tutors taking clients is a clause that is largely unenforceable in India
Where: Week 3 "Tutor agreement: independent contractor; a 12-month non-solicitation clause stopping tutors from taking clients directly"; Section 8 risk table "Tutors take clients directly | Non-solicitation clause, ...".
Problem: Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 voids agreements in restraint of trade. Indian courts have consistently refused to enforce restrictions that apply after the contract ends against independent contractors (e.g. Superintendence Co. v. Krishan Murgai; Percept D'Mark v. Zaheer Khan). In a tutoring agency the tutor sees the family every week, and the family can simply rebook the tutor directly. The plan lists this as its first risk and leans on the clause as the first remedy. It gives no economic lock-in that works, such as tutor exclusivity bonuses or agency-owned curricula and diagnostics, to replace it. The plan also recruits "DP2 students" as tutors. Those are minors, and by the plan's own Fact 2 a minor's agreement is void, so even the rest of the tutor agreement fails for that group.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The milestones and stop rules contradict each other, and there is a gap with no decision in it
Where: Phase 1 milestone "15+ paying families ... 70% of families renewing" vs "If this fails: fewer than 5 families renewed by 31 March → stop Track A"; Tranche 2 "Only if the Phase 1 milestone is met"; Phase 2 "Hire a part-time operations coordinator ... 3 months of coordinator salary [from tranche 2]".
Problem: Counterexample: 9 paying families with 6 renewing on 31 March 2027. The milestone is missed, so tranche 2 is withheld and there is no money for the coordinator. The stop rule does not fire either, so Track A continues. Phase 2 is built entirely on "hire people to replace the founder's time". Without the coordinator the student either goes over the 10-hour cap during DP1 exams and IAs or the business stalls, and the plan says nothing about which. The dates also clash. Section 3.2 says ₹1L profit is plausible "within 12–15 months", but the Phase 2 milestone demands it by 31 Aug 2027 (month 10). The scoreboard puts it at month 12. Stop rule 2 only fires at "₹50,000 by 31 December 2027" (month 14). Missing the Phase 2 milestone has no stated consequence, while Phase 3's digital-resources launch depends on tranche 3, which is withheld in that case.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The "skill income in the student's name" money flow cannot happen as written
Where: Week 1 "minor's savings account ... receives the student's personal teaching income"; Week 2 "Razorpay or Cashfree account ... and set up UPI for the business, both in the parent's name"; Phase 1 "the income counts as skill income in the founder's own name"; Section 6 table.
Problem: Every diagnostic leads to an agency package ("10 hours at the full price"), paid through the parent's gateway and current account. So the founder's teaching hours are sold and collected as part of the parent's proprietorship. That income is the parent's business receipts, not the minor's skill income. Moving it later to the minor's account is a gift, which the plan's own table says is clubbed if it earns anything further. The plan never says how a parent is billed separately for the founder's own hours, under whose invoice, or how that fits the GST registration in the parent's name. The user cannot carry out the tax structure the plan promises without guessing.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] It recruits tutors on IB scores that do not yet exist
Where: Weeks 5–6 "DP2 students who scored 7 in specific subjects (for Grade 8–10 work)".
Problem: In October–November 2026, DP2 students are in their final year and sit their IB exams in May 2027. They have no awarded IB grades, only school predicted grades. The screening criterion as written cannot be checked. It has to become "predicted 7" or "IGCSE A*", and those carry different credibility with parents paying ₹2,000+ an hour.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The grade-protection trigger depends on data the student will not regularly get
Where: Week 1 and Section 7 "if any predicted grade drops by 1 point, business hours are halved until it recovers"; Scoreboard row "IB predicted grades | No drop" at months 2, 6 and 12.
Problem: Most schools issue formal IB predicted grades only once or twice, usually in DP2 for university applications, not on a monthly or weekly basis during DP1. The only safeguard for the IB, which the task context says is ahead of the student, is therefore a rule that cannot fire for most of DP1. The plan needs a measurable trigger such as school assessment or term grades, or missed internal-assessment deadlines.
