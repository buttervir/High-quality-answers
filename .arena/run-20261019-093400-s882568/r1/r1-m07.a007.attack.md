ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The headline "job replacing" number fails once the plan's own GST and tax rules are applied
Where: "that reaches about ₹1,10,000 a month in profit ... close to a ₹12–13 lakh a year salary"; target table "Fees: 30 × ₹6,500 = ₹1,95,000"; GST section "register once turnover crosses ₹20 lakh ... after that you charge 18%"; Tax section "set aside about 35%".
Problem: The target state runs at ₹1,95,000 × 12 = ₹23.4 lakh a year, above the ₹20 lakh GST threshold. So the steady state the plan aims for is GST-registered by its own rule, not an edge case. At fixed fees, the GST inside them is 1,95,000 × 18/118 = ₹29,746 a month, so profit falls from ₹1,10,000 to about ₹80,000. (The alternative, raising fees 18% to ₹7,670, undoes the Step 1 price test.) The plan then says Step 4 profit is clubbed at the parent's top rate and tells you to set aside 35%, which leaves about ₹52,000 a month. Meanwhile a ₹12 LPA salary is close to tax-free under the new regime (the s.87A rebate covers taxable income up to ₹12 lakh, plus the ₹75,000 standard deduction), so its in-hand pay is roughly ₹90,000+ a month. The plan compares pre-tax, pre-GST profit with a post-tax salary. Measured on the same after-tax basis, the plan reaches about 55–60% of the income it claims, so the task's core requirement ("job replacing money") is not met by the numbers given.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] Annual cohort churn is ignored, so the ramp to 30 students and the "3 hours a week" DP2 role don't hold
Where: Step 2 "Grow to 3 groups (9 students)" of "MYP 4–5 / IGCSE / Grade 9–10"; Step 3 "its 16 students are where your DP2-year groups will come from"; Step 4 "Hand over your own 3 groups".
Problem: Grade 10 customers sit MYP eAssessment or IGCSE in May and then leave the Grade 9–10 product. The plan says demand "peaks January to May" and then gives no churn figure. In practice about half the base leaves every May: in May 2027, just before the handover, and again in May 2028, during the founder's own finals, when Step 5 forbids taking new students. The 16 bridge-course students are rising DP1 students, so they don't feed the Grade 9–10 groups the plan prices and staffs. They need a different product (DP Maths AA/AI tutoring), DP-qualified tutors and a syllabus, and none of that is specified. Getting from 9 students to 30 between June and October 2027 therefore means replacing the leavers and recruiting about 25 new students, mostly in a product line the plan never defines. Marketing on that scale doesn't fit in "3 hours a week".

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The FY 2027–28 GST estimate of "about ₹16 lakh" is understated, and the ₹15 lakh trigger lands in the middle of DP2 exams
Where: "In this plan, FY 2027–28 turnover comes to about ₹16 lakh. If the running total in your sheet reaches ₹15 lakh, ask the CA to register".
Problem: Adding up the plan's own months for FY 2027–28 (Apr 2027 to Mar 2028): April 2027 groups ₹58,500; the bridge course ₹2,40,000; the existing groups in May–June about ₹1,17,000; a July–September ramp from 4 to 10 groups of about ₹3,90,000; and October–March at ₹1,95,000 × 6 = ₹11,70,000. That totals about ₹19.7 lakh, right at the ₹20 lakh threshold, not ₹16 lakh. The ₹15 lakh trigger would fire around January–February 2028, when the founder is doing mocks and final IA/EE submissions. Also, "register (in a parent's name)" puts the supply under the parent's PAN. If that parent already runs a business, which is likely for an $8M family, aggregate turnover is counted per PAN, so 18% GST could apply from the first rupee. The plan doesn't flag this.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The grade stop rule depends on a number that usually doesn't exist in October of DP1
Where: Stop rules: "if any predicted grade drops 1 point below where it was in October 2026, halve your hours".
Problem: Most Mumbai IB schools give no IB predicted grades in October of DP1. Predicted grades usually come in DP2 for university applications, and DP1 term reports often use school-specific scales. The only safeguard the plan gives for the student's diploma, which it calls more important than the business, therefore has no baseline. The user would have to guess what to measure. The rule should name an actual data point, such as the DP1 end-of-term-1 report grade for each subject.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] Tutor cost assumes 8 hours a month for twice-weekly sessions, which understates cost
Where: "Tutors: 10 groups × 8 hrs × ₹1,000 = −₹80,000"; product is "two 1-hour online sessions a week".
Problem: Two sessions a week over 52 weeks gives 104 hours a year, or 8.67 hours a month per group. Fees are charged per month ("₹6,500 a month ... paid in advance for 8 sessions"), so either families get only 8 of the roughly 8.67 sessions a calendar month holds, which the schedule "Mon and Thu" does not reflect, or tutors are paid for about 8.67 hours. In the second case cost is ₹86,700, not ₹80,000. The same gap affects the line "every extra group adds ₹11,500" (it is actually about ₹10,800). The schedule and the billing unit need to agree. As written, a "Mon and Thu" group in a month with 9 such days breaks the "8 sessions" promise.
