# The plan: ₹1 lakh a month during DP2, from one business that runs without the student after school ends

## Assumptions
- **"Job replacing money" = ₹1,00,000 a month (₹12 lakh a year).** That beats most starting graduate salaries in Mumbai. It is also the most a resident individual can earn under India's new tax regime and pay no income tax, thanks to the rebate. That is why I chose this number.
- **Timeline:** today is October 2026, so the student is about 2 months into DP1. DP2 final exams are in May 2028.
- **The IB Diploma comes first.** Final grades decide university options, and those are worth more than any income a teenager can earn. So the business gets **8 hours a week at most**.
- **The student scored a 7 or an A/A\* in Grade 9–10 Maths.** If not, use their strongest subject that has a Cambridge IGCSE syllabus with past papers. The plan works the same way.
- **Legal and tax points** reflect current Indian rules as I understand them. The family's CA should confirm them once, in Week 1.

---

## 1. The finished result (March 2028)

- **3 batches of 4 students each, in Cambridge IGCSE Extended Maths (0580)**, drawn from several Mumbai schools. Each batch has **one curriculum and one grade**:
  - **Batches A and B:** Grade 10, recruited as Grade 9s in 2026–27, so they stay for two years.
  - **Batch C:** Grade 9, recruited in June 2027.
- **Fee: ₹8,000 per student per month**, paid in advance. Each batch meets **2 × 1 hour a week, online**. Total: 12 × ₹8,000 = **₹96,000 a month**.
- **Workload:** 6 teaching hours a week plus 2 hours of prep and admin. One lesson plan per grade is reused.
- **One vetted substitute tutor**, engaged by a parent, teaches during mocks and finals.
- **A waitlist of at least 4 families**, which covers students who drop out mid-year.
- **Paperwork:** the student's bank account and PAN, one spreadsheet, one tax return a year. No company, no GST, no app, no investors.
- **Proof it is real:** **₹90,000 or more collected in each of January, February and March 2028.** That window comes before Grade 10 students stop for their May–June IGCSEs.
- **A handover already designed (Section 7)**, so the income continues after May 2028 when the student leaves for university.

**What I left out, and why:**
- **No D2C brand, e-commerce, app or reselling.** Each needs inventory, GST, payment gateways, adult signatures or capital that could be lost. A minor cannot sign a binding contract in India (Indian Contract Act s.11), so every extra counterparty becomes a parent's job.
- **No use of the $8M as capital.** What is scarce here is time, trust and skill, not money. The family's real advantage is its **network**: friends whose children attend IGCSE schools.

## 3. The steps

### Week 1 (now): setup, about 4 hours
1. **Fix the product.** It is IGCSE 0580 Extended Maths for Grade 9 students, and nothing else. A single syllabus has one topic order and one past-paper bank, which official Cambridge papers cover for every topic. Mixing MYP or Grade 10 students into these batches would break the weekly routine.
2. **School check (pass/fail).** Ask the DP coordinator whether students may do paid tutoring outside school.
   - **Allowed:** proceed. If they object to tutoring pupils from your own school, recruit only from other schools.
   - **Allowed with written permission:** a parent writes a letter stating the 8-hour cap and the rule on grades (Section 4). Proceed once it is approved.
   - **Banned:** tutor a free Grade 9 group instead. Because it is unpaid, it counts as **CAS Service**. Build the same results, notes and testimonials, then launch the paid batches in May 2028, after finals. Section 7 still applies.
   - Paid tutoring never counts as CAS.
3. **Bank account (ask first, then open).** Ask Kotak, HDFC or ICICI, in writing, for the self-operated minor account's limits on monthly credits, balance and UPI/NEFT receipts. RBI allows minors over 10 to operate accounts themselves, but each bank sets its own caps.
   - **Caps of ₹1.5 lakh/month or more:** open it. The student operates it.
   - **Lower caps:** open a **guardian-operated minor's account** instead. It is still in the student's name and under the student's PAN, so the income is still the student's.
   - Either way, fees go into this account and nothing else does.
4. **PAN for the student:** apply on Form 49A (NSDL/Protean). A parent signs as representative. It costs about ₹110.
5. **Equipment, ₹40,000 at most:** a drawing tablet (Wacom One or an old iPad), a USB mic, Zoom Pro.
6. **The offer, as one WhatsApp message:**
   > "I'm [Name], DP1 at [School], A* in IGCSE Maths. I'm starting a small-group class for **Grade 9 IGCSE Extended Maths (0580)**: 4 students max, 2 × 1 hr/week online, Tue/Thu 6–7 pm, running through the IGCSE exam in Grade 10. ₹8,000/month, paid in advance. **Free demo Saturday [date], 11 am.**"

### Weeks 2–6: Batch A
7. **Parents send the offer** to about 30 families with **Grade 9 IGCSE** children. Use personal messages and ask each family for one more name. **Target: 8 at the demo.**
8. **The demo:** 60 minutes, 20 of them on a hard Grade 9 topic (for example simultaneous equations). End with a 5-question quiz and send each parent their child's score that evening.
9. **Close:** "4 seats, first 4 to pay for [month] get them." Payment by UPI or NEFT.
10. **Decision rule:**
    - **4 pay:** the batch is full.
    - **2–3 pay:** start anyway. Fill the empty seats at the next demo.
    - **0–1 pay:** repeat the demo with a new list at the same price. If that fails too, change **every** batch to **5 students at ₹6,500**. 3 batches × 5 × ₹6,500 = **₹97,500**: same hours, same 3 batches, same substitute cost.

### Dec 2026 to May 2027: run it, open Batch B
11. **Fixed routine.** Classes follow the plan's own 0580 sequence, not any single school's calendar. Topics come in the order most IGCSE schools teach them, and that order is shared with parents at signup.
    - **Tuesday:** new topic.
    - **Thursday:** official past-paper questions on that topic.
    - **Last class of the month:** a 15-minute test.
12. **Progress note on the 1st of each month:** 3 lines per child (test score, what improved, what's next), sent with the invoice. This note drives referrals. Never skip it.
13. **Fees by the 1st, before class.** A student who has not paid by the 5th loses the seat to the waitlist. With no credit there is never a debt to enforce against anyone.
14. **Batch B (Grade 9):** run a second demo once Batch A has been full for 2 months, around March 2027. **Target: 8 students, ₹64,000/month by May 2027.**

### Jun–Dec 2027 (DP2 begins): Batch C, substitute, freeze
15. **June intake:** families plan tutoring for the new school year in June. Run a demo for the **incoming Grade 9s** and fill Batch C. A and B move up to Grade 10 together, so nobody leaves. Total: 12 students, ₹96,000/month.
16. **Engage the substitute by October 2027.** Pick a strong DP2 student from another school, or a first-year maths undergraduate.
    - **Engaging:** a **parent** engages them with a one-page letter (rate ₹500/hour, paid monthly, either side can end it with 2 weeks' notice). A minor cannot validly contract.
    - **Checks:** verify a college or school ID and call 2 references, one of them a teacher.
    - **Paying:** pay by UPI from the business account. Log each payment in the spreadsheet as an expense.
    - **Training:** for one month they shadow the student and teach the Thursday classes. After that they can cover any batch.
17. **Freeze growth.** New families go on the waitlist. No Batch 4 during DP2, because EE, IAs and TOK need that time.

### Jan–May 2028: prove it
18. **Mocks (Jan–Feb) and finals (May):** the substitute teaches all batches.
    - Cost: about 24 hours × ₹500 = ₹12,000 in a full month.
    - Net in that month: about ₹84,000.
19. **The proof target:** ₹90,000 or more collected in each of January, February and March 2028. Grade 10s keep studying into April, then stop for their exams.
20. **May 2028:** run the June-intake demo during finals week through parents' WhatsApp, or have the substitute run it. Batch A and B students have finished, so this demo recruits 8 new Grade 9s.

## 5. Money, tax and law

- **Whose income it is.** A minor's income is normally added to a parent's. The exception is income from the minor's own **skill or specialised knowledge**, which tutoring is: the old Act's s.64(1A) proviso, carried into the Income-tax Act 2025. So the fees are the **student's own income**, taxed under the new regime.
- **Tax.** Up to ₹12 lakh a year, the rebate means no income tax. The family's CA still files the student's **ITR every July**.
- **GST:** no registration needed below **₹20 lakh a year** in receipts.
- **No company or LLP** before 18.
- **Child safety:**
  - Groups only, online, cameras on, Zoom waiting room on.
  - Parents get the link and may watch any class.
  - When the substitute teaches, the student or a parent of the student joins as **Zoom co-host** for the whole call.
  - No 1-to-1 sessions and no in-person sessions.
- **Records:** one spreadsheet. Income columns are Date | Student | Parent | Amount | Mode | Month. Expense columns are Date | Payee | Amount | Purpose. Keep every receipt.

**Total cash risked: about ₹40,000** of equipment the student keeps whatever happens.

---

## 6. Every June: replace the leavers

Each June, the Grade 10 batches (about 8 students) finish their IGCSEs and leave. This is planned, not a shock:
- Run **one Grade 9 demo every May or June**, filled through current parents and waitlist families.
- That demo refills the batches that have just finished.
- The Grade 9 batch from the year before moves up to Grade 10.
- Income in June and July can dip by up to half. The table in Section 7 counts the business at 3 full batches, so treat June and July as lower months.

---

## 7. After May 2028: turn it into income that does not need the student's evenings

The student turns 18 around this time and probably leaves for university, possibly abroad. The business has to run without the student teaching. These steps happen in **June–July 2028**:

1. **Register a sole proprietorship** in the student's name: Udyam registration and a current account. Payments move to UPI on that account.
2. **Hire 2 tutors at ₹600/hour.** The substitute can be one of them. Use the same checks and a one-page agreement, which the student, now 18, signs.
3. **Hire a part-time coordinator in Mumbai at about ₹15,000/month** for about 10 hours a week. A trusted family member or a hired assistant can do it. The job:
   - collect fees by the 1st
   - run the waitlist
   - book the June demo
   - send the monthly notes, which the student drafts.
4. **The student's remaining job, about 3 hours a week, remote:** write and update the course sequence and tests, review each month's notes, interview tutors, and look at the spreadsheet.
5. **Grow to 5 batches** (20 students), filled from the waitlist and the June demo.

| Monthly, 5 batches | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Fees (20 × ₹8,000) | 1,60,000 |
| Tutors (10 hrs/week × 4.33 × ₹600) | –26,000 |
| Coordinator | –15,000 |
| Zoom and tools | –2,000 |
| **Net to the student** | **about 1,17,000** |

- **Tax and GST.** Above ₹12 lakh net a year, some income tax is due (roughly ₹80,000–₹1 lakh a year at this level). The CA files. Fees reach about ₹19.2 lakh a year, so **register for GST before adding a 6th batch.**
- **If studying abroad:** before doing *any* work for the business from abroad, get a one-time opinion from an immigration adviser on the student's visa work rules. Many student visas restrict work but not owning a business. If the answer is restrictive, the coordinator also takes over the student's 3 hours, and a parent signs off on the course content. The student is then purely the owner.
- **Family money comes in only now,** for example on ads or a second subject, and only if 19+ months of records show that a paying family is worth more than it costs to win one.
