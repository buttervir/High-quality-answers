ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The worked pass-test example uses revenue that the plan's own DP2 rules make impossible
Where: Step 5 "The pass test": "July (₹1,02,500) + August (≈ ₹40,000, as term restarts mid-month) + September (≈ ₹44,500) averages about ₹62,000"; Step 6 point 1 "July–August 2027 (summer, up to 20 hours a week)"; point 2 and the calendar row "Sep–Oct 2027 | Maintenance mode | ≤5".
Problem: The ₹44,500 September figure comes from the 34-hour term budget (8 h/week, the student teaching both groups and keeping all ₹28,000). But Step 6 caps September–October 2027 at 5 h/week (about 21 h/month) and hands the groups to helpers who take 40%. That leaves the student about ₹16,800 of the ₹28,000 tutoring revenue. Redone under the plan's own rules, September comes to about ₹16,800 + ₹7,500 retainers + ₹9,000 kit average, which is about ₹33,000, not ₹44,500. The dates also contradict each other. Assumption 3 puts the summer break in June–July and DP2 starting in August, yet Step 6 treats August as "summer, up to 20 hours a week", and Step 5 has August "as term restarts mid-month". The one worked proof that the ₹50k "job-replacing" line can be reached uses numbers the plan forbids elsewhere. The user can't tell which hour cap and which revenue split apply in August–October 2027, and that is the core test window.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] It never produces recurring "job replacing money", only a 3-month average that depends on one holiday spike
Where: Task: "earn 'job replacing money', meaning income large enough to replace a job." Plan: "So in term, 8 hours a week tops out at about ₹45,000, not ₹50,000"; "1 January 2028 until your last exam (May 2028): zero hours"; the pass test is "3 consecutive months ... average ₹50,000".
Problem: A job pays every month. By the plan's own numbers, every term month earns less than its own ₹50k line, and January–May 2028 is planned at zero income for the student. The only way to "pass" is to average one summer month of ₹1,02,500 (which needs the once-a-year head-start cohort) against term months under the line. That proves the student can sell, but it is not income that replaces a job. The plan also never gives a concrete path to recurring job-level income before 18. Step 8 only says to "upgrade properly" and lists funding milestones ("₹2 lakh MRR"). It gives no steps, pricing or hours for getting from about ₹33–45k/month to a stable ₹50k+/month. So the headline requirement is redefined into a proxy and then only half met.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The grade tripwire has no usable baseline on the dates the plan sets
Where: Family Venture Agreement clause 4: "The baseline: the last report before the venture starts (for most students, the DP1 Term 1 report, around Oct–Dec 2026)"; the calendar sets selling from "Early Nov 2026"; the checklist says "this week: Write down your grade baseline (latest report, each subject on the 1–7 scale)"; the outreach message says "(currently a [7] in HL [Maths AA])".
Problem: The plan starts on 19 Oct 2026 and selling starts in early November. A DP1 Term 1 report usually arrives in December, so for most students the "last report before the venture starts" is a Grade 10 IGCSE/ICSE/MYP result. That is on an A*–G, percentage or MYP 1–8 scale, in different subjects, and there are no "school's own published grade boundaries" for converting it to IB 1–7 in six DP subjects. For this case, a student whose last report is IGCSE (A* Maths, A Physics, ...), the clause can't be applied. The first real DP grades then look like "drops" or "rises" against a made-up conversion. The same gap breaks the WhatsApp script, which asks for a current DP grade that doesn't exist yet. The tripwire is what the plan calls its "most important defensive step", and as written it can't be run.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The January–May 2028 continuity plan relies on helpers who may be sitting the same exams
Where: Step 6 point 1: "recruit up to 2 helpers. These are strong students from the year below (incoming DP1) or from your own year"; wind-down: "A group continues only if its helper has already taught it alone for 4 weeks ... One parent collects fees and pays helpers ... Jan–May 2028".
Problem: A helper "from your own year" is a DP2 student with the same EE/IA deadlines, the same January mocks and the same May 2028 finals that the plan uses to justify zero hours for the founder. The plan explicitly allows recruiting them, then relies on them to keep paid groups running through exactly that period. The four-week "proven helper" test checks teaching quality, not whether the helper is available January–May. The result: groups take January fees on the strength of a helper who drops out in March, and the student's parent is left owing refunds. The plan has no rule for this (helpers must be incoming DP1 only, or every January–May fee must carry a refund clause).

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] "3 retainers" by April–May 2027 assumes every kit client, including the free pilot, converts
Where: Calendar: "Jan–Mar 2027 ... 1 paid Shop Online Kit by 31 Mar"; "Apr–May 2027 | 3 retainers"; the Step 5 term budget uses "3 retainer clients × 1 h | ₹7,500".
Problem: Retainers are sold as an optional add-on to kit clients. By the end of May 2027 the plan schedules at most: the 1 free pilot (February), 1 paid kit (by 31 March), and 1 more kit in April–May (the "1 kit every 2 months" pace). That is 3 kit clients at most, so 3 retainers needs a 100% conversion rate, including the client who got everything free. The plan doesn't say where retainer clients come from if conversion is lower, or whether retainers can be sold without a kit. ₹7,500 of every month's modelled revenue therefore sits on an unstated best case.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] It wrongly says up-front payment makes the venture "legal for a minor"
Where: Approach C: "It's legal for a minor if payments are collected up front"; Step 2 test 1: "Legal for a minor if paid up front?"
Problem: Under s.11 of the Contract Act and Mohori Bibee, which the plan itself cites, the minor's agreement is void whatever the payment timing. Paying up front only removes the student's need to sue. It does not make the contract valid, and it does not protect the student from the other side's claims or disputes over refunds (which the plan itself promises for May blackouts and unfinished kits). A user relying on the "legal" claim will think ₹18,000 kit and retainer deals in their own name are binding when they are not. Those deals should be in a parent's name, or the risk should be stated correctly.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] It misstates the resident-director rule, and the hour cap mixes weekly and monthly limits
Where: Step 8: "Section 149(3) ... at least one director who stayed in India for 182 days or more in the previous calendar year"; clause 5: "At most 8 hours a week ... If a month's log goes over the cap".
Problem: Since the Companies (Amendment) Act, 2017, s.149(3) tests 182 days in the financial year, not the previous calendar year. That matters for a student who flies out in August–September 2028, which is mid-financial-year. Separately, the cap is set per week but enforced per month. The term budget hits exactly 34 hours only by averaging a 12-hour kit over 2 months. In a month when a kit is delivered, the student actually works 22 + 12 = 34 hours, which uses all of the "6 hour buffer" and is about 7.9 h/week. Any overrun breaks the weekly cap, while the monthly check still shows it as compliant.
