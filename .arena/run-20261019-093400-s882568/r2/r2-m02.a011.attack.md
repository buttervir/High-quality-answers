ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The exam-month clause covers 3 months a year, but the plan's own 2 h/week windows last about 5 months in DP2
Where: Section 5 Phase 2, "In up to 3 named months a year, service runs in 'lite' mode"; Section 5 Phase 4 time table, "Mock and finals windows (2 h/week) ... Yes, because of the exam-month clause"; Rule 2 table.
Problem: Rule 2 sets 2 h/week for "6 weeks before DP2 mocks, and during them". With mocks in December 2027 to January 2028 (A1), that runs from about late October to late January, roughly 3 months. It also sets 2 h/week from "Mid-March 2028 to the end of IB finals", roughly 2 to 2.5 months. That makes about 4.5 to 5.5 capped months between August 2027 and July 2028, against 3 lite months in the contract. In the 1.5 to 2.5 uncovered months, 8 clients are owed full service. That is about 13 h of delivery plus 5 h of admin, roughly 18 h a month, against a 9 h/month cap. The student must either break Rule 2, which triggers a red cell (Section 7), or under-deliver on full-fee contracts. So the "Fits? Yes" row is false for exactly the months the plan calls most dangerous. Gate 4 also falls inside this period: 3 consecutive months up to July 2028 almost all overlap these windows.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] Gate 4 can be passed at about ₹43k of real profit, below the plan's own definition of job-replacing money
Where: Gate 4, "₹50,000 or more a month in after-tax profit, measured on contracted full-price retainers, with actual cash collected at 85% or more of that"; Section 3 worked numbers, "8 clients at ₹7k average, about ₹5k of tools and travel → ~₹51k".
Problem: Take the plan's own solo case: contracted revenue is 8 × ₹7k = ₹56k. At the allowed 85% collection, cash is ₹47.6k. Minus the ₹5k of costs, that leaves about ₹42.6k of actual profit, and the gate counts this as a pass. The task's goal is "job replacing money", and A5 sets the floor at "₹50k–1 lakh a month of after-tax profit, not revenue". The headline success measure therefore counts contracted money that was never received. This also matters for the odds: the 5–8% figure is the probability of passing this looser test, not of earning ₹50k.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] "Summer leverage, June–July 2027" falls in school term for the June-start Mumbai schools the plan itself allows
Where: A1, "DP1 started June–August 2026"; Phase 3, "Summer leverage (June – July 2027) ... up to 20 h/week on the business"; Gate 3 (31 July 2027): ₹40k MRR, ₹35k profit.
Problem: Many Mumbai IB schools start in June, and A1 allows for this. Their long break is roughly May to early June, which sits on top of the DP1 internal exam window (April–May, capped at 3 h/week with no selling). For these students, June–July 2027 is DP2 term time, so the cap is 6 h/week (Rule 2), not 20. A1's fix, "Shift every date by the same offset", does not help, because the problem is where the holiday falls, not when the calendar starts. Gate 3 asks for growth from ₹25k to ₹40k MRR in 8 weeks. That growth depends on the 20 h/week holiday block, so for this student it is not reachable. That breaks the chain behind the ~11% and ~5–6% odds.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The Plus tier's 2.5 h/month includes an on-site shoot but no Mumbai travel time, and the DP2 budget is already at its cap
Where: Phase 2 tier table, "Plus ... a monthly 1-hour photo shoot on site ... ~2.5 h"; Phase 4 time table, "DP2 normal term (6 h/week) | ~26 | ~13 | ~5 | ~8 | Yes".
Problem: The ₹7k average needed for Gate 4 requires about half the clients on Plus, so about 4 shoots a month. A round trip across Bandra, Andheri or Powai in Mumbai traffic takes about 1 to 2 hours, which adds about 4 to 8 h a month. The DP2 budget already adds up to 26 h against a ~26 h cap (13 + 5 + 8). Adding the travel puts it at 30–34 h, about 15–30% over Rule 2, every normal month. "Hours over cap 2 months running" then triggers "Drop the lowest-paying client" (Section 7). That pushes the client count below the 8 that Gate 4 depends on.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] The odds and profit tables contradict the plan's own funnel and arithmetic
Where: Section 6, "₹25k+ profit sustained at mid-2028 ... about 20–25%"; "₹25,000 | ~5–6 Standard clients, solo".
Problem: (a) Only about 27% pass Gate 2, and that gate requires only about ₹21k of profit. Section 6 also says "the most common pattern is a strong start that fades in DP2", and only 35–45% of Gate 2 passers reach Gate 3. Yet 20–25% are said to hold ₹25k or more through DP2. That needs almost every Gate 2 passer to grow and hold, which the plan's own conditional figures rule out. (b) 5–6 Standard clients at ₹4–6k, minus the plan's ~₹5k costs, gives ₹15k–31k, with a midpoint of 5.5 × ₹5k − ₹5k = ₹22.5k. That is below the ₹25k the row claims.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The pre-Gate-1 budget is stated three different ways
Where: Rule 3, "At most ₹2 lakh of family money until Gate 1"; Phase 0, "Phase 1 budget at ₹75,000"; Gate 1, "unlock up to ₹1.25 lakh more (₹2 lakh total)"; Phase 1, "at most ₹20,000 on tools and printing" vs Section 6, tools ₹10k + printing ₹5k = ₹15k.
Problem: The family cannot tell whether the cap before Gate 1 is ₹75k or ₹2L. If it is ₹2L before Gate 1, then passing Gate 1 unlocks nothing new, so the gate does no work. The tools-and-printing line is also ₹20k in one place and ₹15k in another. A family enforcing "Freeze spending" at over 100% of the cap (Section 7) has to guess which number is the cap.
