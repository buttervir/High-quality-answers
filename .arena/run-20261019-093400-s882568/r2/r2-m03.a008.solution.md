# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old DP1 Student in Mumbai

## 1. The answer in one paragraph

Start an **IB-specialist tutoring and exam-bootcamp business for Mumbai's international-school students** (IGCSE / MYP / early DP). Begin by tutoring yourself. Then grow it into a small agency of vetted tutors (IB graduates aged **18 and over, with the right to work**), with paid group bootcamps held only in exam seasons. Until you are 18, run it legally through a **sole proprietorship in one parent's name**, because a minor cannot sign enforceable contracts in India. Your own tutoring income goes into **your own PAN and bank account**. Cap family capital at **exactly ₹5 lakh (about US$5,700, under 0.1% of net worth) until you turn 18**, and release it in tranches only when the business hits written targets.

There are two honest targets, kept separate:
- **Business profit ≥ ₹1 lakh/month (Level 1).** In the base case, the first full year after GST registration (**Aug 2027–Jul 2028**) averages about **₹1.15 lakh/month**, after 18% GST, a paid coordinator and two months of a General Manager. The first month at or above ₹1 lakh is **Nov 2027**, once 9 tutors are active. The margin is thin: about **12% fewer tutor-hours, or the lower price tier, puts you below Level 1** (Section 8.4). The conservative case earns about **₹33k/month** and does **not** reach Level 1 while you are at school. Gate 2 (31 May 2027) tells you which case you are in.
- **Your own personal income before 18** depends on the phase: about **₹24k → ₹28k → ₹43k → ₹19k → ₹5k per month** as school pressure rises (Section 8.5). The proprietorship's retained profit is **your parent's money**. Section 8.6 says exactly how, and how not, it can become yours. What becomes yours is the **business itself**, moved into a company in which you own 75%.

Your IB grades act as a hard brake on all of this.

## 2. Assumptions (where the request is ambiguous)

1. **"Job replacing money"** means recurring **net profit** (after GST, costs and staff, before income tax), averaged over 12 months because tutoring is seasonal:
   - **Level 1: ₹1,00,000/month** (₹12 lakh/year). That is above typical entry-level graduate pay in India.
   - **Level 2: ₹3,00,000/month** (₹36 lakh/year). That is closer to a well-paid Mumbai professional.

   Level 1 is measured on the **business**: the proprietorship's profit before your founder fee, plus your own tutoring. Both are pay for your founding work. It is always measured **as if GST-registered**, because registration is certain at base-case volume. Your **personal** cash is reported separately (Section 8.5).
2. You started DP1 in mid-2026. IB final exams are in **May 2028**, results arrive in early July 2028, and university applications run from mid-2027 to early 2028.
3. You turn 18 at some point **between late 2027 and late 2028**. The plan works for **any** birthday in that range (Phase 5).
4. Your family supports the plan, and a parent acts as the legal operator. You are **not** being asked to manage family wealth.
5. Exchange rate: **₹88 ≈ US$1**. All Mumbai prices are **estimates that you verify in Phase 1** against at least 10 real parent quotes.
6. This is not legal or tax advice. Every legal or tax step is marked for confirmation by a Chartered Accountant (CA) or Company Secretary (CS). Budget about ₹30–40k/year once GST is registered.

## 3. The hard constraints (they drive everything)

| # | Constraint | Exact implication |
|---|---|---|

In DP2 it is **≤4 hrs/week**, falling to **0–1 hr/week from 15 Feb 2028**. **This is the tightest constraint.** Every schedule below is budgeted hour by hour inside it. When any instruction conflicts with the cap, **the cap wins**. |

A minor cannot be a company director (a DIN requires majority), and **cannot subscribe to a company's Memorandum**. | An adult holds the contracts until you are 18. A minor **can** hold **fully paid** shares through a guardian once they are acquired after incorporation (Phase 5). |

| C4 | **Income-tax clubbing.** A minor's income is added to the parent's income, **except** income from manual work or from "skill, talent, specialised knowledge and experience". This was s.64(1A) of the 1961 Act; the Income-tax Act 2025 (in force from 1 April 2026) carries the rule over, and your CA confirms the section number. Clubbing **stops at 18**. | Fees you personally earn by tutoring are yours and taxed on your own return. Under the new regime, the rebate makes income up to ₹12 lakh effectively nil-tax. **Profit from other tutors' work is the parent's business income**, taxed at the parent's slab rate. | C5 | **GST.** Registration is mandatory once **aggregate turnover in a financial year (1 April–31 March)** exceeds **₹20 lakh** per PAN for services in Maharashtra. You apply within 30 days of becoming liable. Private coaching is taxed at **18%**. | The proprietorship sits under the PAN of a parent **with no other business turnover**. | C6 | **Capital is not a constraint.** The family has about US$8M (≈₹70 crore). | The risk is **overspending**. Money cannot buy time, experience or customer evidence, so capital is released only against evidence. |
| C7 | **IB academic integrity.** | Never write, substantially edit or "polish" anyone's IA, EE or TOK work. Never sell IB past papers or markschemes (IBO copyright). Breaking either rule could cost **your own diploma**. |
| C8 | **Child safety and data.** Customers are minors. The DPDP Act 2023 and its 2025 Rules (phasing in through 2027) require verifiable parental consent to process children's data. POCSO applies. | The parent is always the paying, consenting customer. You need a written safeguarding code, sessions recorded or held in a common area, minimal data collection, and **tutors who are adults only**. |
| C9 | **Studying abroad and visas.** On a US F-1 or UK Student visa you generally **cannot actively work**, and that includes remote freelancing for an Indian business. Passive ownership is usually fine. Studying abroad also typically makes you a **non-resident under FEMA**. | This applies to **you** from about Aug–Sept 2028, so the business must run without you by then. It applies **equally to tutors**: anyone on those visas is excluded from the tutor pool. Confirm with an immigration adviser and a CA. |
| C10 | **Location: Mumbai.** | There is a dense cluster of IB and Cambridge schools (Bandra–Juhu–Andheri, Powai, Lower Parel–Worli, BKC, Malabar Hill) and a large paid-tuition culture. Commute times make **online-first** delivery the only way to fit C1. |

**Design rule from C1 and C2:** choose a business that (a) produces cash with under 8 hrs/week of your time, (b) needs no contract signed by you, (c) uses an advantage only a current IB student has, and (d) can be handed to a manager by mid-2028.

## 5. The business, exactly

**Working name:** something neutral, such as "[YourName] Academic Studio" or "Bridge to IB". **Do not** use your school's name or the IB logo, because "International Baccalaureate" and "IB" are IBO trademarks. Describe the service in words ("tutoring for IB Diploma students") without implying endorsement.

**Customers:** parents of students at Mumbai IB, IGCSE and international schools.
1. **Grade 9–10 (IGCSE/MYP):** subject tutoring and "DP readiness".
2. **DP1 students a year behind you**, and DP2 students only in subjects where you are clearly ahead. Otherwise use an alumni tutor.
3. **Exam seasons, and therefore the only bootcamp windows:** Dec–Jan (DP2 and IGCSE mocks) and Mar–Apr (before the IB, IGCSE and MYP May exams). That gives **4 bootcamp cohorts a year** and none outside these windows. DP Readiness runs in **June–July only**.

**Products and prices.** All proprietorship prices are **GST-inclusive** from launch. Before registration the proprietorship keeps the whole price. After registration, 18/118 of it goes to GST, and customers see no change.

| Product | Delivered by | Price to parent (incl. GST) | Net of 18% GST | Paid to tutor | Margin after registration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1 tutoring, you personally (subjects where your school grade is 6–7) | You, on **your own PAN** (no GST at your turnover) | ₹1,400/hr | ₹1,400 | n/a | ~100% (your skill income) |
| 1:1 tutoring, alumni tutor (Section 5.1) | Tutor via the proprietorship | ₹2,100/hr | ₹1,779.66 | ₹1,200/hr | **₹579.66/hr** |
| Exam bootcamp (8 students, 8 sessions × 2 hrs = 16 hrs) | Tutor alone | ₹16,000/student | ₹13,559 | ₹2,000/hr × 16 = ₹32,000 | 8 × 13,559 − 32,000 = **₹76,475/cohort** |
| "DP Readiness" summer course (Grade 10 → DP1: subject choice, IA/EE/TOK orientation, study systems), 10 students | Tutor, with you teaching 2 sessions in the holidays | ₹21,000/student | ₹17,797 | ₹40,000/cohort | 10 × 17,797 − 40,000 = **₹1,37,966/cohort** |

**Price rule:** collect at least 10 real parent quotes in Phase 1, and keep alumni-tutor prices **15–30% below established adult IB tutors**. If adult tutors charge ₹2,500–3,000/hr, then ₹2,100 is 16–30% below them. If the quotes show adult tutors at or below ₹2,400, drop to ₹1,800 inclusive and tutor pay to ₹1,100. That gives a net margin of ₹425/hr, and **at that tier the base case misses Level 1** unless volume rises (Section 8.4).

**Never sell:** IA, EE or TOK writing or editing; grade promises; IB past papers. Your own notes, and explanations of the criteria in your own words, are fine.

### 5.1 Tutor supply: who is eligible, and is there enough of them

**Eligible (all must be true):**
1. **Aged 18 or over.** Check this against a government ID. No current school students, including DP2 seniors, because they are minors (C8).
2. An IB Diploma graduate (**class of 2025 or earlier**) with 40+ points or a 7 in the subject. **Fallback:** an Indian-resident adult with a degree in the subject, at the same pay.
3. **Has the right to do paid freelance work** from where they are. In practice that means they are in India for the whole engagement: at an Indian university (Ashoka, FLAME, St Xavier's, NMIMS, IITs and others), on a gap year, or working. **Excluded:** anyone holding a US F-1, UK Student or similar visa, including during their vacations. They declare their status in writing, and the tutor agreement includes a warranty and immediate termination if the declaration is false.

**Is the pool big enough?** Mumbai has roughly 30+ IB World Schools offering the DP (verify on ibo.org's "Find an IB World School"). Assume each graduates about 40 students a year, take 2 graduating classes, assume about 35% study in India, and assume about 25% scored 40+ or a 7 in a relevant subject: 30 × 40 × 2 × 0.35 × 0.25 ≈ **210 eligible people**. You need 6 tutors by April 2027 and 9 by November 2027, which means converting about 4% of the pool. The base case is sized on that. If **Gate 1** shows fewer than 2 eligible tutors recruited, switch to the subject-graduate fallback before Phase 2.

**Sourcing:** your school's alumni office and alumni WhatsApp/LinkedIn groups (graduates only), Indian university IB societies, LinkedIn searches ("IB Diploma" + Mumbai + an Indian university), and referrals from your first tutors (₹2,000 per referral who completes 20 hours).

**Money flow (keep it exactly like this):**
- **Your own tutees** pay **you** by UPI into your account. This is skill income on your PAN, and you file your own ITR (a guardian signs while you are a minor). Your own tutoring turnover will be well under ₹3 lakh/year, far below ₹20 lakh.
- **Alumni-tutor and bootcamp customers** pay the **proprietorship**. The proprietorship pays tutors against their invoices and keeps the margin, which is your parent's taxable business income.
- **Founder fee (hourly, so it stays arm's-length).** The proprietorship pays you **₹1,200 for each logged hour of management work**: the same rate it pays an adult alumni tutor, and below your own ₹1,400 tutoring rate. It is paid monthly against your Sunday hours log. The monthly **cap is ₹10,000 from Gate 1 and ₹25,000 from Gate 2**, and there is **no fee before Gate 1**. Because the fee always equals logged hours × a market rate, it remains defensible as a related-party payment (s.40A(2) of the 1961 Act or its 2025 equivalent) even when your hours fall in DP2. Your CA confirms whether it is your skill income or is clubbed with the parent's income; at these amounts the tax is small either way.
- **Retained profit** stays in the proprietorship's account and is recorded monthly on the board sheet. **It is your parent's after-tax money.** Section 8.6 sets out the only clean ways it can become yours.

## 7. The plan, week by week

**Hours rule (takes precedence over everything below):** at most **8 hrs/week in term**, **2 hrs/week in school exam and IA-deadline weeks**, **15 hrs/week in holidays**, **4 hrs/week in DP2**, and **1 hr/week from 15 Feb 2028**. Log your hours every Sunday. In a 2-hour week, an alumni tutor covers your own students (you are not paid for those sessions; the proprietorship bills them), and your 2 hours go only to the Monday coordination call and urgent parent messages. If you go over the cap two weeks in a row, cut scope, not sleep.

### Phase 0: Set up (26 Oct – 15 Nov 2026, term cap, ~5 hrs/week)
Diwali (Lakshmi Puja) falls on **Sunday 8 November 2026**, so most Mumbai schools break around early to mid November. These tasks fit the normal term cap. Use any break days for steps 2 and 4.
1. Complete Section 6 steps 1–7. The parent does most of the work; your part is about 3 hours.
2. Build a **Carrd** page (about US$19/year) or a Google Site with: who you are, subjects, prices, a booking and parental-consent Google Form, and your safeguarding rules.
3. Set up **WhatsApp Business**. Every student chat is a group that includes the parent.
4. Write the 1-page **Safeguarding & Integrity Code**: sessions online only, in a common area or recorded with consent; no private chats; no writing of assessed work; adult tutors only; incidents reported to the parent operator.
5. Pick **your 2 strongest subjects** (e.g. Maths, Physics or Economics).
6. **Academic baseline:** record your school-reported 1–7 grades from your first DP1 tests. Replace them with the **first DP1 term report** (Nov–Dec 2026) as soon as it arrives. If your school issues official predicted grades, use those instead.
7. **Tranche 0 released: ₹25,000.**

### Phase 5: Company, handover and age 18 (start **by March 2028**, so it works for every birthday; the parents and a CS do the work, and your time is about 0)
1. **Incorporate a private limited company**, whatever your age:
   - **Subscribers to the Memorandum: both parents** (adults). A minor cannot subscribe (C2). Use, for example, ₹1,00,000 capital as 10,000 shares of ₹10, fully paid.
   - **Directors: both parents.** At least one must have been resident in India for 182+ days in the year (Companies Act s.149(3)).
   - File SPICe+ on the MCA portal: about ₹10–25k all-in. Annual compliance, including the mandatory audit, is about ₹40–80k.
2. **Put 75% in your name (immediately after the Certificate of Incorporation):**
   - A parent **gifts 7,500 fully paid shares** to you by a share transfer deed (Form SH-4), which your guardian executes on your behalf, with stamp duty of 0.015% of value (a few rupees). The board approves the transfer.
   - The register of members records: "**[Student], minor, through natural guardian [Parent]**".
   - Tax: the gift of shares from a relative is exempt for you. Any dividend on them is clubbed with the parent's income until you turn 18 (C4). A CS confirms the recording, and your CA confirms the tax treatment.
3. **Move the going business into the company (April–June 2028):**
   - **What moves:** brand, domain, content, customer relationships and the trademark (assigned), plus **fresh tutor and coordinator contracts signed by the company**.
   - **How:** a **valued transfer** at fair value, set by your CA or a registered valuer. Expect a modest value, because a two-year-old tutoring agency's value lies mostly in its people. The consideration is **deferred and paid in instalments over 12 months out of the company's own profit**, so no new family capital is needed and the ₹5 lakh cap holds.
   - A tax-neutral proprietorship-to-company conversion (s.47(xiv) of the 1961 Act; check its successor in the 2025 Act) needs the proprietor to keep ≥50% of the votes for 5 years, which conflicts with your 75%. So use the valued transfer.
   - **GST:** register the company for GST from its first day of trading (new PAN, and its turnover will clearly exceed ₹20 lakh). A transfer of a business as a going concern is generally GST-exempt; your CA confirms. Afterwards, cancel the proprietorship's GST registration (Form REG-16).
   - **From the transfer date, the business's profit belongs to the company**, and 75% of that company is yours.
4. **Hire a General Manager** by June 2028, contracted **by the company**, which signs through your parent-directors. ### 8.6 The retained profit: whose it is, and how it can become yours

**Base-case retained profit, Aug 2027 until the business moves to the company:** proprietorship profit minus your founder fee, about **₹1 lakh/month before your parent's tax**. It is your parent's business income, taxed at their slab rate (likely the top rate plus surcharge for this family).

| Route | Works? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Parent gifts the cash to the **company** | **No** | A closely held company receiving money without consideration is taxed on it as income. The "relative" exemption covers only individuals and HUFs (s.56(2)(x) of the 1961 Act, carried into the 2025 Act). |
| Parent uses it to **buy company shares** | **No** | The shares would be the parent's, not yours. |
| Parent **gifts it to you before 18** | Legal, but inefficient | The gift is exempt for you (gift from a relative), but **all income on it is clubbed with the parent's** until you turn 18 (C4). |
| **Parent gifts it to you after your 18th birthday** | **Yes, cleanest** | It is exempt for you, clubbing has ended, and the money is unconditionally yours, ready to invest. |
| **The business itself moves to the company** (Phase 5 step 3) | **Yes. This is the main mechanism.** | **All profit after the transfer date** is earned by the company, 75% of which you own. The valued consideration is a modest one-off amount, paid from the company's own profits in instalments. |

So the honest position is this. **Before the transfer, profit is your parent's. After it, three quarters of all future profit, and of the business's value, are yours.** Whether the parent also gifts you the pre-transfer profit after you turn 18 is a family decision, and the Memorandum records it.

## 10. Kill, pivot and pause criteria (pre-committed)

- **Pause for 4 weeks** (a tutor covers your students, and you do no business work) if any subject's school-reported grade falls by 1+ from the baseline, or your total falls by 2+. Restart only once it has recovered.
- **Pivot** if Gate 1 fails twice, in this order:
  1. a different segment (Grade 9–10 DP Readiness only)
  2. a different channel (IB specialist for an existing tutoring centre, paid per head)
  3. a different product (written guides and recorded courses)
- **Stop permanently** on any substantiated safeguarding incident, any tutor writing assessed IB work, or any tutor found to be a minor or working in breach of their visa. Refund the affected families in writing.
