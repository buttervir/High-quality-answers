ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The month-12 ₹1 lakh target depends on founder teaching that the plan's own Phase 3 removes
Where: Section 3.2 "The path to ₹1 lakh a month in profit (month 12, October 2027): ... plus the founder's own 4 hours a week (about 17 hours a month) × ₹1,200 ≈ ₹20,000" vs Phase 3 "(September 2027 – May 2028; 3–5 hours a week) The founder's job shrinks to: the weekly review (30 minutes); tutor quality checks (watch 2 recorded sessions a week); 1 hour a week of strategy."
Problem: October 2027 is inside Phase 3. Phase 3's 3–5 hours are fully used by review, quality checks and strategy, and none of it is teaching. Yet the headline ₹1L figure counts ₹20k of founder teaching, and the scoreboard still shows "Founder-session income ₹20k" and "Founder hours 6–8" for month 12, which breaks the Phase 3 cap. October 2027 is also when Oxbridge/medicine applications are due (15 Oct), US ED is due (1 Nov) and DP2 IA/EE deadlines fall. If the plan's own Phase 3 rules are followed, profit is 150 × ₹800 − ₹35,000 = ₹85,000. That misses the ₹1L target the plan calls "success" in Section 10. The headline number only works if the plan breaks its own IB-protection limit.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] "Job-replacing" is measured before tax against a salary that is almost tax-free, so ₹1L of profit does not replace the job it names
Where: Assumptions: "net profit of ₹1,00,000 a month (about ₹12 lakh a year, roughly what a good graduate job in Mumbai pays). These are profit figures"; Section 6: agency margin is "taxed in the hands of Parent", and after incorporation "Company (corporate tax, about 25%...) ... dividends ... clubbed with a parent's income".
Problem: Under the new regime (carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025), a ₹12 lakh graduate salary pays roughly zero tax after the rebate and standard deduction. The plan puts the same ₹12 lakh of business profit in the parent's hands. A parent in an $8M family is almost certainly in the 30% slab, and with surcharge and cess the marginal rate is about 34–39%. That leaves about ₹61–66k a month after tax. After incorporation the profit is taxed twice: about 25% corporate tax, then dividends clubbed at the parent's rate, which leaves about ₹48–50k. So the plan's "job-replacing" money is 35–50% below the salary it is meant to equal. The task's core requirement, "job replacing money", is never checked after tax, and the plan never says what the student actually takes home.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The margin model ignores the plan's own loyalty pay rise, its leakage allowance and its compliance costs
Where: Section 3.2 "Tutor pay (62% of net) ... plan with ₹800/hour"; "Leakage allowance ... about 10% of tutor-hours a year are lost ... This is already inside the student targets below"; Section 8 "tutor pay steps up from 62% to 66% of the net price after 6 months"; Phase 2 "move tutors with 6+ months' service to the higher pay step"; Phase 2 incorporation "around ₹40,000–70,000 a year in compliance".
Problem: (a) The 5 tutors hired in Nov 2026 have about 11 months' service by month 12, so most hours are paid at 66%, not 62%. At 66% the margins are ₹1,864×0.34−44 = ₹590 (IGCSE) and ₹2,966×0.34−70 = ₹938 (IB), an average of about ₹764, not ₹800+. (b) Leakage is not "already inside" the targets: 35 students × 1 hr/week × 4.33 weeks = 151.6 hours, which is exactly the 150 used, so there is no 10% buffer. (c) Fixed costs of ₹35,000 leave out the company compliance and GST-filing cost the plan itself quotes (about ₹3.5–6k a month). Corrected: 135 hrs × ₹764 + ₹20k − ₹35k − ₹5k ≈ ₹83k, or about ₹63k without founder teaching (see Attack 1). The sensitivity check, the stop rules and the month-15 fallback are all built on the overstated ₹800 figure.

ATTACK 4 [MINOR] The pre-GST "cushion" margin is miscalculated
Where: Section 3.2 "Before GST registration, the same prices leave about ₹1,000–1,400/hour."
Problem: Before registration the net price equals the sticker price, so tutor pay at 62% gives IGCSE ₹2,200×0.38 − ₹44 = ₹792 and IB ₹3,500×0.38 − ₹70 = ₹1,260. The range is ₹790–1,260, not ₹1,000–1,400. The low end is overstated by about 26%. The early scoreboard rows and the Checkpoint-1 cash expectations rest on this inflated cushion.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] The checkpoint rules contradict each other, and the worst failure (a safeguarding incident) has no decision
Where: Phase 1 "Green means: 15+ paying families, ₹2.5 lakh+ total revenue, 70%+ of families renewing, and zero safeguarding incidents" vs the Section 9 decision table, where Green is "15+ paying families and 70%+ renewing" only; also "Every outcome has a stated next step, so there is no gap with no decision in it."
Problem: Counterexample: 16 paying families, 75% renewing, ₹2.1L revenue, and one safeguarding complaint. This is Green under Section 9 (release ₹1.5L and hire a coordinator at 20–25 hrs/week) but not Green under Phase 1. Neither table says what to do after a safeguarding incident, which for a business serving minors is the event most likely to end it. The plan's claim that "every outcome has a stated next step" is false for exactly the case that most needs one.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The grade-protection trigger will fire almost every month, so the plan either stalls or gets ignored
Where: Section 7 "Trigger (any one is enough): a school test, assessment or term grade in any subject comes in 1+ point below that subject's baseline"; "Consequence: business hours are halved ... until the next assessment in that subject is back at baseline".
Problem: Six subjects each produce several school tests per term, and single DP1 school-test grades often move by ±1 on the 1–7 scale. "Any one test in any subject 1 point below" will therefore trip most months, even for a student whose grades are stable. One October baseline set at a high mark makes this worse. The halving then sticks until that subject's next test, by which time another subject has dipped. Either the founder's hours are permanently halved, which breaks the Phase 1–2 hour budget the targets assume, or the family stops enforcing the rule, which removes the only IB safeguard. The rule needs a smoothed measure, such as a term average or two consecutive drops, to be usable.
