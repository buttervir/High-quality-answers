ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The "steady state" of ₹1,05,000/month is a one-cohort peak, not a steady state, so the job-replacement claim does not hold past May 2029
Where: Step 5 "The steady state from December 2027 (13 groups: 6 Grade 9–10, 7 DP)" and the headline "From your 18th birthday ... You keep about ₹1,00,000 a month in hand"; Step 6 "The bridge course refills the DP1 groups by August 2028, just as Step 3 did in 2027."
Problem: All 21 DP students come from one cohort (the 2027 bridge plus outreach). They all sit their exams in May 2029 and leave together. The plan's only refill tool is the bridge course, and in 2027 the bridge produced just 8 DP converts. The other 13 came from 6 months of the founder's outreach (180 messages, 5 hrs/week), which Step 6 does not repeat. The founder will also be in finals, and then at university, possibly abroad. Counterexample: in June 2029 the 2027 cohort is gone. If the 2028 bridge again converts 8, the business holds about 8 DP + about 9 Gr 9–10 students. Profit is then about 3 × ₹9,500 + 3 × ₹7,500 ≈ ₹51,000/month, half the "job-replacing" figure. The plan also models zero churn for every non-Grade-10 student over 2 years, and that is never stated or tested. "Job replacing" means income that keeps coming, and the plan shows only the peak month. It does not show the yearly May cliffs, which grow from −₹22,000 in May 2028 to roughly −₹2,00,000 of fees in May 2029.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The plan scales on a 15% conversion rate taken from a sample of 20 warm contacts, then extrapolates it to 180 messages
Where: Step 4 "your Step 1 test converted about 15% (3 of 20), so plan on about 180 messages over 6 months"
Problem: 3 of 20 has a 95% interval of roughly 3–38%. Also, the first 20 are the warmest contacts (parents' friends, neighbours), so later messages will convert worse. 180 more messages means the family's network needs about 200 households with a Grade 9–10 or DP1 child taking the subject the founder teaches, and the plan never checks that. Counterexample: at a realistic 7% on colder contacts, 180 messages bring about 13 students instead of 27. Then the business sits at about 8 groups, profit is about ₹65,000/month before tax, and the ₹1 lakh target fails. The stop rules have no trigger for this ("if by Sept you have fewer than X new students, ..."). The plan fails quietly: it keeps hiring the 5 tutors it promised and drifts below target without any signal to change course.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Early GST registration needlessly gives up about ₹87,000
Where: Step 5 "The running total crosses ₹15 lakh in December 2027 and ₹20 lakh in February 2028. Ask the CA to register for GST in the first week of December 2027."
Problem: For a service supplier in Maharashtra, registration is compulsory only once aggregate turnover passes ₹20 lakh, and then within 30 days. The plan's own table puts that in February 2028. Prices are "all-inclusive", so registering in December means 18/118 of the December and January fees (2 × ₹43,475 ≈ ₹87,000) goes to GST instead of profit. That is about 8% of the year's profit lost by the plan's own timing instruction. The right instruction is to watch the running-total cell and register when it is about to cross ₹20 lakh, in practice when it reaches about ₹17 lakh. The plan's December date is a mistake in its own numbers, not a safety margin.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The tax set-aside of 36% is too low for this family, so the minor-period figure is overstated
Where: Step 5 "taxed at their top rate, about 36%. The family keeps about ₹67,000 a month"; Tax rules "set aside 36% of the Step 4–5 profit"
Problem: 36% is 30% plus a 15% surcharge plus 4% cess, which applies to income between ₹1 crore and ₹2 crore. The family is worth about $8M (about ₹70 crore), so the higher-earning parent, whose income the minor's is clubbed with, quite plausibly earns over ₹2 crore. Under the new regime that means a 25% surcharge and an effective 39%. Under the old regime above ₹5 crore it is 42.74%. Counterexample: with the parent at ₹3 crore, the clubbed ₹12.6 lakh is taxed at 39%, about ₹4.9 lakh. The 36% set-aside leaves the family about ₹38,000 short for the year, and "₹67,000 a month" falls to about ₹64,000. The plan should tell the CA to use the parent's actual marginal rate and should default to 39%.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The bridge course is timed on top of the customers' own board exams and the founder's DP1 exams
Where: Step 3 "Bridge to DP Maths AA/AI ... May – June 2027"; roster rows "May 2027 ... (+ bridge ₹1.2 L)" and "Jun 2027 ... (+ bridge ₹1.2 L)"
Problem: The target customers are "your own leaving Grade 10s" and IGCSE students. The Cambridge IGCSE May/June series runs until about mid-June, and MYP eAssessment runs in May. Families will not pay ₹15,000 for 24 hours of DP preview while their child is sitting board exams. Meanwhile the founder personally teaches one of the two batches (12 × 2 hrs) during their own DP1 end-of-year exams, and the plan never lists this as a time cost, even though it sets "6 hours ... the most you should take on in DP1". The ₹1.2 lakh booked in May is therefore unlikely to arrive, and moving the bridge to late June or July pushes the DP1 product's July start back. The note "check your school's exam calendar" does not fix the dates the revenue table depends on.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Running ₹30+ lakh a year of business receipts through a minor's savings account will probably hit bank limits
Where: Step 0 item 4 "A separate savings account with UPI, which a minor aged 10 or older can operate"
Problem: The RBI's 2025 direction lets minors aged 10+ operate accounts, but each bank sets its own balance and transaction limits for them, usually low ones. Savings accounts are also not meant for commercial turnover. At ₹2.85 lakh a month of inflows from about 39 payers, plus outflows to 5 tutors, a minor's account will typically breach its limits or be flagged for review. Then fees bounce or the account is frozen in the middle of a collection cycle. Before Step 4 the plan should move collections to a parent-operated current account, or an account the CA picks, and it doesn't.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] The minor's own-skill income is unclubbed, but the plan never tells the user to file the minor's own return
Where: Tax "Income from the minor's own skill is not clubbed. Your own teaching in Steps 1–3 probably counts as skill income"
Problem: By the plan's own figures, Steps 1–3 bring the minor about ₹58,500 × 4–5 months plus the ₹2.16 lakh bridge, roughly ₹4.5–5 lakh in FY 2026-27/2027-28. If that is the minor's own income, the minor needs their own ITR, and from FY 2027-28 that may include the GST-registered turnover. The only tax instruction for this period is "set aside 36% of the Step 4–5 profit". So the plan says nothing about who files for Steps 1–3 income or how much to set aside for it.
