# A Business Plan for a 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai

## Assumptions

- **Today is October 2026.** The student started DP1 around June–August 2026, sits final IB exams in **May 2028**, and turns 18 around then.
- **"Job-replacing money"** is measured the way a salary is felt: **₹1,00,000 a month that the founder actually keeps after tax.** That is what a good ₹12 lakh-a-year graduate job in Mumbai pays, and under the new tax regime such a salary pays almost no income tax (the standard deduction plus the rebate up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income). The business target that delivers it is **₹1,00,000 a month of pre-tax profit**, because from age 18 the company can pay that profit to the founder as salary, which is deductible for the company and almost tax-free for the founder (Section 6). Before 18 the same profit is taxed at the parent's or the company's rate, so the plan shows both figures. The stretch target is ₹1,50,000 a month.
- **Exchange rate:** about ₹88 to US$1. The $8M family net worth is about ₹70 crore.
- **The parents support the plan.** They will act as legal signatory and guardian, and give a limited amount of money as described below.
- **Most universities expect a full IB score.** The student cannot sacrifice the IB, and the plan is built around that.
- **Tax and legal points below reflect Indian rules as of late 2026.** India's new Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, and section numbers changed. Have a chartered accountant (CA) confirm each point before you act on it. This plan gives you the questions to ask; it does not replace the CA.

## 1. The facts the plan has to work within

Every decision below follows from these seven facts:

1. **Time is the scarcest resource, not money.** The IB Diploma means 6 subjects, Theory of Knowledge, a 4,000-word Extended Essay, internal assessments in each subject, and CAS. A realistic business budget is **8–10 hours a week during term, 2–3 hours a week in exam periods, and 30+ hours a week in holidays**. The business has to run without the founder being present at every hour.
2. **A 16-year-old cannot sign binding contracts in India.** Under the Indian Contract Act, 1872, a minor's agreement is void. A minor also cannot be a company director or an LLP partner, and cannot be the KYC holder for most payment gateways. A parent or a company has to sign contracts and collect money until the student turns 18.
3. **Money is not the bottleneck.** ₹5 lakh is about 0.07% of the family's net worth. Raising capital won't decide success. These will: a skill customers pay for, access to customers, and the founder's time. Spending a lot of family money early mostly buys expensive mistakes.
4. **The family could already "replace a job" with capital.** About $1M invested at 6–7% yields roughly ₹55–60 lakh a year. So the real purpose of the business is not survival income. It is skill, independence, a track record, and a strong university profile. That means the downside has to stay small, and learning should be fast.
5. **Profit = customers × price × margin × how often they buy.** With little time, the business needs one of two things: a high price per hour of the founder's time, or a model where other people deliver the work.
6. **The student has real advantages.** They are inside Mumbai's IB/IGCSE school network, have English fluency, have direct experience of exactly what IB and IGCSE students struggle with, and have a family network of affluent parents and business owners.
7. **Minor's income is normally added to the parent's income for tax** (the "clubbing" rule, formerly Section 64(1A)). The exception is income the minor earns from their **own skill, talent or specialised knowledge**, which is taxed in the minor's own name. This affects how the money should flow (see Section 6).

## 3. Track A in full: an exam-preparation and tutoring agency

### 3.1 What you sell

A tutoring and revision business for **students in Grades 8–12 on IGCSE, IB MYP and IB DP**, at Mumbai's international schools (Dhirubhai Ambani International, Cathedral, Bombay International, Ecole Mondiale, Oberoi International, JBCN, Aditya Birla World Academy, B.D. Somani, Jamnabai Narsee International, and others), and online for families in Pune, Bengaluru, Dubai and Singapore.

There are three products, launched in this order:

1. **One-to-one tutoring packages.** Prepaid blocks of 10 hours, delivered by adult tutors and sold by the agency.
   - **"Founder sessions"** are a separate product. The founder teaches Grade 8–10 subjects where they are strong, billed and paid separately in the founder's own name (see Section 6). Founder hours are never part of agency packages.
2. **Small-group revision bootcamps.** For example, "IGCSE Maths Paper 4 in 5 days" or "IB Chemistry Paper 1 sprint". Run them in the peak seasons (January–April, and the summer and winter holidays), with 6–10 students per batch.
3. **Digital revision resources (from month 9).** Original notes, worked solutions and short video walkthroughs, sold online. This is the product that can scale without the founder's time.

**Never sell:**
- Writing or editing of IAs, Extended Essays or TOK essays for clients. That is academic misconduct under IB rules and would end the business and harm the founder's own IB standing.
- Copies of IB or Cambridge past papers or markschemes. Those are copyrighted. Teach with them in class, but don't resell them.

### 3.2 The numbers (after GST and payment fees)

**Three pricing rules make the margin survive tax:**

1. **Principal model.** The agency (the parent's proprietorship, later the company) invoices parents for the full package and pays tutors. This keeps prepaid packages and the parent relationship with the agency, which is what holds clients (Section 8). The agent model, where tutors bill parents directly, is rejected for that reason.
2. **Quote prices GST-inclusive** (Mumbai parents expect one all-in number). From the day the agency registers for GST, 18/118 of every rupee collected is GST, not revenue.
3. **Tutor pay is a % of the net price** (the price after GST is removed), never a % of the sticker price. So GST comes out of the price before the split, not out of the agency's share alone.

**Worked example per hour, once GST-registered** (gateway fee taken as 2% of the amount collected; direct UPI to the current account's QR code is usually free, so this is conservative):

| | IGCSE/MYP | IB DP |
|---|---|---|
| Price to parent (GST-inclusive) | ₹2,200 | ₹3,500 |
| minus GST (18/118) | ₹336 | ₹534 |
| = Net price | ₹1,864 | ₹2,966 |
| minus Tutor pay (62% of net) | ₹1,156 | ₹1,839 |
| minus Gateway fee (2% of price) | ₹44 | ₹70 |
| **= Agency margin per hour (new tutors, 62%)** | **₹664** | **₹1,057** |
| Tutor pay at the loyalty step (66% of net, after 6 months) | ₹1,230 | ₹1,958 |
| **= Agency margin per hour (loyalty step, 66%)** | **₹590** | **₹938** |

**Plan with ₹750/hour** once GST-registered. That is the 50/50 average at the 66% loyalty step (₹764), rounded down, because by month 12 the first tutors have 11 months' service and carry most of the hours. Newer tutors at 62% are upside, not plan.

**Before GST registration** (net price = sticker price, tutors at 62%): IGCSE ₹2,200 × 0.38 − ₹44 = **₹792**, IB ₹3,500 × 0.38 − ₹70 = **₹1,260**, so about **₹1,000/hour** on a 50/50 mix. This applies only to the first 9–10 months.

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Founder's own teaching (Grade 8–10, Phases 1–2 only) | ₹1,000–1,500/hour, sold and collected separately in the student's name (Section 6). No GST: the student's own turnover is far below the ₹20 lakh threshold. **Stops in DP2 term time** (Phase 3) and is never counted in the ₹1 lakh target. |
| Bootcamp | 8 students × ₹12,000 (GST-inclusive) = ₹96,000 collected. Minus GST ₹14,644, gateway ₹1,920, tutor ₹30,000 and venue/tools ₹6,000 → **≈ ₹43,000 profit** (≈ ₹58,000 before GST registration). Bootcamps are run by tutors and are extra, not part of the target. |
| Fixed costs once scaled | Coordinator ₹20,000 + software/ads ₹15,000 + company compliance and GST filing ₹5,000 (₹40,000–70,000 a year, Phase 2) = **₹40,000/month** |
| Leakage allowance | Assume about 10% of booked tutor-hours are lost: tutors going direct (Section 8), cancellations and holidays. Every hours figure below is **billed** hours = student-subjects × 4.33 weeks × 0.9. |

A **student-subject** is one student taking one subject at one hour a week. A DP student taking Maths and Physics counts as two. In practice 40–48 student-subjects is about 30–38 families.

**The path to ₹1 lakh a month of pre-tax profit, agency only, no founder teaching:**

| | Month 12 (Oct 2027) | **Month 15 (Jan 2028): target** | Month 18 (Apr 2028) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student-subjects | 40 | **48** | 55 |
| Billed hours (× 4.33 × 0.9) | 156 | **187** | 214 |
| × ₹750 margin | ₹1,17,000 | **₹1,40,000** | ₹1,60,500 |
| − fixed costs | ₹40,000 | **₹40,000** | ₹40,000 |
| **= Pre-tax profit** | **≈ ₹77,000** | **≈ ₹1,00,000** | **≈ ₹1,20,000** |
| plus bootcamps in peak months | — | +₹43,000 each | +₹43,000 each |

So the honest date for steady ₹1 lakh/month is **month 15 (January 2028)**, the start of the January–April exam season when demand peaks. Month 12 at about ₹77,000 is on track; ₹1 lakh at month 12 is possible only with a strong bootcamp month and counts as a stretch.

**Sensitivity check:** if parents push back, you cut prices 10%, and tutors at the loyalty step keep their rupee pay, the margin falls to about ₹530/hour (IGCSE ₹1,678 − ₹1,230 − ₹40 = ₹408; IB ₹2,669 − ₹1,958 − ₹63 = ₹648). ₹1 lakh would then need about 265 billed hours (about 68 student-subjects). That is unlikely before the founder finishes the IB. Expect steady profit of about ₹60,000–80,000 instead, and treat ₹1 lakh as a post-IB goal (Phase 4). Do not chase it by adding founder hours in DP2.

In a city with thousands of IGCSE/IB students paying for tutoring, 48–55 student-subjects is plausible within 15–18 months. Assume it will not happen in 3.

**When GST registration happens:** registration is mandatory once **aggregate turnover** in a financial year (April–March) passes ₹20 lakh. At the scoreboard's pace, FY 2026–27 (October–March) stays under it. FY 2027–28 would cross it around October–November 2027 (about ₹12 lakh collected April–August, then ₹3.5–4.5 lakh a month). You incorporate in August–September (Phase 2), so register the new company for GST voluntarily at incorporation rather than switching mid-season. The scoreboard therefore uses GST-registered margins from September 2027. **Trap:** "aggregate turnover" counts every business under the same PAN. If the parent whose proprietorship you use **already has a GST registration** for another business, GST applies from the first rupee. In that case, use a different parent's PAN or incorporate at the start, and plan on the GST-registered margins (about ₹860/hour at 62%, ₹750 at the loyalty step) from day one.

### 3.3 Calendar, phase by phase

#### Phase 0: Set up (October 20 – November 30, 2026; about 6 hours a week)

**Week 1**
- [ ] Hold a family meeting and sign a one-page **family founder agreement**. It covers:
  - the parent is the legal signatory;
  - the money is a recorded loan or investment (amount, tranches, milestones);
  - profits belong to the business and then to the student;
  - IB grades come first. Record a **grade baseline** per subject: the average of the first two DP1 school assessments in that subject (October–November 2026). The **grade protection rule** in Section 7 is written into this agreement.
- [ ] Ask the parent with the fewest existing business registrations to act as proprietor. **Check whether that parent already holds a GST registration** (see Section 3.2).
- [ ] Get the student a **PAN card**. A minor can hold one; apply through a parent.
- [ ] Open a **minor's savings account** with UPI enabled if the bank offers it for minors, or NEFT/IMPS details otherwise. RBI's 2025 rules let minors aged 10+ operate their own savings accounts. This account receives **only** the payment for Founder sessions (Section 6).
- [ ] Choose a brand name. Check that the name, the domain, and the Instagram and WhatsApp Business handles are all free. Search the trademark register online for Class 41 (education).

**Week 2**
- [ ] Set up the business under the **parent's sole proprietorship** at first. This avoids company costs before you have revenue. The parent's PAN is used for the Udyam (MSME) registration, which is free online, and for the current account.
- [ ] Open a **Razorpay or Cashfree account** (about 2% fees) and set up **UPI for the business**, both in the parent's name.
- [ ] Book a one-hour session with a **CA** (₹3,000–5,000). Ask:
  1. We plan a principal model with GST-inclusive pricing and tutor pay as a % of the net price. Is that correct, and from what date must we register?
  2. Does the founder's separately invoiced teaching count as skill income in the student's own hands, separate from the agency margin?
  3. What TDS applies to tutor payouts?
  4. At what revenue should we incorporate?

**Week 3**
- [ ] Draft three documents. Use a lawyer for a one-time ₹15,000–25,000, or adapt templates and have a lawyer review them.
  - **Tutor agreement** (adults 18+ only): independent contractor; while engaged, no direct dealing with agency families (a restraint during the contract term is generally enforceable; one that applies after it ends is not, under Section 27 of the Contract Act, so don't rely on it); the agency owns diagnostics, reports and materials; loyalty pay steps (Section 8); academic-integrity clause; safeguarding clause.
  - **Parent terms:** prepaid packages, a 24-hour cancellation rule, refunds of unused hours only. Include an **introduction fee**: if the family hires an agency tutor directly within 6 months of their last session, a one-time ₹15,000 fee applies. Ask the lawyer how enforceable it is. It mainly works as a deterrent and a clear signal, not as a guarantee.
  - **Founder-session terms:** a one-page invoice and terms in the student's name, countersigned by the guardian as signatory.
  - **Consent and safeguarding form:** parental consent to collect and store the child's data, which India's DPDP Act 2023 and its 2025 Rules require for anyone under 18; consent to session recordings; meeting rules.
- [ ] Write a **safeguarding policy**:
  - every tutor gets an ID check and two references;
  - online sessions are recorded and kept for 30 days;
  - home sessions only with an adult present at home;
  - no private social media contact with students;
  - a named person handles concerns (the parent, while the student is a minor).

**Week 4**
- [ ] Build a one-page site (Carrd, Framer or Wix; ₹5,000–15,000 a year). It needs subjects offered, tutor profiles and their results, prices, a WhatsApp click-to-chat button, and a Google Form or Calendly booking link.
- [ ] Set up a **Google Business Profile** for search and map listings, plus a WhatsApp Business catalogue.
- [ ] Set up tools: Google Workspace (one account), Zoom or Google Meet, Google Sheets as the CRM (columns: lead, source, grade, subject, status, hours bought, hours used, renewal date), and Notion for tutor notes.

**Weeks 5–6**
- [ ] **Recruit 5 tutors, all aged 18 or over** (a minor's contract is void, and adult tutors make safeguarding simpler). Look for:
  - recent IB graduates with awarded scores of 38–40+, now at university in India or abroad (online sessions);
  - first-year university students with IGCSE A* or IB 7 results in the subject (for Grade 8–10 work);
  - 1–2 working teachers for DP-level subjects.

  Check every candidate's **actual result document** (IB results certificate or Cambridge statement of results), not predicted grades. Screen each with a 20-minute demo lesson, which the student and one parent watch.
- [ ] **Find the first customers** with this message, sent to the family's network, school parent WhatsApp groups (where allowed) and younger siblings of friends:

  > "Hi! I'm [Name], a DP1 student at [School]. I've started [Brand] — focused IGCSE/MYP/IB tutoring and exam bootcamps taught by high-scoring IB graduates and teachers. First diagnostic session is free: a 45-minute session that maps exactly where your child is losing marks. Reply 'diagnostic' to book. — [Name], [phone]"

- [ ] Check your school's policy on students tutoring other students for money. Do not market inside school without permission.

**Phase 0 exit test:** 5 tutors signed, 10 free diagnostic sessions booked, payments working.

#### Phase 1: Prove it works (December 2026 – March 2027; 8–10 hours a week)

This is the IGCSE and board-exam peak, the best possible time to launch.

- **Every diagnostic session ends with a written 1-page "mark-loss report"** and a package offer: 10 hours at the full price, or 20 hours with 10% off.
- **The founder personally teaches 3–4 hours a week** of Grade 8–10 Maths or Science as **Founder sessions**. These are sold as their own offer ("10 sessions with [Name]"), invoiced in the student's name, and paid by UPI or bank transfer into the student's minor account. They are never paid through the agency's gateway. This builds credibility and keeps the income in the founder's own hands.
- **Winter-break bootcamp (late December):** 1 IGCSE subject, 6–10 students.
- **January–March:** 2 more bootcamps before the April–May IGCSE exams.
- **Referral rule:** any family that refers a paying family gets 1 free hour.
- **Weekly 30-minute review every Sunday:** check new leads, conversion rate (diagnostic → paid, target 40%+), hours delivered, cash collected, and any complaints.

**Checkpoint 1 (31 March 2027):** grade the result Green, Amber or Red using the decision table in Section 9, which is the only definition of each grade. A safeguarding incident is handled by the separate protocol in Section 8, which overrides any grade. Total family money spent by then should be under ₹1.5 lakh.

#### Phase 2: Systems and delegation (April – August 2027)

The student's time is limited here: DP1 end-of-year exams and IAs fall around April–May, and Extended Essay writing starts over the summer. So the goal of this phase is to **hire people to replace the founder's time**.

- **Hire a part-time operations coordinator**, for example a college student or an experienced admin. They handle scheduling, payment reminders, rescheduling and tutor timesheets. Write a 2-page operations manual first.
  - **Green at Checkpoint 1:** 20–25 hours a week (₹15,000–25,000 a month). Tranche 2 covers up to 3 months until revenue pays for it.
  - **Amber at Checkpoint 1:** 10 hours a week (about ₹8,000–10,000 a month), paid from revenue only. The founder's hours stay capped either way (Section 7). If the work does not fit in the cap, take fewer new families. Do not take more of the founder's time.
- **Raise tutors to 10–12.** Add DP-level subject specialists for Maths AA/AI, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Economics and Business Management.
- **Summer bootcamps (May–June):** "IGCSE head start" for students entering Grade 10, and "DP1 to DP2 bridge" sessions.
- **Start a content channel.** Post one 60-second exam-tip reel a week, recorded in batches in a single afternoon each month, plus a monthly email to parents. It brings in leads for free.
- **Decide on incorporating.** Once revenue passes about ₹3 lakh a month, or you hire staff, incorporate a **Private Limited Company** through the government's SPICe+ form. Costs: about ₹10,000–20,000 in professional fees, and around ₹40,000–70,000 a year in compliance (CA, ROC filings, audit).
  - **Directors:** two adults (both parents, or a parent and a trusted relative).
  - **Shareholders:** the student holds the majority of shares through their guardian, with a parent holding the rest.
  - **At 18,** the student gets a DIN and becomes a director.
  - Do not use an OPC or an LLP: a minor cannot be the OPC member or an LLP partner.
- **GST.** Private tutoring is taxable at 18%. Registration becomes mandatory once aggregate turnover in the financial year passes **₹20 lakh** (services, Maharashtra). Under the principal model chosen in Section 3.2, the full fee counts as turnover. Turnover would cross it around October–November 2027, but register the company voluntarily at incorporation (August–September 2027) and file monthly or quarterly returns through the CA. The prices and margins in Section 3.2 already allow for it.
- **Tutor retention (Section 8):** move tutors with 6+ months' service to the higher pay step, and give them first pick of bootcamp slots.

**Checkpoint 2 (31 August 2027, month 10):** grade it using the table in Section 9. Green means **₹60,000+ pre-tax profit in at least one month**, with the coordinator running daily operations. The ₹1 lakh target comes at month 15 (31 January 2028), not here.

#### Phase 3: Run on autopilot through DP2 (September 2027 – May 2028; 3–5 hours a week)

University applications (US Early Decision about 1 November, UCAS about 14 January, Oxbridge/medicine about 15 October), mock exams and final exams all happen here. The founder's job shrinks to:

- the weekly review (30 minutes);
- tutor quality checks (watch 2 recorded sessions a week);
- 1 hour a week of strategy.

**The founder stops teaching in term time.** Existing Founder-session families are moved to agency tutors by the end of August 2027. Founder sessions run only in the winter break, if at all, and are never counted in the profit target. The coordinator moves to 25 hours a week to carry the operations the founder drops.

Also in this phase:
- **Launch digital resources.** These are original notes and worked-solution packs for IGCSE Maths and Sciences, priced at ₹999–2,999 each and written mostly by tutors. Sell through Gumroad, Razorpay payment pages or a Shopify store. This income needs no founder time.
  - If Checkpoint 2 was **Green**, pay tutors a fixed fee per pack from tranche 3.
  - If it was **Amber**, pay tutors 30% of each pack's sales instead, so nothing is paid upfront.
  - If it was **Red**, skip this.
- **Pre-book January–April 2028 bootcamps in November 2027**, run by tutors.
- **Freeze rule:** from the start of IB mocks to the end of finals, there are no new products, no new hires, and only the weekly review.

**Phase 3 targets** (all agency profit, with no founder teaching):
- About ₹75,000+ pre-tax profit in October 2027 (month 12). Hitting ₹1 lakh this early is a stretch, not the plan.
- **₹1 lakh+ pre-tax profit by 31 January 2028 (month 15).**
- ₹1–1.5 lakh a month through spring 2028 (bootcamp months at the top end), with 48–55 student-subjects.
- Profit stays **inside the company** until the founder turns 18 (Section 6). That way it is taxed once at about 25%, not again as a dividend at the parent's rate.

#### Phase 4: Full-time summer and hand-over (May – August 2028, after the exams)

- The founder works 30–40 hours a week.
- Expand online to Dubai, Singapore and Bengaluru families.
- Add a SAT/AP/university-essay coaching line. Coach only; never ghostwrite essays.
- **At 18:**
  - get a DIN and join the company board;
  - move signatory authority from the parent to the founder;
  - review the family agreement;
  - **switch on job-replacing pay:** a board resolution and an appointment letter for the founder as a whole-time director or employee at **₹1,00,000 a month**, paid only while the trailing 3-month average pre-tax profit (before this salary) is at least that much. Otherwise set the salary at the trailing average. This is the step that turns business profit into job-replacing income (Section 6).
- **Decide what happens during university:**
  1. keep the business with a hired general manager and the founder as owner (the most likely option);
  2. sell it to a larger edtech company or tutoring group;
  3. close it after documenting what was learned.

## 4. Track B in brief: an AI-assisted digital agency for local businesses

**Use this if** the student is strong at design, video or tech, or if Track A fails its March 2027 test.

**Who buys it:** restaurants, clinics, salons, boutiques, real-estate brokers, coaching classes and family-network businesses in Mumbai that have poor online presence.

**What you sell:**

| Package | Price | What's included |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | ₹25,000–40,000, one time | Google Business Profile tidy-up, WhatsApp Business catalogue, a 5-page website, review-request system |
| Monthly retainer | ₹15,000–30,000 | 12 social posts and reels made with AI tools plus human editing, monthly report, review replies |

**The numbers:** quote business clients prices **plus GST**. They are mostly GST-registered and can claim it back, so GST doesn't eat your margin. 6 retainers × ₹20,000 = ₹1.2 lakh revenue. Paying 2 freelance editors (about ₹30,000) and tools (about ₹5,000) leaves about **₹85,000 a month**, plus setup fees. Reaching ₹1 lakh takes 7–8 retainers.

**Steps:**

1. Build 3 free or discounted case studies for family contacts in weeks 1–4, with before-and-after numbers (calls, reviews, map views).
2. Go door to door on one high street (for example Linking Road or Hill Road) with a printed one-page audit of each business's Google listing.
3. Aim for 2 paying clients by week 8 and 6 retainers by month 6.
4. Hire 2 freelance editors on Upwork, Internshala or from local colleges, and a part-time account manager by month 6.

The legal and tax setup is the same as Track A: the parent's proprietorship signs, and you incorporate later. Note that GST applies at 18% once turnover passes ₹20 lakh.

**Test by month 4:** 3 paying retainers, or stop.

## 5. Track C in brief: a consumer product brand (only with a family supply chain)

**Use this only if** the family already makes or trades a product, such as textiles, food, packaging or cosmetics, and can provide production and warehousing.

**Steps:**

1. Pick one product with a **gross margin of 60% or more** that sells for ₹800 or more.
2. Make a test batch of 300–500 units, using about ₹3–5 lakh of family business capacity.
3. Sell first through your own Shopify store and Instagram, then Amazon/Flipkart. Selling on marketplaces usually requires a GSTIN. Food needs FSSAI registration (basic registration below ₹12 lakh turnover). Register a trademark in your product's class (₹4,500 per class for individuals or small enterprises filing online).
4. **Kill test:** if 300 units haven't sold within 90 days at a positive margin after delivery and ad costs, stop.

This track takes the most working capital and the most time. It is the most likely of the three to hurt IB grades. Choose it only with a family operations team doing the daily work.

## 6. How the money flows, and tax

| Income | Received in | Taxed in the hands of |
|---|---|---|
| Founder sessions: the student's own teaching (skill income) | Student's minor savings account, paid **directly by the family being taught** | **Student** (the skill-income exception to clubbing). The student files their own return and the basic exemption applies. At about ₹2–2.5 lakh a year, no GST registration is needed. |
| Agency margin, bootcamp profit, resource sales (sole proprietorship stage) | Parent's proprietorship current account | **Parent** (it is the parent's business). Money later given to the student is a gift from a relative, which is tax-free for the student. However, income the student then earns on that money is clubbed with the parent's until 18. |
| After incorporation | The company | **Company** (the 22% concessional rate plus 10% surcharge and 4% cess = **about 25.2%**). Dividends paid to the student before 18 are clubbed with a parent's income and taxed again, so **pay no dividends before 18**: keep the profit in the company. |
| From age 18: founder salary | Founder's own bank account | **Founder.** Salary is a deductible expense for the company, so the profit it uses carries no corporate tax. In the founder's hands, ₹12 lakh a year minus the ₹75,000 standard deduction is ₹11.25 lakh of taxable income. That is under the ₹12 lakh rebate limit of the new regime, so the income tax is nil. Maharashtra Professional Tax is ₹2,500 a year. |

**What "₹1 lakh a month" is worth after tax, by stage** (₹1,00,000 of monthly pre-tax profit):

| Stage | Tax paid | Left over | Does it replace a ₹12 lakh job (about ₹95,000–1,00,000 take-home)? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proprietorship, profit in the parent's hands (to about Aug 2027) | Parent's slab: 30% + surcharge (10–25%) + 4% cess ≈ 34–39% | ≈ ₹61,000–66,000 | No. Profit is small at this stage anyway (under ₹60,000 a month), and it is why you incorporate around ₹3 lakh/month of revenue. |
| Company, profit retained (Sept 2027 – founder's 18th birthday) | ≈ 25.2% | ≈ ₹75,000, building up inside the company the student mainly owns | Not yet as pay, but kept as the student's capital. Do not pay it out as dividends. |
| Company, paid as dividend to a minor | 25.2% company tax, then the parent's rate on the dividend | ≈ ₹48,000–50,000 | No. This is why there are no dividends before 18. |
| **Company, paid as the 18-year-old founder's salary (from about May 2028)** | Nil income tax; ₹208 a month Professional Tax | **≈ ₹99,800** | **Yes.** This is the job-replacing stage. |

So the honest answer is that ₹1 lakh a month of business profit **becomes** job-replacing money at 18, through salary. Before that it is retained capital, which is worth about ₹75,000 a month and owned mostly by the student. The founder's own Founder-session income (about ₹2–2.5 lakh a year) is tax-free under the basic exemption throughout.

**Salary checks for the CA:** the salary must be reasonable for the work done (related-party payments can be disallowed if excessive), so keep a written role description and time log. If the founder goes to university abroad and becomes a non-resident, Indian-source salary and director's fees are still taxed in India, and the founder's actual role changes. Review the structure before departure.

**How Founder sessions are billed, step by step:**

1. Make a separate one-page price list: "Founder sessions with [Name], Grade 8–10 Maths/Science, ₹1,200/hour, 10-hour block".
2. Issue a numbered invoice from a Google Sheets template in the student's name, with the student's PAN, "through guardian [Parent name]" and the parent's countersignature.
3. The parent of the student being taught pays by UPI or NEFT **directly into the minor's account**. Never pay this through the agency's gateway or current account.
4. Log every session and invoice in a separate tab. Keep agency-booked hours and Founder sessions apart: no family's package mixes the two.
5. **Don't move agency profit into the student's account and call it skill income.** Money the agency passes on is a gift from the parent, and anything it later earns is clubbed with the parent's income until 18.

**Bring these questions to the CA:**
1. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, do separately invoiced Founder sessions qualify as skill income in the student's hands?
2. Do you agree with the principal model and GST-inclusive pricing? Does the parent's existing GST registration, if any, pull the agency into GST from day one?
3. What TDS applies to tutor payments above the threshold?
4. When should we register for Professional Tax and the Shops & Establishments Act? Under Maharashtra's 2017 Act, fewer than 10 workers needs only an intimation. A home-based, online-only business may not need it at all.
5. How should the family's money be recorded: as a loan, as equity, or as a gift?
6. Do you confirm the 22% concessional company rate, no dividends before 18, and a ₹1 lakh/month founder salary from 18 as the way to deliver job-replacing income? What rebate and standard-deduction figures apply in the year the salary starts?

## 7. Protecting the IB and university applications

**Weekly time plan in term (Track A, Phases 1–2):**

| Day | Business time |
|---|---|
| Mon–Thu | 30 minutes a day replying to leads and tutors on WhatsApp (batched at one time each day) |
| Sat | 3–4 hours: own teaching, demo lessons, a bootcamp when one is running |
| Sun | 30-minute weekly review, 1 hour on content or hiring |
| **Total** | **about 8–10 hours** |

- **Grade protection rule** (written into the family agreement). Formal IB predicted grades are rarely issued during DP1, so the rule uses data the student gets every month:
  - **Baseline:** for each subject, the average IB-scale (1–7) grade of the first two DP1 school assessments (October–November 2026). Averaging two results stops one unusually good test from setting the bar.
  - **Smoothed measure:** for each subject, the average of its two most recent assessments. A single test that comes in 1 point low is normal noise in DP1 and triggers nothing on its own.
  - **Trigger** (any one is enough):
    - in any subject, the two-assessment average is 1+ point below that subject's baseline (in practice, two weak results in a row);
    - the total across all six subjects, on the same smoothed measure, is 3+ points below the total baseline (a broad slide even if no single subject trips);
    - any internal deadline for an IA, the Extended Essay or TOK is missed;
    - a teacher raises a written or formal concern at a parent–teacher meeting.
  - **Check:** at the first Sunday review of every month, read the school portal or report together with a parent.
  - **Consequence:** business hours are halved for **one full month**, and the coordinator and tutors cover the gap. At the next monthly check, hours go back to normal if the smoothed measure for that subject is back within 1 point of baseline and no deadline is overdue. Otherwise the halving runs another month.
  - **Escalation:** if the rule triggers in 3 months out of any 6, the founder's business time is capped at 3 hours a week (the Phase 3 level) until the next school term report, whatever the phase. The business is built to survive this: the coordinator and tutors deliver all agency hours, and the ₹1 lakh target never depends on founder hours.
- **CAS:** paid work generally isn't suitable for CAS. A **free** part of the business can be a strong CAS service project, for example weekly free tutoring for students at a municipal school or an NGO such as Teach For India partner schools or Akanksha Foundation, run by your tutors.
- **University applications:** keep a simple impact log covering students taught, hours, grade improvements (with consent), revenue, team size, and problems solved. That gives the activities list and essays real, checkable numbers. Admissions readers value **verified results and genuine responsibility**. Don't inflate any figure.

## 8. Risks and how to handle them

| Risk | How to handle it |
|---|---|
| Tutors take clients directly | A clause that applies after the contract ends is largely void under Section 27 of the Contract Act, so rely on economics, not the clause. **(1) Assets the tutor can't take:** the agency's diagnostic, written mark-loss reports, progress tracker and question banks, plus bootcamps a solo tutor can't run. **(2) Loyalty pay:** tutor pay steps up from 62% to 66% of the net price after 6 months, plus first pick of bootcamp slots. That leaves going solo only a small gain, with the work of finding clients and collecting money themselves. **(3) Relationship ownership:** prepaid packages; all scheduling and messages go through the business WhatsApp and the coordinator. **(4) Spread the risk:** no tutor holds more than 15% of monthly hours. **(5) A parent-side introduction fee** (₹15,000) as a deterrent, and a restraint that applies only while the tutor is engaged. **Budget for some leakage:** about 10% of hours a year (already in the targets). |
| Safeguarding incident with a minor | Prevention: background checks, recorded online sessions, an adult present at home sessions, no private social-media contact. **Response protocol (overrides every checkpoint grade and every target):** see below. |
| Academic misconduct claims | A written "we teach, we never write your coursework" policy in the tutor agreement and the parent terms; remove any tutor who breaks it |
| Grades slip | The grade protection rule, the freeze rule during exams, and a hired coordinator |
| Customers come in seasons (peaks in Jan–Apr, quiet months) | Bootcamps in holidays, digital resources, online international families in other school calendars |
| Paying too much tax or breaking rules | A CA from week 2, quarterly check-ins, and incorporating when revenue passes the threshold |
| Wasting family money | Tranches tied to milestones, a ₹5 lakh hard cap, and stop dates written down in advance |
| Founder burnout | The 10-hours-a-week cap, delegating operations by month 6–8, and no founder teaching in DP2 term time |

**Safeguarding incident protocol.** An incident is any allegation or sign of abuse, sexual or inappropriate contact, grooming, harassment or serious boundary breach involving a tutor, staff member or student, whether it is reported by a child, a parent, a tutor or seen in a recording.

1. **Same day:** the named safeguarding lead (a parent while the founder is a minor) takes over. The tutor is suspended from all sessions with pay held, and their sessions are reassigned. The founder, being a minor, does not investigate or interview anyone.
2. **Within 24 hours:** if the concern involves any sexual offence against a child, report it to the local police or the Special Juvenile Police Unit. Under the POCSO Act, anyone who knows of such an offence must report it, and failing to report is itself an offence. Inform the child's parents unless the police advise otherwise. Preserve all recordings, messages and session logs, and change nothing.
3. **Within 48 hours:** brief a lawyer. Tell the insurer, if a professional-indemnity or liability policy is in place (get one quoted at Phase 2 incorporation).
4. **Business status:** all checkpoint grades are suspended. No new families, no marketing, and no tranche release until the matter is closed by the police or by an independent review (a lawyer or a qualified child-protection professional, not a family member).
5. **Outcome:**
   - **Unfounded:** resume normally, and record what was learned.
   - **Substantiated, and the policy was followed** (checks done, recordings in place, a single individual at fault): remove the tutor permanently, tell affected families in writing, re-check every tutor's references and recordings, then resume at Amber at best for the next checkpoint.
   - **Substantiated, and the business's own safeguards failed** (no checks, unrecorded sessions, ignored warnings): close Track A. This overrides every number in Section 9.
