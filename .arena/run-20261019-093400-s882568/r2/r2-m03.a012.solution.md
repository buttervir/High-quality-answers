# The Plan: A 16-Year-Old IB DP1 Student in Mumbai Building Job-Replacing Income

## Assumptions

- **Timeline.** It is October 2026. DP1 began mid-2026 and the DP2 final exams are in **May 2028**. The plan covers those ~19 months, then hands over to whatever follows school (university in India or abroad). The student turns 18 somewhere between late 2027 and late 2028, depending on their birthday. That date matters for the money (see below).
- **"Job-replacing money" means the student can take ₹1,00,000 per month, after tax, for their own use.** That is roughly the take-home pay of a good Mumbai graduate job. In business terms, the company makes **operating profit before founder pay of at least ₹1,25,000/month for 2 consecutive months**: ₹1L to pay the founder, plus ₹25k reserve.
  - **From age 18:** the company pays the student ₹1L/month as director remuneration. The company deducts it as an expense. Under the new personal tax regime, income up to ₹12L/year is effectively tax-free after the rebate (and salary also gets a standard deduction), so about ₹1L/month reaches the student, spendable. There is no double tax, because the money is not paid out as dividends.
  - **Before 18 (the honest limit):** a minor cannot reasonably be paid ₹1L/month for 8 hours a week. So the student takes a **₹40,000/month founder fee** they can spend. The rest stays in a company the student owns 99% of, as reserves taxed at ~25%. **Before 18 the business replaces a job in earning power, but only ₹40k/month of it is the student's spending money.** The full ₹1L becomes spendable on the 18th birthday with no change to the business.
- **Family wealth** of about $8M (≈ ₹70 crore) is a safety net and a source of introductions. It does **not** fund the business. If it did, it would be the parents' business with the child's name on it.
- **The student is a minor until 18.** Every legal and tax step below is built around that.
- Indian tax law changes often, and the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. **Before registering anything, book a 1-hour paid Chartered Accountant (CA) consultation (₹3,000–₹10,000).** Part 8 lists the exact questions to ask.

## Part 1: How this goes wrong, and the rule that prevents each failure

| # | Failure mode | Why it happens to *this* student | Guardrail in this plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Grades crash** | The IB takes 30–40 hrs/week plus IAs, the EE, TOK and CAS | **8 hrs/week cap in DP1, 4 in DP2**, a per-task hour budget for each phase, a drop order (Part 4A), and exam freezes |
| 2 | **Rich-kid vanity startup** | ₹50 lakh feels "affordable", so the brand and office come before any customers | Money is released in **tranches tied to revenue gates** (Part 5). The seed is ₹25,000 |
| 3 | **Parents end up running it** | Parents sign everything and decide everything | Adults sign only where the law requires. The student runs sales, pricing, hiring and delivery, and holds 99% of the shares |
| 4 | **Contracts that can't be enforced** | A minor's contract is void (*Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*, 1903) | Customers pay **in advance**. **No adult tutor works until a company exists to contract with them** |
| 5 | **Can't collect or pay money** | Gateways, current accounts and GST need an adult or an entity | Stage A: UPI into a minor account, for the founder's **own teaching only**. Stage B: a company account and gateway before any tutor is paid |
| 6 | **Clubbing tax trap** | A minor's income is normally added to the parent's (30%+) | Own-skill income only in personal hands. The tutor margin is earned **by the company**. **No dividends**: money goes out as fee/remuneration |
| 7 | **"Income" that is only on paper** | Retained profit can't be spent | Target defined as **spendable founder pay**. The pre-18 limit (₹40k spendable) is stated openly |
| 8 | **Model assumes full groups** | Absences, half-full groups, refunds | Plan at **3.5 paid students per 5-seat group**, with a sensitivity table. Absences get make-ups, not refunds |
| 9 | **Seasonal churn** | Grade 10 leaves after May/June exams. Diwali and winter dips | Seasonality calendar. Grade 10 capped at 30% of students. Target measured **Sep–Dec 2027** |
| 10 | **No customers to fill tutors** | Growth from 12 to 84 students has to be won | Funnel model with channel conversion rates, a CAC cap, weekly enrolment targets, and **hire-on-demand** tutors |
| 11 | **Forgetting GST** | Above ₹20L/year, coaching attracts 18% GST | Prices are **GST-inclusive**. The math already deducts GST |
| 12 | **Wrong business type** | Cafés, franchises and D2C need capital and daily presence | The 6-test filter (Part 2) |
| 13 | **Gambling disguised as business** | F&O and crypto look like income | **Banned.** SEBI found ~93% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY22–FY24 |
| 14 | **Academic misconduct** | "Just write my IA" | **No writing or editing of assessed work, ever.** This is in contracts and parent terms |
| 15 | **Child safety and data** | The clients are minors | Safeguarding policy, 18+ tutors, DPDP Act 2023 parental consent |
| 16 | **School conflict** | Some schools restrict commercial activity | Email the DP coordinator in Week 1. No marketing in school-run groups |
| 17 | **Company paperwork and compliance missed** | A Pvt Ltd needs 2 directors, an audit and annual filings | Two adult directors plus a **compliance calendar** (Part 3) |
| 18 | **Quitting or option paralysis** | Too many ideas | One primary path, one costed fallback, numeric gates |
| 19 | **Dies when the student leaves for university** | Founder-dependent | A manager and an operations manual by DP2. A zero-founder test in Jan–May 2028 |

## Part 2: Which business, and why

### The 6-test filter
1. Starts for under ₹25,000.
2. Paid in advance.
3. The founder can start it alone, on their own skill (clean personal tax in Stage A).
4. Fits in 8 hrs/week, and the work can be handed to others.
5. Has leverage (one person's hours can't reach ₹1L/month in 8 hrs/week).
6. Uses the student's real advantages: being inside the IB system, an affluent Mumbai network, and family business contacts.

### Primary path: small-group academic support for MYP/IGCSE (Grades 6–10)

**What it is.** Small-group tutoring and exam preparation for Grade 6–10 students at Mumbai's international schools. The founder teaches at first, to learn the product. After that the founder sells, builds the curriculum, checks quality and manages, while **18+ tutors deliver**.

**Why it fits.**
- Mumbai has a dense cluster of IB and Cambridge schools (Bandra, Juhu, Powai, Lower Parel, Worli, BKC, Malabar Hill, Andheri, Navi Mumbai) whose parents already pay for tutoring.
- A current IB student is credible to the parent of a Grade 8 child.
- It is paid in advance and scales by adding tutors.

**Tutor pool (all 18+).**
- Recent IB/IGCSE/A-level graduates on a gap year or at Mumbai colleges (St. Xavier's, Jai Hind, NMIMS, IIT Bombay, ICT, private-university campuses).
- Strong college students in the subject (they can also teach online from anywhere in India).
- 18-year-old DP2 students with a predicted 7, online only, max 3 hrs/week.
- **Screening:**
  - a 7 (IB) or A* (IGCSE/A-level), or a college major in the subject;
  - a 30-minute demo lesson to the founder and one volunteer parent;
  - 2 references;
  - ID and police verification for in-person work.
- **Pool check:** if fewer than 10 qualified applicants arrive within 3 weeks of posting, widen the bar to a 6 or an A plus a passed demo, and pay ₹900–₹1,000/hr.

**Product rules.**
- Groups have **5 seats**, meeting 90 minutes once a week.
- Families buy a **monthly package** paid by UPI or the payment gateway in advance.
- **Absences:** the student gets a make-up in another group or a recorded session with a practice set. **No refund for absences.**
- **Leaving:** unused future sessions are refunded in full on 7 days' notice.

**Pricing** (verify in Week 1 with 5 provider calls and 15 parent interviews). All prices are **GST-inclusive**, so nothing changes for parents when GST registration happens.
- Group: **₹700 per student per hour**.
- 1:1: ₹1,000–₹1,500/hr.
- Crash courses (8 sessions): ₹8,000–₹15,000 per student.
- Tutor pay: ₹800/hr. Session plans written to the template: ₹1,000 each. Running a parent workshop: ₹1,500.
- **Annual price review every August** (target +5–8%).

### The real math, with GST, empty seats and tax

Per tutor-hour at **3.5 paid students on average** (70% of 5 seats):
- ₹2,450 including GST → ₹2,076 after 18% GST
- minus ₹800 tutor pay and ~2% payment costs (₹49)
- = **₹1,227 contribution**

One tutor teaches 6 group-hours/week (4 groups) ≈ 26 hrs/month → **₹31,900/month contribution**.

**Sensitivity: operating profit before founder pay, per month (₹700/student/hr)**

| Stage | Tutors | Paid students at 3.5 | Overhead | Profit at **3.0** fill | Profit at **3.5** fill (plan) | Profit at **4.0** fill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 3 (part-time ops manager) | **6** | 84 | ₹60k | ₹86k ✗ | **₹1.31L ✔** | ₹1.77L |
| DP2 (full-time manager) | **8** | 112 | ₹1.0L | ₹95k ✗ | **₹1.55L ✔** | ₹2.16L |
| Full scale | 12 | 168 | ₹1.5L | ₹1.43L | ₹2.33L | ₹3.23L |

The target is ₹1.25L. **Reading the table:** at an average of 3 paid students per group, the plan fails the target at every stage. So **fill rate is the number to watch every week**, ahead of tutor count. Two levers fix a low fill:
1. **Merge groups:** two groups of 2–3 become one, and the tutor count drops.
2. **The August price review:** ₹750 at 3.5 fill raises contribution per tutor-hour from ₹1,227 to about ₹1,372.

Overhead breakdown:
- **Phase 3:** ops manager ₹30k + tools ₹5k + marketing ₹10k + CA/compliance ₹8k + contingency ₹7k.
- **DP2:** manager ₹50k + tools ₹8k + marketing ₹20k + CA/audit ₹10k + contingency ₹12k.
- **Full scale:** manager ₹60k + lead tutor ₹15k + tools ₹10k + marketing ₹30k + CA ₹12k + contingency ₹23k.

**Where the profit goes (at the Phase 3 target of ₹1.31L):**
- **Before 18:** founder fee ₹40k (deductible for the company; within the ₹12L personal rebate, so ≈ ₹0 tax) → **₹40k spendable**. Retained: ₹91k − ~25% tax ≈ **₹68k/month of reserves** in a company 99% owned by the student.
- **From 18:** director remuneration ₹1L (≈ ₹0 personal tax up to ₹12L/year) → **₹1L spendable**. The remaining ₹31k − 25% tax = ₹23k/month of reserves.

**Hire-on-demand rule:** a new tutor is hired only when **existing groups average ≥ 3.5 paid students and there is a waitlist of ≥ 8 students**. Tutor count follows demand and never runs ahead of it.

### Fallback path: WhatsApp and Google lead systems for small businesses in the family network
The fallback triggers if the primary path fails its Week-8 gate, or if the student dislikes teaching. Its full plan is in **Part 4B**.

### Explicitly rejected
| Idea | Why |
|---|---|
| Café / cloud kitchen / franchise | ₹20–80L capital, a lease, staff, daily presence |
| D2C product with paid ads | Ad burn and inventory. Family money hides the losses |
| Stock/F&O/crypto trading | Not a business. Most participants lose money. Minors can't trade F&O |
| Creator as the *main* plan | 12–24 months to income, outside the student's control. Use it as a marketing channel later |
| Investing family money in a friend's startup | That is the parents' decision and the parents' money |

## Part 3: Legal, banking, tax and compliance (exact steps)

### Stage A, Weeks 1–12: the founder teaches personally
This stage covers only the founder's **own teaching**. It is skill income, taxed in the student's own hands and not clubbed with the parent's income. (In the 1961 Act this was the s.64(1A) proviso; the CA confirms the 2025 Act section.)

1. **PAN** for the student (Form 49A via Protean/UTIITSL, signed by a parent as representative, ≈ ₹100).
2. **Minor savings account** in the student's name, with self-operated UPI and a debit card. Ask the branch for the UPI and daily limits.
3. **Payment:** UPI in advance for each monthly package.
4. **Records:** a Google Sheet with date, payer, child, sessions, amount and UPI reference.
5. **Tax:** within the ₹12L new-regime rebate, tax is nil. **File an ITR anyway**, because it builds a clean income record for visas and university financial forms.
6. **GST:** not needed at this scale.
7. **School:** email the DP coordinator (activity, hours cap, no-assessed-work rule). Keep the reply.
8. **No one else is paid from this account.**

### Stage B, from Week 9 (the day the Week-8 gate passes): the company
Incorporation **must finish before any tutor teaches**.

**Structure: Private Limited Company.**
- **Two adult directors** (Companies Act 2013, s.149(1)): both parents, or a parent plus a trusted adult relative. At least one must be resident in India. A minor cannot be a director. An OPC cannot have a minor as a member.
- **Shares:** the two adults subscribe to the memorandum. Immediately after incorporation, **transfer or allot 99% of the shares, fully paid, to the student, held through a parent as guardian**. One parent keeps 1%, because a Pvt Ltd needs at least 2 members. The share money is a documented gift from a parent.
- **Why this gives the student real control:** directors legally owe their duties to the company (s.166), so no family agreement can bind them to obey a 16-year-old. But the 99% shareholder (votes cast by the guardian) can remove and appoint directors by ordinary resolution (s.169). Control rests on ownership, not on a promise.

**Incorporation timeline (≈ ₹15,000–₹25,000 all-in via a CA/CS):**
- **Week 9:** DSCs for both directors; name reservation.
- **Weeks 10–11:** SPICe+ filing (including AGILE-PRO for GST/EPFO options, and the bank account).
- **About Week 12:** certificate of incorporation, PAN, TAN.
- **Weeks 12–13:**
  - deposit the subscription money;
  - open the current account;
  - set up the Razorpay/Cashfree gateway on the company's KYC;
  - file the Maharashtra Shops and Establishments intimation.
- **From Week 13, all customer revenue goes to the company**, including the founder's own groups.

**Compliance calendar (the CA/CS runs it; the founder checks it off):**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Within 30 days of incorporation | **First board meeting.** **Appoint the first statutory auditor** (s.139(6)). Board resolution approving the founder-fee arrangement |
| Within 180 days | **INC-20A** declaration of commencement of business, with proof that the subscription money was received. File it as early as possible. Do not rely on the 180 days |
| Monthly, from the first tutor payment | **TDS on tutor fees.** Treat it as professional fees at 10% once a tutor's annual payments cross the threshold (₹50,000/year under current rules; the CA confirms the section and rate under the 2025 Act). A tutor on ₹20,800/month crosses it in Month 3. Deposit by the 7th of the following month |
| Quarterly | TDS return (26Q) and Form 16A to tutors |
| When trailing-12-month turnover reaches ₹15L (≈ Month 6–7) | **GST registration**, then monthly or quarterly GSTR-1/3B |
| Every year | Statutory **audit**, AGM, **AOC-4** (within 30 days of the AGM), **MGT-7/7A** (within 60 days), **DIR-3 KYC** for directors, **ITR-6** with audited accounts |

Budget for this: ≈ ₹8,000–₹12,000/month in professional fees, already included in overhead.

**How the money reaches the student:**
1. **Company profit** (the margin on tutors' hours) is taxed in the company at ~25% (the concessional rate, ≈ 25.17% with surcharge and cess). It is not clubbed with anyone's income.
2. **Before 18: founder fee** of ₹25,000–₹40,000/month for documented work (teaching, curriculum, sales, QA, management). It needs a **board resolution**, because the student is a relative of both directors (related-party arrangement, s.188). Keep a monthly timesheet. Ask the CA to confirm the fee counts as the minor's own-skill income and is not clubbed. If the CA says no, cut it to a token amount and keep the profit in the company.
3. **From 18:** get a DIN, appoint the student as a director, and pay **director remuneration of ₹1L/month** (board resolution, and any shareholder approval the CA advises). This is the job-replacing income.
4. **No dividends** before 18: they would be clubbed. After 18, avoid dividends too, because they are taxed twice. Remuneration is the efficient route.

**Contracts:** tutor and staff agreements are signed by a director on behalf of the company. They cover:
- independent contractor status and the hourly rate;
- the no-assessed-work clause;
- safeguarding and data duties;
- no private poaching of company clients for 12 months.

### The family agreement (one page, signed in Week 1)
1. The business belongs to the student. Adults sign only where the law requires it.
2. Parents advise when asked. They do not hire, price, sell or deliver.
3. Capital comes only through the Part 5 tranches, each logged as a loan repayable from profit or as a gift.
4. If any predicted grade drops by 1 point or more, the business goes to 2 hrs/week until it recovers.
5. A quarterly review of numbers, grades and hours.
6. The directors will consult the student on every business decision and follow those decisions unless that would breach their legal duties to the company. In that case they say so in writing. (This is a good-faith commitment. The binding control is the 99% shareholding.)

#### The customer-acquisition model (how 12 students become 84)

**Need:** from ≈ 12 paid students at end-Feb 2027 to **84 by end-Sep 2027** (6 tutors × 4 groups × 3.5).
- Churn ≈ 5%/month on an average of ~45 students ≈ 16 lost.
- Grade 10 exits in June ≈ up to 15 (with the 30% cap).
- **Gross adds needed ≈ 72 + 16 + 15 ≈ 103 over 7 months ≈ 15/month ≈ 3.5/week.**

| Channel | Volume per month | Assumed conversion | Paid students/month | Who runs it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Parent workshops (2/month, online or society clubhouse, via parents' and PTA introductions) | 2 × 25 attendees | 15% | ~7 | A tutor presents (₹1,500), the ops manager handles logistics, the founder closes |
| Referrals (one free session for both families per paid referral) | ~45 active families | ~10% refer each month | ~4–5 | Ops manager asks at every progress report |
| Free 30-min diagnostic test (from Instagram, housing-society notice boards and WhatsApp groups, never school-run groups) | ~8 trials | 40% | ~3 | A tutor runs it, the founder closes |
| **Total** | | | **~15** | |

- **Cost per acquired student (CAC) cap: ₹3,000.** One student is worth ≈ ₹4,550/month (₹700 × 1.5 hr × 4.33). Marketing is ₹10k/month in Phase 3 and ₹20k in DP2.
- **Weekly enrolment target: 4 new paid students.** It is tracked on the funnel sheet: leads → trials/workshop attendees → paid.
- **If enrolments run below 3/week for 4 weeks:** stop hiring tutors, merge thin groups, and move the founder's sales hours to the strongest channel. **The target date slips; the hours cap does not.**
- **Summer boost:** Jun–Jul bootcamps for 30–40 students, converting about 30% (≈ 10 students) into August groups.

## Part 4B: The fallback plan (if the Week-8 gate fails)

**What it sells:** a **"Lead System" package** for small businesses in the family network. At first it uses only the founder's own skill, so the income is personal skill income paid into the minor account (Stage A rules).

**Fallback Weeks 1–2: niche and demo (≤ ₹5,000)**
- [ ] Pick **one niche** where the family has 10+ contacts: clinics, CA firms, jewellers, wholesale traders, salons, or coaching classes.
- [ ] Interview 10 owners:
  - "How do customers reach you?"
  - "How many enquiries are lost or answered late?"
  - "What do you spend on marketing now?"
- [ ] Build a free demo for one family business, and record before/after numbers (enquiries per week, response time).

**The package** (10–15 hrs per setup):
1. **Google Business Profile:** optimised, with photos, services, Q&A and a review-request link.
2. **WhatsApp Business app** (free): catalogue, quick replies, greeting and away messages, lead-stage labels.

## Part 6: Safety, compliance and integrity (non-negotiable)

1. **Academic integrity:**
   - Teach skills and set practice.
   - **Never** write, edit or "fix" an IA, EE, TOK essay, coursework or homework.
   - This is in the parent terms and tutor contracts. Terminate a tutor on the first breach.
2. **Safeguarding:**
   - 18+ tutors with ID, 2 references, and police verification for in-person work.
   - Online sessions on the company account, with parents able to join.
   - In-person sessions in common areas or with a guardian present.
   - No private DMs with children.
   - Complaints go to an adult director.
3. **Data (DPDP Act 2023 and Rules):**
   - Collect the minimum data.
   - Get verifiable parental consent.
   - No tracking or targeted ads at children.
   - Delete data 12 months after a client leaves.
4. **Child labour:** a 16-year-old running their own non-hazardous enterprise is permitted.
5. **Refunds:** absences get make-ups. Unused future sessions are refunded in full on 7 days' notice of leaving.
6. **CAS:** paid work doesn't normally count. Run a separate **free** weekly session for an NGO or municipal school using the same kit. It counts as Service and becomes a tutor pipeline.
