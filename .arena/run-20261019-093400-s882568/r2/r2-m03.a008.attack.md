ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The "job-replacing income" target is only met on paper: most of the money is locked away and the plan's own numbers fall short of it
Where: Assumptions: "'The student's side' means the fee paid to the student plus the after-tax profit kept in a company the student majority-owns"; Part 3: "allot or transfer 74–90% of the shares ... to the student"; "No dividends while the student is a minor"; table row "Phase 3 target ... ₹1.03L ✔".
Problem: The task asks for income that replaces a job. Under this plan, the only money the student can actually receive and spend before age 18 is the founder fee of ₹25,000–₹40,000 a month. The other ₹60–75k is retained company profit. The plan itself bans paying it out as dividends until the student turns 18, which is around 2028, after the plan ends. Even on paper the number is wrong. The student owns 74–90%, so their share of ₹1.03L of after-tax profit is ₹76k–₹93k, below the ₹1L target the plan ticks as met. Paying it out later also adds dividend tax at the student's slab rate on top of the 25% company tax. So the "after-tax" column is not after tax for the person who receives the money. On a like-for-like test (cash the student can spend, after all taxes), every row of the table misses ₹1L. The plan never says so.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The profit model assumes every group is always full; one empty seat per group wipes out the target
Where: "Group of 4 at ₹700 per student per hr = ₹2,800 per tutor-hour"; "One tutor at 6 group-hours/week ≈ 26 hrs/month"; the Phase 3 target row (5 tutors → ₹1.38L pre-tax).
Problem: Every row assumes all 4 seats are filled and paid in every session, every week, all year. There is no allowance for absences, part-filled groups, or the plan's own Part 6 rule "unused sessions are refunded in full on 7 days' notice". Counterexample: groups that average 3 students instead of 4. Revenue falls to ₹2,100 per tutor-hour, which is ₹1,780 after GST. Subtract ₹800 tutor pay and ₹50 payment costs and the contribution is ₹930. Five tutors × 26 hrs × ₹930 = ₹1.21L, minus ₹60k overhead = ₹61k pre-tax, or about ₹46k after tax. That is less than half the target. No fill-rate assumption or sensitivity check is given, so the user cannot tell how fragile the plan is.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The target window lands right after the biggest customer group leaves
Where: Month-9 gate: "The realistic window for reaching the target is Jul–Oct 2027"; the requirement to hold the target "for at least 2 consecutive months"; Phase 3: "Sell crash courses ... before the May 2027 exam season".
Problem: The customers are Grade 6–10 MYP and IGCSE students. Grade 10 students sit MYP eAssessment and IGCSE exams in May and June 2027 and then leave the product, and crash-course revenue stops with the exams. So the client base shrinks in June and July, which is exactly the start of the window where the plan expects to sustain ₹1.38L/month pre-tax for 2 months in a row. Nothing in the plan replaces the churned students, deals with seasonal demand (summer, Diwali), or plans the timing of enrolment.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] No customer-acquisition plan behind the jump from about 12 to about 88 students
Where: Week-16 gate "≥ ₹50,000 collected" (about 12 students at ₹700 × 1.5 hrs × 4 weeks ≈ ₹4,200 each); Phase 3 "Grow to 5 tutors ... about 22 groups of 4" (about 88 students); founder sales time "2.0 hrs/week"; marketing "≤ ₹10k/month".
Problem: The plan has to grow paying students about 7 times in roughly 5 months. Those months include a 3-week founder freeze for exams and the founder stopping teaching. Phase 1 needed 6 weeks of sales to win 6 students. The plan gives no lead numbers, conversion rate, cost per acquired student or churn rate, and no weekly enrolment targets that add up to 88. The user is told to "grow to 5 tutors" with no way to check whether 2 hours of sales a week and ₹10k a month can produce that demand. This is the step the whole income target depends on, and it is left to guesswork.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The fallback's path to ₹1L breaks the plan's own hour cap and its own definition of income
Where: Part 4B: "12 retainers × ₹8,500 = ₹1.02L/month recurring ... Retainer upkeep for 12 clients is ~18 hrs/month, about 4 hrs/week, so it fits the DP2 cap"; Part 4A: "Zero founder time from January 2028 to the end of May 2028".
Problem: 18 hrs/month is about 4.2 hrs/week. That already goes over the 4 hrs/week DP2 cap before counting any sales, selling 1–2 setups a month, or managing freelancers. It also cannot continue through the plan's own "zero founder time" months from January to May 2028, and the fallback never hands the retainers to anyone else. Separately, ₹1.02L is gross revenue. Once a freelancer is hired, the plan routes the money through the company, so freelancer pay (₹300–₹500/hr), tool costs and 25% company tax all come off it. By the plan's own "₹1L after tax" definition, the fallback does not reach job-replacing income.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The company setup leaves out required steps, which matters because the task asks for EXACT DETAILS
Where: Part 3, Stage B steps (DSC → SPICe+ → COI/PAN/TAN → bank/gateway/Shops Act), and "Both adult directors agree in writing to act as directors on the student's instructions".
Problem: The required post-incorporation filings are missing:
- appoint the first auditor within 30 days (Companies Act s.139(6)); every Pvt Ltd needs a statutory audit;
- file the INC-20A declaration of commencement of business within 180 days, without which the company cannot do business;
- file AOC-4 and MGT-7 every year;
- deduct TDS on tutor fees. A tutor earning ₹800 × 26 = ₹20,800/month passes the ₹30,000/year threshold in the second month, which brings in deduction and quarterly return duties. The plan lists this only as a question for the CA, not as a step.
In addition, the founder fee is paid to the directors' own child, which is a related-party transaction that needs at least a board resolution under s.188. And directors owe their duties to the company (s.166), so a family agreement to act "on the student's instructions" is not binding in the way the plan implies.
