ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The "job-replacing money" is never the student's money, and once it reaches them it falls short of the plan's own ₹1 lakh bar
Where: Assumption 2 ("₹1,00,000 a month in net profit before income tax"), Q10 ("30 June 2028: ₹1,00,000+ a month net profit"), Phase 2 ("your parent's taxable income, not yours yet"), Section 5 table
Problem: The task asks for the student to "make job replacing money". The plan's success test measures *pre-tax profit of a business the student does not legally own*. From April 2027 to June 2028 every rupee is the parent's income under the plan's own Section 5. In a family worth ₹70 crore, that income is likely taxed at the top slab. From July 2028 the profit belongs to a Pvt Ltd company. It pays roughly 25.17% corporate tax (s.115BAA) before any dividend, and the student holds only 90%. Working through the plan's own numbers:
(a) At the stated break-even of "about 225 hours a month" (₹1 lakh pre-tax), the student's share is ₹1,00,000 × (1 − 0.2517) × 0.9 ≈ ₹67,000 a month in gross dividends.
(b) At the full target of ₹1,52,000, the share is ≈ ₹1,02,000 gross. From August 2028 the student is abroad and becomes a non-resident, so Indian dividends carry about 20% TDS (s.115A/195, or a treaty rate). That leaves about ₹82,000 in hand.
(c) Before 18, dividends are clubbed with the parent's income anyway (the plan says so).
So the plan's "₹1 lakh" test can pass while the student receives about ₹0 (to June 2028) or about ₹67–82k (after that). The plan never states when or how much money first reaches the student after March 2027, so the main requirement is only nominally met.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The July 2028 business transfer is presented as paperwork, but as written it triggers capital-gains tax and misstates GST
Where: Section 5, July 2028 row: "Shareholders: you 90% ..., parent 10%. The proprietorship transfers the business to the company under a business transfer agreement ... and GST registration moves to the company."
Problem: (1) Income-tax Act s.47(xiv) is the provision that makes a proprietorship-to-company conversion tax-neutral. It requires the proprietor to hold at least 50% of the company's voting power for 5 years and to receive only shares as consideration. The plan's own 90/10 split (parent 10%) breaks that condition. The transfer is therefore a taxable slump sale under s.50B, and since Finance Act 2021 the full value of consideration is deemed to be the undertaking's fair market value. For a business the plan values at "₹80 lakh+ a year in billings" and about ₹18 lakh a year in profit, the parent faces a real capital-gains bill. The plan never mentions this or budgets for it. (2) GST registration is tied to the PAN, so it cannot "move" to a company with a different PAN. Under CGST Act s.22(3), the transferee must take a fresh registration effective from the date of transfer. The proprietor's GSTIN is then cancelled and its input tax credit is moved via ITC-02. A family following "registration moves" would invoice from the company with no GSTIN. Both points sit on the plan's "fixed date" ownership step, which is the only way the business ever becomes the student's.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The parent-selection rule for GST is incomplete, and it breaks for exactly this kind of family
Where: Phase 0 step 1: "Choose a parent who doesn't already run a sole-proprietorship business under their own PAN. GST turnover is aggregated by PAN ..." and Day 2 of Section 10.
Problem: "Aggregate turnover" (CGST Act s.2(6)) covers *all* supplies under the PAN. That includes exempt supplies such as residential rent, and taxable ones such as commercial rent, as well as consultancy fees, professional receipts and director sitting fees. It is not limited to proprietorship businesses. Counterexample: a parent with no proprietorship who rents out one Mumbai flat at ₹1.75 lakh a month (₹21 lakh a year, very ordinary on ₹70 crore net worth) passes the plan's test. Under the plan's own Q4 reasoning, their PAN is already over ₹20 lakh, so the tutoring business owes 18% GST from its first agency invoice in April 2027. If the parent has commercial rent, they are already GST-registered, and the tutoring income lands inside an existing GSTIN's returns. The plan's "register around October–November 2027" timeline, and the "before GST the margin is higher" cushion, are both wrong for this family. The instruction should be "ask the CA to compute the parent's existing aggregate turnover across all receipts", not "pick the one without a proprietorship".

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The calendar contradicts the plan's own start-date assumption, so for a June-start school the holiday hours and the bootcamp fall in term time
Where: Assumption 1 ("You started DP1 around June to August 2026 ... Nothing in this plan depends on exactly when"); Section 8 ("May – Jul 2027 (holiday) | 25"); Phase 2 header ("25 hrs a week in the May–July holidays"); Phase 2 step 6 ("mid-June–July 2027: the 'DP Head Start' bootcamp ... for students about to start DP1")
Problem: The plan explicitly allows a June 2026 start, which is common for Indian IB schools. For that student, DP1 ends around April–May 2027 and DP2 starts around June 2027. "May–July 2027" is then mostly DP2 term time with EE and IA drafts, not a 25-hours-a-week holiday. The students the bootcamp targets ("about to start DP1") have already started DP1 by mid-June, so the bootcamp's market is gone. Phase 2's tutor onboarding, the bootcamp and the July recruiting round all depend on that 25-hour window. The claim that "nothing in this plan depends on exactly when" is false. The plan needs a branch for a June start (holiday around April–May) versus an August start.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] Path B is mandatory for many users but has none of the "EXACT DETAILS" the task demands
Where: Assumption 5 ("predicted 38 or more ... If you aren't, use Path B in Section 7") and Section 7
Problem: Task: "EXACT DETAILS, steps and plans required." Any student predicted below 38, or one whose school forbids tutoring, is sent to Path B. Path B has:
- no unit economics (no cost per setup, no freelancer pay rates, no margin);
- no fixed costs and no break-even;
- no dated checkpoints ("the same phases" refers to tutoring phases whose milestones, such as tutor counts and DP hours, don't apply);
- no account of how a student with 6–8 hours a week of term time maintains "20 clients × ₹5,000" retainers. Servicing 20 WhatsApp API, website and GBP accounts alone is not feasible in that time, and the hiring needed to do it is never described.
"₹1 lakh a month, less any freelancer costs" leaves the actual net figure unknown. For this group of users the plan is general advice, not an executable plan.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The tutor contracts and checks break on the plan's own minor-age rule
Where: Phase 2 step 3 ("2–3 fresh DP2 graduates ... Contract: a simple independent contractor agreement ... non-solicit clause"; "police verification certificate ... for anyone over 18")
Problem: Fresh DP2 graduates in May–June 2027 are often still 17. By the plan's own Q4 (Contract Act s.11: a minor's contract is void), their contractor agreement, including the non-solicit and "no graded work" clauses, is unenforceable. The PCC rule ("over 18") also means these 17-year-olds tutor children with no police check. Separately, a 12-month post-termination non-solicit is a restraint of trade that Contract Act s.27 makes void or at best uncertain in India. Yet the risk register relies on it as the main defence against "Tutors take clients directly".

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Phase 2 gives conflicting hour budgets for the busiest setup month and leaves agency operations without staff
Where: Phase 2 header ("6 hrs a week in term") vs Section 8 ("Apr 2027 (DP1 exams/IA drafts) | 3"); coordinator hired only in "September 2027"
Problem: April 2027 is when the plan has the student set up the proprietorship, open the current account and gateway, and recruit 3–4 teachers and 3–4 college tutors. One section gives this 6 hours a week, the other 3. In term months up to September, the student alone must handle matching, scheduling, invoicing, parent calls and payment follow-up for about 150 billed hours and 8–12 tutors, plus a second recruiting round. The plan never allocates time to these tasks. A student following both tables cannot tell which budget applies or who does operations before September.
