ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The headline "Level 1 by Q3 2027" has no phase that produces it, and Gate 2 cannot tell the base case from the conservative one
Where: Section 1 "The base case reaches this by about Q3 2027"; Section 8.1 "steady state ... (from about Q3 2027)"; Gate 2 "Revenue run-rate at least ₹1.5 lakh/month"; Phase 4 "DP2, hold not grow"; Section 8.2 "Gate 2 data (May 2027) tells you which case you are in"
Problem: The base case needs proprietorship revenue of ₹3,82,200 + ₹1,28,000 = about ₹5.1 lakh/month: 182 alumni-tutor hours plus one 8-student bootcamp every month. Gate 2 (31 May 2027) only asks for ₹1.5 lakh/month. So revenue has to more than triple between June and September 2027. But June–July is the summer phase, with EE first and effort on DP Readiness cohorts, and from August 2027 the plan bans growth ("No new products, and no growth that costs you time"). No phase in the plan does the 3.4x scale-up. "1 cohort/month" of bootcamps all year also contradicts the plan's own seasons (Section 5 names only Jan–Feb and May–June exam seasons). On top of that, the Gate 2 pass mark (₹1.5 lakh/month) is below even the conservative case's revenue (₹1,91,100 + ₹64,000 = ₹2.55 lakh/month). A business could pass Gate 2 and still be under both cases, so the claim that Gate 2 "tells you which case you are in" is false. The task's core ask, "job replacing money", rests on this number, and the plan gives no steps to reach it on its timeline.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The ₹43k/month personal income and the "arm's-length" founder fee break on the plan's own DP2 hours cap
Where: Section 8.1 "Your own tutoring (your PAN) 3 hrs × 4.33 × 1,400 = 18,186" in the Q3 2027+ steady state; Section 6 "For about 6 hrs/week that works out to roughly ₹960/hr ... defensible as an arm's-length related-party payment"; Phase 4 "≤4 hrs/week", "Your week: ... That is 2.5 hrs", "0–1 hr/week from 15 Feb 2028"
Problem: From August 2027 the cap is 4 hrs/week, and the plan's own DP2 week already uses 2.5 of them on coordination. That leaves 1.5 hrs, so the 3 hrs/week of own tutoring in the steady-state table cannot happen. Personal income drops from about ₹43k to about ₹25–35k. The ₹25,000 fee for 2.5 hrs/week is about ₹2,300/hr, and for 0–1 hr/week from Feb 2028 it is ₹5,800+/hr. Both are well above the student's own ₹1,400/hr tutoring rate, so the plan's own reason for calling the fee defensible (it is "below your own tutoring rate") falls apart. The fee then becomes an excessive payment to a relative that can be disallowed (s.40A(2) of the 1961 Act / its successor). The "≈ ₹43,000 personal income" figure in Sections 1 and 8.3 is therefore wrong for most of the period it covers.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] "Retained profit ... moves to your company" has no working mechanism, and the obvious routes defeat the purpose
Where: Section 6 "Retained profit stays in the proprietorship's account ... and moves to your company under the Memorandum (Phase 5)"; Section 8.3 "The retained profit is held for the transfer to the company you own 75% of"; Memorandum clause 4
Problem: Most of the business profit (about ₹1.06 lakh/month, or ₹12+ lakh/year) is the parent's money. The plan never says how it reaches the student. (a) If it is paid into the company as a gift, it is taxable income of the company, because the "relative" exemption for gifts covers only individuals and HUFs, not companies (s.56(2)(x) of the 1961 Act, carried into the 2025 Act). (b) If it buys shares, those shares belong to the parent, not the student. (c) If it is gifted to the student who then buys shares, any income from it is clubbed with the parent's income (the plan admits this only for the initial capital). (d) A valued asset transfer, which the plan itself recommends in Phase 5 step 2, means the company pays the parent, so the money goes back to the parent. The user is told the business is theirs, but the plan gives no step that legally moves the accumulated value to the student.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The alumni-tutor supply the whole base case depends on is mostly legally unavailable, and the sourcing step breaks the 18+ rule
Where: Section 5 "alumni tutor (IB graduate, 18+, scored 40+ ...)"; Phase 1 step 3 "recruit 3 alumni tutors (school alumni groups, seniors from 1–2 years ahead, LinkedIn)"; Section 8.1 "6 tutors × 7 hrs/week"
Problem: Seniors "1 year ahead" of a DP1 student in Oct 2026 are DP2 students aged 16–17, not 18+ graduates, so the plan's own sourcing instruction breaks its own safeguarding rule. Seniors "2 years ahead" (class of 2026) are in their first year at university, and a large share of Mumbai IB graduates go to the US or UK. Constraint C9 of this very plan says F-1 and UK Student visa holders "cannot actively work" for a business. That applies equally to freelancing for an Indian proprietorship. Screening checks the transcript, a demo and references, but never the tutor's visa or work-rights status. So a parent-operator following the plan could contract tutors who are breaching their visa conditions, and the 42 tutor-hours/week the base case needs is sized on a pool the plan has not checked.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] Incorporating with the minor as a 75% founding shareholder fails as written at SPICe+
Where: Phase 5 step 1 "Incorporate a private limited company ... Shareholders: you, at 75%, holding fully paid-up shares through your guardian"; Memorandum clause 4
Problem: A minor cannot sign (subscribe to) the company's founding documents, its Memorandum of Association, because they lack the capacity to contract (the plan's own C2). A minor can only acquire fully paid shares after incorporation, by transfer or allotment through a guardian. A user who files SPICe+ with the student as a 75% subscriber will be rejected. The correct sequence is: adult subscribers incorporate, then shares are allotted or transferred to the minor through a guardian. The plan defers this to "a CS confirms" instead of stating it. That is a real step the user would get wrong.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Phase 0 is placed in the wrong dates for the Diwali break
Where: "Phase 0: Set up (26 Oct – 8 Nov 2026, Diwali break, ~6 hrs/week)"
Problem: Diwali 2026 (Lakshmi Puja) falls on 8 November 2026, so Mumbai school Diwali breaks run around early to mid November, not from 26 October. The plan schedules the setup phase, which depends on holiday hours, mostly in term weeks, where its own 8 hrs/week cap and exam-week rule apply. Every later date (Phase 1 start on 9 Nov, Gate 1) is keyed off this window.
