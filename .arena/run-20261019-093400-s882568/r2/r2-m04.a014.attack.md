ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Agency prices contradict the plan's own market research, and every income target depends on them
Where: Step 5 "Expect about ₹800–1,500 an hour for student or college tutors and ₹2,000–5,000+ for specialists. Set your price at the 40th percentile ... likely ₹1,000–1,200" versus Phase 2 price ladder "MYP / IGCSE 1:1 ... Parent pays tutor ₹1,800" and "IB DP 1:1 ... ₹3,000", with tutors who are "IB alumni now at Mumbai colleges".
Problem: The agency's tutors are exactly the "student or college tutors" the plan itself prices at ₹800–1,500, yet the agency charges ₹1,800 (20–125% above that band) and ₹3,000 for 18–19-year-old IB grads. All the fee math rests on the ₹2,160 weighted price (₹720 fee/hr). Re-run it at the top of the plan's own band (₹1,200 MYP/IGCSE → ₹400 fee; ₹1,500 DP → ₹500 fee; weighted ₹430). Milestone M becomes 151.6 × 430 − 27,500 ≈ ₹37,700 a month, not ₹81,650. Target 1 becomes 194.9 × 430 − 27,500 ≈ ₹56,300 pre-tax, roughly half of the ₹1 lakh after-tax target. The plan never says why parents would pay a premium over the student-tutor rate, and it has no gate that tests whether they will at ₹1,800. So the headline "₹1.06 lakh a month by late 2028" depends on an unvalidated price that the plan's own data argues against.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] Gate 2 cannot be met with the plan's own funnel, and no further outreach is scheduled
Where: "Outreach list of exactly 30 families"; "Conversion assumption: 30 contacts → 10 diagnostics → 4 paying clients"; GATE 2 "all 5 weekly slots full (4 × 1:1 plus 1 group of 3 = 7 students), and 3+ families waitlisted".
Problem: Gate 2 needs 7 paying students plus 3 waitlisted families, so about 10 converted families. The plan's own funnel turns the only outreach list (30 families) into 4. Between 16 Nov 2026 and 31 Mar 2027 the only other source is one referral request "at session 4". Nothing says when or how to contact a second batch, how many contacts to add, or where they come from. Following the plan as written, the expected result is failing Gate 2. That stalls everything after it, because the agency only starts if Gate 2 passes.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Growing from 7 students to 35 hours, and then to 45 hours, has no client-acquisition plan
Where: Phase 2–3 (Apr–Dec 2027) target "8 tutors, about 20 clients" and 35 hrs/week; Phase 5 "June–August 2028 ... Grow to 45 hours a week"; Target 2 "Personal network under 30% of new clients".
Problem: Tutors are hired against a waitlist, but the plan never says what fills that waitlist. It names no channel, no outreach volume, no conversion targets and no launch date for marketing between April and December 2027 (marketing appears only as a "₹5,000" overhead line). The student has 6–8 hours a week. On top of that, many March–May 2028 clients are IGCSE Grade 10 and DP2 students whose exams end in May–June 2028, so a large share of Milestone M volume leaves just as the plan asks for growth to 45 hours. June–August is also the off-season for tutoring. The plan never accounts for this graduating-cohort churn, so Target 1 has no route to it.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The student's "arm's-length" ₹1,000/hr fee is about 3.6x what the plan pays a coordinator for the same work
Where: "Paying you: the DAH pays you ₹1,000 per logged hour for sales, matching and quality work ... A related-party payment must be at market value, so don't inflate it" versus "coordinator (a college student, about 15 hours a week, ₹18,000 a month)".
Problem: ₹18,000 ÷ (15 × 4.33) ≈ ₹277 an hour for the coordinator, an adult who runs day-to-day operations. The 16-year-old's Phase 4 duties are a "1-hour Sunday review plus at most 2 escalations", paid at ₹1,000 an hour. The plan's own numbers show the payment is not at market value. That undercuts the claim that this is the minor's own skill income rather than income diverted from a parent (clubbing), and it is a s.40A(2)-type related-party disallowance risk for the DAH. The plan marks it [CA] but still builds the ₹15,000-a-month "your own" figure on it.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] Target 2 relies on a ₹37 lakh salary to a part-time student director, and the company has no buffer if it is challenged
Where: Target 2 table "Director salary ₹37.0 L ... Company residual ₹1.1 L"; "GM ₹7.2 L" plus a 7.5% share (₹3.1 L); "The student works 15 hours a week or less" while at university.
Problem: A director is a specified person under s.40A(2)(b) (same rule in the 2025 Act). A full-time university student working 15 hours a week or less is paid ₹37 lakh, while the full-time GM gets about ₹10.3 lakh. On those facts the excess over fair value is open to disallowance. If even ₹20 lakh is disallowed, the company owes about ₹5 lakh of extra tax at 25.17%, but it keeps only ₹1.1 lakh. The ₹2.5 lakh-a-month after-tax figure then fails, or the salary has to drop. The plan presents the salary/dividend split as settled and never flags this.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Phase 2 teaching cap contradicts keeping legacy clients
Where: Phase 2 item 2 "Your own legacy clients stay with you at your own price"; item 5 "Your role: teach at most 2 hours a week"; Gate 2 left the student teaching 5 weekly slots (7 students).
Problem: The student cannot keep 5 hours of legacy teaching and also teach "at most 2 hours". The plan never says which 3 slots, covering about 4–5 students, are dropped or handed to tutors, or what happens to the ₹28,145 a month of Phase 1 income. A user following it has to guess. Phase 4's "Stop your own legacy teaching by 31 March 2028" suggests the teaching was meant to continue, which conflicts with the cap.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Track B's GST reasoning fails for the niche it picks
Where: Track B step 5 "Quote B2B prices plus GST ... registered clients claim it back"; step 6 "specialise in clinics".
Problem: Clinical healthcare services are GST-exempt, so most clinics are not registered and cannot claim input tax credit. For the chosen niche, the 18% is a real 18% price increase on ₹7,500 retainers (₹8,850), not a pass-through. Either the Target 2 revenue of ₹7.5 lakh a month or the plan's margins are overstated.
