ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The weighted ₹590 fee ignores the plan's own 20% and ₹300 fee tiers, so Milestone M fails by its own numbers
Where: Section 6.3 "Weighted fee = 0.7 × 500 + 0.3 × 800 = ₹590 an hour", which is also used for Milestone M, Target 1 and the downside case. Compare the Phase 2 price ladder row "Client the tutor brought in themselves | Same prices | 80% | 20%", the legacy handover "agency fee ₹300", and the Section 6.8 table, which counts tutor-sourced clients as a planned channel (Jun–Aug 2027: 0.5 a month; Sep–Dec 2027: 1 of 4–4.5 a month; Jan–May 2028: one of the three inbound sources).
Problem: Every client the plan's own funnel gets from tutors pays a 20% fee (MYP ₹300, DP ₹480, weighted ₹354), not 33%. By the plan's own table, about 1.5 (Jun–Aug) + 4 (Sep–Dec) + roughly 1 (Jan–May) ≈ 6 of the ~22 Milestone M clients (about 27%) are tutor-sourced. The blended fee is then 0.73 × 590 + 0.27 × 354 ≈ ₹526 an hour. That gives 151.55 × 526 − 30,500 ≈ ₹49,200 a month, which is below the ₹55,000 that Milestone M requires. The headline "₹58,915" and the "₹59,000" in the bottom line are overstated, because the plan never prices the channel it schedules. Target 1 drops to about ₹1,00,000 after tax with no margin left (at ~15% tutor-sourced: 242.5 × 555 − 33,000 ≈ ₹1,01,500 pre-tax), so the claimed "₹1.03 lakh" cushion is gone.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] If the commission model is reclassified, the plan has no fallback, and every target collapses
Where: Section 6.1 "Why the commission model … [CA: confirm both conditions and that s.52/s.24 e-commerce-operator registration doesn't apply.]" and Section 6.7 "DAH's PAN has no GST headroom → … register and plan on ₹45k". The only GST-absorbed variant (₹45,275, and Target 1's "66 hours") puts 18% on the fee only.
Problem: The plan's own facts point toward the agency being the principal. The agency sets the parent's price, books through its own form, owns the parent group and reports, runs the diagnostic, and gives "a free tutor swap" and "continuity if a tutor leaves" (Phase 2 item 2). If the CA (question 4) or a GST officer says it is the principal, turnover is the full billing. The plan itself puts that at "about ₹40 lakh in FY2028-29", so registration is mandatory and GST is 18% of the whole ₹1,770 hour (₹270 an hour) instead of 18% of the fee. Net fee falls from ₹590 to about ₹320 an hour, and Target 1 becomes 242.5 × 320 − 33,000 ≈ ₹44,600 a month pre-tax, less than half the target. The plan's whole income path depends on one [CA] answer, but its risk register only models the milder case (GST on the fee). The real kill scenario has no trigger, no numbers and no response.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The swap and continuity guarantee that justifies the ₹1,500 price can't be carried out under the plan's own money rules
Where: Phase 2 item 2 "A free tutor swap if the parent isn't satisfied after session 2" and "Continuity if a tutor leaves"; Phase 3 item 5 "Parents prepay the tutor for 8 or 12 sessions"; Section 6.1 condition (i) "The agency never collects the parent's money on the tutor's behalf"; risk register "2 strikes and the tutor is dropped and their clients rematched".
Problem: Take a parent who prepays Tutor X ₹12,000 for an 8-session pack, is unhappy after session 2 and is swapped to Tutor Y. ₹9,000 of unused prepayment sits in Tutor X's account. The agency can't move it, because condition (i) forbids touching parent money. It can't make X refund it either, because the coordination agreement has no refund or transfer clause. Tutor Y won't teach 6 sessions for free. The same thing happens when a tutor is dropped for not paying fees or leaves for college. Either the parent loses money, which breaks the promised guarantee, or the agency handles the funds, which breaks the GST structure (Attack 2). The plan gives no exact mechanism, such as a pro-rata refund clause in the tutor agreement, a per-session payment rule, or escrow, and the user can't act on this without guessing.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] Gate P can't reach its own sample size in the window, and Gate 3 can't pass without it
Where: Phase 2 item 4 "Gate P … 1 May – 31 July 2027 … After 20+ diagnostics per arm" and Gate 3 pass condition "Gate P decided"; Phase 2 item 7 "2 hours of diagnostics (about 2 a week …)"; Section 6.8 new clients of 3 a month (Apr–May) and 3–4 a month (Jun–Aug) at the plan's 40% diagnostic close.
Problem: 40+ diagnostics are needed in 13 weeks. The plan's capacity is about 2 a week × 13 = 26. Its funnel (about 3–3.5 new clients a month ÷ 0.40) also gives only about 23–26 diagnostics, so about 12–13 per arm. Even with 20 per arm, a 40% vs 34% conversion difference (the 85% rule) is well inside sampling noise: the standard error of the difference is about 15 points. So the test either isn't "decided" by 31 July, which blocks Gate 3 as written, or it is decided on noise. A wrong "pass" moves every new client to ₹1,800/₹3,000 for no real reason, and the plan's ₹78,616 "upside" rests on that.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The Path A transfer steps fail for students who are still 17 when they leave
Where: Assumptions "The student turns 18 around mid-2028 … If the birthday is earlier or later, move the steps marked 'at 18' to match", and Path A "June–August 2028: Incorporate the Pvt Ltd company before leaving. Transfer the business into it … You resign as director before the visa starts".
Problem: A 16-year-old in DP1 on 19 Oct 2026 can be born as late as Oct 2010. Counterexample: a student born 15 September 2010 who goes to a US university starting late August 2028, or a UK one starting mid-September 2028. They are 17 at departure. They can't take a gift deed of a going concern and re-sign tutor agreements, or be a director of the company. The plan's "move the steps to match" rule pushes the transfer past the departure date it must come before, and the visa rules (Section 6.6) then bar the student from doing it while abroad. No alternative is given, for example the DAH incorporating and gifting shares later, or minor shareholding through a guardian. For a meaningful share of DP1 students on Path A, the plan as written can't be carried out.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Milestone M assumes 35 hours through May, but the plan's own churn model removes exam-year clients in May
Where: Milestone M "35 hours a week and ₹55k+ a month pre-tax enterprise profit for March–May"; Section 6.8 Apr–May 2027 row "(+2 exam-year exits in May)" and churn assumption "In May–June each year, every exam-year client leaves … about 40% of hours". The 2028 table, though, puts all ~9 exits in June.
Problem: IGCSE, MYP eAssessment and DP2 exams are in May, so exam-year tutoring ends in late April or early May. The plan applies this in 2027 but not in 2028. With ~40% of hours gone during May 2028, May runs at about 21–28 hours, not 35, and the March–May average needed for Milestone M is not met even before Attack 1.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Internal counts contradict each other in places the user would plan around
Where and problem:
(a) Working-backwards item 1 says "about 35 tutors" for FY2031-32. The plan's own hiring rule (Section 6.8 "1 per 2.75 active clients") applied to its ~135 clients gives about 49 tutors (217 h ÷ 4.4 h ≈ 49). (c) Section 6.3's FY2027-28 hours use "176 (Apr–Aug, about 8 a week)". The plan's own table says 13 hours a week by May and 16 by August, so the Apr–Aug hours are about 300+. The GST headroom figure for choosing the DAH (₹6.2 lakh, so "a candidate at ₹10 lakh still has room") is understated by about ₹0.8 lakh or more.
