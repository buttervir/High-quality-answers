ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Gate 2 says "Base" for results that the plan's own sensitivity table says miss Level 1
Where: Section 7, Gate 2(b): "Base (both needed) | >=100 (model: 123) | >=Rs3.0 lakh (model: Rs3.44 lakh) | ≈ Rs1.15 lakh/month → Level 1", and Section 1: "Gate 2 (31 May 2027) tells you which case you are in." Compare Section 8.4: "Level 1 holds down to about 12% fewer hours, and fails beyond that."
Problem: The Base thresholds sit about 17-19% below the model, but the plan's own sensitivity says Level 1 fails beyond 12% below. Counterexample: Mar-May averages 103 tutor-hours/month, with the Mar and Apr bootcamps. Revenue is 103 x 2,100 + 2,56,000/3 = Rs3,01,633/month, so both Base conditions pass and the plan projects "≈ Rs1.15 lakh/month → Level 1". But 103 is 16.5% below the modelled 123.3. Using Section 8.4's -Rs1.42 lakh per 10% fewer hours, the Level-1-year profit becomes about 13.77 - 2.34 = Rs11.43 lakh, or about Rs95k/month. That misses Level 1. So the gate that is meant to tell the user a year ahead which track they are on sends them down the wrong track: they keep hiring and spending for Level 1 when they cannot reach it. The Base thresholds would need to be at least about 109 hours and about Rs3.15 lakh to match the plan's own 12% margin.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] "Job replacing money" is counted as the parent's pre-tax profit; the student's actual take is far below Level 1
Where: Section 2.1: "Level 1 is measured on the business: the proprietorship's profit before your founder fee... before income tax"; Section 8.2: "Rs1,14,749/month → Level 1 met"; Section 8.6: "Before the transfer, profit is your parent's."
Problem: The task is for the student to earn money that replaces a job. Of the Rs1.15 lakh in the base case, Rs1.12 lakh is the proprietorship's profit, which the plan itself says is the parent's income taxed at the top slab. At about 39% (30% plus 25% surcharge plus cess) that leaves about Rs68k after tax, and it is still not the student's. After the transfer, the student owns 75% of company profit. That profit is first taxed at about 25.17% (s.115BAA) and then paid out as a dividend: 1,14,749 x 0.748 x 0.75 ≈ Rs64k/month. Before 18 that dividend is clubbed with the parent's income; after 18, if the student is abroad, it carries 20% TDS. In the first 12 months after transfer the deferred business consideration is also paid "out of the company's own profit", and from Aug 2028 the GM takes 10% of operating profit (not modelled). The model also leaves out the company's own audit and compliance cost of Rs40-80k/yr, which Phase 5 itself states. The student's real job-replacing income in the base case is therefore about Rs50-64k/month, and the plan's own Section 8.5 puts it at Rs5-43k/month before 18. That is below the plan's own Level 1 threshold, yet the plan states "Level 1 met".

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Level 1 rests on an unexplained jump in demand during DP2, with no sales owner
Where: Section 8.1: Jun 27 = 100, Jul 27 = 120, Aug 27 = 182, Sep 200, Oct 230, Nov 27 = 273 tutor-hours, then 273 flat in Jan/Feb/Mar 2028; Phase 4: "the coordinator recruits and onboards 3 more tutors... from the waitlist."
Problem: Tutor-hours must grow 2.3x between July and November 2027. In those months the founder has 3.5 hrs/week (0.5 hr of it for sales), ads stay at Rs10k/month, and Phase 3 says DP2 leavers have just dropped out. The 62-hour jump from Jul to Aug happens with only one new tutor. The "waitlist" appears only in Phase 4: no earlier step builds it, sizes it or checks it, and no gate measures it. Gate 2 measures Mar-May, which is peak exam season, so it does not test whether about 32 steady students (273 hrs at about 2 hrs/week each) exist in Nov 2027. Because Level 1 fails at 12% fewer hours (Section 8.4), this unsupported ramp is what the headline result depends on. The user cannot act on "recruit from the waitlist" without guessing where the extra demand comes from and who sells to it.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The "no other business turnover" parent test ignores exempt supplies, so GST registration can be missed
Where: Section 6 step 3: "A parent with no other business turnover (C5)"; C5: "Monitor FY-to-date turnover"; Section 8.1: "FY 2026-27 ≈ Rs6.2 lakh (no GST)."
Problem: Under CGST s.2(6), aggregate turnover per PAN includes exempt supplies, such as rent from residential property. For a family worth US$8M in Mumbai, a parent with flat rent in their own name is likely. Counterexample: the operating parent receives Rs1.5 lakh/month residential rent (Rs18 lakh/yr). FY 2026-27 aggregate turnover is then 18 + 6.2 = Rs24.2 lakh, so registration was required in about Feb-Mar 2027. The plan's "apply at Rs17 lakh in Aug 2027" then leaves about 5 months of coaching revenue (Rs20+ lakh in Section 8.1) supplied while unregistered: 18/118 GST owed out of receipts already treated as profit, plus interest and penalty. The sheet's FY-to-date turnover cell only tracks the proprietorship, so it would never catch this. The test should be "no taxable or exempt supplies on this PAN", or the rent must be counted in the turnover cell.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] The plan breaks its own safeguarding and legal-capacity rules for the founder
Where: Phase 0 step 4, Safeguarding Code: "adult tutors only"; C8: "tutors who are adults only"; Section 3 design rule (b): "needs no contract signed by you"; Section 6 money flow: "Your own tutees pay you by UPI into your account."
Problem: The 16-year-old founder tutors minors 1:1 from Phase 1 (5 hrs/week). That breaks the Code the plan tells them to publish on the website, and it is the exact reason the plan gives for excluding DP2 seniors as tutors ("they are minors"). The founder's own tutoring is also a services agreement between a paying parent and a minor, which the plan's own C2 calls void. Rule (b) is therefore not met for the main personal-income stream. The plan needs either a supervised-session exception written into the Code, or a rule that all the founder's own sessions are contracted through the parent's proprietorship.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Feb-May 2027 operations have no costed owner
Where: Phase 2 hour table: "Sales calls and tutor coordination | 2.0"; the coordinator starts only in June (Phase 3 step 3).
Problem: From Feb to May the business grows to 6 tutors, about 15+ students and 3 bootcamps. It also recruits 3 more tutors, each with an ID check, transcript, work-rights declaration, 20-minute demo and 2 references (Phase 1 step 3 process). Add scheduling, payment follow-ups, tutor invoices and parent reports. The plan gives all of this 2 hrs/week of the student's time. The rest falls silently on the parent operator, unpaid and outside the model. Either the student's hours cap is broken, or the Feb-May profits (Rs1.3-2.1 lakh/month) leave out a real labour cost. The user cannot tell who does this work.
