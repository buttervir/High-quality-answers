ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Voluntary GST registration at incorporation throws away about ₹2.8 lakh and pushes the ₹1 lakh month back 3 months
Where: Section 3.2, "You incorporate in August–September (Phase 2), so register the new company for GST voluntarily at incorporation rather than switching mid-season. The scoreboard therefore uses GST-registered margins from September 2027." Also Section 9: "The profit falls between August and October 2027 ... Do not read it as failure."
Problem: The plan states the rule itself: "aggregate turnover counts every business under the same PAN." The new company has its own PAN, so its turnover starts from zero in September 2027. The ₹12 lakh the parent's proprietorship collected in April–August does not count toward it. The statement that turnover "would cross it around October–November 2027" applies to the parent's PAN, which stops trading at incorporation. Here is the worked example using the plan's own scoreboard, interpolating between month 12 and month 15: Sep about ₹4.0L, Oct ₹4.45L, Nov about ₹4.7L, Dec about ₹5.0L. That totals about ₹18.2L, so the company reaches ₹20L only in January 2028. Prices are GST-inclusive, so registering voluntarily in September hands 18/118 of ₹18.2L, about ₹2.8 lakh, to the government for nothing. That is more than half of the whole ₹5 lakh family cap. It also creates the "profit dip" the plan then tells the user to ignore. Without GST, month 12 at the loyalty step makes IGCSE ₹2,200×0.34−44 = ₹704 and IB ₹3,500×0.34−70 = ₹1,120, about ₹912/hour on average. 156 hours × ₹912 − ₹40k ≈ ₹1.02 lakh. So the plan's own numbers reach the headline target at month 12, not month 15. The plan never puts a cost on the voluntary registration or weighs that cost against the "switching mid-season" reason it gives.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The plan's own growth path fails its own Checkpoint 1 Green test, so the main plan runs on Amber by default
Where: Section 9 decision table, Checkpoint 1 Green: "15+ paying families and 70%+ renewing and ₹2.5L+ total revenue". Scoreboard: Month 6 (Apr 2027) Student-subjects 15. Section 3.2: "40–48 student-subjects is about 30–38 families".
Problem: The plan's ratio is about 0.77 families per student-subject. The scoreboard reaches only 15 student-subjects by April, which is about 11–12 families. Interpolating to 31 March gives about 12.5 student-subjects, about 10 families. Green needs 15+ paying families, so the plan's central forecast lands on Amber. Amber means only ₹50k of tranche 2, a 10-hour coordinator paid from revenue, and no new subjects. Yet every later figure (₹35k fixed costs from April, 12–15 tutors, the month 12/15/18 profits) assumes the Green branch. If the 30 June re-test also comes out Amber, the plan says "stay a small side business through DP2", and the ₹1 lakh path is gone. The user cannot tell which branch the headline numbers belong to, because the gate and the forecast contradict each other.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Salary switches on in May 2028, exactly when the exam-year clients leave, and the forecast never models that drop
Where: Section 3.3 Phase 4, "switch on job-replacing pay ... ₹1,00,000 a month, paid only while the trailing 3-month average pre-tax profit ... is at least that much". Scoreboard Month 18 (Apr 2028): 55 student-subjects, ₹1.2L.
Problem: The target market is "Grades 8–12 on IGCSE, IB MYP and IB DP", and the plan's own bootcamps target the April–May exam peak. IGCSE Grade 10 and IB DP2 students finish their final exams in May and stop buying tutoring. The scoreboard simply grows from 48 to 55 student-subjects with no churn. Worked counterexample: suppose 40% of the 55 student-subjects are exam-year students. In June 2028 that leaves 33 × 4.33 × 0.9 = 129 hours × ₹750 = ₹96.5k − ₹40k fixed = about ₹56k. The trailing 3-month average for May–July falls well below ₹1 lakh. By the plan's own rule, the salary is then cut to that average, so "job-replacing money" fails in the very month it was supposed to start. The risk table mentions seasonality in general terms, but no figure, target or salary rule accounts for the May exit.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The end state (founder at university, business run by a hired manager) leaves no room for the ₹1 lakh salary, and the "nil tax" claim fails if the founder becomes non-resident
Where: Phase 4, "keep the business with a hired general manager and the founder as owner (the most likely option)". Section 6: "₹11.25 lakh of taxable income ... under the ₹12 lakh rebate limit ... income tax is nil". Assumptions: "turns 18 around then [May 2028]".
Problem: (a) A general manager in Mumbai costs real money. At ₹50–70k a month, the plan's month 18 profit of ₹1.2L falls to ₹50–70k, so a ₹1 lakh founder salary would mean running at a loss. The plan calls this "the most likely option", yet never costs it. (b) The plan also says the salary needs a "written role description and time log" to be reasonable. A full-time undergraduate abroad cannot credibly be a whole-time director paid ₹12 lakh. (c) The rebate the "≈ ₹99,800" figure relies on (old s.87A, carried into the 2025 Act) is for **resident** individuals only. A founder who becomes non-resident cannot claim it, so any salary taxed in India pays slab tax: about ₹54,600 a year on ₹11.25L at FY2026-27 new-regime slabs including cess. (d) A 16-year-old in October 2026 turns 18 anywhere between late 2027 and late 2028. If the birthday is August–October 2028, the salary step coincides with leaving for university, so it may never run in India at all. The task asks for money that replaces a job. The plan's only route to that ends in the period the plan itself says is most likely, and that route does not hold up there.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] The fixed costs understate the coordinator the plan itself prescribes, so the month 15 target figure comes out below ₹1 lakh
Where: Section 3.2, "Fixed costs once scaled: Coordinator ₹20,000 ..."; Phase 3, "The coordinator moves to 25 hours a week"; Phase 2, "20–25 hours a week (₹15,000–25,000 a month)".
Problem: By the plan's own pay band, a 25-hour coordinator costs up to ₹25,000, not ₹20,000. Phase 0 also says to quote a professional-indemnity/liability policy at incorporation, but the fixed costs include no premium for it. With the coordinator at ₹25k, month 15 comes out at 187 × ₹750 − ₹45k = about ₹95k. The headline "₹1 lakh+ by 31 January 2028" therefore has no margin, and it fails on the plan's own inputs.
