ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The yearly intake engine cannot hold the 22 DP seats the ₹95,000 steady state needs
Where: Step 6, "To hold 39 students you need about 23 new students every year ... The bridge course: about 16 attendees, of whom about 8 become new DP1 students", and "from August 2028 you run about 33 DP students: the 2027 cohort in DP2 plus about 11 new DP1s"
Problem: The plan's DP intake comes from the bridge, about 8 a year. Its own refill table says about 11 DP2 students leave each year. Two cohorts of 8 is 16 DP students, not 22. Outreach is given "2 hours a week" with no student number attached. The plan also says 11 new DP1s in 2028 in one paragraph and 8 in the other. With 16 DP students (6 fewer, 2 fewer DP groups), fees fall by ₹48,000, GST by about ₹7,300 and tutor pay by ₹21,600. Profit drops from about ₹95,000 to about ₹76,000 a month. That is below the ₹90,000–97,000 job benchmark the plan sets for itself. So the headline "job replacement" number is not supported by the plan's own engine after the May 2029 drop.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The Grade 10 calendar is wrong for Indian IGCSE candidates, so the April revenue and the bridge timing break
Where: Step 3, "IGCSE papers run until about mid-June ... Grade 10 groups end after April. Their families pay for April, which is the revision month", and the bridge default "21 Jun – 3 Jul 2027", and the roster row "Apr 2027 | 9 | ... ₹58,500"
Problem: Cambridge runs a February/March exam series that only India can take, and many Mumbai IGCSE schools use it. For those students, papers finish in March. No family pays a ₹6,500 "revision month" in April after the exams are over, so the 5 Grade 10 fees in April (₹32,500) disappear. The bridge also starts more than 2 months after these students have finished. Separately, many Mumbai IB schools start their academic year in early-to-mid June, so 21 Jun – 3 Jul falls in term for the student and often for the target DP1 students. The plan does give fallbacks (a "jump-start" course, and the helper teaching both batches at ₹24,000 more). But the default dates and the roster table are built on the less likely calendar, and Step 2 never tells the user to check whether their Grade 10s sit the March or the May/June series.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The GST threshold tracker ignores the parent's other supplies on the same PAN, such as residential rent
Where: Step 0, "the financial-year running total of fees, which turns red at ₹17 lakh", and Step 5, "That parent must have no existing GST-registered business"
Problem: Under CGST Act s.2(6), "aggregate turnover" for the ₹20 lakh threshold is per PAN and includes exempt supplies. Renting a home for residential use is an exempt supply. A parent in a family worth about $8M (about ₹70 crore) in Mumbai very often owns let-out flats. Counterexample: the parent receives ₹6 lakh a year in residential rent. The PAN then crosses ₹20 lakh when tutoring fees reach about ₹14 lakh, in about December 2027, not February 2028. The sheet's red cell (₹17 lakh of fees only) fires after registration was already due, so the business is collecting unregistered past the 30-day limit. The plan's only filter, "no existing GST-registered business", does not catch this. The sheet has to add the parent's other supplies, and the CA has to be asked about them before choosing the PAN.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] "From your 18th birthday the profit is your own income" is wrong for a business the plan puts in the parent's name
Where: The answer paragraph, "From your 18th birthday, the profit is your own income ... you keep about ₹95,000 a month in hand". Also Tax, "The year you turn 18: the CA splits that year's income at your birthday", and Step 6, "With the CA, move the GST registration to your own PAN"
Problem: From July 2027 the plan collects all fees in the parent's current account, under the parent's GSTIN, with tutor agreements the parent signs. That profit is the parent's own business income. It is not a minor's income clubbed with the parent's, so it does not become the student's at the birthday. It becomes the student's only after the business is formally transferred. A GSTIN is tied to a PAN and cannot be "moved". The student needs a new registration (they are immediately over ₹20 lakh), a transfer of business, and the parent's registration amended or cancelled. Until that happens, every month stays taxed at the parent's 39%, so the family keeps about ₹58,000, not ₹95,000. Also, the "nil tax at 18" claim assumes ₹11.4 lakh is the student's only income. Once the student turns 18, interest and dividends on investments in their own name stop being clubbed and are added to their own income. For a family this wealthy, any amount over about ₹60,000 a year in the student's name takes taxable income past ₹12 lakh. The s.87A rebate then falls away (there is only marginal relief just above the limit), and the "keep ₹95,000" figure is wrong.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The whole ramp-up falls in the student's DP2 first term, at a workload the plan says is 5 hours a week
Where: Step 4, "You need 27 new students ... about 180 messages ... You need 5 tutors by December", "August to December: about 5 hours a week". Stop rules, "Time: if the business needs more than 5 hours a week in DP2, stop taking new students"
Problem: The student started DP1 in mid-2026 (the plan's own assumption), so August to December 2027 is DP2. That is when IA and EE final drafts are due, and when a student from this background usually applies to universities, with early deadlines on 1 November. In those 5 hours a week the student must:
- send 180 outreach messages and follow them up
- run free trials for about 27 new families
- vet and hire 5 tutors (a demo session and a reference check each)
- set up and run 14 parent WhatsApp groups
- collect fees from 39 families, and do quality drop-ins
The plan gives no time budget per task to show this fits. If it goes over 5 hours, the plan's own Time stop rule halts intake in the middle of the ramp, so it never reaches the 39-student target the profit table depends on.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The 2028–29 bump year can cross the Coaching Centre Guidelines 2024 threshold, and the plan does not mention them
Where: Step 6, "from August 2028 you run about 33 DP students ... 11 DP groups". Step 4, tutors are "IB alumni at Mumbai colleges or on gap years"
Problem: The Ministry of Education's January 2024 Guidelines for Regulation of Coaching Centres apply to coaching more than 50 students. They require registration and graduate tutors, and they bar enrolling students under 16. In the bump year the plan has about 33 DP students plus about 17 Grade 9–10 students, which is about 50, so a few referrals push it over. Its tutors are undergraduates and gap-year students, and its Grade 9–10 students are 14–15. How far this binds depends on whether Maharashtra adopts the guidelines. But the plan tells the user to "treat 2028–29 as a bonus year" without warning them to stay at 50 students or fewer.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] The Step 1 decision rule leaves 1–2 paying families undefined
Where: Step 1, "If 3 families pay by 30 Nov, go on to Step 2. If not, change one thing ... If you still have no paying families by 31 Jan 2027, change the skill"
Problem: The most likely result of a first test is 1 or 2 payers. The plan does not say whether to run a group of 1–2 at ₹6,500 (and on what economics), whether to refund them, or whether to keep them while changing the subject, grade or slot for the other seats. "Change one thing" could strand the students who already paid. The user has to guess at the plan's very first decision point.
