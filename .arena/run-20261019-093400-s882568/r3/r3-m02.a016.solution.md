# From IB DP1 to Job-Replacing Income: An Exact Plan for a 16-Year-Old in Mumbai

## Assumptions

- **Today is 19 October 2026.** The student started DP1 in mid-2026, so IB final exams fall in **May 2028**. A 16-year-old on 19 October 2026 was born between 20 October 2009 and 19 October 2010, so **the student turns 18 somewhere between late October 2027 and October 2028**, and 21 between late 2030 and October 2031. Every step marked "at 18" happens in the month of the actual birthday. Two consequences are handled explicitly: the business is always in the student's own name before Target 1 (January 2029), because the latest possible 18th birthday is October 2028; and a Path A student may still be 17 on departure, which has its own transfer route (Phase 5, Path A).
- **"Job-replacing money"** means money the student can legally call their own and live on instead of a salary, **after tax**. There is one milestone and two targets:
  - **Milestone M (checked 31 May 2028, end of IB):** the enterprise makes **₹50,000+ a month pre-tax, averaged over February–April 2028**, at base-case prices. The expected figure is **₹53,500** (₹71,000 only if the premium-price test in Phase 2 passes, which is never assumed). The window ends in April on purpose: exam-year clients (IGCSE Grade 10, MYP 5, DP2) sit their exams from late April and leave during May, so May is already the start of the trough. Before 18, most of this is legally a family adult's income (Section 6.1). So it is a business milestone, not yet "your money".
  - **Target 1:** **₹1,00,000 a month after tax, in the student's own name**, for 3 months in a row. **Due January–March 2029** (Path I below). That window is chosen on purpose: it is the peak pre-exam tutoring season, and it falls after the May 2028 exam-year churn and the summer rebuild (Section 6.8).
  - **Target 2:** **₹2,50,000 a month after tax** (₹30 lakh a year, roughly a top-tier fresher package), with the student working 15 hours a week or less. Pay comes as a market-rate salary plus dividends, so it doesn't depend on a salary a tax officer could challenge. **Due FY2031-32, by 31 March 2032** (Path I).
  - **Path I** = university in India, so the student can keep working. **Path A** = university abroad on a UK/US student visa, which in practice means the student cannot work in the business (Section 6.6). Path A has its own, lower numbers. **The university choice is the biggest single driver of this plan's income, so it's shown openly, not hidden.**
- **Currency:** ₹88 = US$1, so the family's US$8M is about ₹70 crore.
- **School comes first.** The IB Diploma decides university options, and it can't be redone cheaply. Business-time caps (all work included, travel too): **8 hours a week in DP1 term time, 15 in summer 2027 after the EE, 6 from Sept–Dec 2027, 4 from Jan–May 2028.**
- **Indian law and tax** are stated as I understand them for late 2026:
  - Income tax, new regime, slabs as set by Budget 2025: 0–4L nil, 4–8L 5%, 8–12L 10%, 12–16L 15%, 16–20L 20%, 20–24L 25%, above 24L 30%. Add 4% cess. A rebate makes resident income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free.
  - Domestic companies: 25.17% all-in under the 22% regime.
  - GST: the registration threshold for services in Maharashtra is ₹20 lakh.
  - Items marked **[CA]** or **[Lawyer]** must be confirmed by a professional before you act. The exact questions are in Section 7.
- **Price evidence.** The plan's own market check (Step 5) expects ₹800–1,500 an hour for student or college tutors and ₹2,000–5,000+ for specialists. **Every base-case number in this plan uses agency prices inside that evidence:** ₹1,500 for MYP/IGCSE (the top of the student-tutor band) and ₹2,400 for DP (the low end of the specialist band). Higher prices are treated as an upside that has to be proven by a test (Gate P), never assumed.

## PASS 1: The answer in one paragraph

Don't spend family money to "buy" a business. At 16 your real assets are what you know, the network you live in, and a safety net that lets you take risks, not the ₹70 crore. Start a **skill-based academic coaching business**: online tutoring for MYP and IGCSE students. It needs under ₹15,000, fits inside 8 hours a week, and its income legally counts as your own. Then turn it into a **tutor-matching agency** staffed by vetted IB graduates. Parents pay the tutor ₹1,500 (MYP/IGCSE) or ₹2,400 (DP) an hour **directly**. The tutor pays the agency a one-third coordination fee (20% for clients the tutor brings in, and every number below prices that tier in). Because only that fee counts as the agency's turnover, GST registration isn't needed until about mid-to-late 2029. That rests on one professional answer (is the agency an agent or a principal?), which is asked in November 2026, before the agency exists, and the plan carries a costed fallback if the answer is "principal" (Section 6.1).

Until you turn 18, a family adult holds the agency layer, because a minor can't contract. That adult pays you market rates for your work: ₹600 per diagnostic and ₹300 per admin hour. The timeline:
- Validate the business in November–December 2026.
- Fill 5 weekly slots, plus a waitlist, by March 2027, using four scheduled outreach batches.
- Hire tutors from April 2027, and keep them busy with a written acquisition plan: a monthly parent webinar, a referral credit and tutor-sourced clients.
- Hand operations to a coordinator by September 2027.
- Freeze growth for IB exams from January to May 2028.

That gets you to **Milestone M**: about ₹53,500 a month pre-tax across the enterprise in February–April 2028. At 18 the adult gifts you the business. Then you rebuild after the May exam-year exits. With a full-time summer and a 5.5-clients-a-month autumn it reaches 60 tutor-hours a week, and **Target 1: ₹1.03 lakh a month after tax**, in January–March 2029. If you study in India, it reaches **Target 2: ₹2.5 lakh a month after tax** in FY2031-32. That runs on about ₹2.5 crore of bookings with a GM, paid as a ₹4.2 lakh salary plus dividends. If you study abroad, you can only be a passive owner. The business then pays roughly ₹34–40k a month, and the targets move to the years after you graduate. Every stage has a numerical **kill/pivot test**, and there is a costed **fallback business** (Track B) if the first test fails.

The rest of this document works backwards from the end state, then gives the forward steps, then goes deep on the parts most likely to break.

### Phase 2: First tutors, April – August 2027 (8 hours a week in term, up to 15 in summer after the EE)

1. **Set up the agency layer under the DAH** (after the CA meeting, Section 7). The DAH signs the tutor coordination agreements. You run the agency day to day and log your hours. The DAH pays you ₹600 per diagnostic and ₹300 per admin hour.
2. **Commission price ladder** (parents pay the tutor; the tutor pays the agency):

   | Level | Parent pays tutor/hr | Tutor keeps | Agency fee | Fee % |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | MYP / IGCSE 1:1 | ₹1,500 | ₹1,000 | ₹500 | 33% |
   | IB DP 1:1 (tutor has a 7 in the subject) | ₹2,400 | ₹1,600 | ₹800 | 33% |
   | Small group (3), MYP/IGCSE | 3 × ₹800 = ₹2,400 | ₹1,400 | ₹1,000 | 42% |
   | Client the tutor brought in themselves | Same prices | 80% | 20% | 20% |

   **Why a parent pays ₹1,500 to the agency rather than ₹1,000–1,200 to a random student tutor:** this is the top of the student-tutor band, not above it, and the parent gets something a lone tutor doesn't offer:
   - A tutor with a **verified** 7 in the subject.
   - A free diagnostic with a written plan.
   - A 3-line report after every session.
   - A mini-test score trend.
   - **A free tutor swap if the parent isn't satisfied after session 2.**
   - Continuity if a tutor leaves.

   Tutors still earn ₹1,000 an hour, which is at or above what they'd charge alone, so they accept the 33% fee.

   **Rules:**
   - The agency invoices each tutor on the 1st for last month's logged hours. Payment is due by the 7th.
   - A tutor who hasn't paid gets no new matches, and their existing clients are offered another tutor.
   - Parents book through the agency form. All reports go through the agency's parent group, so the agency owns the relationship.
3. **Your legacy clients (fixes the 2-hour teaching cap):**
   - **You keep** the Saturday group of 3 (1 hour) and one 1:1 (1 hour). That is exactly 2 teaching hours, at your own price, under your own PAN: (3 × ₹700 + ₹1,100) × 4.33 = **₹13,856 a month**.

4. **Gate P, the price test (1 May – 31 July 2027):**
   - Quote new leads alternately, by odd or even lead number in the Funnel tab. Odd numbers get ₹1,500 MYP/IGCSE and ₹2,400 DP. Even numbers get ₹1,800 and ₹3,000.

## PASS 3 (part 3): Deep dives on the parts most likely to break

### 6.1 Legal structure while you're under 18

- **Contracts.** Under Section 11 of the Indian Contract Act 1872 a minor can't contract. In *Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose* (1903) the Privy Council held a minor's agreement void. You can't be sued on your agreements, but you can't enforce them either.
- **What you can do in your own name:** deliver your own prepaid services and receive payment into your own account. Prepayment means you never need to enforce anything.
- **What needs an adult:** engaging tutors and freelancers, issuing invoices with enforceable terms, and holding retainers. This is the **Designated Adult Holder (DAH)**.
- **Choose the DAH by three tests, in this order:**
  1. **GST headroom.** GST registration and the ₹20 lakh threshold work on **aggregate turnover per PAN across India, including exempt supplies.** Examples: a doctor's healthcare fees, rent from a residential let, any existing consultancy or proprietorship receipts. Salary is not a supply and doesn't count. Ask the CA to compute each candidate's aggregate turnover. **[CA]**
     - The agency's fees are about ₹6.2 lakh in FY2027-28, and about ₹1.8 lakh in April–May 2028 before the gift.
     - So a candidate already at ₹10 lakh of such turnover still has room (₹16.2 lakh in total, under the ₹18 lakh trigger). A candidate at ₹12 lakh or more doesn't.
  2. **Tax slab.** Agency profit is taxed in the DAH's hands. A salaried or non-earning parent or grandparent with total income under ₹12 lakh may pay little or no tax on it. A parent in the 30% bracket with a surcharge pays up to about 39%.
  3. **Reliability.** The DAH signs agreements, holds the bank account, and must actually gift the business at 18.
- **If no candidate has existing aggregate turnover under ₹12 lakh:** the DAH registers for GST and the agency absorbs 18% on its fee. The base case then falls to ₹45,275 a month (Section 6.3).
- **Why the commission model:**
  - If the agency billed parents as principal, its turnover would be the full average hourly price of about ₹1,770. That is about ₹18.7 lakh in FY2027-28 and about ₹40 lakh in FY2028-29, well over the threshold, and GST would apply to every hour.
  - In the commission model the agency's supply is a coordination service to tutors, so its turnover is only the fee: about ₹6.2 lakh in FY2027-28 and about ₹13.3 lakh in FY2028-29.
  - Each tutor's own receipts (about ₹3–5 lakh a year at 4–6 hours a week) stay under the threshold too.
  - **Two conditions keep this clean:**
    - (i) The agency never collects the parent's money on the tutor's behalf. Collecting it could make it look like a principal or an e-commerce operator.
    - (ii) Agreements and invoices describe it plainly as a coordination fee.
  - **[CA: confirm both conditions and that s.52/s.24 e-commerce-operator registration doesn't apply.]**
- **Paying you (arm's-length rates):** a related-party payment must be at market value, or the DAH's deduction can be challenged and the payment looks like the DAH's income routed to you. So each task is priced at its own market rate:
  - **Diagnostics: ₹600 each.** A diagnostic is a 30-minute teaching session plus about 15 minutes for the written plan. ₹600 for about 45 minutes is ₹800 an hour, *below* the ₹1,100 an hour parents already pay you for your own sessions. That is the market price of your teaching skill, evidenced by your own invoices.
  - **Admin, matching and quality work: ₹300 an hour.** That is the same as the coordinator (₹18,000 ÷ 65 hours ≈ ₹277 an hour), rounded up for after-hours scheduling.
  - Both are skill income, so they're yours and not clubbed. Keep a timesheet and a diagnostic log. **[CA]**
  - **The result:** your pre-18 income from the agency is small (about ₹10,000 a month in Phase 2 and about ₹4,550 in Phase 4). That's on purpose. The value comes to you through the gift at 18, which is tax-exempt, not through an inflated fee.
- **Company, partnership, LLP:**
  - A minor can't practically be a director.
  - Under Section 30 of the Indian Partnership Act 1932, a minor can only be admitted to the benefits of a partnership.
  - An LLP partner must be able to contract.
  - **So no company before 18.**
  - A Pvt Ltd needs at least 2 shareholders and 2 directors, with at least one director resident in India, so a parent holds 1% and is the second director.
- **At 18:** the business is gifted to you (Phase 5). In Path I it stays a proprietorship until FY2029-30, because at ₹13.2 lakh of profit, personal slab tax (about 6.2%) beats the company route (25.17% + dividend tax). Incorporate when you hire the GM, need limited liability, or go abroad.

### 6.6 University and visas

- **UK Student visa:** the conditions prohibit self-employment and business activity. Running your proprietorship is self-employment, and acting as an active director is risky.
- **US F-1:** unauthorised off-campus work is prohibited, and actively running a business while in the US is generally treated as a risk. Passive shareholding is generally fine.
- **Therefore, in Path A:**
  - (a) Incorporate and transfer the business **before departure**.
  - (b) Promote and incentivise an ops manager.
  - (c) Resign as director and stay a shareholder.
  - (d) Get one immigration-lawyer consultation for your destination before you go **[Lawyer]**.
- **In Path I** none of this applies. You can work 12–15 hours a week, which is what makes Targets 1 and 2 reachable on time.
